Artificial intelligence

AI is a catalyst.
Not a reagent.

A catalyst lowers the energy a reaction needs — it does not change what the reaction is. The first randomised trials say exactly that: designed well, AI multiplies teachers and tutors; designed lazily, it makes practice look brilliant and learning quietly worse.

Without With the catalyst

It accelerates.

Across 50 controlled evaluations, intelligent tutoring moved the median student from the 50th to the 75th percentile — the reaction runs faster when each child gets a patient, adaptive step-by-step partner.

It does not create.

The machine has never beaten the human tutor — it approximates one (d = 0.76 vs 0.79). What it creates is supply: expertise where there was none, at the price of electricity.

It needs guardrails.

The same model that lifts practice 127% with teacher-designed guardrails drags unassisted exam scores 17% below control without them. The design of the interaction — not the model — decides whether children learn or outsource.
