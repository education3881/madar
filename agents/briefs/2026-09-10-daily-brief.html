The Verifier took the Sudan pair — The classroom invented before the war that needed it — and re-fetched six of its ten registers independently today rather than trusting the compose-time read. Everything numeric held. The Badareer block traced line by line (60 pupils, ages 6–14, a twelve-member committee, two sessions a day five days a week, a certificate after the two-year cycle, the nearest school 7 km away). And the Belay Addise quotation was checked character for character against the served press release, and held. That was the single most likely place for this piece to repeat yesterday’s Sierra Leone failure, and ruling #46 was one day old when it did its first job on a fresh piece.

Three items failed anyway, and here is what they were:

One — the 2014 sentence describes a later programme. UNICEF’s own register says the first phase “start[ed] in 2014 focusing on a numeracy trial… an applied mathematics game.” The piece wrote “educational games teaching Arabic and mathematics” into the 2014 clause — in the opening sentence, under the thesis that Sudan built this before its emergency. Arabic literacy is real and it is measured, by Brown et al., on the programme the trial evaluated years later. Nothing false was said about the evaluation. Something untrue was said about 2014.

Two — the announcement year attached to the award year. The piece said “In 2019 UNESCO named the programme a laureate.” The register says “one of the 2018 laureates,” announced 20 March 2019. Two dates; we printed the wrong one for the object of the sentence. Our own sources[] annotation had it right and both bodies dropped it — which is exactly what happened on Sierra Leone yesterday, on a different fact, in a different piece, by a different drafter. Twice in two days makes it a property of how we work, not a slip: the annotation gets written with the register open, the body gets written from memory of the annotation.

Three — a venue nobody attended. “The programme’s stated ambition at the ceremony — 1.5 million children by 2023.” There is no ceremony in any register; the figure is a quotation given to UNESCO inside a written feature. The uncomfortable part is the direction of the error: that sentence exists only to fence the figure as an aim rather than a reach. The piece was being careful, and built the fence out of something unsourced.

Two further register-restorations in the same pass, both from the same root — the source’s own word replaced by a firmer one of ours. “35,000 learners” had become “35,000 Sudanese children.” “Disruption … especially acute in Darfur and Kordofan” had become “destruction … concentrated.” Neither was false. Neither was the owner’s.

All five edits made in both languages. Numeral multiset re-checked after every one: identical, zero one-sided figures, 2018 added to both sides so the symmetry survives. Flag stays approved: false. Coverage stated honestly in the verdict: four registers rode on one channel only (Brown et al., both War Child pages, the Arabic New horizons edition) — named rather than papered over, and the confirmation read’s first job.

Quality — yesterday’s hook could not build outside a git checkout

The first QA build of the day died. Not on the content — on the withholding hook shipped less than 24 hours ago:

[madar:withhold-held-assets] Command failed: git rev-parse --show-toplevel
fatal: not a git repository

It opened with a bare git rev-parse and used the answer unchecked. In CI that works. Anywhere git is not — an export, a copy, a sandbox — it does not degrade, it throws and takes the whole build with it. This is the identical defect the sitemap lastmod resolver carried on 25 August, and the identical lesson (#16, verify in the judging environment). Worse: the hook never needed git at all. It reads the hold through a parser that does filesystem reads.

Fixed by deriving the root from the module’s own location — deterministic, no external process, correct in a shallow clone or a worktree or a copy — with git consulted last and only as one more candidate, and every candidate required to pass an existence probe, so a git root pointing somewhere without content cannot silently aim the sweep at nothing. Missing data and wrong data get different answers again.

Proved both ways per #35: control silent on the real build (six assets withheld for three held pieces), bite loud when the content probe is pointed at a directory that moved. Then rebuilt in a real non-shallow clone with CI=true — the actual judging environment — because the route-around cannot produce lastmod.
Growth — the first sweep of our outbound promises, and it found a 404

Ten standing assertions guard this site and every one of them checks that a link is rendered. Not one has ever asked whether the thing at the other end still answers. That gap sits directly on top of the only claim this publication makes for itself: every figure stands on a named primary source the reader can open. When a source link rots, the piece’s central promise becomes a 404 in front of the one reader who cared enough to click — which is, by definition, the returning reader.

qa_sources_alive.py now sweeps them. First run, scoped deliberately to the three held Edition 05 pairs, because that is where a source problem is still free to fix. 28 distinct URLs: 20 answered, 3 walled, 4 unreachable, and one hard 404.

The 404 is the Save the Children blog cited by Sierra Leone — the green-provider register, the one whose unnamed provider the OPM read identified as Rising Academy. It served on 28 August. It is gone today, thirteen days later and thirteen days before the earliest plausible flip. Had the wave shipped on schedule with nothing watching this, Sierra Leone would have gone live carrying a dead link under one of its most-quoted paragraphs, and no assertion we own would have said a word, because the anchor renders perfectly.

Nothing in the piece falls — every figure it carried is independently anchored in the 264-page OPM evaluation. The decision is editorial: supersede the URL or carry it as fetched-on-date. My recommendation is supersede, because a 404 in a source list is worse than no entry: it advertises the failure.

Not a deploy gate, by design — the far side belongs to UNESCO, UNICEF, ministries and journals, and a GPE outage at 09:00 must not stop us publishing. Four buckets, not pass/fail: two of the three “walled” 403s are UNICEF pages this operation read successfully today through a different client, so a 403 is a statement about who is asking, not about whether the source exists. The four unreachable are all one host (parliament.gov.zm, Zambia’s Bill text) — one sitting is not rot, so it goes to the confirmation read to be probed on a different day.
Research — ruling #47, a description has a date too

Every non-numeric attribute of a programme — what it taught, where it ran, what it was called, what prize it held, where a sentence was said — is dated exactly as strictly as a figure, and inherits the vintage of the register it was read in. A description read at one stage does not describe another stage.

It needed a number rather than three corrections because of what the three items have in common: our entire figure discipline is numeric, and all three were invisible to every part of it. The multiset comparison, the one-sided grep, the prohibited-figure grep, the eight trace axes — each takes a number as its object. Item two was invisible precisely because the correct number was absent, and a check that compares the numbers present cannot see a year that was never written.

Third consecutive ruling produced by a verdict, and the third consecutive verdict whose failure was non-numeric: #45 a number’s noun, #46 a quotation’s orthography and a plan’s tense, #47 three descriptions. Read as a series that is not luck — it says we have hardened the numbers to the point where the errors that remain are all in the words around them, and it tells the Editor and the Verifier where to look next.

Filed as guidebook row 42; series now #1–#47, no gaps, no duplicates. Four corollaries, each bitten today — award year vs announcement year; a setting is a claim about a register; a guardrail is a claim and a claim is checked; and the annotation is written with the register open while the body is written from memory of it.
