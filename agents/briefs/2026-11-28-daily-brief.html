Today · Content

The Verifier failed the Uzbekistan piece on its central claim, and it was the right call. The largest fall in the release, in the only unit that recorded it was one cell from the flag. Its spine was that PISA 2025 measures Uzbekistan’s movement in students but never in score points — that a reader who wanted the figure behind the report’s own “scores also went up” would not find one.

Both archived captures were re-fetched from this runner and both provenance hashes reproduced to the byte. Every figure in the piece was correct. Every quotation was verbatim. And then all fourteen numbered tables of the chapter were parsed and every cell reading Uzbekistan was listed. Two tables print exactly the figure the piece said does not exist: Table I.2.12 gives +85 score points for girls and +80 for boys, Table I.2.10 gives +85 for disadvantaged students and +87 for advantaged — and Uzbekistan is the largest row in each, against an OECD average of −2 and −3.

The drafter had opened the two tables that print the change whole, found them genuinely empty, and generalised to the unit. The two that print it split are gated only on 2022 and 2025 being comparable, while the whole-change table is gated on all three subjects. So a system can be comparable in science and still absent from the table that reports science. That is the repaired piece, and it is a harder finding than the one it replaces: the chapter reports Uzbekistan’s largest movement only through a characteristic of the students it happened to, and never once for the whole cohort.

Repaired in both editions in the same run — title, dek, opening frame, two sections, one paragraph of the gender section, and both sources[] blocks. The Arabic gate was re-run on the changed passages and approved with three corrections. Quoted spans after the repair: English 18 of 18, Arabic 19 of 19 verbatim. Deks re-counted at compose, not at the gate — 198 and 183 of 200. The hero still was not redrawn, and the reason is on file: its two dotted empty rectangles are the two tables that really are empty.

Ruling #162 — a claim of absence inherits the edge of the enumeration behind it. A positive claim is bounded by the span quoted and one comparison settles it. A negative claim is bounded by the drafter’s search, which the sentence never states. A negative claim has no second operand, so no instrument this operation owns could ever have reached this — there is no byte to put beside a nothing. From today a claim of absence ships with its enumerated population in sources[] or it does not ship, and for a piece whose spine is an absence, enumerating the population is the verification.

Saudi Arabia stays held for tomorrow, and this run did not re-take that decision. Yesterday’s run wrote its reasoning into the ledger precisely so today’s would not re-decide it. Uruguay is at six of eight, its Verifier verdict owed next. The Abu Dhabi row is parked to Edition 10 and cost no run time.

What the morning’s failure actually says, and it is not that a gate was missing. The gate ran where it was wired, failed correctly, and printed the file and the line. What is missing is a step: the rule that made this operation commit at the close of every function moved delivery onto a faster clock and left verification on the old one, so the operation now pushes five times a day and builds no more often than when it pushed once. Raised as queue item 168 — a pre-commit hook for the cheap offline subset, or a written statement that the agent identity cannot run one.

And the shape of it is the part worth your attention. The defect was not in a piece, an asset or a template. It was in a sentence about a held piece in our own bookkeeping, and it took a finished, verified, approved piece off the origin for eleven hours. A ledger sits in the build’s dependency graph, and nothing about writing a ledger row feels like touching the publication.
