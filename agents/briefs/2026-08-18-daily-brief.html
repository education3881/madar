Content — Ed05 recon 8 · Rwanda · the East Africa slot
The slate's regional balance was West, North and continental with nothing from East or Southern Africa. Rwanda closes the first of those, and under the positive posture it turns out to be the most instructive accomplishment the edition has found — because the accomplishment is bookkeeping.

Rwanda built classrooms faster than it could staff them. So it relaxed the hiring bar on purpose: secondary-school graduates with subject knowledge but no teacher training went into classrooms, and roughly 25,000 of them were carried on the payroll under a category the system invented for them — uncertified — with the training obligation written down beside the job. Then it discharged the obligation. From April 2024: four condensed college modules taught on weekends and public holidays so no lesson was lost, 36 facilitators training 600 trainers across all 30 districts, peers teaching peers against observation checklists, childcare provided at the venue so mothers could attend, and an examination at the end, set by the assessment authority, that a teacher could fail. 24,000 certificates. And from September 2026 — three weeks from now — teacher training college goes from three years to five, so the debt does not recur.

The frame is incur, disclose, retire, and the question it puts to every system expanding access faster than it can staff it is not whether to take on quality debt — that is often unavoidable — but whether you write it down where someone can see it.
Three gaps close before commission, named in the recon rather than papered over: the funnel (one number, 24,000, standing in for five — enrolled, sat, passed, certified, still uncertified), the denominator (24,000 out of what workforce?), and the date on one load-bearing page. Voice tier is unusually complete — a named primary teacher, quoted at length, with something at stake — and unusually single-channel: every voice reaches us through the funder's or the ministry's own publication. The recon says so.
Research-to-learn — guidebook row 25 · ruling #32
A credit line is not an output line; an attribution is a figure. The World Bank's account of the programme says that under its project the ministry "completed construction of over 22,000 classrooms." The same partnership's own progress note credits the project with "over 11,000 classrooms." Both are true of different things — the national programme built ~22,505, financed by a government contribution alongside the loan — and a draft quoting the first sentence would credit a loan with twice what it built.

Why it earns a number rather than a note: our seven figure axes all ask what the number counts. None asks whose number it is. An attribution can be false while every underlying figure is correct — right value, right population, right vintage, right scope, wrong agent. It passes every check we own.

And it will recur, because of your own posture. Nobody claims credit for a failure. In a lament, attribution is contested downward; in an accomplishment, it is contested upward — ministry, financier, implementing agency and donor each hold a legitimate share and each writes the largest true sentence available to it. Not fabrication: correct figures orbiting an unstated denominator of credit. Every story on the Africa slate is co-financed.
Series now #1–#32, no gaps, no duplicates.
