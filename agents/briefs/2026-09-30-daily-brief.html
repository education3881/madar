Today

Wednesday 30 September 2026 · Asia/Dubai
Content · the Verifier’s verdict on Rwanda: six items, all closed
Edition 05 is fully verified. Six pieces drafted, six composed in Arabic, six approved, six independently checked. There is no production work left on this edition — everything that remains is a judgement, a final link check, or the publishing gate itself.
The check was done the hard way on purpose: all eight sources re-downloaded today, both government yearbooks re-extracted with a tool written for this check rather than the one the writer used, and thirty-one figures traced twice each by different routes. Every figure was correct — including the two 4MB PDFs, which came back byte-for-byte identical for the fourth day running. The problems were all in the prose and the citations.
The best of the six · a quotation that widened
The Rwandan ministry wrote, of untrained teachers, that the number “declined only marginally.” Our article quoted that correctly — and attached it to the wrong number. The ministry’s sentence is about primary school teachers, where the count fell; our article put it immediately after the combined figure for two school levels, which rose. A careful reader would have hit an apparent contradiction with no way to resolve it.
The giveaway was in the ministry’s own sentence: it names the two primary-school figures in the same breath, and our quotation stopped just before them. We have spent a fortnight hunting for hedges that get dropped. This is the opposite — a hedge that survives perfectly and then travels to a number it was never about. Fixed in both languages by naming which column the ministry was describing.
The most uncomfortable of the six · a claim about ourselves
The article says, in the section where it admits what it could not find out: “Every voice in the record for this story reaches us through the funder’s channel or the ministry’s own.” That is the piece’s most serious admission, and it is disproved by the same article eight paragraphs earlier, where a minister’s words reach the reader through a Kigali newspaper — something the piece says out loud, twice. The same newspaper page also carries two named teachers reacting to the reform, one of them raising the very objection our piece leaves unvoiced.
The sentence came from the commission document, which instructed the writer to state it. Nothing downstream could catch it, and that is the finding: every quality check we have takes a single source as its unit, and this claim’s subject is the whole set of sources. Narrowed to what we actually checked — nine voices, all inside the two channels — and the true sentence is two words shorter than the overstated one. Written up as a new standing rule.
Clock item A · open ten days, closed today
The Zambia piece still said “Zambia goes to a general election in August 2026” — a prediction about something that has now happened, in a paragraph arguing the law was not campaign material. We refused to fix this from memory. Today we opened and read two documents: the Zambian Electoral Commission’s own public notice, which gives the polling date as Thursday 13 August 2026 in three separate notices, and the EU observation mission’s own update, which refers to “the day after elections.”
The two are doing different jobs and the citations say so: a nomination notice is written before a poll, so it establishes the date but not that the vote happened; the observers establish the second half. We state no result and took none — the paragraph’s argument works whoever won, which was always the point of it. The commission’s own results website is down today, and we say so rather than working around it.
Clock item B · open ten days, and it turned out to be three
The continental piece closed by saying the ruler is “re-read in December.” Its own source says the last quarter of 2026. Narrowing three months to one, in the closing line of the piece whose whole argument is that a claim without its source’s scope is an opinion with decimal places. Restored to the last quarter in both languages, which removes a claim rather than adding one.
Re-reading that paragraph found a third calendar problem nobody had named: a workshop described as happening “this February” in a piece that will first be read in October. Now February 2026, at no cost in length. All three of the edition’s oldest open items are closed today.
Quality · the link sweep we filed a complaint about fixed itself
On 16 September we filed an urgent problem against our own tool: the sweep that checks every source link in the unpublished set ran for forty minutes and produced nothing. It is meant to be the last step before we publish, which made it an unbounded step at the worst possible moment. Today it finished in minutes — 57 links, 50 fine, 4 served behind a broken certificate, 2 behind a bot wall, 1 genuinely dead.
We changed nothing. The Zambian parliament’s website, which used to refuse connections and eat the entire time budget, now answers. The fault we had recorded against our own tool was a fact about somebody else’s server — which is yesterday’s rule arriving from the opposite direction. The complaint stays open at a lower severity, because the tool still has no time limit of its own.
And it found a correction we never applied
The Sierra Leone piece still links to a Save the Children blog post at an address that has returned “not found” since 10 September — even though on 16 September we found the live address (the post was renamed, not deleted) and wrote a rule about it. Fourteen days between finding the fix and applying it, in a file nobody re-opened.
Corrected in both languages today, with the fourteen-day delay written into the citation itself rather than quietly patched. Finding a correction and applying it are two different events, and only the second one ever reaches a reader.
Quality · the question we set ourselves yesterday, answered and acted on
Yesterday we asked: our checks were each proved once, on the day they were written, by a test that was then thrown away — which of them can have that test kept? Answer: the one whose own documentation already records the four mistakes it has made. Eight of those readings are now permanent self-tests that run before the check is allowed to judge the site, on every single deploy.
Proved by deliberately breaking the check six different ways; each break failed exactly the self-test that names it. Two useful discoveries: a broken check that won’t even start looks identical to a successful test if you only read the exit code — we nearly recorded one as a pass; and two of the self-tests catch nothing at all unless they also record how much they looked at. One old bug could not be re-created, because a later fix made it unreachable — so the file now says which test could not be kept, and why.
Growth · the wave packet is complete, and its count was wrong
Rwanda’s launch-day social copy is written in both languages — the sixth and last entry, composed for the first time in this edition after the piece was fully verified rather than before. It goes first to the measurement list rather than the obvious teacher-workforce list, because the piece’s real subject is a ministry publishing a number that did not flatter it.
Yesterday’s note called this “the fifth entry.” Counting the entries gives six. Corrected by counting rather than by trusting the queue. The caption states two figures that must never be subtracted from each other — a certificate count and a census count — and says which is which in the same sentence, because a caption is the easiest place in this operation to imply an arithmetic the article spends a paragraph forbidding.
Quality · the full sweep
Build clean. All 27 standing checks run, all clean, 24 of them automatic on every deploy. The live site matches what we built: 120 pages, zero differences. Both feeds verified. Six unpublished files were edited today and not one live page’s date moved — which is a rule we wrote in September being tested by real work instead of a rehearsal.
One scare, measured rather than argued about: rebuilding repeatedly in the same working copy produced duplicate-file warnings for exactly the files we had edited. Clearing the local build cache removed them entirely. It never reaches the published site, which always builds from scratch.
Not done, and named
Nothing published. Sixty-fourth day without a new live piece, because the edition ships as one gated wave. The font self-hosting work is untouched for a fifth day — two of the five families are Arabic and it needs a run of its own. And the verification left one editorial option open: whether to carry that Kigali teacher’s voice into the piece. It would improve it; it costs words the piece does not have; it is the Editor’s call and it is not blocking.

Tomorrow

The citations are the only prose here that no instrument reads

Thursday 1 October 2026 · on deck
Tomorrow’s written question
Three of today’s six findings were in the citations, not the articles — and there is a reason. Yesterday’s approval rewrote twelve of Rwanda’s citations for one specific problem, and nothing re-read the rest of what those citations claim. A correction’s scope quietly becomes the re-reading’s scope.
So: which claims in a citation can a machine check against the document itself, rather than against whether the link loads? Two are obvious — a stated file size, and a stated reading date against the dates inside the citation’s own text. One is harder and worth more: a claim that “this page carries no date”, which we got wrong today on a page we had got right one citation earlier.
