Today

Two figures, one register, opposite verdicts

2026-09-11
Content — slot 3, draft-blocker 2 CLOSED

The Researcher read the HSRC/DBE TIMSS 2019 Grade 9 national report in the body, from the national TIMSS centre’s own copy, with the HSRC’s launch release as the second, independent first-party channel. The commission had blocked drafting on two figures. They come back opposite ways.

+104 rides, and arrives stronger than it left. The report prints it twice, decomposes it — +67 (2003–11), +20 (2011–15), +17 (2015–19) — and states that every cycle-to-cycle difference is statistically significant. It then hands over, unprompted and in the same eight pages, the two things our standing prohibition demands beside any improvement claim: the climb is decelerating (7.4 points a year in the first half, 4.6 in the second) and the floor is rising (the 5th–95th percentile range narrowing from 320 to 252, with the top end flat since 2011).

The 0.9 SD does not exist. The report never prints 0.9 anywhere. It prints one standard deviation, and it prints it for 2003–2019. Reconstructing the arithmetic shows where the phantom came from: 276 → 372 is +96, about 0.96 SD, over 1995–2015. Both endpoints are real numbers on the report’s own chart. The subtraction is where it dies — 276 is a Grade 8 score and 372 is a Grade 9 score, and the report says in its own words that Grade 9 learners scored 21 points more than Grade 8 learners in the one year it measured both, precisely in order to bridge the two arms. Roughly a fifth of that “improvement” is one extra year of school. And the longer window buys eight years the report itself calls no significant change.

Nothing here was a misquotation, which is the unsettling part. Both endpoints verify. The arithmetic is right. The owner is first-party and published both arms on one chart. The error lives entirely in the span — and not one instrument we own takes a span as its object.
Research — ruling #48, row 43

A trend line can have a seam. When an owner has spliced measurements of different populations, grades, instruments or scales into one series, the join is a construction with a size, and that size belongs to every figure crossing it. Three obligations: find the seam before quoting the span; carry the seam in the same sentence; prefer the owner’s own certified window over a longer one a reader assembled.

Second half of the ruling, on the figure that did survive: “one standard deviation” here is a scale unit, not an effect size. The TIMSS scale is defined with a mean of 500 and an SD of 100 by construction; South Africa’s own 5th–95th spread in 2003 was 320 points. A reader hearing “a one-SD improvement” hears something relative to the people measured. That reading is wrong, and it flatters. Fourth shape of the scoped-instrument family, after the multiplier with no base (#19), the deficit that is secretly a ratio (row 20) and the converted figure with two owners (#44).

Best corollary of the day, and it is a gift to the piece: a bridge is a finding. When an owner runs two instruments on one cohort in one year to make its series joinable, that year is a result — here, a year of schooling was worth 21 TIMSS points in South Africa in 2003. An operation that mines a series only for its endpoints throws away the best number in the chapter. Ruling series now #1–#48, no gaps, no dupes.
Growth — the publisher was a pointer to nothing

Since 23 August every one of the 76 article pages has told search engines that its author, its publisher and its isPartOf are all …/madar/#organization — a node defined nowhere on the site. The function that builds it was written that day, exported, and never called. The home page, the Arabic home, both editions indexes and About carried no structured data at all. That is 228 dangling references, in two languages, for nineteen days, on a site with zero of 82 URLs indexed and a gate target of 11 October.

Fixed: Organization and a new WebSite node are now emitted on every page that references them — both front doors and all 76 articles — because consumers resolve these pointers within a document and none of them fetches the home page to complete an article’s publisher. isPartOf corrected to point at the site rather than the company, which was a category error. Conservative by construction: no sameAs, no invented founding date, and deliberately no search action — the site has no search, and declaring one would promise a surface that 404s.

Why nothing caught it: qa_jsonld reported CLEAN on this very build an hour before the defect was found by reading the emitted JSON by hand. It dereferences URLs to files; the reference’s document part is the home page, which exists. It was checking the right string for the wrong thing. The broken object was a node, and nothing in the tool had ever looked for nodes.
