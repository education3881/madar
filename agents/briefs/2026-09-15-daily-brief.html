Today

A verdict that failed, a name that was in the citation and not in the sentence, and the first pixel

2026-09-15
Content — slot 3 is banked

The Editor ran the five tests on Edition 05’s continental pair — Four rulers, four crowns, and one about to be re-read — and returned it with four notes. All four were closed in-run, in both languages, the pair was re-read after the edits, and it passes. Zambia, Sierra Leone, Sudan and now this one: four banked pairs of six, all still held.

Three of the four notes are worth your time, because they are the same defect wearing different clothes — a sentence claiming more ground than the page it was read on.

One. The report prints a statistical-significance mark against the mathematics column at the end of primary. The piece carried it as “at that level”, i.e. both subjects — which the same report contradicts, and which the piece’s own fourth section depends on contradicting, because that is where its Senegal crown comes from. The piece would have argued against itself four sections apart. Nothing numeric could see it: every figure in the sentence was correct.

Two. “On that ruler Mauritius was the continent’s outlier.” What was actually read is one World Bank country brief, against one comparison country. Nothing read for this piece ranks African countries at all. A rank asserted without the table behind it — in the piece whose entire argument is that a rank without its ruler is an opinion with decimal places, and while the commission keeps a neighbouring island state deliberately unnamed. It now reads that the crown is Mauritius’s, handed out on a single country brief read against the regional averages the brief itself prints, not on a continental table. The crown survives, stated as our act, which is what it always was.

Three. Two named humans — Dr Vijay Reddy, who is quoted, and Hilaire Hounkpodoté, whose sentence closes the whole piece — were named in the citations, in both languages, and in neither body. She was “the Principal Investigator … she”; he was “the man who has spent his career building the francophone instrument”, which is also a biographical claim no register we read supports. The weekly review named this shape two days ago: the citation is written with the source open, the sentence afterwards from memory of the citation, and memory smooths. What it smoothed this time was a name. Both are now in both bodies.

The fourth note is our own rule, unexecuted: improvement is never reported without its floor. The piece honours it for South Africa and for the region — and not for Niger, the country it crowns for improvement. The report prints Niger’s distribution on the same page as the example the piece already uses: the weakest tenth gained 51.6 points where the strongest gained 139.3. That sentence is now in both bodies, with both figures added to both citation blocks.

Quality — assertion 14, and the first pixel

Yesterday’s QA log ended with a named question, which is a binding rule here: what can today’s sweep not structurally see? The answer was whether a page renders. All thirteen assertions read structure — a thing is present, resolves, parses, matches, agrees. Not one had ever looked at a pixel, while three render defects sat in the record and every one of them was found by a human happening to look: the broken Arabic letter-joins on a share card, the brief that rendered edge-to-edge for two days, and yesterday’s wrong-coloured label on 76 pages that was visible to the eye and invisible to all thirteen checks.

qa_render.py now rasterises four pages and one share card with a headless browser and asserts properties of the rendering, never its identity — a screenshot comparison would fail on every legitimate edit, which is why this was a question and not a task. It asks three things, each taken from a defect that has actually happened: the page drew something; its text is dark on a light ground (the white-on-white case); and the accent colour actually reaches the pixels — which is yesterday’s defect, instrumented.

Proved four ways before it was allowed to gate anything: silent on the real 120-page build, and loud on three separate injuries — the accent token neutralised, the body text set to the paper colour, and one page emptied. Each injection was checked to have actually changed the file before the check was run on it, because a bite that changes nothing reads exactly like a pass.

And proving it produced the day’s ruling. The accent check fired on the article pages and not on the home pages — because the hero drawing carries the same orange as a literal inside the image, so it sat in the region being measured and masked the thing being checked. A floor set there would have read green forever with the colour entirely dead. It is the same error as notes one and two above, committed by an instrument instead of a sentence: measuring wider than the thing you are measuring. The check is now scoped per page to the region that was proved able to fail.

Growth — the share card finally says what it is

Every page has declared a share image since the first publish on 25 May. Not one has ever declared what the image shows. For 113 days, in both languages, every share of every piece has travelled with a picture and no description of it — which is what a reader gets when the image fails to load, when they use a screen reader, or when the platform keeps the card and drops the art. It is not something we can add later; the scraper takes what the page offers and caches it.

The sharp part: the text already existed. Every piece carries a long, specific description of its still, composed in both languages by the people who commissioned the drawing. It was being spent on the page and withheld from the share. It now rides on all 120 pages, per language, cut to the last whole sentence inside the 420 characters the consumer accepts — a machine may choose where to stop, never how to phrase. The brand card got its own description in each language. VALENCE, which is hand-written and outside the layout, had half of it since August and was missing the other half.

Kept honest by extending an assertion that already runs in CI, proved both ways. And one thing found by looking at a card for the first time and deliberately not fixed today: the article share cards carry no wordmark, so every share arrives on someone else’s timeline as an unattributed drawing. That is the Designer’s call, and putting Arabic type on a generated image is exactly the work that broke in August. Not work to start at the end of a run.

Research — ruling #51
A claim inherits the scope of the register it was read in. A qualifier belongs to the cell it was printed in; a comparison belongs to the set that was actually opened; carrying either one wider is a new claim, and nobody made it. Earned three times in one run — twice in the piece, once in our own instrument, which is the test of whether a rule is about writing or about care. It adds a ninth axis to the Verifier’s trace, scope, and a line to the drafter’s hand-off naming the comparison set behind any best, largest, only or outlier. Fifth member of the family the weekly review named on Sunday — the errors that have no number in them.
