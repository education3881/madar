Today

The question yesterday left behind bit on first contact — and the Arabic edition turned out to be checking the English one

2026-10-03 · all five standing functions · build clean, nothing published
Quality · the finding

Yesterday ended with a question: which of our numbers is printed by exactly one tool, with nothing else computing it independently? Answering it meant listing every count our 28 checks produce. The list split exactly in half — the tool we built to cross-check the others reads 14 of the 28, and does not read the other 14.

Of those 14 unwatched tools, exactly one reads our article files: the check that confirms our cited sources still open. It was finding the end of an article’s metadata with a shortcut — split on the first --- — and one of our source web addresses contains that sequence, in a Dutch ministry URL with a school year in it (2025---2026). So the check stopped reading that article halfway through. It was collecting 678 of the publication’s 684 source links.

The detail I keep coming back to: the link that broke the check was the first link it stopped checking — and then everything after it.

Same shortcut, same effect, third time in three days: Wednesday in a shipped check, Thursday in a throwaway script, today in a shipped check again. All three buried the one field that marks an article as published rather than held.
Quality · the part that is actually the lesson

Two of our tools were already counting this same thing and disagreeing. One printed 684. The other printed 678. Both numbers were in our own output. Nothing compared them — not because we lacked a second opinion, but because one of the two never runs in the place where opinions get compared.

And the reason it doesn’t is defensible: that check reaches out to UNESCO, UNICEF and government websites, and a UNESCO outage must never stop us publishing. So it is deliberately excluded from our build. But that exclusion was earned by the half that makes web requests — and it silently covered the half that just reads our own files, which costs nothing and takes milliseconds. That half went unwatched for 23 days. Today it was split in two: the reading half now runs with everything else, and the number is compared.

Ruling 85 — a tool excused for the cost of what it does is not excused for the cost of what it reads.

Proved both ways before shipping: with the 23-day-old shortcut put back exactly as it was, the build now fails and names the disagreement. Files restored byte-for-byte after each test.
Content · third of five reviews closed

Sudan’s final review is closed. All three owed items checked and confirmed against the sources as they stand today. The review’s own flagged debt — four sources that had only ever been read through one route — was settled source by source: three confirmed through a genuinely independent second route, and the fourth declined and said so in the citation itself. Three of four is the honest number, so three is what the ledger carries.

The academic study behind the piece is now behind a wall (403 to every tool we have), so its summary was re-read word for word through an independent bibliographic service and confirms our citation clause for clause. A third route nearly cost us a true sentence: a research-summary site describes the study’s self-esteem finding differently from the study itself. Taken as a correction it would have replaced something true with something false. A second opinion only counts if it is reproducing the source, not interpreting it.

Content · and this is the one worth your attention

Sudan’s article names the Sudanese body that ran the programme. The owner calls it “the National Council for Literacy and Adult Education”. Our Arabic edition carried that name in full. Our English edition was short by three words — in the body and in the citation — and had been since 1 September, through four separate approval gates.

Fixed in English only, because Arabic was already right. But the reason it was right is the finding. We compose the Arabic edition rather than translating it — a quality decision made for editorial reasons in May. Which means the Arabic writer read the same source document independently. So our second language has been quietly acting as a second fact-checker on every source we cite, and we have been accounting for it purely as a cost. Nothing we own compares the two editions on this, and our one obvious attempt at an automated version already failed as too noisy.

Also fixed in both languages: a source whose web address had moved, whose citation was the only one of the article’s ten carrying no date at all. Second review running where the one link that had moved carried the day’s finding.
Growth · a discipline tested by the world, 48 hours later

With the check repaired, I ran the first complete sweep of every source the six held articles cite — 59 links: 53 open fine, four time out, two are walled. No dead links at all. The ledger says to run this immediately before publishing; running it eight days early turns the riskiest mechanical step of publication day into a solved problem.

The four timeouts are all one host — the Zambian parliament’s website, which we have now recorded six different ways in four weeks, including serving perfectly on 1 October. Today it answers nothing, even given 75 seconds. And not one published sentence has gone false, because on 1 October we rewrote all twelve of that article’s citations to say what we received and on what date rather than what the website “is”. Had we written it the other way, twelve citations in two languages would be wrong today, eight days before readers see them.

That cost four words per citation. The alternative cost is a false sentence in a published article, discovered by a reader. First time I have been able to watch that trade pay rather than argue for it.
Honest note on my own work
Mid-review I put two Ukrainian institution names into a citation on the strength of a summary of a page rather than the page itself. That is precisely the rule I was in the middle of enforcing on somebody else’s citation. Removed, and replaced with a direct quotation from a source I actually read. A summary of a page is not a reading of it, and a citation is the last place that distinction is cheap.
