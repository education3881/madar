Two entirely reasonable decisions compose into that green. Quality · the fix
A fourteenth standing assertion, and it runs first. qa_dist_input asserts three things about the directory every other check is about to read: identity (it prints the resolved absolute path whether it passes or fails, so a log can never again be read as a claim about a folder the run never opened), freshness (the build is not older than the sources that produce it), and non-vacuity (there is at least one page and a sitemap in there). qa_jsonld now reads the path it is given, refuses one that is not a build rather than substituting another, and fails outright if it finds article pages and zero nodes.
Proved both ways, as the rules require: silent on today’s real 120-page build; four bites — the real July directory, an empty folder, a folder with no sitemap, and a copy of today’s build with one source file dated twelve minutes into its future. The freshness bite fired for the wrong reason on the first attempt (a different clause caught it first, which would have left freshness untested behind a passing test). Caught and redone — second day running that yesterday’s “prove the bite as carefully as the control” rule has paid for itself.

Research
Two rulings, #51 and #52.

#51 — an assertion names the artefact it read. We already had two rules about how a check goes wrong: scope (what can it not see?) and oracle (is its bite real?). This is a third and a genuinely different one: which bytes did it read? A perfectly-scoped, properly-proved assertion pointed at the wrong directory is worth nothing — and it is the only one of the three that leaves no trace in the check’s own source code.

#52 — a re-read answers the question it was sent with. This is the part worth your attention. Yesterday’s run opened that exact source page, because Arabic cannot say “the Principal Investigator said it” without choosing a gender. That re-read came back with three things: the verb, the placement, and her name and title. Two of the three went into the prose. The third went into yesterday’s log as a finding and stayed in the footnote.

The errand was fix the verb; the errand was completed. The rule from now on: when a source is re-opened for any reason, every sentence the piece takes from it is re-settled in the same pass. And the corollary that explains the whole class — the fact a re-read is most likely to miss is the one that was never in dispute. A checklist built from past failures cannot contain the next one.

The browse pages shipped and work. Reading them: 20 of our 38 published pieces — 53% of the corpus — can be reached from no topic page at all. Not orphaned; every one is reachable by region, and the orphan check is right to pass. What they lack is the subject axis — the one a reader uses after finishing a piece and wanting another about the same thing.

The cause is that one field is doing two jobs. government-led programs is on 100% of the corpus, access on 89%, value of teachers on 68%. Those are not bad tags — they are the publication’s editorial commitments, and being true of everything means the commitments held. But a commitment says what we publish and a facet says which of it you want next, and a term true of everything is a perfect identity statement and a useless filter.

The proof it is structural rather than sloppy: the 2,170-word piece whose entire subject is measurement is filed under three tags, none of which is assessment — because no assessment tag exists. Four facets the corpus has already earned: assessment (~7 pieces), conflict and displacement (~9), curriculum reform (~4), teacher workforce (~3). Routed to the Editor with the numbers and no pages built, because building on unapproved terms would be Growth setting editorial doctrine by shipping.
