The one thing worth your attention today

The document the Abu Dhabi piece says it could not read is being served to us today. On 22 November the regulator’s framework sat behind a firewall that answered every client we own with a blocked page, and the draft wrote that measurement into its body as the honest reason the framework is unread. Three days later the firewall is gone: the site serves a quarter of a megabyte, and the framework itself serves 130 pages. We read it.

What it says is the piece’s own argument arriving a second time. The framework puts Good at the level every school is expected to reach and Acceptable at the minimum required; the regulator’s news page puts Good at the minimum. And the framework sets no inspection cadence at all — the word biennial does not occur in it once — so the two-year cycle is the emirate’s own, not a national rule it inherited. Neither finding can be used yet, because the document carries no edition statement and we cannot yet prove it is the edition the 2025 page means. That is now the piece’s open question, and it is a better one than the one it replaced.

The cost is a day. The piece came back from the Editor rather than going through, and the ledger says so. The gain is that a sentence we would have published about our own limits has stopped being true before a reader met it — which is the only kind of correction that costs nothing.

One is not: the policy says schools have ten working days to appeal, and the draft says a school ha[s] them. The brackets are honest — they declare the change — and the change is unnecessary, because a plural subject takes the document’s own word. The piece’s own source note already quotes it correctly, so the two halves of the piece disagree and the body is the one that moves.

The gap between twenty-four and twenty-five is a missing ledger, and it is today’s ruling. Comparing a quotation to a document means normalising both sides first, and four different normalisations were needed here: the PDF’s own broken spacing on fifteen quotations, punctuation at the end of a sentence on six, a capital letter at the start on three — and a changed word on one. The first three are invisible to a reader and perfectly legitimate. The fourth is the only difference a quotation check exists to find. A single number prints the same value for all four. From today the count carries its working: how many of each, and words changed: 0, which is the figure with teeth.
