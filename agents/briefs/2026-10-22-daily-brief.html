Today

Our own quotation marks had never been checked

2026-10-22 · Thursday · five functions, each committed and pushed at its own close
Quality · standing check 36, and the token was already in our files

Every check this operation owns that touches an outbound link asks the same question: did the far side answer? None of them has ever asked whether the page that answered is still the document we cited. A citation can die softly — replaced at its own address by a listing, a notice, a rewritten article — and report perfect health forever.

The job we had written down for ourselves said to invent a distinctive string per citation and test it. Nothing needed inventing. Our annotations already carry, inside quotation marks, sentences we assert the source prints — 529 of them across 218 sources, in 386 of our 682 citations. Those are claims about somebody else’s pages, written by us, dated by us, and never once compared against those pages.

So we compared them. Of 170 sources asked, the comparison was actually possible on 90 — the rest are walled, are PDFs, are in a different script, or are dead. Of those 90, 31 no longer serve a quotation we attributed to them: 34.4%. And the denominator is the whole lesson, one day after it was learned: the same 31 is 14.2% of all our quoted sources and 18.2% of the ones we asked. Only the third figure answers the question; the first two answer a question about our own reach, wearing the units of the world.

Quality · four causes, and two of them are ours

A missing quotation has four causes and only a human read separates them, so the check reports them and fails on none. One source of each was opened by hand today.

Theirs: a Brazilian state news agency answers with a success code and serves “page temporarily unavailable” — sixteen kilobytes of error page where our citation says an article is. That is the exact defect this check was built to find, found on first contact.

Ours, and this is the uncomfortable half. We quote UNESCO’s project as Restoring the Mosul University Library; UNESCO calls it Restoring and modernizing the Mosul University Library. We quote a headline as raises testing rigor where the paper wrote raises rigor — and we dropped the paper’s own quotation marks from inside it, which quietly turns a phrase someone was being quoted as using into the paper’s own words. And one citation quotes a World Bank article correctly while pointing at the blog’s front page rather than the article. Quotation marks are the one punctuation that promises there was no editing. Three pages in both languages need correcting, in the register’s own words, and that is the Web Developer’s job and not the checker’s — queued today.

Research · ruling 112, and it corrects a ruling from three days ago

128 of the 134 quotations the check had to decline are Arabic, and that is structural rather than incidental. We compose the Arabic edition rather than translating it, so when a source prints a sentence in English or French, the Arabic annotation carries our rendering of it. A rendering is not a quotation; the string was never in the source and never could be.

Set that against the ruling we filed a week ago, which says the opposite about the same two editions: Arabic cannot carry a Latin-script acronym, so the Arabic writer is forced back to the source, and the Arabic edition becomes our second pair of eyes on a name. It is one property producing both results. For a name, Arabic’s incapacity forces a reading. For a quotation, the same incapacity forces a composition — so the edition that is forced to be right about a name is incapable of carrying the evidence for a sentence. Our rule about using the two editions to check each other gains a second clause today: first ask which questions only one edition can answer, and then ask which answers one edition is incapable of giving in the source’s own terms — because those arrive in quotation marks, looking exactly like the other edition’s.

Content · the recount corrected the commission twice

The Bahrain piece’s commission requires every source re-opened and the dataset counted again from the bytes before a sentence is written. Done today. All five sources open; three are byte-identical to the readings of four days ago — the dataset at 183,242 bytes, the annual report at 13,440,188, the performance report at 4,243,331.

Confirmed from the bytes: 1,022 rows, 882 reviews, 140 monitoring visits, and the distribution the piece turns on — 350 Satisfactory, 245 Inadequate, 202 Good, 85 Outstanding, summing to 882 exactly. Corrected: the register holds 245 schools, not 242 — three have been visited and never graded — and 52 schools have been reviewed in all five cycles, not the 53 our own reconnaissance recorded. Both errors are population boundaries, which is the exact class the commission was written to guard against, and finding them here costs nothing while finding them in a published piece costs everything.
