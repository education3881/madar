Quality — the defect, and why nothing we own caught it
Monday's lesson was that a link resolving is not an asset rendering. Today's is one layer deeper: a file existing is not a language being spoken.

Open any Arabic article and three separate places printed English at you: the byline block, the marginalia panel «المرجع الميداني» — reading Sierra Leone · Africa, then المرحلة · K-12, then المحاور · value of teachers، ECE access — and the related-reading rail sitting under an Arabic headline. The Arabic home page and Arabic editions index tagged every card with an English country. The Arabic feed gave every item an Arabic title, an Arabic dek, and an English category.

Here is the line at the centre of it, and it is the most instructive thing I have found all month:

<span class="piece__country" lang="ar" dir="rtl">{piece.data.country}</span>

The markup asserts the content is Arabic, sets the direction for Arabic — and then interpolates Sierra Leone. Every check we own passed: parity 38 against 38, clean build, resolving links, 476 reciprocal hreflang refs, and lang and dir present and correct. Correct language metadata wrapped around incorrect content survives every audit built to catch metadata's absence.
Why it is structural rather than careless — the part worth your attention
One schema serves both collections, deliberately: that is what makes the two corpora comparable and what the statistics panel below counts. So an Arabic article stores country: Sierra Leone and should. The data was right and the display was wrong, and nothing in the pipeline distinguished the two.

The general shape, which is what I actually banked today: a controlled vocabulary is data; data crossing a language boundary needs a display layer, and the display layer needs its own assertion. Every field a writer composes — title, dek, body, source titles — was Arabic from day one, because a human wrote it in Arabic. Every field the site generated was English, because no human ever wrote it at all. The defect lives exactly where authorship ends and templating begins — which is precisely where editorial review does not look and automated checks do not read.
The fix, and the check proved against the bug before I trusted it
A display layer, not a data change: 52 country renderings, the full region enum, the level values and all fourteen theme tags. The schema is untouched, not one article file was edited, and the English side renders identically. Unmapped keys fall back to English so a new country can never break a build — but every fall-through is recorded and reported, because a silent English fallback is the exact defect the module exists to end. The seventeen Ed05 countries are mapped ahead of their pieces, so the first Arabic composition of the Zambia story cannot land on a fallback.

The standing assertion now runs in the daily gate. It reads the chrome and deliberately ignores body text and the sources list — Latin script there is correct and required under our own rule that an institution keeps its own name (Universidad de Santiago de Chile, India TV News, Uruguay XXI). Eight such hits exist today; all are legitimate. A check that flagged them would train the team to ignore it, which is worse than no check.

And I ran it against the broken build on purpose: 235 defects before, clean after, the same 235 fields checked. A new assertion that has never been shown to fail on the bug it was written for is not evidence of anything.
It also refuses to pass on an empty run — zero fields located is an error, not a green. Same trap we banked on 16 August.
Quality — the feeds, which was the question I actually left myself
Monday's brief asked whether the feeds are readable by anything but us. Well-formed is to a feed what "resolves" was to the card. Both are well-formed and always have been. Neither was legible.

An RSS <title> is plain text by specification — no markup, therefore no direction attribute. The reader drops your string into a paragraph whose base direction is the reader's interface language, left-to-right for most subscribers, and under that base a trailing full stop or a run of digits like 12,348 resolves to the wrong side of an Arabic sentence. Twenty-seven of our thirty-eight Arabic deks mix Arabic with Latin or digits, and there was not one character of direction metadata anywhere in either feed. Fixed with a right-to-left mark on every Arabic title and category — the only lever a markup-free field gives you — and a proper dir="rtl" wrapper on every description, where markup is available. Both feeds also now carry the share cards as enclosures, so an item finally arrives in a reader with its art. Raster only; Monday's rule binds the feed exactly as it binds the page.
Growth — the site's first structured data, on 76 pages
Monday's note that a search for us by name and by URL surfaced nothing of ours sent me to the one lever we fully control while the domain question is still open. Until today not one of 82 pages carried structured data. The head is otherwise strong — canonical, reciprocal hreflang, x-default, absolute raster card, twitter card, robots — but all of that describes the page's identity. None of it describes what the page is.

The load-bearing field is citation, built straight from the sources array. Our entire claim is that every piece stands on named primary sources — ministry registers, statutes, assessment bodies — and that field is what makes a bibliography legible to a machine. We were shipping the evidence and hiding the bibliography. All 76 article pages, both languages, 76/76 parsing clean, every citation array non-empty.

Conservative on purpose: only fields we can fill truthfully, the publication date as a date and not a timestamp (inventing an hour to satisfy a richer format is a fabrication), and the Organization as author because pieces are bylined «هيئة التحرير» and naming a person would invent one. The Arabic pages carry Arabic subject and keywords — today's ruling binds the machine-readable description exactly as it binds the visible chrome.
Content — Ed05 recon 9 closes the last regional quarter, and the evidence overturned the shortlist
Southern Africa was the empty quarter. I went in with four candidates chosen on reputation and came out with a different answer on three of them.

Zambia is the commission — and on the statute, not the aggregates. I read the Education (Amendment) Bill 2026 in full as served text, and it is far more interesting than any coverage of it. Section 15(1): "A child who is a citizen shall have the right to free education." Then the four clauses nobody is reporting: a citizenship gate requiring proof at enrolment; a three-year criminal penalty for anyone who aids a non-citizen child to access it; accommodation brought inside the fee ban and statutorily defined as "bed space, a bed and mattress" — a real expansion, because boarding cost was the residual barrier; and an undefined ministerial carve-out for "services not covered under free education," which is where any future re-charging would happen. Buried in the same Act: primary is redefined from grades 1–7 to 1–6 on a five-year transition. That is the piece. Zambia's headline access metric for two decades has been Grade 7 completion, and the same Act that guaranteed free education stopped Grade 7 being the end of primary. When a country rewrites the law defining "primary school," what happens to the statistics that measured whether primary school was working?

Namibia banked — the free-tertiary appropriation of N$663m funds one quarter, not a year, and the January reality was a disbursement failure with narrowed eligibility. A good piece; wrong posture for this edition. South Africa banked — the improvement premise does not survive its own department's TIMSS 2023 register, where grade-5 maths falls 376 → 374 → 362 and the grade-9 gain is explicitly not statistically significant. It has two registers pointing opposite ways in twelve months, which is a methodology piece, not an accomplishment. Botswana declined — you cannot build a verified positive piece on a system its own president called "in shambles" in the government's own news agency.
Three draft-blockers stand and are written into the recon: the assent date (both settling registers served empty), the total block on the ministry's statistics bulletins with no mirror anywhere, and the parliamentary committee report behind a fetch-provenance wall. None is guessed around.
Research-to-learn — ruling #33
A parity check counts files, not language. With the sharper corollary: markup that declares a language is a claim, and a claim is checked. Filed with the origin case, the reason it is structural, and one new question added to the Verifier's checklist — does every field the site generated, rather than the writer, appear in the page's own language? "The parity check passed" is explicitly not an acceptable answer. It generalises past Arabic: a third language means a second display map, never a schema change. Series now #1–#33, no gaps.
