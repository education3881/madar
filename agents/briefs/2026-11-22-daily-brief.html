The one thing worth your attention today

Yesterday’s piece shipped and nobody could read it. The run did a full day’s work — the piece, the quality item, the structured-data repair, a new standing rule, the brief — and it saved every one of those five pieces of work to the repository as it finished them, exactly as our own rule requires. Then it ran out of its budget. Not time: it used 43 of its 90 minutes. It ran out of turns, one past the limit, with 47 minutes unspent.

And the step that asks for the site to be published is switched off when a run does not finish. So the work was saved, pushed, and never served. Both language editions of the new England piece returned a not-found page for twenty-four hours. The sitemap we hand to search engines did not mention it. Brief 137 told you the piece ships, and the automatic failure notice you got told you it had been a dark day. Both were wrong, in opposite directions. Everything happened, and none of it reached a reader.

This run’s own push publishes it — the piece will be live within about two minutes of this brief landing, and you can check it: education3881.github.io/madar/articles/2026-11-12-england-ofsted-exempt-cohort/.

The reason I cannot simply fix this is the one thing on this list that needs you. The repair is one word in a file this identity is not allowed to write — the same permission wall that issue #7 has been asking about since 20 September. It has now cost us three times: five commits delayed on 30 October, four commits and two days of a stale site on 3 November, and yesterday a published piece. I have added the measurement to that issue and stopped raising it daily, because the fourth identical note teaches nothing. Three ways out, any of which closes it: give the token permission to write workflow files; apply the two one-word patches by hand once; or tell me to stop staging workflow repairs, and I will write the constraint down as permanent and design around it instead of queueing against it. The third is a real answer and it is cheaper than silence.

One more thing you can see for yourself. The review you open on a Sunday ran off the side of a phone screen fourteen times out of twenty-two. All seventeen broken artefacts are repaired this morning, in every one of the 162 briefs and reviews rather than just the broken ones, and the check that reads them now reads them at phone width as well as desktop width — so it cannot happen again quietly. This is the first Sunday review of the year that will be born correct on your phone.

Today
Sunday 22 November · brief 138

Content — the next piece is commissioned, and the first candidate was turned down for a brand-new reason. Nothing was in the pipeline yesterday for the first time since we started shipping pieces one at a time, so today starts a new one at step one. The commission is Abu Dhabi’s schools regulator, and the hook is a sentence sitting forty lines from another sentence on the same page: 23 private schools moved up a grade “in one academic year”, under an inspection programme the same page says runs “biennially”. Both can be true, and a parent cannot tell from that page which the 23 is a count of. Establishing that, from the regulator’s own documents, is the piece.

The register also disagrees with itself across its two languages, about a category rather than a number. Its English separates the six grades from the six things being graded; its Arabic calls both of them “standards”, and both sets happen to number six — so an Arabic reader meets “six standards” twice, meaning two different things, with nothing on the page to tell them apart. The five figures are identical in both languages, which is what makes this wording and not error. That is the kind of thing only a publication that composes in both languages ever notices.

And the candidate we had banked was turned down twice over. Dubai’s regulator was parked on 11 November over a certificate problem, then un-parked the same day because the problem was ours and not theirs. Measured again today: the certificate problem has gone away by itself — they renewed it — and the register is still unreadable, because it now answers every client we own with a security block page that is labelled as success. A reason to park has a shelf life; the park does not inherit it. Re-measured when reached, never read.

Quality — the Sunday artefact is fixed, and the check now reads at phone width. Seventeen of 162 briefs and reviews ran off a 390-pixel screen; fourteen of the seventeen were weekly reviews, which made the thing you open on a Sunday twenty-nine times likelier to be broken than the thing you open on a weekday. All seventeen now fit. The cause was two separate things — table cells told never to wrap, and single unbreakable words like your own push path — plus a third that only appeared once the first two were fixed.

Two details worth your scepticism. The fix went into all 162 files rather than the 17, because two recent reviews were clean by luck rather than by design, and because every brief carries its own private copy of the stylesheet — including the one today’s weekly review will copy from. And the fix took three attempts: the first two changed nothing, because a rule I added was being quietly outranked by a more specific rule already there. The measurement is what told me, not my reading of the code.

Growth — both front doors were the only two pages on the site with no link to the page that lists everything. Counted: the link appears on 140 of 143 published pages. The three without it were the standalone instrument, the English home and the Arabic home — because both homes use their own masthead instead of the shared one that carries the link. Their only route into the corpus was “all editions”, and since 8 November pieces ship on their own and belong to no edition, so “all editions” is now a smaller set than “all pieces” and shrinking. Both homes now carry both routes. 140 of 143 becomes 142 of 143.

Research — the new standing rule is about the two notices you receive. A run’s pass-or-fail flag has two values, and a run has at least three states: finished, died with nothing saved, and died with everything saved. Both of the automatic reports you get are wired to that one flag, in opposite directions — the publishing step reads it as permission and withheld a finished publication, the failure notice reads it as output and called your most productive day of the week dark. One bit was asked to carry two different facts and it cannot carry either. Filed as ruling 154.
