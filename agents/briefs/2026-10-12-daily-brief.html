Nothing owed by you · the edition’s last gate is judged · and judging it found something

Edition 05 was one judgment away from the live site this morning. I made the judgment, and it failed the thing it was judging. So the six pieces do not ship today, and that is the right outcome rather than a setback.

Deploy 38138046612 · success · the origin is serving yesterday’s commit — checked against the Actions API, not assumed.
Saturday’s daily: failed at 181 turns against a cap of 180, delivering none of a full day’s work. Sunday’s weekly: success. Today committed five times instead of once.

The gate was on the six captions that go out with the edition. Five are clean. The sixth says four African education systems are crowned by four different countries — and the piece it advertises stopped saying that on 18 September, when I widened one of the four rulers from two countries to four and wrote, in the piece’s own body, that naming two of them would be our narrowing. The caption has been carrying the narrowing ever since. Twenty-four days, both languages, past five separate readings of this edition.

The part worth your attention is not the error. It is why it survived. On the day I made that correction I did ask what else it touched — properly, in writing, over four paragraphs. I found one place. There were seven. The one I found was the only one with an automatic check pointed at it. Ruling 98: you trace a correction as far as your instruments reach, and then you stop, and you cannot feel yourself stopping.

Quality · the queue’s head, closed

The queue chose the day’s first job for the sixth time in seven runs, and chose the file that logs these statistics. It held 108 rows for 95 dates — ten days recorded twice, one recorded four times — because the tool appends and the file is supposed to be one row per day.

One measurement turned it from a tidying job into a rule. Nine of those ten duplicated days are byte-identical to their twin. So anyone asking “what was true on the 14th?” has had the right answer on all 95 days. The only reader who gets a wrong answer is one who counts backwards — the row seven back — and that reader is the week-over-week comparison this file exists for, which does not exist yet.

Content · the gate bit, and the edition holds

Five of six captions pass. The sixth names Senegal and Benin on the income-adjusted ruler. The piece crowns Gabon, Senegal, Benin and Burkina Faso, because the analyst whose chart it is never separates the four, and the piece says so in the sentence beside it.

The English caption carried a second one: re-read this December, against the piece’s own the last quarter of 2026 — a month invented where the source gives a quarter, in a sentence we had deliberately made relative so it could not go stale.

The figure check was green on this the whole time and was right to be. The faulty clause contains no number. That is the thing worth carrying: a green light beside an artefact gets read as a green light about it, and five consecutive runs read it that way.

The flip, and why it waited a day

Nothing is left on Edition 05 except a clean build and the publish gate written out in full. Every piece has its editor’s verdict, its independent verification, its Arabic gate, its illustration and its share card; the last outstanding judgment was taken today.

It waited because a sentence at the spine of one piece was found stale this morning and repaired hours ago. The gate date was 11 October and it has passed. The rule that outranks it has not moved since the first day of this operation: hold rather than ship thin. Seventy-one days without publishing is a long time, and shipping a correction the same day it is made is how a correction becomes the next defect.

And the thing today could not see

Since August these briefs have been inside the quality perimeter, and three things have been checked about them: that they exist, that they have a frame, and — from today — that they call nobody. All three are facts about the file. Nothing has ever checked that one of them looks right.

I found that out by accident. Photographing Brief No. 01 to prove the typeface swap, I looked at it: its right-hand column has been one word wide since 28 May — 137 days — because a long file path with no break in it shoves the other column off the page. The current template does not have the fault. But one brief out of 101 has ever been looked at this way, today, for an unrelated reason, and it was broken. That is tomorrow’s question, tested before anything new is added.
