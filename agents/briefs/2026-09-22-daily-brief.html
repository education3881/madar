Manager → Founder

Four hundred pounds is not a price. It is a permission nobody has used.

The Editor read the Egypt pair against its own eleven sources today — every URL fetched again, every claim matched against the text it came from — and returned five notes. Four of them are ordinary good editing. The first is the one worth your time.

Egypt’s new certificate charges nothing for a first sitting and caps a retake. Our piece said the cap was “two hundred pounds per attempt and not exceeding four hundred per subject.” The statute says something different in its second half: the Minister may, with Cabinet approval, raise the two-hundred limit gradually by decree, to a total not exceeding four hundred.

Four hundred is the ceiling on a decree that has not been issued. We printed it as what a retake costs. And the reason we did is the finding under the finding: three Arabic reproductions of the law were read, and they compress that clause three different ways. We took the unit from a newspaper and the ceiling from a law firm’s plain-language summary — while the one reproduction that carries the whole clause was open on the same screen, the same day.

Note 1 · the sixth consecutive non-numeric finding in this edition, and it is about a number
The fee clause lost two things at once. Its unit: the statute’s two hundred pounds is per subject per attempt, and we printed per attempt. And its modality: the four hundred is a permission the Minister may exercise with Cabinet approval, and we printed it as a bound that already applies. Wrong in both languages — and in both source annotations, which is why the hand-off check that compares the two could not see it.
Where each half came from is the ruling. Source 1 carries the clause entire. Source 3 carries the escalation clause but gives the unit short. Source 2 truncates its quotation with an ellipsis exactly where the escalation clause begins and prints “200 – 400” only in the plain-language summary it sets beside the text. The piece already carried a guardrail saying those three disagree — about article numbering. They also disagree about the text, and having written one disagreement down is what made the second invisible. Filed as ruling #61: three reproductions are three compressions.
Notes 2 to 5
A quotation missing one word — we printed “not less than two hundred pounds” where the register serves “shall not be less than” (ruling #46’s origin case, repeated). A citation naming a headline the document does not carry. A “why now” that arrived in section six of eight, fixed with one paragraph and no new fact. And one note that added: the paragraph arguing that a critic objected to a draft rather than to the enacted law had left the enacted text unread on the very point — as issued it caps that fee at a thousand pounds with no floor, where the bill set 200–2,000.
After every edit: EN 2,037 words against a 2,300 cap, and the figure sets still match exactly across the two languages — 40 tokens each side, nothing on one side only. It remains the only pair in this edition clean in both directions.
Quality · yesterday’s question, and it bit hard
Yesterday asked whether any surface disagrees with the file about which day a piece was published. Yes — every printed date, on every page, on any machine set behind UTC. A date in our files is a bare 2026-09-19, parsed as midnight UTC; eight formatters then asked the build machine what day that instant was.
Proved against the built pages, not the source: the same commit, built once as UTC and once as Los Angeles, differed on 116 of its 121 pages, and a piece dated 7 July printed July 6. Our servers are UTC and our calendar is Dubai, so both land on the right side of midnight. Four months of correct dates were luck, not a guard. Fixed at all eight sites; after the fix a Los Angeles build is byte-for-byte identical to a UTC one. Filed as ruling #60: a date is not a moment.
Quality · standing assertion 22
qa_date_identity compares 76 article datelines and 232 listing dates against the file that produced each one, in the language of the page, Arabic in Arabic numerals. It builds its expected answers in Python from its own month tables, so it shares no clock, no locale and no formatter with the thing it judges — a check that asked the same library would have agreed with the defect. 20 of 22 assertions now gate the deploy.
Proved six ways, and two of the proofs are the useful part. The first draft failed its own control with 46 findings on a known-good build, because one date element swallowed every list item after it. And one bite did not bite while the tool printed PASS: an Arabic date reprinted in Western numerals — yesterday’s real defect — was being silently skipped, and the only trace was that the tool’s own total fell from 232 to 231. A number that moves is a check that stopped checking something, and nothing in the word PASS tells you which way its total went.
Growth · the feed, read as a subscriber will read it on flip day
The feeds are the only channel we own end to end, so today we asked what they do on the one day that matters. The decision, and it is ours not yours: we do not re-date the wave. The packet carries the shape instead, in one line, in both languages. Measured while there: 38 items, 28.8 KB English and 36.5 KB Arabic, every Arabic item carrying its direction, and — because today’s ruling demanded it — all 76 feed dates checked against their files, zero disagreements.
