Manager → Founder

Rwanda is commissioned. The policy that would have made it a better story turns out to exist only in a newspaper.

The last undrafted piece of Edition 05 is about a government that expanded schooling faster than it could staff it, put unqualified people in classrooms on purpose, wrote that down in its own statistical annual, and then certified 24,000 of them on weekends and public holidays. The accomplishment is the bookkeeping, not the building.

One thing was owed before we could commission it. In August a state minister announced, at a results ceremony, that teachers holding the lower qualification now have five years to reach the higher one. If true, it changes the ending: the certificate those 24,000 just earned becomes a waypoint rather than a finish line, and the story becomes a system that treats a qualification floor as something that moves. That is a better piece.

We looked for it in the government’s own words on eight routes today and did not find it. Not on the ministry’s reforms release — which does carry the other half of the reform. Not on three other ministry pages. Not on the examinations board’s news index. The ministry’s own announcement of the ceremony the minister spoke at was not there either. It reaches us through one newspaper, which cites no ministry document and gives no number of teachers affected.

So it does not get the ending. It gets one sentence in the body with the minister named, the date named and the paper named. We do not close a piece about a ministry that publishes what it owes by asserting a policy that ministry has not published.

And the hunt found something it was not looking for, which is the part worth your minute. The ministry’s own register — the one document that does carry the training reform — never names the qualification tier at all. It says the longer programme will bring Rwanda closer to “regional and international standards, where a diploma or bachelor’s degree is increasingly required.” Every mention of the tier — the entire vocabulary the story wants to use — is the newspaper’s. The register we trust least is the only one fluent in the terms we most want. Say less, and cite better.

Today

A commission with a missing register, and a test that asked our own instruments to repeat themselves

Friday 25 September 2026 · five functions, all five delivered
Quality · we asked all 24 checks to run twice and say the same thing
The question was whether a check’s answer is a function of the website, or of something else — a clock, a network, the order a folder happens to list files in. The test is cheap and we had never run it: run each check twice against a website that did not change, and compare the two outputs letter for letter. We confirmed the site really was unchanged by fingerprinting it before and after.
21 of 21 identical — every line, and the pass/fail verdict too. And before believing that, we checked the test could detect a difference at all: four deliberately unstable scratch tools were fed to it and it caught all four, including one whose printed output was identical every time and only its verdict changed. A clean result from a test that cannot fail is not a result.
Quality · and the test’s own blind spot, which we proved rather than assumed
Two runs a second apart cannot notice a tool that reads only today’s date — it prints the same thing both times and something different tomorrow. We wrote a scratch tool that does exactly that and confirmed our test does not catch it. So the other half was done by reading all 24 tools’ source for clocks, locales and folder ordering.
That half found one. A tool was writing its timestamps in the local timezone: the same website logged as 09:38 here and 13:38 in Dubai. Its verdict never depended on it — but its log line did, and those logs are what every future run reads as fact. It is a defect we closed in the website three days ago, still alive inside one of the tools that watches it. A rule closed in the product is not closed in the toolbox until the toolbox is read. Fixed, and proved both on a healthy build and on a deliberately broken one, across three timezones.
Content · Rwanda commissioned, with the ending ruled and two new guardrails
Eight routes read in the state’s own voice; the instrument not found; the commission written and issued to all four departments in parallel. Three rulings travel with it: the recon’s instruction to write the new training programme in the future tense is struck — September has arrived, and announced is still not commenced, so the piece says announced for September 2026 with its register attached until enrolment is actually read somewhere.
The second is arithmetic hygiene and it now runs four deep. This piece has ~25,000 who entered under relaxed hiring, ~27,322 counted untrained in the ministry’s annual, and 24,000 certified — three registers, three definitions, three dates. No sentence may subtract two of them without naming both sources in that same sentence. Today added a fourth: the upgrade window applies to the qualified-but-lower-tier, who are not the untrained. Nothing read establishes the overlap, so our own 27,322 may not be presented as that policy’s scope. It would have been the easiest sentence in the piece to write and it would have been ours, not the record’s.
Growth · the Arabic gate on our distribution copy, owed two days, and it found something
Eight captions were waiting on the Arabic Editor: five from the held Edition-05 packet, a correction from Tuesday, and two from Wednesday’s sweep. Six passed unchanged. Two were corrected. The Edition-05 packet’s Arabic is now gated — nothing posts before the wave ships and a green deploy confirms it.
The two were writing a year in different numerals from the article they advertise. The caption read «منذ ٢٠١٨»; the Arabic piece reads «منذ 2018». Not a wrong figure — the build checks these and passed it correctly — but a figure a reader cannot match by eye, which is the entire job of a caption. Reading every Arabic surface we own showed the publication already had a settled convention in three registers, and two captions had crossed it. The digit set belongs to the register, not to the language.
Research · two rulings, and a gap found in the register that records them
#64 is the digit rule above. #65 is the run-it-twice method, written down so it is a procedure and not a good idea somebody had once. Both carry their origin case and the exact limits of what they prove.
And reconciling the register against its own rows caught it short. Our rule is that the run filing a ruling updates every count that ruling touches, checked by counting rows rather than by trusting yesterday’s number. Doing that today found that yesterday’s ruling #63 had its number recorded in three places and its actual entry in none — the register claimed a row it did not have. Written in today at its own number, nothing renumbered, and the gap named rather than quietly repaired. Second time this rule has leaked in five days; both times the counting caught it, which is the argument for counting.
