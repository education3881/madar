The one thing worth your attention today

Your front page is showing work that is two months old, and nothing we own can notice. I measured, for the first time, how far a reader has to walk to reach each piece. The answer is reassuring: nothing is unreachable, and nothing is more than two clicks from its own front door. But only six pieces are reachable in one click — those are the only ones a reader meets without deciding to go looking — and all six belong to Edition 05, whose last piece is eight weeks older than our most recent. The two pieces we have published since are one click further in, behind a listing.

Every check passes, and they are right to. The front page says “latest edition: Edition 05”, and that is true — the two newer pieces belong to no edition at all, because that is how the per-piece ledger works. The label is honest, the links all resolve, nothing is broken. Nothing we own asks what the front door chooses to put first, so this could have stayed like it is indefinitely.

This is the part of your edition decision nobody had costed. On 8 November I applied the stated default on issue 13 and started shipping pieces as singles, because it was the only option I could execute without hearing from you. That changed how pieces are made. It did not change the shape of the home page, which was built around editions and still is. I am not going to redesign your front door on my own judgment — it is the publication’s public face, which is yours. The measurement is on file and the question is one line: when there is no open edition, should the front page lead with the newest pieces, with the last edition plus everything since, or with something the Editor chooses each week? Issue 13 is still open and this is now part of it.

Yesterday
Monday 23 November · brief 139

Complete, five functions, nothing missed and nothing dark. The tree arrived this morning clean and exactly level with the published site. I checked the publication record before building anything: the last deploy succeeded and there is nothing sitting on the repository that the site is not serving.

Yesterday named today’s two jobs in writing, and both were done: write the Abu Dhabi piece now that its gate had lifted, and take the oldest open item on the board. One correction to yesterday’s own work is in today’s, and I would rather you heard it from me. Yesterday’s reading of the regulator’s policy quoted a sentence with square brackets inserted into it, as if letters were missing from the document. Nothing was missing. The letters are all there; the file simply stores them with spaces between them. Square brackets are how an editor says a text is damaged, and we had no business saying that about somebody else’s policy. It is corrected at the source and it became today’s ruling.

Today
Tuesday 24 November · brief 140

Content — the piece is written, and reading the document to the end closed a job we had queued separately. The English draft of the Abu Dhabi piece landed today, the second of its eight gates. Both documents were fetched again and open in front of the drafter, which is the rule for anything load-bearing, and all twenty-five quotations in it were compared against the documents’ own bytes by a script rather than by eye — the kind of check that is easy to claim and easy to skip. One of the twenty-five failed at first, and the failure was ours: the document writes its apostrophes in an old encoding, and our comparison was reading four characters where a reader sees one. It passed once that was decoded.

And the regulator’s policy answers a question we had scheduled as a separate piece of work. We had four reads owed on this piece; the fourth was are individual schools’ ratings published anywhere? The answer was two pages past where yesterday’s read stopped, in a document already on our disk: schools are required to publish their own inspection rating on their own website, and the regulator separately reserves the right to publish ratings itself. A duty on the school, a right for the regulator. That is now the piece’s closing line, because it is the one useful thing a parent can actually do. The same page also gives a school ten working days to appeal its result, so a published set of figures is an after-appeal set of figures — worth knowing and nowhere in the press release.

Two reads are still owed, and the draft says in writing why it does not wait for them: one of them was commissioned for an argument the piece no longer makes, and the other gates percentages the piece does not write. The draft also withdraws its own working title and hands four open questions to the Editor rather than settling them, including whether this should ship before those reads land. Nothing was approved today; the piece is with the Editor.

Quality — our citation checker can now tell which form of a headline it matched, and five of our quotations are the wrong one. This was the oldest unanswered question on the board, twenty-seven days old. Four of our instruments ask whether a quoted sentence is somewhere in a document; none asked whether it is the sentence the document leads with. Many sites carry two different headlines — one in the browser tab, another on the page itself — so a search of the whole document confirms either of them forever. It now reports which one: the page’s own heading counts as evidence, the tab’s string counts as a warning, and a sentence that exists only in the web address counts as a counterfeit. On the first twenty readings, five quotations are confirmed only by something other than the page’s own heading. They are handed over as a list to re-check, not quietly fixed.

Two things about how that went are worth a sentence each. The first reading showed me something I had got wrong within the hour: two of those warnings are pages that carry no heading at all, and we cannot have quoted the wrong heading from a page that only has one. A single bucket mixing we quoted badly with they publish oddly would have handed a person a list that was two-thirds noise, so it now separates them. The second: I pointed the same new code at our own pages as a test, and it misread them — a flaw in code that had shipped thirty minutes earlier, found because our own site is the one set of pages we can check for free.

Growth — the walk a reader takes, measured for the first time. We have always checked that every page can be reached. We had never measured how far. From either front door: six pieces at one click, forty at two, nothing unreachable, and the other language always exactly one click further. The graph is shallow and symmetric, which is good news. The finding is not the depth but which six pieces hold the one-click slots, and that is the front-page question above.

Research — a ruling about what a document actually is. Today’s file is a PDF, and a PDF does not contain text the way a web page does: it contains positioned letters, and the “text” is something our own tool reconstructs. So when I tell you all twenty-five quotations were checked against the document, one side of twenty-five of those comparisons was produced by a tool we wrote this afternoon, and a different tool would produce a slightly different string. That is worth writing down rather than glossing: it cost us a wrong diagnosis yesterday and a false alarm today. And the instrument that would normally catch a misquotation cannot read this document at all — it declines PDFs by design, which means today’s piece rests on a document our own checking cannot reach. The honest response is to say so in the published annotation, which is what the draft does.

One small thing that worked exactly as designed. When I filed today’s ruling I updated the four places its number has to appear. There is a fifth, and I did not know it — and the gate that watches those numbers named it for me before anything shipped. That is the difference between a number a person remembers and a number something checks.
