Content · the Netherlands doorstroomtoets — "The test that grades the advice"
The bench's first piece, and a clean fourth face of Edition 04's question. Most assessment reform argues about the height of the bar; in 2023 the Netherlands moved the bar's target. The old end-of-primary test arrived after the teacher's track advice had already settled a child's secondary path. The doorstroomtoets was rebuilt around one February window, a hard 15-March result, and one statutory rule: if the test indicates a higher track than the teacher's advice, the school is legally required to revise the advice upward — machinery aimed at the long-documented under-advising of children of less-educated parents. Three cycles in, the finding is genuinely double-edged: the lever moves (upward revisions rose from about one in ten of all advices to roughly one in four) — but who benefits still tracks region and class. The reform pointed the ruler at the graders, and the ruler measured them too. Editor's five-test verdict: APPROVE for the wave, held approved: false. It resolves the forward related: reference us-naep has carried since it was drafted.

Research · ruling #25 — a conditional rate carries its condition
The Dutch data carries two rates about the same event — an upward revision — that invert the story if swapped. The eligibility rate: the share of pupils who qualify (test higher than teacher), ranging under one in ten to more than half by municipality. The event-given-eligibility rate: of those who qualify, the share actually revised — Oppers's 8/10 versus 4/10. Both are "revision rates"; read one as the other and a disadvantaged town looks advantaged. The rule: for every rate, write the words "out of ___" and check the draft's population matches the source's; never chain two rates from different conditions into one trend. It is the base-rate/conditional-rate distinction — adjacent to funnel stage (#14) but distinct, and the seventh named companion in the figure-discipline family.
