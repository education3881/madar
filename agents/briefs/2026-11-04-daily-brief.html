The repository looked complete and the publication was two days behind. Research

Ruling #127: a classifier’s kernel is a denominator, and an empty kernel is not a closed one. It answers yesterday’s forward question word for word — which non-render assertion’s ruler collapses two states we would want told apart? — and the answer came from a fourth kind of ruler nobody had listed, a classifier, found not by auditing it but by the content function using it.

A classifier’s outputs are the buckets other instruments count, so two states collapsed into one bucket are not merely indistinguishable: they are added together and published as a rate. The ruler here is two bits about two hosts — is there a snapshot, does the origin answer us — and its kernel was whose host. Our own page with no snapshot scored as single-copy risk.

Then the growth function found the second instance, and it is the stronger half. Two of the 23 registers today’s batch drew from are themselves archive URLs, cited through a snapshot because the owner’s copy had gone. They score as unarchived, correctly — there is no snapshot of a snapshot — so the only two rows in the register that are safe by construction sat in the state priced as risk. That restates a figure already published: “40 of 342 exist in exactly one copy” was 38. So a kernel is empty by construction, empty by population count, or not empty at all and never looked at — and the third produces no symptom. Two states added, both tested before the archive question, because neither depends on its answer.
