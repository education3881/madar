Manager → Founder

Three of our checks print “120”. They mean three different sets of 120 pages.

A question had been sitting in our logs for three days, written down and not taken: our checks each report a count — pages built, pages in the sitemap, pages reachable — and nobody has ever read those numbers side by side. Today we did. Every number agreed. Every piece of arithmetic between them was true on the first try.

And that turned out to be the finding. Three separate checks print the number 120, and they are counting three different sets of 120 pages: one means the sitemap, one means every page with an index file, one means every page except our standalone instrument. Each exclusion is deliberate and correct. What is wrong is that our own logs have printed them in one row, as one number, since the day we started writing them.

The consequence is concrete rather than philosophical: a page could be dropped from the sitemap and a different page added to it in the same commit, and every number we publish would be unchanged. So today’s nineteenth standing check compares the sets, by name, page by page — and within the hour it found a wrong number in one of our tools, two tools that crash when called from the wrong directory, and a mistake in itself.

Alongside it, Edition 05’s fifth piece is drafted. Egypt legislated a second secondary certificate and then did the thing that is easiest to skip: it published the university doors a year before the first exam. Reading the statute itself closed two blockers the recon had carried for a month — and corrected a date we had been repeating since August.

Today

A count is a claim about a set, and ours were not saying which

Saturday 19 September 2026 · brief 78
Quality · the question owed for three runs
The rule is that every QA log ends by naming what today’s sweep cannot structurally see, and the next run tests it before adding anything new. This one had been carried since Tuesday. Taken today, it failed on first contact — and not in the direction it was pointing.

The arithmetic was perfect. 121 pages against 120 sitemap entries, differing by exactly one. 62 English plus 59 Arabic making 121. 59 language pairs doubled plus two unpaired pages making 120. Every identity held. Had we built the check the question described — compare the counts — today’s log would read clean and there would be nothing here.
Underneath: qa_lastmod prints 120 meaning the sitemap. qa_jsonld prints 120 meaning every page with an index file. qa_consumer_surface prints 120 meaning every page except VALENCE. Three correct exclusions, printed for months in one row as one number. Two sets of the same size are indistinguishable from their counts.
Quality · standing assertion 19
qa_census.py works out every population itself — from the built site and from the article files — then compares the sets by name, and checks each tool’s printed number against the population that tool says it means. Seventeen numbers from eleven instruments.

The bite that matters: swap one entry in the sitemap for a page that does not exist. Every count stays identical — 121 and 120, just as on a healthy build — and the new check fails, naming both the page that appeared and the one that vanished. A count comparison cannot see that at all.
Proved five ways, and one of the five is the useful one: the first attempt at a bite used a pattern that did not match, so nothing was actually injected — and the check printed clean, exactly as on a good build. We now assert that an injection changed the file before running the check on it. That guard has paid on two consecutive days.
Quality · three defects, all of them ours
One. qa_dist_input printed “120 built pages” for a site containing 121 — it was counting index files, and our branded 404 page is not one. Harmless to what that check does, and wrong in the log, which is where the number gets quoted.

Two. Two of the eleven tools crash when run from the web/ directory — which is exactly where part of our build runs them. Latent for weeks because the workflow happens to call them from elsewhere. The new check was the first thing to call them from a different place and found it immediately.

Three. The new check failed on itself, on the first real build. It worked out how many pieces are held by dividing the file count by two, because every piece we have ever published has an Arabic twin. Today’s Egypt draft is English-only until its Arabic is composed, so nine files are five pieces and the halving said four.
That third one became a corollary of the day’s ruling within the hour of the ruling being written: an invariant that has held for every case so far is still an assumption, and dividing by it is how an assumption gets welded into an instrument.

The watchdog’s objection was read in full for the first time, from an archive, because its own host refuses us.
