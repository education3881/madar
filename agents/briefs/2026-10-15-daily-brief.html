Nothing owed by you · one question is now yours · issue 11, with a default and a date

Today the publication corrected a fact, and the Arabic edition is the reason it could. The Kenya piece has been calling a principals’ association by the wrong name in English since 4 July — the Kenya Senior School Heads Association, where the register says Kenya Secondary Schools Heads Association. The Arabic edition had it right for all 103 days. That is not a coincidence and it is worth one paragraph of your attention: Arabic cannot carry a Latin acronym as a name, so whoever composed the Arabic had to go back to the source document and find out what KESSHA stands for. The English writer had the option of leaving the letters alone, took it in the citation, and then expanded them from memory in the sentence.

None of our checks could have caught it, and the reason is exact rather than embarrassing: our strongest editorial check compares the sentence against the citation beside it, and the citation said only KESSHA. A check that reads the citation cannot see a claim the citation does not make. The citation’s silence read as agreement. That is now ruling 101.

And one decision is genuinely yours, asked properly for the first time. The Edition 05 social packet has been ready to post for 45 days; every editorial gate on it is cleared; the only thing missing is a channel to post it to, which the operation cannot and should not create for itself. Four growth notes in a row diagnosed that correctly and none of them asked you — they carried it to a weekly review instead. Issue #11 now asks it, with three options, a recommendation (send Substack Issue 01 — it is one action, and the only option that turns on a number we are allowed to report), and a stated default: if nothing is said by 22 October, the packets get parked in writing rather than left looking pending.

Today

A checker that cannot support its answer now refuses to give one

2026-10-15 · Brief 102 · six commits, one per function plus the queue head
Quality · the oldest job on the board, closed

Two of our instruments compare “when did this page change” against “what does this page now contain”. The date comes from the commit history; the contents come from the folder on disk. Before a commit exists, those are two different moments, and the comparison was reporting the gap between them as a finding. It fired both ways: on 8 October it flagged 119 of 120 pages as silently changed, every one a false alarm; yesterday it reported zero drift while eight files had changed, which is the worse half, because the loud version gets investigated and a clean one does not.

The fix is not in either instrument — the fault was never in them. It is one shared precondition that refuses to answer when the folder has changes the date cannot yet see, and it refuses per question: the drift checker has three questions and only one of them depends on dates, so it keeps the other two and simply loses the right to say “clean”. A refusal is reported as its own outcome, distinct from a finding and from a network failure, because the whole defect was an unsupportable number being read as one of those.

Proved thirteen ways, the failing cases first, and the last two ran on the real repository in the real state. Same files, same build, one commit apart: “no claim about dates” becomes 47 named findings — where the old behaviour printed clean, zero drift. The test harness also caught itself twice, which is recorded in the log rather than smoothed over.

Research · ruling 101

An acronym is a name with its evidence removed. Either the citation spells the institution out as the source prints it, or the sentence keeps the acronym — the expansion and its evidence ship together or not at all. And where the two editions describe one institution, the edition that was forced to spell it out is the control, because a forced answer is a reading and an optional one may be a memory.

We then asked how widespread it is, across all 44 published English pieces, and the honest answer is three answers: a strict detector found 3 instances and would have missed today’s; a loose one found 28; a filtered one found 10, of which three turn out to be fragments of names the text states correctly. Ten candidates, seven unread, one confirmed — and the confirmed one was found by the Arabic edition, not by any of the three detectors. That is on the board rather than in a conclusion.

And a red build, caught and fixed in the same run

After the sixth commit was already pushed, the final build failed. The cause was two rows this run had itself added to the guidebook an hour earlier, each missing its last column — exactly the defect that check was built for after the same thing happened in October’s first week. Fixed, rebuilt clean, pushed.

The part worth your time is why it took an hour. The run did check that gate after editing — but read the result through a text filter that did not match the way the tool prints a failure, and never read the exit code. Every line that came back said ok while the tool was saying FAIL. A green light manufactured by the reader rather than by the instrument. Our whole rulebook is about checks that cannot see a defect; this one saw it, said so, and was filtered out. From today a gate’s verdict is read from its exit code, never through a filter.

Whether a source still says what we said it says

2026-10-16 · Friday · the queue head is already decided by rule
The forward question, and it goes first

A 200 is not the document we cited. Every instrument we own sorts a source link by status code — alive, dead, blocked, timed out, bad certificate — and nothing anywhere asks whether the words at that address are still the words the citation describes. Today’s listing-page-with-a-200 is the proof that the gap is reachable by accident.

It compounds with yesterday’s rule. If a dead link is the only event that ever makes anyone re-read a published source, then a source that dies quietly — rewritten at the same address, replaced by a section front, moved behind a paywall — never triggers the re-reading and reports healthy forever. Of 341 promises, 283 answer; how many still carry our figure, our headline or our quotation is unknown and has never been asked.
