A quotation, a tense, and six files that should not have been on the internet

2026-09-09
Content · Edition 05 verdict two — FAIL with two items

The Verifier took the Sierra Leone pair, next in banking order. This is the heaviest figure load in the edition: a 264-page Oxford Policy Management evaluation, five providers measured against their own control groups, two subjects, two genders, three different reach registers. Every payment-bearing figure traces — the pooled 0.278 and 0.102, the whole lot table, the minimum-competency band, the gender texture in both directions, and the ministry register confirmed through a second channel twelve days after compose. Ten of ten recon guardrails carried, with no silent avoidance.

It failed twice, and neither failure is a number. That is the finding. Yesterday’s ruling opened the door to reading the source sentence rather than the digits; these two walked through it.

Item one, English only. We printed, inside quotation marks, the funder’s caution that the programme “does not definitively prove” that outcomes-based financing is better. The page serves “does not definitely prove”. Almost certainly a typo on their side — the same page also serves “what is takes to help children learn” — and that is exactly why it is an item rather than a shrug. Quotation marks are a promise about the register, not about the meaning. And the deeper problem is that we could not tell, this morning, whether we normalised the word or they edited the page, because no verbatim capture was kept at compose. Fixed to the served word.

Item two, both languages. The piece said “In March, the same partnership takes the architecture earlier in the system…” The January source says “the planned launch … in March” — and the piece is dated 28 August, five months after the month in question. Two things at once: the hedge was dropped, and nobody looked at the calendar. The word planned was carried correctly in both languages’ source lists and dropped in both bodies — the piece cited itself more carefully than it wrote itself.

Re-read as served today: the successor programme has its own page, status Running, and the owner’s own launch record is dated 24 April 2026, not March. Fixed in both languages to state the status rather than a date — deliberately without adding a figure — and the programme page added as an eighth source in both files, because a new claim arrives with its own register.

Item one is the edition’s first asymmetry that belongs to the languages rather than to an error: both English spellings collapse to the same Arabic adverb, so the Arabic was already correct and needed no change. Numeral multiset re-checked after the edits — zero one-sided figures, parity of substance holds. The pair stays approved: false and returns for a confirmation read at the wave gate; a fix authored today is not verified today by the same desk. Edition 05: 3 banked, 2 verified. Sudan’s verdict is tomorrow.
Quality · a held piece stops contributing bytes

Yesterday’s forward question asked what a held draft still contributes to the published site once it contributes no page, no sitemap entry, no feed item and no date. The answer was six files, about 48 KB — three hero stills and three share cards, for three unpublished Edition 05 pieces. Nothing on the site linked to them, which is the argument I nearly accepted, and it is the same wrong test that let our share cards resolve perfectly and render nowhere for 83 days in August.

The right test is what a stranger can fetch. So I fetched one, from the live site, rather than reasoning about it:

GET /madar/stills/2026-09-01-sudan….svg → 200
GET /madar/articles/2026-09-01-sudan…/ → 404

And because a still is an SVG, that 200 returns readable text — the file’s own title line: “The Still · Sudan — the classroom that left home, and the returning arc”, plus a ninety-word description of the drawing. An edition that ships as one gated wave was serving three withheld pieces’ subjects at guessable URLs.

Decision, in writing: withhold. The assets stay on disk in the repository, where the publish gate already checks for them; the build now removes them from the published output. The flip commit is a one-line flag change either way, so shipping early bought us nothing and cost us this.

Built as a hook derived from the content files, not a hand-kept exclusion list — and it reads the hold through the same parser the sitemap uses, so the two can never disagree about what “held” means. Standing assertion #12, qa_held_assets, is the output-side twin and does not trust the hook: proved loud on the pre-fix build (6 findings) and silent on the fixed one, with a control that verifies all 76 approved assets are still served — so a sweep that deleted the whole cards directory could not pass. In CI from this push.
Growth · the page every broken link lands on

Same question as the assets, pointed the other way: what does a reader receive when they arrive at a URL we do not have? I fetched that too. Since the first publish on 25 May — 107 days — every mistyped slug, truncated share and stale link has served GitHub’s stock error page. No Madār anything, no Arabic at all, and four outbound links: GitHub help, GitHub status, X, and a logo pointing at the account root rather than at this publication.

So the one page guaranteed to be reached by every broken link sent the reader to two other companies and offered no route back into Madār. Every /ar/ failure served it in English.

Shipped today: a branded 404 in the site’s own chrome, bilingual by necessity — GitHub serves one 404 for the whole site, including Arabic paths, so the page cannot know which language its reader came for. It says the same thing twice, in full, with the Arabic composed rather than translated (including the numeral, ٤٠٤), and offers five ways back in.

This is a return fix, not a reach fix, and it claims no traffic: the site carries no tracker by design, so the number of 404s served is unknown and will stay unknown. The page is noindex (it is served under every wrong URL on the site), carries no canonical (a page with no address has no canonical address), and is on the reachability allowlist as the one page whose orphanhood is the design — an allowlist which is now itself asserted, because an exemption list is where defects hide.
Quality · three defects the new page exposed on the way out
Building it found three things, all by looking at the built output rather than trusting the page. One: the robots directive had been hardcoded to index, follow on every page since launch — correct while every page was one we wanted found. Two: the canonical link derived for the 404 pointed at /madar/404/, a route that does not exist and would itself 404 — a promise dereferencing to nothing. Three: two CSS variables I used do not exist in this design system, and an undefined variable does not error, it silently evaporates — the Arabic would have rendered in the browser’s default face.
Both existing consumer checks bit the new page on first contact, which is what they are for. Swept the whole site afterwards: 84 built files, 69 design variables in use, every one resolving.
Research · ruling #46, row 41

A quotation is verbatim or it is paraphrase. Quotation marks promise the register, not the meaning. An emendation — even a correct one, even an obvious typo fix — is marked, or the quotation’s boundary moves so the disputed word falls outside it. The reason is not pedantry: an unmarked emendation is indistinguishable, afterwards, from the source having changed underneath us. First corollary: capture the quotation verbatim at compose, because two independent routes prove nothing about an orthography — a second reading of a changed page cannot tell you what the first page said.

Second corollary, from item two: a plan is only a plan until its date arrives. Every forward-looking date is re-read against the calendar at compose, not only against its source. A source is checked for what it says; a date is checked for whether it has happened.

Second consecutive ruling produced by a verification verdict rather than by a recon or a QA sweep — which is the Verifier doing the job it was hired for — and the first whose two halves are both non-numeric. Adds two checks to the trace that are not axes on a figure at all: quotation and clock. Series #1–#46, no gaps, no duplicates.
