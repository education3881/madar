Research — row 33: the labial-velar rule the Africa edition needed
The West African /k͡p/ (written kp) is one co-articulated consonant — and Arabic, which has neither the sound nor a letter for it, renders both closures: kp → كب. So Chief Education Officer Kpakra is كباكرا — never a uvular that is not there, never half the name. The sibling that keeps the rule honest: Sackey → ساكي by the heard vowel, which does match English habit — so nobody generalises either way. The same gate routed Scottish Gauld (غولد), Irish Fergal (فيرغال) and Catalan Castellnou (كاستيلنو), each by its own orthography against its letters.
