Manager → Founder

We went to write the Rwanda piece and found that the document our own research said did not exist has been published since April.

Two days ago this operation recorded that Rwanda’s 2024/25 education statistics yearbook — the register the piece needs for its central number — had not been published, and gave a reason: the ministry’s publication schedule. It has been sitting on the ministry’s own downloads page since 21 April 2026. Five months. It is listed there with its upload date, next to every previous edition, on one page.

We found it because of a rule we wrote a fortnight ago: a load-bearing sentence is composed with the source open in front of you, never from the note you took about the source. The draft was today’s job. Opening the register is the first thing drafting means. The rule paid for itself inside an hour.

And the newer register contradicts the piece we were about to write. Our commission said Rwanda certified 24,000 teachers on weekends and thereby discharged most of that debt. The ministry’s own annual, for the school year that ended after the programme had been running fifteen months, says the number of untrained teachers went up — 27,322 to 27,409. Primary fell by 443. Pre-primary rose by 530. The ministry’s own word for the primary movement is “marginally,” and it names the cause itself: “newly recruited primary school teachers.”

Neither number is wrong and neither register is hiding anything — and this is the part worth your minute, because it is a mistake we will now never make again. 24,000 is a flow: certificates issued over a period. 27,409 is a stock: people standing in classrooms untrained on census day. Subtracting one from the other silently assumes nobody was hired untrained while the programme ran, and Rwanda hired 5,539 more teachers that year. The subtraction feels like arithmetic. It is a forecast. No figure in our commission was wrong. The verb was.

The piece gets better, and the posture holds. The story was never that the debt was retired — our own commission says the accomplishment is the bookkeeping. What the bookkeeping now shows is a government publishing, in its own statistical annual, the number that undercuts its funder’s headline, and naming its own hiring as the reason. That is rarer and harder than paying the debt off. It is the same thing we credited Brazil with: a state that prints the unkind number beside the kind one.

So the draft is not written today, for the second day running. The run that re-scoped the argument is not the run that writes to it — that is how a guardrail gets composed in the morning and forgotten by the afternoon. Fifteen days to the gate target, and the date is what gives way, never a verdict.

Today

Six of our own past defects, put back. Four were caught. One is caught by nothing we own.

Saturday 26 September 2026 · five functions, all five delivered
Quality · we stopped writing our own test cases
Every test this operation has ever written for one of its own checks was written by whoever wrote the check, against the defect they were already thinking about. That is the strongest possible test of the problem we imagined and no test at all of the problem. So today we used material we did not choose: the six real defects this publication has shipped, found and fixed — put back into the website exactly as they originally appeared, and then we watched whether the check that now exists for each one still notices.
Each of the six was proved to have actually landed before the check ran on it — a sabotage that quietly fails to apply looks identical to a check that passed, and we have been caught by that once already. Every file was restored afterwards and the restoration verified by fingerprint.
Result · four caught, one harmless blind spot, one hole
Caught: the social caption that rounded 49.3% to 49% (it was wrong for 90 days); the share-card image served in a format no social network renders (83 days); “Skip to content” printed in English on an Arabic page, which is the first thing a screen reader says (93 days); and a machine-readable breadcrumb pointing at a page that does not exist (19 days). Blind, and acceptably so: a newsletter subject line that claimed “ten years” of a programme that has run since 2018 — our caption check reads digits, and ten is a word. We are leaving that alone rather than making a good check worse.
The hole. For 83 days and 35 quality passes, the RSS link in the footer of every page pointed at an address that did not exist. We found it in August, fixed it, and wrote a rule so it could not happen again. Put that exact broken link back today and all three checks that might plausibly catch it say the site is fine.
Why · the rule was written and the tool never was
The August rule says the quality pass must check every link the page layout emits. It was run once, by hand, the next morning — 98 links, none broken — and that clean result was recorded as the rule working. It never became a file. For 41 days the operation has had a rule where it believed it had a check, and none of our counting could see the difference, because a tool that does not exist cannot be counted as missing.
The reason it is worth a ruling rather than an apology: the twin of this rule, written one day later, did get built. That one asks “is every page linked to?” and became a real tool the same day. This one asks “does every link go somewhere?” and became prose. The difference is that this one had a clean hand-run to point at. A manual check that comes back green is the most effective way to stop a real check being written.
Fixed · standing assertion 25, and it is a file
qa_chrome_links reads every link and image reference on all 121 pages — 4,755 of them — and asserts that each internal one resolves to a file the build actually produced, and that none escapes the site’s address prefix. Proved four ways: clean on the real site; fails on the historical footer link; fails on a stylesheet pointed at a file that does not exist; and fails with a distinct code when pointed at an empty folder, because a check with nothing to examine has failed rather than passed.
It also prints the layout’s own links by name, footer RSS link included. “0 broken of 4,755” does not tell a reader that the footer was looked at, and the entire history of this defect is reports that did not say what they had read. Wired into the build in the same commit. 25 assertions, 22 gate the deploy.

Growth · a sentence we have printed about eighty-five times, now scoped
The new link checker prints the layout’s shared links by name, and three of them are not ours: every page of Madār, in both languages, requests fonts from Google before a letter appears. That is not a tracker — no cookie, no identifier, no analytics — and this note does not call it one. It does hand a third party the reader’s IP address, their browser, and the address of the article they are reading, at the moment they read it.
“The site carries no third-party tracker by design” is true and narrower than it sounds. We would not accept that gap in a ministry’s register. From today the line says both halves. The fix — serve the fonts ourselves — is specified, costed and deliberately not done today: two of the five typefaces are Arabic, and a font swap is exactly what our three Arabic rendering checks exist to catch. It gets its own run, with those margins recorded in advance. (This brief loads the same fonts. Saying so is the point.)
