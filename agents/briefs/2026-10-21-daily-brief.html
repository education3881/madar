Daily brief · No. 108 Wednesday 21 October 2026
Nothing owed by you · the site stood still for two days · repaired this morning

The publication did not move on Monday or Tuesday, and the work did. Monday’s run finished green, pushed five commits, and wrote you a brief. Seven minutes later the deploy failed, and nothing said so. The website you can open has been serving Sunday’s build ever since, while two days of work sat in the repository waiting for a step that had already refused it.

The cause was small and the lesson is not. Monday’s growth work gave forty pages a machine-readable pointer to “the publisher” and “the website” and did not also give those pages the thing being pointed at — eighty pointers with nothing at the other end. A check we own caught it exactly as designed. But that check does not run on this machine. Twelve of our thirty-two gates live in the deploy file, which this identity is not allowed to write and therefore never runs; the other twenty run locally. So Monday built the site four times, saw twenty green lights, and reported the gates green. It was telling the truth about twenty of thirty-two, and a green two-thirds looks exactly like a green whole. Both halves are fixed today: the pointers resolve, and there is now one command that runs the twelve by reading them out of the deploy file rather than out of a sentence in a handbook — a sentence which, it turns out, said eleven.

The day’s own work went to a question seven days old: which of our published claims can nobody re-verify any more, including us. Every instrument we had counted links. None could say that a sentence had become uncheckable. The answer needed one question we had never asked in five months of publishing — not can we open this source but can anybody — and that turns on whether a public archive kept a copy. Of 342 sources cited across this publication, 205 have now been asked: forty exist in exactly one copy, on somebody else’s server, and twelve of those forty already refuse us. The figures resting on them come to twenty-four — and twenty of the twenty-four are on a single piece. Counted per link that reads as a tolerable percentage spread across the corpus. Counted per claim it reads as one article whose spine we could not re-open. Only the second sentence tells anybody what to do.

And the readers get the other half of the same measurement. A hundred and sixty-five of those sources are archived, and we had never told anyone. Every article in both editions now offers the archived copy beside the live link — 339 of them across 85 of 88 pages. When a ministry PDF disappears next year it will stop taking a figure with it.

Traffic, plainly: the site carries no third-party tracker, no analytics and makes no third-party request, by design — so there is no visitor number here and none is estimated. The archive link is an ordinary link; nothing is fetched automatically, and all 641 automatic requests on the site are still to ourselves. Return rate stays unmeasurable until there is a channel to measure it on, which is issue #11; no reply yet, and its stated default — park the wave packets in writing — falls tomorrow, 22 October, and has not been applied.

What the gap was for

The honest accounting is that Monday’s own closing note predicted the shape of this: it wrote down that it had measured four things by hand, built a check for one, and that the only reason it re-measured anything was that it happened to remember. The failure that followed is the same sentence one layer out — a measurement nobody is obliged to take is a measurement that will eventually not be taken.

Quality · standing check 35, and only half of it can gate

qa_claim_registers is the first check we own whose subject is a property of the world rather than of anything we make. So it is split, and the split is the point: the half that asks an archive about every source we cite gates nothing — an outage at archive.org must never stop this publication deploying — while the offline half gates the build and asserts only that the record covers every source we cite. If the measurement ever quietly stops describing some sources, the build goes red instead of the number narrowing in silence.

Proved four ways with the failing case first, and a control run green on either side of each.

The honest item — our first reading measured us, not the world

The first run of the measurement asked all 342 sources at once. It got a hundred answers and then 242 refusals in a row, because the archive rate-limits a burst that size — and wrote a file in which 242 entries read unknown, which is indistinguishable from a source no archive has ever held. That file was a measurement of our own request rate wearing the shape of a finding. I had already written it up as 6.4% and quoted the number in a commit message, where it stands. Against the sources actually asked it is 19.5% — three times larger. A rate whose denominator contains unmeasured units is not cautious; it is wrong in the flattering direction.

Content · the reason to write the piece was in our own archive

The Bahrain commission is re-issued, and the strongest thing in it is not new research. Our own piece of 26 May ends by saying that the authority’s announcement does not give the cross-tabulation, and that “the question is the kind that earns a follow-up piece.” The register found last Sunday is that cross-tabulation: 882 graded reviews over five cycles, published by the state, sitting on its open-data portal the whole time. So this is a dated follow-up 148 days later, the predecessor is named and quoted in the body, and nothing it already reported may carry a paragraph here.

Still not drafted, and no slot claimed. The licence on that dataset reads unknown, so the counts are reportable and the table is not ours to serve.

Growth · a second route to every citation an archive kept

339 archived-copy links — 169 English, 170 Arabic — across 85 of 88 article pages. The live source keeps the title link, because a cached copy is evidence about what a page said and the owner’s own address is what the piece cites. Three pages have no route yet; their sources are among the 137 the paced measurement has not reached, and the number rises on its own.

Asking the archive to take a snapshot of the forty one-copy sources is the obvious next move and it was deliberately not done: it is this operation’s first outbound write to somebody else’s service, and a growth function should not quietly be the first to make one. Written down, recommended, queued.

Knowledge · ruling #111

The unit of re-verifiability is the claim, and a per-link rate hides it. Four count homes moved and not five, because this ruling joins a family that deliberately keeps no running total — inventing one would write a number nobody maintains.

Make a finished run and a delivered one tell themselves apart

2026-10-22 · Thursday
First, before anything new

No run has ever seen its own publication. Every check we own runs inside the run; the deploy happens after it ends. A rule says each brief must carry the previous deploy’s result — and that rule is a sentence, not an instrument, so nothing failed when Monday’s deploy failed. The fix is cheap: read the previous deploy’s conclusion through a permission the job already has, and go red when it failed. Then no run can absorb a dark day by not looking.

Second, and it is today’s own debt

Wire the twelve unreachable gates into the local build, so a green build here means a green publication there by construction. Today gave them a command; a command somebody has to remember is not a gate.

Content

The Bahrain piece can be drafted against the re-issued commission. Arabic is composed from the register’s own Arabic column, which this authority prints beside the English, so neither edition may paraphrase and a disagreement between them is a defect rather than a choice.
