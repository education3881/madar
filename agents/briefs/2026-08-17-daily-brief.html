Content — Ed05 recon 7: the Egypt carry-over, re-angled positive
Your 11 August redirect kept Egypt in the Africa edition but re-pointed the angle from deficit-scrutiny to accomplishment. This is that re-angle, executed — and it is a different instrument from the teacher recon, so by ruling #30 the two do not merge.

On 18 August 2025 — almost exactly a year ago — Egypt ratified amendments creating the Egyptian Baccalaureate (بكالوريا مصر), a second secondary-leaving certificate standing beside the 120-year-old Thanaweya Amma rather than replacing it. Seven subjects instead of 32. Four tracks. Two exam sittings a year with retakes, on a 700-point scale replacing a 320-point one. And Article 37bis, which guarantees that taking it is optional.

The reported opt-in: about 95% in the first year, and 99% — on the order of 832,000 students — for 2026-27.

That number is the piece, and it is the piece precisely because it cuts both ways. Near-unanimous take-up of a voluntary alternative is either the most emphatic verdict a school system has ever received from its own families, or it is not a verdict at all. Egypt's public record carries both readings: the ministry's, that it built a better track and families recognised it; and MP Freddy Elbaiady's, filed after the minister met some 4,000 school directors on 31 August 2025 — "This is not persuasion. This is coercion masquerading as awareness-raising" — with his party alleging that students who preferred the old system were transferred to distant or less-equipped schools. We do not adjudicate that from here. We print the rate, the statutory guarantee, the allegation and the ministry's answer in one passage, and let the reader hold all three.
Seven registers mapped, six read in served text — including the ministry's own. The single most persuasive thing found: on 25 July 2025 the two ministries jointly published which faculties and majors each baccalaureate track opens, explicitly engineering parity with the old streams — two years before the first cohort sits its final exams. A system is only as real as the doors it opens, and Egypt published the door list in advance. That is the accomplishment your positive posture is for, and it is also what makes the 99% legible: families were choosing between two certificates whose university consequences had been published side by side. Verdict PROCEED-recommend; one draft-blocking item, which is the 99% itself — single register, body unread, and it needs the ministry's own count and an explicit denominator before the spine can be written.
Research-to-learn — guidebook row 24, filed as ruling #31
A voluntary-uptake figure carries its counterfactual. A participation, opt-in or take-up rate is evidence of preference only if the unchosen option remained genuinely available on comparable terms. Where the alternative was administratively disfavoured — not offered locally, thinly staffed, reached only by transfer, discouraged by officials carrying enrolment targets — the same percentage measures availability, not preference. Three things are stated before it rides: what the alternative was, who could still get it, on what terms. All three answered, and it may be written as a choice. Any unanswered, and it is written as a record: "N% enrolled," never "N% chose."

Why it earns a number rather than a note: our seven figure axes all interrogate a measurement — who administered it, at what coverage, stage, vintage, scope, direction, series position. A take-up rate is not a measurement of children; it is a record of an administrative process, and its validity turns on a counterfactual none of the seven reach. It passes every check we have and still misleads. It is the mirror of #29: that one rejects a number whose ruler is unpublished, this one qualifies a number whose alternative is unexamined.
Binding on our own copy, and not retroactively — Vietnam's fee waiver, Morocco's 626→4,626 and Poland's enrolment gain stay as shipped; every future one carries the clause. Poland is the instructive contrast: its gain came under a mandate, and no counterfactual is owed for a compulsory instrument. #31 attaches to the word "optional," wherever it appears. The Verifier's checklist gains one question — what was the alternative, and to whom was it actually available? Series now #1–#31, no gaps, no duplicates.
