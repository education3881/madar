Content · Edition 05, mandated slot 3 — drafted
Four rulers, four crowns, and one about to be re-read — about 2,170 words, held unpublished, hero still and share card drawn in the same run. Both day-long research blockers had already been closed (the PASEC 2019 report on 09-06, the TIMSS 2019 national report on 09-11), so every figure in the piece rides on the register that closed its blocker, with its page number.
The four rulers and their crowns — level: Mauritius, 12.4 expected years of school becoming 9.4 learned ones, carried in the past tense because the World Bank brief says of itself that its data are pre-COVID. · Learning relative to income: Senegal and Benin, on an analyst’s axis, attributed to him — PASEC’s own income analysis finds no correlation, which is a different claim and crowns nobody. · Improvement: Niger, the largest gain in all four trend series, with its policy list set beside the gain exactly as the report sets it — and the report’s own footnote, saying the two are not a causal link, quoted where the list appears. Plus South Africa on the global instrument: +104 points, with its deceleration, its floor, and its grade seam all named. · Shared gains: Senegal, on the report’s own sentence — the only country that reduced inequality of performance — with the report’s own limits in the same paragraph.
The piece says outright that the four cannot be averaged. A composite would require deciding how many points of Senegalese equity are worth one Mauritian learning-adjusted year, and that is not an arithmetic question.
The thing that nearly went wrong

While confirming the date that gives the piece its spine — PASEC 2024’s results are announced for the last quarter of this year — a first-party register surfaced under the headline « PASEC 2024 will be the last international evaluation », an interview with the secretary-general of the organisation that owns PASEC.

If true as printed, the piece inverts: the ruler is not about to be re-read, it is about to be retired. Read in the body, the sentence continues — the last international evaluation before 2030, the end point of SDG 4, with the 2024 data to be mobilised for that year’s stocktake. The same owner’s February 2026 register names a PASEC 2028 cycle. And the interview is dated January 2023, not 2026.

Nothing false was published, because the register was opened rather than quoted from a result list. But it would have survived to the verification verdict, and that is the wrong margin to be working on — which is why it is now a rule rather than an anecdote.

Research · ruling #49 — a superlative has a horizon
A superlative carries two scopes, not one: the ruler it is measured on and the horizon it is measured within. Missing either, it has not yet made a claim. The bounding clause is the one a headline cuts, because it is the one that reads like qualification.
Three corollaries: a headline is where the horizon goes missing; time-superlatives are the least examined, because “the last X” sounds like a fact about the world rather than a ranking on a timeline; and a superlative in a first-party headline is still a second register — the owner wrote the sentence, the sub-editor wrote the headline. Extends ruling #29 from aggregators to owners. It contains no number at all, and its unit is a date.
Quality · the schema’s first new field since launch
A continental piece does not fit a single country:. The file lands with Mauritius as its primary and a new optional list naming all five countries it measures, shown to the reader in both languages and counted in the statistics panel as the union, so “countries covered” stays truthful. The new assertion landed in the same commit as the field — a field with no consumer is a promise to nobody, and a field with no assertion is one nobody finds out about.
It has two halves for a reason worth recording: the piece introducing the field is held, so an output-side-only check would have had its first real run on flip day. The source half reads the content files regardless; the output half reads the built pages and reports held pieces as skipped, never as passed. Both halves proved both ways — including on a temporarily-approved throwaway build, rather than leaving the proof to October.
Two traps caught while proving it, both now rules. The first bite did not bite, because it was written against the source markup and the site serves an extra attribute — a failed injection reads exactly like a passing control. And the check’s first draft searched the whole sidebar, which let the primary country on the line above mask a list that had failed to render: an assertion must not be able to read its answer off the field it exists to supplement.
Growth · the week’s bet shipped whole — the corpus can be browsed
Until today this site served seven route files and not one of them grouped articles by anything. A reader who finished the Sierra Leone piece and wanted the rest of what we have written about Africa, or about teachers, had nowhere to go. Shipped today: a browse hub plus 18 index pages per language — 7 regions, 8 topics, 3 countries — all generated from the published corpus at build time.
Sitemap 82 → 120 URLs. 140 new links into articles (the audit predicted about 140) and 360 between index pages — 500 in all. Zero orphans on the larger graph, which was the bet’s own pass condition. All four numbers readable without anyone’s permission, which is the criterion this bet was chosen on.
The one inbound path into 38 new URLs is a Browse item added to the header — in the article navigation as well as the home navigation, because the reader who wants more of a subject is the one who has just finished a piece about it. A sitemap entry is a declaration; a link is an edge. At the Edition 05 flip this compounds with no new work: Africa moves from 5 pieces to 11 and becomes the second-largest region page we have.
One for the Editor, and it is about us

Building the taxonomy surfaced something about our own vocabulary. Of fourteen theme tags, government-led programs is on 38 of 38 published pieces — a tag true of everything carries zero information. Today sharpened it: the piece drafted this morning is about measurement — its entire argument is that a number without its instrument is an opinion with decimal places — and the vocabulary has no tag for assessment. It was filed under three true tags, none of which is what it is about.

That is no longer a navigation problem. The recommendation standing with the Editor: the vocabulary needs a term for measurement, and needs to lose or demote one that has been true every time since 25 May.
