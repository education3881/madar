The week's compounding asset: the scoped-instrument family, now named

Four rows turned out to be filing four shapes of a single rule. Consolidated:

ShapeRowThe failure it names
A pledge is a scoped instrument15Progress figure quoted without its scope-definition-with-date
A multiplier without a base19"Ninefold" with no disclosed baseline
A deficit is a ratio, not a headcount20A closure claim with no published formula and a moving denominator

The one rule underneath: a claimed quantity is unreadable without the instrument that produced it — scope, base, denominator and ruler are four names for the same missing half of a figure. The verification move is a single question asked of any headline number: "…out of what, produced how?", with both answers required from the source rather than reconstructed by arithmetic. This is the seven-axes checklist's sibling — the axes ask what a number is conditioned on; this family asks what made it. None of the four needed a new number; they ride #21/#22/#24/#25, which is what a maturing checklist looks like.

Two rulings: #29 — a ranking is not a measurement · #30 — a lineage claim is a figure

#29, from the daily run (row 23, out of Ed05 recon 6): a composite rank is citable only if its ruler is published — named input indicators, weights, vintages, and coverage/comparison set, all four. Absent any one, report the underlying indicator direct from its own register and let the reader compare; a composite may not appear as evidence at any level of hedging, and attributing it to its ingredients ("a ranking based on UNESCO and World Bank data") launders it, inheriting none of their authority and all of the blender's arbitrariness. It takes a number because it rejects a source class outright rather than telling a writer how to caveat one. It also binds Madār's own copy — which is exactly what your instruction to find the best system in Africa by research, not assumption requires, and it is why recon 6 opens by choosing the ruler rather than by naming a winner.

#30, from this review. Row 22's Sudan method note moves from row-level to numbered: a claim that instrument B is, descends from, continues or is a phase of instrument A is a citable figure, carrying its own provenance axes — same programme, formal successor, rebrand, or independent parallel — and it must be traced verbatim to a source that states the relationship, exactly as a number is traced to a source that states the value. Same place plus same technology plus same look ≠ same instrument.

It earned the number on three tests. Nothing existing covers it — the seven axes and the scoped-instrument family both scope a number; this scopes an identity, and a draft can carry every figure perfectly and still be wrong in its spine. It caught a live one: the tempting Sudan spine, "the wartime digital learning spaces run the platform Sudan invented," is supported by place, technology and idea, by no served-text source stating the identity, and is contradicted by one register — War Child's 2025 country list, which does not include Sudan. It generalises across the slate: Egypt's drive-versus-drive, Rwanda's CBC descent, every "Phase II," and every "building on the success of" sentence in a press release, which is where lineage claims are manufactured. The Verifier's trace list gains a lineage claim traced to a source stating the relationship: ☐ row whenever a spine depends on it. Drafted as #29 and renumbered at reconciliation per §2 — first-filed keeps the number. Series check after reconciliation: #1–#30, no gaps, no duplicates, verified file by file.
