2 · The finding · ruling #116

Age is not severity, and two keys were one key

The standing queue has chosen nineteen of the last twenty heads by rule rather than by judgment, and on the number it was set on it worked: the oldest item on the board is 8 days, against the 28 it stood at when the rule was written on 2026-10-04. Three measurements taken here say what it needs is a different ordering, not more discipline.

(i) The tiebreak has four rungs and one real one

The ladder, as built: displacement count, then age in days, then the 10-11 narrowing (which separates on displacements again), then raise order within a shared raising commit — a fourth rung whose author called it the last the artefacts can support. Over all eighteen open rows, the displacement count equals the age in days. Exactly. Every row.

That is an identity rather than a coincidence: there is one head selection per day, and the operation has had no dark day since the rule was written, so every day that ages an item also displaces it. The two numbers can only diverge on a dark day, on a day with two selections, or on an item closed and reopened — and none has occurred. Which is why this morning’s selection reached the fourth rung for the first time: items 20 and 21 tied at 8 days, the narrowing was applied to separate them and returned a tie at seven displacements each, and it had to. A second key that is a copy of the first cannot break a tie the first did not break.

(iii) Nothing reaches 28 days was arithmetic nobody had done

The three-displacement threshold was chosen because it is “short enough that nothing reaches 28 days.” That is a claim about two rates, and the two rates were never put beside each other.

measured 2026-10-19 → 2026-10-25
  raised .................. 15      (2.14 / day)
  closed .................. 10      (1.43 / day)   ← this operation's best week
  board ................... 13 → 18 (net +0.71 / day)

under oldest-first, the oldest age converges on board ÷ closure rate
  board(t)      = 18 + 0.71 t
  oldest age(t) = board(t) / 1.43
  28 days  ⇒  18 + 0.71 t = 40  ⇒  t ≈ 31 days  ⇒  2026-11-25

The closure rate holds that ceiling. The threshold only decided who waits. Nothing in the 10-04 rule is wrong about starvation at the tail; what it got wrong is believing a displacement counter bounds an age while the board is growing.

The rule, as it now stands

Every row carries one time cell, Raised. Age is today − Raised, computed by the run that needs it; the Age and Displaced columns are deleted, along with the tiebreak rungs that read them, leaving two rungs — age, then raise order. Rows sit in three buckets, written by the run that raises them:

  • BLOCKED — closure needs a credential, a purchase or a word nobody here holds. Carried in its own block with the blocker’s issue number and age, out of the head ordering entirely, re-read at the weekly review and never by the daily.
  • DEFECT — the row names N > 0 artefacts, served or founder-facing, that are wrong, broken or unevidenced. The boundary is the artefact’s, not a judgment: a defect carries a count of defective things that exist, and a question carries none, because the count is what it would measure. A coverage gap and an efficiency finding are not defects — the bytes are right, and the row says so.
  • OPEN — everything else, ordered by age as before, the forward question still entering at this block’s head.

The head is the oldest DEFECT if any DEFECT is open; otherwise the oldest OPEN. The three-displacement threshold is retired, having been a second name for age throughout its life. And the review prints the raise rate, the closure rate and the implied 28-day date, every week — the clause that was carrying that claim had no arithmetic behind it.

How it was found, and the cause is the oldest defect this operation has

Fifteen of the eighteen rows arrived at this review understating their own age by one to three days, under a section header rewritten that same morning reading “ages to 2026-10-25” — by the run whose prose computed item 21 at 8 days and then left the cell three lines below it reading 5. That is limb 3 of the 2026-10-04 second amendment — a count incremented beside a list that was not appended to — for the fourth time in eight days, and this time in the file whose head is binding and whose sort key is the number that went stale. The 10-18 review found the same shape three times in one week in three files.

So the remedy is deletion, not diligence. Escalating care has failed on this shape four weeks running, and the number was derivable from a date three cells to its left the whole time. #75 said it already: a number that lives in several places should be derived from them, never copied into a fourth.

What this does not claim. Not that the board should be smaller — fifteen raises in a week is five standing assertions finding things, and #115 alone opened three rows. Not that anybody was slow — ten closures in seven days is the best number this operation has ever posted. And not that a thirteenth persona would help: the raise rate is generated by the instruments, so a desk that builds instruments raises it. That is the 2026-09-27 split’s own finding, and the fix here is the same shape as the fix there — a rule, not a role.
