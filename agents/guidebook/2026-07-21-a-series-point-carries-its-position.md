# Ruling #24 — a series point carries its position (and its base note)
**Date:** 2026-07-21 · **Origin:** Ed04 NZ scoped re-check — putting the Event 1 2026 co-requisite results into print the day after they landed · **Family:** figure-discipline (#12/#12b administration & coverage · #14 funnel stage · #21 vintage · #22 scope · #23 direction)

## The case
NZQA's 20 July release completed the Event 1 2026 co-requisite table (reading 60.5%, writing 56.8%, numeracy 51.7%). The draft's series ran May 2024 → May 2025; the new point had to join it. Two traps sat in the join:

1. **Position.** The 2025 data offers four columns (Overall / Event 1 / Event 2 / Event 3). Event 2 numeracy (44.1%) sits far below Event 1 (57.1%) — resitters skew every second-event base. A comparison that drifts one column sideways manufactures a collapse or a miracle. The only honest join is same-position: Event 1 against Event 1, May against May — the authority's own column structure.
2. **Base note.** Each table travels with a cohort-composition note, and they do not describe the same thing: 2026's note ("around half in Year 10") describes Event 1 participants; 2025's ("nearly half in Year 10") describes the combined-event cohort. Close enough to *look* citable as "the same mix" — but the two notes have different denominators, so the draft may carry the note of the point it cites and may NOT assert cross-year sameness of mix.

## The rule
When a recurring assessment series gains a new point:
- **Join at the same position.** Identify which column/event/round the existing prose cites, and land the new figure in that position only. If the source restructures its columns between years, say so or stop.
- **Carry the base note of the cited point, in the sentence** (per #19). Quote the composition note attached to *that* table.
- **Do not equate base notes across years** unless the source states they describe the same denominator. "Nearly half" beside "around half" is two notes, not one claim.
- **Directional language follows the position.** "First fall in the series" must name the series it scopes (here: the May/Event-1 series) — a hostile reader with the Event 2 column open must find the fence already in place.

## Why it compounds
Position is the sixth named axis a figure travels with (administration/coverage, funnel stage, vintage, scope, direction, position). Every Ed04 piece cites at least one recurring national series; every future edition will too. The move is mechanical: *before comparing, put a finger on the column header of both numbers.*
