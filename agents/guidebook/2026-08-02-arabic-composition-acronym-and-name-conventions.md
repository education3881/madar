# Ruling #28 — an English acronym keeps its Latin tag; an unheard name stays provisional

**Filed:** 2026-08-02 · **Family:** composition / transliteration (Arabic side) · **Occasion:** composing the Arabic for `australia-naplan-reset`, the first Ed04 AR of the wave and the opening move on the now-binding AR critical path.

Arabic at Madār is **composition, not translation** — the Arabic Editor's standing frame. Two conventions recurred often enough while composing the NAPLAN piece to be worth naming, because the AR wave (7 of 8 Ed04 drafts) will hit both repeatedly.

## The rule, two parts

**(a) An English-language institutional acronym keeps its Latin tag on first mention, then takes an Arabic short-name thereafter.**
An English assessment authority's acronym (ACARA, NAPLAN, AERO, AARE) is not transliterated into an Arabic initialism — Arabic readers searching for it, and the source URLs, both carry the Latin form. Convention: **Arabic gloss of the full name + the Latin acronym in parentheses on first mention**, then either the Latin acronym or a naturalised Arabic short-name for the rest. NAPLAN earns a naturalised short-name (نابلان) because it reads as a word and recurs; ACARA/AERO/AARE stay as gloss-then-(Latin) and are referred to afterwards as "الهيئة"/"المنظمة"/"الرابطة". This mirrors the shipped-corpus habit already visible in us-naep (NAEP/NAGB/NCES all carry their Latin tags beside an Arabic gloss). It keeps the AR piece **searchable in both scripts** — a bilingual-discoverability asset, not just a style choice.

**(b) A named human whose pronunciation cannot be confirmed from a served-text/audio primary is rendered best-effort and flagged `PROVISIONAL` for the pre-ship transliteration audit — never asserted.**
Transliteration of a person's name encodes a *sound*, and a spelling does not always reveal it (silent letters, non-obvious stress, family-specific pronunciations). The Arabic Editor's job is to verify named-human transliterations before any AR ships. When the sound is confirmable (common name, or a source that voices it), transliterate and move on. When it is **not** confirmable from the text alone, render the most defensible phonetic form, mark it `PROVISIONAL` in the AR gate log, and resolve it at the pre-ship sweep — do not let an unverified spelling harden into a shipped name. This is the named-human sibling of #26 (*a number you can see but cannot quote is not a citable figure*): a name you can spell but cannot **hear** is not yet a verified transliteration.

## Applied today (NAPLAN AR)
- Acronyms: ACARA → الهيئة الأسترالية للمناهج والتقييم والإبلاغ (ACARA); NAPLAN → نابلان (البرنامج الوطني لتقييم القرائية والحساب); AERO → المنظمة الأسترالية لبحوث التعليم (AERO); AARE → الرابطة الأسترالية للبحث في التعليم (AARE).
- Named humans (2): **David de Carvalho → ديفيد دي كارفالو** — CONFIRMED (Portuguese-origin surname, Arabic-media-standard كارفالو). **Stephen Gniel → ستيفن غنيل** — **PROVISIONAL**: the surname's pronunciation is not confirmable from the served-text primaries (ACARA PDFs render it in print, not in sound); غنيل is the best-effort form pending an audio/phonetic check at the pre-ship sweep.

## Why it compounds
The AR wave is now the binding parallel path; every remaining Ed04 AR carries English/other-script names and English acronyms. Fixing the convention once (a) removes a recurring decision from each composition and (b) turns the Arabic edition into a bilingual **search** surface rather than a mirror. Sits beside #15 (tansiq/tawjih + Kenya transliteration) in the transliteration family and #26 in the "confirmable-primary" method family.
