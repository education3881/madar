# Ruling #104 — the house spelling is not ours to apply to a name

**Filed** 2026-10-17, by the daily run, from the content function. **Family:** the
non-numeric family — *the figure is right, is the sentence?* — of which it is the
**thirteenth** member, and the first whose unit is a name's **orthography**.

---

## What was true

`2026-05-28-kuwait-ccet-arabic-tests` named one programme **four times in three places and
in four different ways**, and every one of them disagreed with the register. For **142 days**.

| | served | register's own words |
|---|---|---|
| `sources[]` annotation | `Multi-sensory Structured Arabic Language **Programme**` | `Multi-sensory Structured Arabic Language **Program**` |
| body, first mention | `… Language **Programme**. The **Programme** —` | as above |
| body, back-reference | `Technical assistance on the **Programme**` | as above |
| body, closing section | `The **Multi-Sensory** Structured Arabic Language **Programme**` | as above |
| body, methodology | `the principles of **Multi-Sensory Language teaching**` | register carries no such capitalised name; it writes `multi-sensory program`, lower case |

The register is the piece's own source 4 — the International Dyslexia Association's February
2013 profile of CCET — and it prints the name once: *"Multi-sensory Structured Arabic Language
Program. This is a one million dollar project that CCET has co…"*. The same document writes
`Center for Child Evaluation & Teaching` ten times and `Centre` never.

## The mechanism, and it is not carelessness

`Programme` is **Madār's** spelling. So are `Centre`, `organisation`, `programme`. The house
voice is International English and it is applied consistently, which is what a house voice is
for. It was applied to a proper name.

> **A house style rule applied to a proper name is a silent rename.** Every other rule in the
> style guide operates on the publication's own prose, where the publication is the authority.
> A name is the one string on the page whose authority is somewhere else.

It is silent because it produces a word that is *correct* — correctly spelled, correctly
capitalised by the house's own rule, and consistent with every other word around it. Nothing
in the sentence looks wrong. Compare #101, where the body invented an expansion the annotation
could not check: there the error at least looked like a fact. This one looks like a spelling.

## Why every gate passed, including the one built for exactly this

* **`annotation == sentence` (09-13) passed, correctly, and both were wrong.** The annotation
  said `Programme` and the body said `Programme`. The discipline compares the body to the
  annotation; it has no third thing to compare the annotation to. This is the first time the
  rule has been observed passing on a **shared error about a name** — #102 was a shared error
  about a name's *date*, caught cross-edition; this one cannot be.
* **`qa_pair_frontmatter` passed, correctly.** Parity is a floor (#86).
* **`qa_sources_alive` passed.** The URL's health says nothing about the name.
* **The numeric toolkit was never in play.** There is no number in a spelling.

## And the Arabic edition is STRUCTURALLY BLIND here — the third question in the #86 series

#86 asked **which questions only one edition can answer.** #101 added **which questions one
edition is FORCED to answer** — Arabic cannot carry a Latin acronym, so the Arabic composer is
driven back to the register while the English composer may take the token from memory.

This adds the third, and it is the uncomfortable one:

> **Which questions can NEITHER edition answer, because the second channel cannot express the
> distinction at all?**

Arabic has no letter case and no `Programme`/`Program` axis. It rendered the name as
«البرنامج العربي مُتعدِّد الحواس المُهَيكَل», identically in both occurrences, and it rendered
the methodology as a **description** («مبادئ التدريس مُتعدِّد الحواس») where the English had
capitalised it into a name. The Arabic edition was right twice and **could not have been wrong
and could not have been the control.** A second channel that is incapable of disagreeing reads
exactly like a second channel that agrees.

Orthography is one such axis. So are capitalisation, hyphenation, and the Latin
abbreviation. The lesson generalises past Arabic: **before trusting two channels, ask not only
which questions only one can answer (#86) but which the second cannot even hold.**

## How it was found, and how it was read

Not by any instrument. Standing-queue item 17 asked for the four remaining of ten
acronym-expansion candidates; **the ten were counted on 2026-10-15 and never written down**, a
third detector over the same corpus returns a third population, and so "four remain" was not
actionable — a count that outlived its membership list, which is limb 3 of the 10-04 amendment
pointed at a queue cell instead of at a register.

What was actionable was a different and *computable* question. A name's **boundary** is not a
textual property (10-16, proved by two disagreeing detectors) — but a name's **self-consistency
inside one document is.** Scanning all 88 published files for multi-word proper names rendered
two ways in one piece surfaced this one: `Multi-sensory` beside `Multi-Sensory`. The
inconsistency was only the tell. Reading the register to settle it found the larger error that
**neither** rendering had.

The register's origin answers **403 to every client available here**, including a Chrome
user-agent — 103 bytes, a hard bot wall, not a rendering wall (#70's bucket, new member). The
read is from the **Wayback snapshot of 2026-04-14**, HTTP 200, 85,291 bytes, in served text,
quoted rather than summarised. *A document we cannot read cannot outrank one we can* (#43) —
and a document we cannot read at its origin can still be read at an archive that serves it.

## Binding

1. **House style stops at the edge of a name.** Spelling, capitalisation and hyphenation inside
   a proper name are the register's, not the publication's, and they are copied rather than
   normalised — `Program` stays `Program` in a publication that writes `Programme` everywhere else.
2. **The hand-off to the Editor carries `proper names copied from register, not normalised: ☐`**,
   enumerating each full institution or programme name and the register clause it was copied
   from — the same teeth as the dek/title cap (07-27), `annotation == sentence` (09-13), the
   named-humans gender line (10-04) and the acronym line (#101).
3. **A name rendered two ways inside one document is a defect regardless of which is right**,
   and it is settled at the register, never by majority and never by the house rule.
4. **Where two editions check each other, name the axes the second edition cannot express**,
   and treat its agreement on those axes as silence rather than as confirmation.
5. **A back-reference in house spelling is an Editor question, not a correction.** This piece
   names the institution correctly once (`The Center for Child Evaluation and Teaching`) and
   then writes `the Centre` seven times. That is a voice decision and this run did not take it.
