## What this index is for

The Bahrain single (`2026-10-23-bahrain-bqa-five-cycles`) closes on a question and asserts it cannot be
answered: *how much of the movement in a published distribution belongs to the schools, and how much to
the instrument — ... because the register that would settle it is one nobody publishes anywhere.* **That
last clause is false**, and this index is the register that falsifies it.

England exempted outstanding primary and secondary schools from routine inspection, then lifted the
exemption and re-graded the cohort whose grade had been frozen **by rule** for up to eight years. The
regulator published the yield against the stock in one document. That is the decomposition Bahrain's
archive cannot perform, done by the regulator's own arithmetic rather than by ours.

## The register

**Ofsted / DfE official statistics — *State-funded schools inspections and outcomes as at 31 August
2022*, "Main findings".** `gov.uk`, read in served text 2026-11-11.

Quoted as the register prints it:

| What | The register's own words |
|---|---|
| The stock | *"Eighty-eight per cent of all schools are good or outstanding."* Figure 1, as at 31 Aug 2022: **21,725 schools — 18% outstanding, 70% good, 9% requires improvement, 3% inadequate.** *"The proportion has increased from 86% in August 2021, and had been stable at 86% since 2019."* |
| The yield | *"Only 17% of previously exempt schools that had a graded inspection this year remained outstanding."* / *"Of these, 17% retained their outstanding grade, while 21% were judged requires improvement or inadequate. The remainder were judged to be good."* |
| By phase | *"Fourteen per cent of previously exempt primary schools inspected this year remained outstanding"*, *"compared with 25% of secondary schools."* Figure 3: primary **263 schools** (14/65/18/3); secondary **104 schools** (25/54/14/7). |
| The exemption, dated | *"Between May 2012 and November 2020, outstanding primary and secondary schools were exempt from routine inspection."* / *"In November 2020, the government lifted its exemption."* |
| The stock is stale, in its own numbers | *"Sixty-four per cent of all schools have not had a graded inspection in the last five academic years."* |
| The frameworks, dated | *"We carried out inspections between September 2015 and August 2019 under the common inspection framework."* / *"Since September 2019, we have carried out inspections under the education inspection framework."* Figure 1 note: dotted lines mark framework changes *"in January and September 2012, September 2015 and September 2019."* |

## What the register does NOT say — recorded so no draft invents it

- It **does not state** that the current stock of grades reflects inspections from different periods. That
  is *implied* by the 64% sentence and the framework notes. Implied is not printed (#49).
- It **does not compare** outcomes across frameworks. The framework changes appear **only in chart notes**.
- It **declines one comparison outright**: Figure 7's note says *"We have not compared the data with the
  2020/21 academic year"* because *"we only carried out a small number of inspections during this year."*
  A piece may not perform a comparison the register refuses and attribute it to the register.
