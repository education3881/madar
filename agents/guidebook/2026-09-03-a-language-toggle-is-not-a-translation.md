# A language toggle is not a translation — and the Sudan Arabic composition (row 37 · ruling #42)

**Filed:** 2026-09-03, on the Arabic composition of `2026-09-01-sudan-cant-wait-to-learn` (Curated 46, Edition 05 mandated slot #2).
**Owner:** Researcher (the rule), Arabic Editor (routing rulings), Arabic Content Creator (composition notes).
**Extends:** #36 (a promise is derived, not declared), #37 (asserted by existence), #40 (a mirror can misname the instrument), row 19 (AR version-drift discipline), row 22 (Sudan source index).

## The rule

**A language switcher on a served page is a promise in the source's chrome, not a document. "An official Arabic edition exists" is a figure with provenance axes, and it is proved the only way a promise is ever proved — by dereferencing it: fetch the toggle's destination and read the target language in the served body. The presence of the toggle proves nothing.**

Three corollaries, each bitten today:

1. **Read the toggle's served state, not its label.** UNICEF's Drupal renders the العربية link on every page; on untranslated pages the anchor carries `class="has-no-translation"` and its `href` is the *English* URL. The 4 May 2026 BRIDGES press release and the 27 Nov 2023 *Safe Learning Spaces* story are both this case. The 08-31 re-verification recorded the press release as carrying "an Arabic edition of the release on-site (العربية toggle)" — the toggle was seen, the destination was not fetched, and the claim lived three days and reached the EN draft's own source annotation before it was checked. Corrected in the EN file today; BRIDGES therefore keeps its Latin name in Arabic with *our* gloss, marked as ours, because the owner serves no Arabic name for it.
2. **When both editions do exist, they are two registers, not one document twice.** The AR editions read today drift from their English on date and on fact: *New horizons* is dated 27 Aug (EN) / 31 Aug 2020 (AR); *A golden opportunity to learn* is dated 1 Nov (EN) / 2 Nov 2022 (AR), and the Arabic body carries figures the English does not — "91 centres across six states", "programme developed in 2020", "centre established three years ago". Row 19 already says cite the edition read; the addition is that **a pair composed from two editions carries only the figures both editions share**, and the AR source annotation names the edition and its own date.
3. **The owner's Arabic edition is the transliteration register of record, and it overrules a plausible phonetic reading.** *Tahir Onoor Ali* — the Sudanese romanisation "Onoor" reads most naturally as النور (the assimilated article, *an-Noor*, is exactly how Sudanese English spells it), and that is what a heard-vowel routing would have produced. UNICEF's own Arabic edition serves **طاهر أنور علي**. A defensible reading is not a served one; where the owner writes the name in Arabic, the gate reads it there and nowhere else.

## Why it is #36/#37 turned outward

Our own QA learned on 08-18 that a promise in *our* metadata (an og:image that resolved and rendered nowhere) is checked against the consumer, not the filesystem; #36 made our promises derived; #37 made them proved by existence. This ruling applies the same dereference to a **source's** chrome: a served page's language toggle is a promise made by someone else's build, and it is trusted exactly as much as we trust our own — after fetching the thing it points at. Same family as #40 (a mirror's headline is not the instrument's name): a toggle's label is not the edition's existence.
