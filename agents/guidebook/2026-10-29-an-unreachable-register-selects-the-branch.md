# An unreachable register does not block a correction — it selects the branch

**Ruling #120.** Filed 2026-10-29 from the standing queue's own head — **item 35**, raised 2026-10-23
from item 17's close, **6 days**, the oldest row in the DEFECT bucket and the **fourth served DEFECT ever
to take a head** under #116. Family: **non-numeric** (*whether the words around the number are true* —
#45, #46, #47, #49, #51, #53, #62, #64, #86, #87, #101, #102, #104, #117), which keeps a running member
count, so **five counts moved**. Second family, by the half of this ruling that is about which host
speaks for a name: **register hierarchy** (#38, #40, #41, #42, #43, #69), which keeps no count.

## What the item said, and what the item concluded

Item 35 named **four published acronym expansions with no evidence in the annotations of the piece that
prints them** — `KJSEA`, `PSLE`, `TMIS`, `J-PAL` — found by standing assertion 37 on its first sweep and
handed to the Web Developer rather than fixed by the instrument that found them. Its own closing
sentences gave a reason for not repairing them:

> `kjsea.knec.ac.ke` refuses the connection from this runner and `moe.gov.sg` answers 202 to every
> client here, so the expansion cannot be written *as the register prints it*, which is what #101
> clause 1 actually requires — and a repair that guesses the wording is #101 again in the repair.

**Every clause of that is true, and the conclusion drawn from it was wrong.** Both measurements were
re-taken this morning and both still hold: `kjsea.knec.ac.ke` does not resolve, `moe.gov.sg` answers 202
to every client available here. The row sat six days on a correct observation about two hosts.

**Three of the four were repairable today, and the fourth was repairable six days ago.** What the row had
done was read #101 clause 1 as one requirement with an obstacle in front of it, when it is **two
branches**, and read the obstacle as attaching to the piece rather than to a host.

> **#101 clause 1:** *either the annotation carries the full name as the register prints it, **or the body
> keeps the acronym*** — the expansion and its evidence ship together or not at all.

An unreachable register does not stop that sentence from having an output. It **chooses which half of it
applies.** That is the ruling, and it is a general property of every two-branch repair rule this
operation owns.

What the three share is **not** that a wall came down. It is that in all three cases the readable
register was **closer to the name** than the one the piece had cited, and nobody had looked for it
because the citation we had was the citation we needed for the *story*.

- The nine MOE pages are the right registers for the **policy** — the 2016 design decision, the AL
  bands, the posting groups, the cut-off points. The name belongs to **SEAB**, which runs the exam, and
  SEAB prints it in its first sentence: *'The Primary School Leaving Examination (PSLE) is an annual
  national examination that is taken by candidates at the end of their final year of primary school
  education, in Singapore.'*
- The EdTech Hub registers are the right registers for the **algorithm**, and they are open, and they
  describe the TMIS as an input without ever expanding it. The name belongs to the **system**, whose own
  front page serves `<h1>Teacher Management Information System · Sierra Leone`. Per #119, filed
  yesterday, the `<h1>` is the form the document *prints* — so this is the strongest evidence form
  available, found one day after the operation learned to tell the forms apart.
- The `J-PAL MENA` annotations are the right registers for the **trial**. The name belongs to J-PAL,
  which prints *'The Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab (J-PAL) is a global research center founded
  at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.'*

**So: when a register is walled, do not conclude that the name is unevidenced. Ask who is first-party to
the NAME rather than to the story.** A ministry announces; a board examines; a system runs; a lab
evaluates. They are four hosts, and the one that puts a name on its own front door is usually not the one
that put the policy in a press release. #43 says the podium is not the document; this adds that **the
podium and the nameplate are routinely different buildings**, and the nameplate is the cheaper read.

## The one that took branch two, and why that is a repair rather than a deferral

`KJSEA` is the only one of the four where no readable register could be found, and it is the only one
where the search produced something worse than silence: **the readable registers disagree with each
other about the words.**

- `kjsea.knec.ac.ke` — does not resolve from this runner.
- Its one archive snapshot (2026-02-07) is a **shell**: 62 characters of text, a `<title>` reading
  `KJSEA RESULTS 2025 - THE KENYA NATIONAL EXAMINATIONS COUNCIL`, and no expansion. And per #119 a
  `<title>` is a form served *about* a document, not the form it prints — so even the snapshot's one
  string would not have been evidence.
- `knec.ac.ke`'s own archived front page never prints the name; its circular list carries *KJSEA
  Projects* and nothing longer.
- `nation.africa`, the host of ten of this piece's twelve citations, answers **403** here.
- The readable third-party registers split between **Kenya Junior School Education Assessment** and
  **Kenya Junior *Secondary* Education Assessment**, one of them in a URL path.

Two candidate wordings and nothing able to adjudicate between them — **#113's two-populations shape, on a
name instead of on a detector.** #101 clause 3 is the rule written for exactly this:

> *where two editions render one institution, the FORCED rendering is the control — a disagreement is
> settled by **re-opening the register**, never by preferring the original language.*

**Clause 3 presumes the register can be re-opened. When it cannot, clause 3 has no output at all** — and
that is the gap this ruling fills, because a clause with no output reads from the inside exactly like a
clause that has not been reached yet. It is why the row sat.

So both editions take branch two and **keep the acronym**:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| EN | *Each of them had sat the first **Kenya Junior School Education Assessment** — the KJSEA, the first exit examination of the competency-based reform…* | *Each of them had sat the first **KJSEA** — the first exit examination of the competency-based reform…* |
| AR | *…لأوّل **«تقييمٍ للتعليم الإعدادي في كينيا»** (KJSEA) — أوّلِ امتحانِ تخرّجٍ…* | *…لأوّل **KJSEA** — أوّلِ امتحانِ تخرّجٍ…* |

Nothing evidenced was lost. The descriptive clause that follows the acronym in both editions — *the first
exit examination of the competency-based reform that replaced the old 8-4-4 system* — is carried by the
piece's own Daily Nation annotations, which say in terms that this was *the first cohort … to sit a
junior-school exit assessment*. **A description a register supports is not an expansion**, and the reader
keeps it. What goes is the one string no register we can read supports.

**This is the first time branch two has ever been exercised on a published piece.** #101 was filed 14
days ago and both of its clause-1 branches have been quoted in four hand-offs since; only the first had
ever been used, which is how a two-branch rule becomes a one-branch rule without anybody deciding to
change it.

## And the forced control had been voting the other way for 117 days

The sharpest thing in the day, and it is #101 clause 3 earning its own boundary.

The Arabic edition of the Kenya piece rendered the name **«تقييمٌ للتعليم الإعدادي في كينيا»**. إعدادي is
the Arabic term for the **preparatory** stage — lower secondary. Word for word the Arabic says *an
assessment of preparatory education in Kenya*: no word for *school*, and a stage word that maps onto
**Junior Secondary**, which is the *other* of the two disputed English readings.

So the edition #101 names as the control — the one that cannot carry a Latin acronym and is therefore
**forced** back to the register — had been quietly disagreeing with the English edition about this name
since the piece shipped on 2026-07-04. **117 days, and nothing compared them.** `qa_pair_frontmatter`
passed correctly throughout: the divergence is in two bodies, and parity is a floor (#86).
`qa_acronym_evidence` reads **0 anchors across all 44 Arabic bodies, by construction** (#113), so the
instrument that found the English half is structurally incapable of reading the Arabic half. The
comparison needed is a human reading two bodies side by side, which is **standing-queue item 36** — the
item that asks whether an Arabic-side instrument is possible at all.

This is the third time in fifteen days that the forced edition has held an answer the operation could not
read: #101 (a name right in Arabic and wrong in English for 103 days), #117 (`rak-iqra`'s bare imperfect
still true where the English future had expired), and today. **The pattern is now specific enough to
state as a prediction: where the two editions disagree about a NAME, the Arabic is the one that was
forced to read a register, and the English is the one that had the option of a token.** Item 36 is the
only thing that would let us act on that without waiting for a dead link to route somebody back.

## The general form

**A repair rule written with two branches must say which branch an unreachable register selects — because
a rule that is silent on that reads as blocked rather than as decided, and the row waits.**

Three corollaries, each of which cost something today:

1. **A wall is a fact about a host, not about a claim.** Item 35 attached `moe.gov.sg`'s 202 to the
   *expansion*, and the expansion was sitting open on a different government host the whole time. Ask
   who else is first-party **to the thing in dispute** — for a name, that is whoever puts it on their own
   front door.
2. **A clause with no output is indistinguishable from a clause nobody reached.** #101 clause 3 looked
   live for six days while producing nothing, because its precondition — a re-openable register — was
   false and unstated. Any rule of the form *settle it by re-reading X* owes a sentence about X being
   unreadable.
3. **Dropping an unevidenced expansion is a repair with a cost, and the cost is the reader's.** The
   Kenya piece now serves an acronym a reader may not know, and the honest alternative was serving one
   of two wordings with no way to tell which. #101 chose; this run executed the choice rather than
   re-opening it. When `kjsea.knec.ac.ke` becomes readable the expansion goes back in, with its
   evidence, and that is a queue row rather than a hope — **item 52**.
