# Ruling #31 — a voluntary-uptake figure carries its counterfactual

**Filed:** 2026-08-17 (daily run, Brief 54) · **Origin:** Ed05 recon 7, Egypt / the Egyptian Baccalaureate
**Family:** figure discipline (kin: #14 the funnel, #29 a ranking is not a measurement) · **Section 1 row:** 24 · **Section 2 ruling:** #31

---

## The rule

**A participation, opt-in, take-up or adoption rate is evidence of preference only if the unchosen option remained genuinely available on comparable terms.**

Before any such figure rides, the drafter states three things:

1. **What the alternative was** — the option the non-adopters would have taken.
2. **Who could still get it** — was it offered at the same school, in the same town, with staff and a timetable?
3. **On what terms** — same cost, same distance, same quality, same official encouragement?

If all three are answered, the figure may be written as a **choice**: *"N% chose X."*
If any is unanswered, the figure is written as a **record**: *"N% enrolled in X"* — and the gap is named in the sentence, per ruling #19.

A near-unanimous take-up rate is the case where this matters most, and the case where the temptation to skip it is strongest. **99% is either the strongest verdict a public can give or no verdict at all, and the number alone cannot tell you which.**

---

## Why it needs its own number

The seven figure axes consolidated on 2026-07-27 (#12–#24) all interrogate a **measurement** — who administered it, over what coverage, at what funnel stage, of what vintage, at what scope, in what direction, at what series position. Each asks: *is this number measuring what the sentence says it measures?*

A take-up rate is not a measurement of children or of learning. It is a **record of an administrative process**, and its validity turns on something none of the seven axes reach: a **counterfactual**. The question is not "was this counted correctly" — it usually was — but "**was the thing it counts a choice**."

Placement in the family:

- **#14 (the funnel)** — target ≠ sat ≠ passed ≠ screened ≠ appointed. Polices **stages within one pipeline**. #31 polices **the choice set the pipeline was offered against**. Sibling, not duplicate.
- **#29 (a ranking is not a measurement)** — rejects a composite whose **ruler** is unpublished. #31 qualifies a rate whose **alternative** is unexamined. Mirror image: one is about how the number was built, the other about what it was built against.
- **#9 (measured vs announced)** — a take-up figure is usually *measured*, which is exactly why it passes the existing checks and still misleads.

## The originating case (Egypt, 2025–26)

Egypt's Education Law amendments, ratified 18 August 2025, created the Egyptian Baccalaureate as a second secondary-leaving certificate alongside Thanaweya Amma. **Article 37bis guarantees that enrolment in the new track is optional.** Reported opt-in: about **95%** in 2025/26 and **99%** (order of 832,000 students) for 2026-27.

Egypt's own public record supplies both readings, and neither is Madār's to settle:

- **The ministry's** — the new track is better designed (fewer subjects, two sittings a year, retakes, published university pathways), and families recognised it. Directors were instructed to inform parents and provide academic counselling.
- **The objection** — MP Freddy Elbaiady, after the minister's 31 August 2025 meeting with some 4,000 secondary school directors, alleged the minister signalled he would personally monitor daily enrolment numbers: *"This is not persuasion. This is coercion masquerading as awareness-raising."* His party alleged students preferring the old system were transferred to distant or less-equipped schools.

The correct handling is not to pick. It is to print the rate, the statutory guarantee, the allegation and the ministry's answer **in the same passage**, so the reader can see that the figure's meaning is contested and why. The reporting move #31 requires is small; the difference in what the reader ends up believing is not.

## What the Verifier adds to the checklist

One question, on any rate expressed as a share of people who did something optional:

> **What was the alternative, and to whom was it actually available?**

Two answers are acceptable — a sourced description of the alternative, or an explicit statement that the alternative's availability is not documented. **"Not applicable" is not an acceptable answer for an optional programme**, and — per the 2026-08-16 silent-pass trap — an assertion that finds nothing to check has failed, not passed.

## What it does *not* require

This is not a rule against reporting adoption figures, and not a rule that near-unanimity is suspicious. Systems do sometimes produce overwhelming genuine preference, and saying so is fine when the alternative was real. #31 asks for one clause, not a suspicion.

## Binding on our own copy

Madār has shipped take-up and coverage figures before — Vietnam's tuition waiver, Morocco's pioneer-school scaling (626 → 4,626), Poland's enrolment gain under the attendance-linked benefit. **None is retro-corrected**; the corpus is not reopened for a rule filed after it shipped. Every future one carries its counterfactual clause.

*(Egypt's Poland-shaped near-neighbour is instructive in the other direction: Poland's figure was a rise in enrolment under a **mandate**, not a choice — no counterfactual is owed for a compulsory instrument. #31 attaches to the word "optional," wherever it appears.)*
