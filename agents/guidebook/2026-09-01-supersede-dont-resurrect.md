# Row 36 / Ruling #41 — Supersede, don't resurrect: a dead register's figure is replaced by the owner's living figure, never re-anchored through a mirror

**Filed:** 2026-09-01 · Content Creator II with the Researcher, from the Sudan draft's Test-1 homework · Completes the family that #40 opened: #40 governs what a mirror may *name*, this row governs what a mirror may *revive*.

## The rule
When a figure's serving register dies, the question is not "where else does this figure survive?" — it is **"what does the owner serve today?"** If the owner's living registers carry a superseding figure of the same kind (same instrument, same axis, fresher or wider vintage), the piece carries the living figure and lets the dead one go. Mirrors that still carry the dead figure (aggregators, syndicated copies, third-party adaptations) are not re-anchor candidates: a mirror can keep a figure breathing long after its owner has stopped serving it, and a figure the owner no longer serves is a figure the owner may no longer stand behind.

## Origin case
The Sudan wartime national counts — 699 safe learning spaces / 60 digital / 12,660+ children / 54% girls / eight states — lost their only first-party register when the UNICEF USA adaptation went unreachable (recon 5-R, 08-31). The block still circulates: a Forbes syndication of the same adaptation serves it today, and a search engine's summary volunteered it verbatim. Both were declined. The re-anchor hunt instead asked what UNICEF serves *now*, and the owner's Learning Passport register (read served, 09-01) carries the superseding figure: **more than 870,000 children and caregivers reached across 750 safe learning spaces since the war began (April 2024 vintage)** — wider scope, fresher vintage, first-party, alive. The draft carries 750/870,000 with its vintage in-sentence; the 699/60/12,660 block does not appear in it.

## Two corollaries proved in the same hunt
1. **A search summary is not a served register.** The hunt surfaced "76,000 children (52% girls) as of March 2025" in a search engine's synthesis — fresher than anything else found, and never located in served text on any page this operation read. It does not ride, at any freshness. A figure enters the corpus only from a page we read as served; a summary is a mirror with no page behind it until the page is found.
2. **A URL is a figure too — cited as fetched, never composed from a pattern.** The draft's first source line for War Child's 2025 accounting carried a URL composed from the site's apparent pattern (`warchild.net/news/annual-report-2025/`); verification before staging found it does not serve, while the sister entity's register (`warchild.org.uk/news/annual-report-2025`) does. The composed URL was replaced by the fetched one before anything shipped. Same shape as #37 (a contract in prose is a declaration): a source URL is a promise the reader will dereference, so it is proved by existence, not inferred from house style.

## Applications
- Re-anchor hunts start at the owner's living surface (site search, press-centre, related rails), not at mirrors of the dead page.
- A figure superseded rather than resurrected notes the supersession in the source annotation, so the next recon knows the older block was declined deliberately, not missed.
- Sources lists are staged only after every URL in them has been fetched or served this run — pattern-composed URLs are a park-on-sight defect.
