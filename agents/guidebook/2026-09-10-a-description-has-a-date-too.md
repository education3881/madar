# Ruling #47 — a description has a date too

**Filed:** 2026-09-10 · **Guidebook Section 1, row 42** · Produced by the Sudan verification verdict (`content-drafts/verdicts/2026-09-10-sudan-cant-wait-to-learn-verification.md`)
**Family:** vintage (#21) · funnel stage (#14) · which-register-speaks-for-the-owner (#38/#40/#42/#43)
**Third consecutive ruling produced by a verdict.**

---

## The rule

**Every non-numeric attribute of a programme is dated exactly as strictly as a figure, and inherits the vintage of the register it was read in.** What it taught, where it ran, what it was called, what prize it held, who ran it, where a sentence was said — each is a dated claim about a moment in an instrument's life, and none of them travels to another moment without its own register.

A description read at one stage does not describe another stage.

---

## What bit

Sudan's *Can't Wait to Learn* has a twelve-year biography: a 2014 first phase, an evaluated programme, a 2018 prize class announced in 2019, a four-country export, a handover to Ukraine. The draft was accurate about every one of those moments **and put three of their attributes in the wrong one**.

1. **Subjects.** The 2014 first phase is, in the owner's own words, *"a numeracy trial … an applied mathematics game."* The evaluated programme taught Arabic literacy too — Brown et al. measure it. The draft wrote *"a set of educational games teaching Arabic and mathematics"* into the **2014** sentence, in the opening paragraph, under the thesis *Sudan built it before the emergency*. Nothing false about the evaluation; something untrue about 2014.
2. **Award year.** *"In 2019 UNESCO named the programme … a laureate"* — the register says *"one of the **2018** laureates"*, announced 20 March 2019. Two dates, one printed, and it was the wrong one for the object of the sentence.
3. **Venue.** *"The programme's stated ambition **at the ceremony**"* — the figure is a quotation given to UNESCO inside a written feature. No register describes a ceremony.

---

## Why it needed a ruling rather than three corrections

**Our figure discipline is entirely numeric and all three items are invisible to every instrument in it.** The numeral-multiset comparison, the one-sided-figure grep, the prohibited-figure grep, the seven axes — each takes a number as its object. "Arabic and mathematics" is not a number. "At the ceremony" is not a number. And item 2 was invisible *precisely because the correct number was absent*: a check that compares the numbers present cannot see a year that was never written.

Third consecutive verdict whose failure was non-numeric (#45 a number's noun, #46 a quotation's orthography and a plan's tense, #47 three descriptions). Read as a series, that is a finding about the discipline: **the operation has hardened its numbers to the point where the remaining errors are all in the words around them.**

---

## Corollaries

1. **An award year and its announcement year are two different dates.** The register that names the laureate class is the one that rides; a publication date is not an award date. (Generalises: an edition year, a fiscal year, a cohort year and a report's publication year are four dates, and a press page usually prints only the last.)
2. **A setting is a claim about a register.** "At the ceremony", "in a speech", "at the launch", "in an interview" must be read off the register, never inferred from the occasion the register happens to mark. This is #43 (*the podium is not the document*) mirrored onto the reporting side: there, the owner's podium could not outrank the owner's report; here, we invented a podium the owner never mounted.
3. **A guardrail is a claim and a claim is checked** (#33 applied to our own hedging). Item 3 sat *inside a fence the piece built in order to be careful* — the sentence exists only to mark the 1.5 million as an aim rather than a reach. Hedges are written fast, at the end, when the register is already closed. Check the fence with the same suspicion as the figure it fences.
4. **The annotation is written with the register open; the body is written from memory of the annotation.** Second consecutive verdict in which `sources[]` was right and both bodies were wrong on the same fact (09-09 item 2; today's item 2). That is a workflow property, not a slip: two different pieces, two different drafters, the same asymmetry.

---

## What changes

**Verifier's trace gains a row:** *for each non-numeric attribute of the instrument — subject, name, scope, location, award, venue, operator — which register, and of which date, carries it?*

**Drafter's compose discipline:** when a piece narrates an instrument across time, the stages are enumerated **before** drafting, and each attribute is attached to the stage its register describes. An instrument with a biography gets a timeline, not a description.

**Editor's fifth test gains a question:** does any sentence describe an early stage using a later stage's properties?

---

## Applied to our own corpus, same run

- Sudan EN + AR: three fixes, no figure added, `2018` added to **both** sides so the numeral multiset stayed symmetric.
- Two register-restorations in the same pass, both non-numeric and both from the same root — the register's own word replaced by a firmer one of ours (*learners* → *Sudanese children*; *disruption … especially acute* → *destruction … concentrated*).
