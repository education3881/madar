## The rule

**A disclaimer the owner prints on a table travels with every figure taken from that table.** When the owner sets a policy list beside a performance change and says in the footnote that the pairing is *informational and indicates no causal link*, the pairing may be reported exactly as the owner set it — list beside change — and the causal reading is **the analyst's claim, attributed to the analyst**, never the report's and never ours. The corollary the origin case forced: when a secondary reports the primary on a *different scale*, the converted figure has **two owners**, and the piece cites the one whose page it actually read.

## Origin case — one table, one footnote, one blog

The 08-16 recon carried, from the CGD blog, the cleanest accomplishment story on the continent: after poor PASEC 2014 results Niger *"evaluated every contract teacher and dismissed those below the required proficiency … Result: among the two largest improvements … Niger bought learning with quality of teaching, not with more teachers."* Read today, the report sets the same list beside the same arrows in **Table 5.13 (pp. 235–237)** — verbatim: *évaluation des enseignants contractuels (70% des enseignants de l'enseignement primaire)*, *résiliation des contrats* of those below the minimum, training for the middle band, initial- and continuous-training reform, director accountability, all lower-grade teachers trained in reading and maths, an assessment culture — and prints, as **footnote 47** on that table: *"Le sens de l'évolution de la performance est donné à titre informatif mais n'indique pas qu'il y a un lien causal avec l'action mentionnée dans la première colonne."*

The owner drew the pairing and refused the arrow between its columns. The blog drew the arrow. Our recon carried the arrow as the story. Nothing in the seven figure axes catches this — every figure is correct, every vintage right, the coverage stated — because the defect is not in a number; it is in a **relation between two columns** that the owner explicitly declined to assert. Same shape as #32 (an attribution can be false while every underlying figure is correct) one level up: a *cause* can be asserted while every underlying figure is correct.

## Three corollaries, all bitten in the same read

1. **A converted figure has two owners — cite the one whose page you read.** The blog reports PASEC in *Harmonized Learning Outcome* points (a World Bank linking); the report reports PASEC points. "−45 HLO for Burkina Faso's bottom decile" is the report's **−60.1** (Table 5.2, p. 222) after conversion — the same fact with a different owner and a different scale, and only one of them is on a page of the primary. "+15 HLO" is the report's **+19.8** (Table 5.5, p. 224). The piece carries the report's scale with the report's page; if it ever needs the converted number, it names the converter. Never both in one sentence as if they were one figure.
2. **A trend chapter has its own coverage, narrower than the study's.** "Inequality rose in all fourteen countries" was false twice: the trend covers the **ten** repeat countries (p. 220), and within the ten the report's own tables show **Senegal's** between-school coefficient *falling* in three of four series while the report crowns it *"le seul pays qui … parvient à réduire les inégalités"* (p. 225). And inside Niger's own trend, the pupils tested in Hausa and Zarma in 2019 are **excluded** from the comparison (p. 220). A comparison set is the chapter's, not the study's, and a sentence about a trend names the set it was measured on.
3. **The owner's own hedge is the vintage clause's best anchor.** The report calls its gains *"une réussite importante, qui devra être confirmée par la prochaine étude PASEC"* (p. 224). A piece that must say "the ruler is about to be re-read" (#21) can let the owner say it first.

## How to apply

- At **recon**: when a secondary reports a primary's table, fetch the table and read *around* it — the footnote, the note under the table, the paragraph after. Record the disclaimer in the index beside the figures it governs.
- At **compose**: a policy list may sit beside a gain in our prose *only in the owner's shape* — juxtaposed, with the owner's disclaimer quoted or paraphrased in the same paragraph; an analyst's causal reading is attributed by name. In Arabic the disclaimer is composed at the same weight (*لا يشير إلى علاقة سببية*), never softened to a passing *قد*.
- At **verification**: the trace list gains a row — **does the primary disclaim any relation this sentence asserts?** — failed if the figure's table carries a note the sentence does not honour. Scale is a column in the trace (PASEC points vs HLO vs SD), and a figure whose scale is not the primary's is an untraced figure.
