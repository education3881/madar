# A total is a figure, and must be added

**Filed:** 2026-08-24 · Researcher → Editor → all writers
**Ruling:** **#34**
**Section:** 1 (source-and-figure discipline), row **27**
**Extends:** the seven figure axes (§0), and the scoped-instrument family (rows 15/19/20)
**Opened by:** Ed05 Zambia, when the provenance wall fell and the Auditor General's audit was read in full

---

## The rule

**Every published table must be added before any number in it is quoted — including, and especially, its own total row.**

All seven of our figure axes check a number against something *outside* itself: its source, its
vintage, its denominator, its funnel stage, its ruler, its counterfactual, its agent. **Not one
checks a table against itself.** A total is not a label on a table; it is a figure, produced by an
arithmetic operation on figures we can see, and it is the only figure in the document we can
verify with no second source, no fetch, and no judgement call.

**The corollary, and it is the sharp half:** when a printed total and its own line items disagree,
the disagreement is the story, not an inconvenience to be resolved by picking the friendlier
number. Quote neither bare. Quote the line items, state the printed total, and say they do not
reconcile.

---

## The origin case

**Report of the Committee on Education, Science and Technology on the Performance Audit Report on
the Implementation of the Free Education Policy in Enhancing Access and Quality of Education to
Learners in Zambia 2020–2024**, National Assembly of Zambia, Fourth Session of the Thirteenth
Assembly. Read in full as served text.
`https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/committee_reports/REPORT_OF_THE_COMMITTEE_ON_EDUCATION%2C_SCIENCE_AND_TECHNOLOGY_ON_THE_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT_REPORT%5B1%5D.pdf`

The Ministry of Education's own submission on teacher recruitment, reproduced verbatim in §3.1.3:

| Level | Planned 2024 | Actual 2024 | Planned 2023 | Actual 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECE teachers | 2,000 | **548** | 435 | 687 |
| Primary teachers | 3,000 | **1,057** | 2,536 | 2,522 |
| Secondary teachers | 3,000 | **2,595** | 3,000 | 4,272 |
| **Total** | **8,000** | **7,841** | **5,971** | **7,481** |

Add the columns:

| Column | Rows sum to | Printed total | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planned 2024 | 8,000 | 8,000 | reconciles |
| **Actual 2024** | **4,200** | **7,841** | **short by 3,641** |
| Planned 2023 | 5,971 | 5,971 | reconciles |
| Actual 2023 | 7,481 | 7,481 | reconciles |

**Three of four columns add exactly. One does not — and it is the one that answers the only
question the table was submitted to answer: did the recruitment happen?**

That the other three reconcile is what makes this usable. It establishes the table's own
convention — these three rows are meant to sum to this total — so the fourth column's failure is a
discrepancy inside the document's own arithmetic, not a difference of scope we are inventing. The
honest sentence is *"the 2024 actual column does not reconcile with its own printed total, while
the other three columns do."* The dishonest sentences are both of the obvious ones: *"Zambia
recruited 7,841 of 8,000 teachers"* and *"Zambia recruited only 4,200."*

**What the difference is worth.** The printed total reads as a **98.0%** delivery rate. The line
items read as **52.5%**. And the shortfall sits precisely where this publication's remit is:

- **ECE: 2,000 planned, 548 recruited — 27.4%**
- Primary: 3,000 planned, 1,057 recruited — 35.2%
- Secondary: 3,000 planned, 2,595 recruited — 86.5%

A free-education statute that writes day care, nursery and reception into law (Education
(Amendment) Act 2026, the eight-key-stage framework) is being staffed at the early years at a
quarter of its own plan. **No axis we had would have surfaced that.** The number was
government-published, correctly attributed, correctly dated, in its own primary register, with its
denominator printed beside it. It passes every check we own. It does not pass addition.

## Why it will recur, and where

1. **A total is written last and read first.** It is the line a press office lifts, the line a
   headline quotes, and structurally the line most likely to be carried over from a previous draft
   or a different scope while the rows beneath it are updated.
2. **Positive posture makes it worse — the same compounding as #32.** In an accomplishment story
   every party has a legitimate interest in the largest true total, and none in the smallest true
   row. Ed05 is Africa under a positive posture and every candidate on the slate is a
   count-of-things-delivered story: teachers recruited, classrooms built, centres opened, children
   returned.
3. **It is the cheapest check in the book.** No fetch, no second source, no wall, no judgement.
   Thirty seconds of arithmetic on a table we have already read. There is no excuse for a shipped
   Madār piece to quote an unadded total.

**The same audit shows what a table looks like when it does reconcile,** which is why the rule is
a check and not a suspicion: 115 GRZ secondary schools decompose as 69 complete and operational +
36 with contractors re-engaged + 10 awaiting final accounts = 115; the ZEEP programme's 202
decompose as 82 operational + 120 under construction = 202; the ZEEL early-years build decomposes
as 110 hub + 112 satellite = 222. All three add. And because they add, the widely-repeated
search-stage claim that Zambia *"completed construction of 151 secondary schools"* becomes
**usable for the first time** — 69 + 82 = 151 is that figure's composition, traced, from two
independently stated programme lines in a primary register. **Addition does not only catch bad
numbers; it rescues good ones.**

---

## Binding

Not retroactive. No shipped piece is reopened for it.

**The Verifier's checklist gains one question — *has every table this piece draws from been
added?*** Two acceptable answers: it adds, or it does not add and the piece says so in the
sentence that uses it. Per the 08-16 silent-pass trap, **a piece that quotes a total and records
no arithmetic check has not been checked.**

**Reporting convention when a table fails to add:** give the line items, then the printed total,
then the delta, in that order. The reader is owed the components before the disputed aggregate,
because the components are the part we can stand behind.

## Also banked from this source

- **The Auditor General's stated sampling limitation, and it must be named if we cite the audit.**
  §2.6, verbatim: resource constraints meant the audit *"sampled schools that were near the
  Central Business District (CBD)."* Mitigation: questionnaires to **61 additional districts,
  responses from 27** — a **44%** response rate on the fix for an openly urban-biased frame. A
  national audit with a self-declared urban sample is still the best independent instrument that
  exists here; it is not a national picture, and a draft may not let it read as one.
- **The data gap is on the record, from the government, and it retires an argument.** The audit
  states the **2022–2023 Education Statistical Bulletin was published in November 2024** — a
  roughly two-year reporting lag — and that stakeholders judged outdated enrolment figures to have
  limited the Ministry's capacity to plan. This is the reason the "2.3 / 2.5 / 2.6 million children
  returned" ledgers never reconciled (recon 9, row 21 participant drift): **the national data
  system was two years behind the claims being made from it.** We now decline that aggregate with a
  government citation rather than a suspicion.
- **The 2 million claim, sourced at last — as an attribution, not a finding.** The audit's own
  motivation section carries *"the Minister of Education reporting that over 2 million children had
  enrolled in schools across the country due to this initiative"* — attributed to the Minister,
  unsourced, inside an audit document. Exactly #32: correct attribution of an uncounted number.
  Still unusable as a figure; now perfectly usable as an example of one.
- **The Grade-7 spine gets its far end.** The audit records that the **2002** Free Basic Education
  Policy abolished tuition *"for grades 1 to 7."* The 2026 Act redefines primary as grades 1–6. The
  policy that opened the series and the Act that closes it are both defined on the same boundary —
  which is the piece's spine, now sourced at both ends from primary registers.
- **Two dimensioning figures worth their line.** An Education Standards Officer may be responsible
  for inspecting **up to 69 schools**. ZICTA supplied **480 computers and 24 printers to 24
  schools** across all ten provinces over **2021–2024** — twenty machines per school, roughly two
  and a half schools per province per four years, which is what "closing the digital divide" costs
  when it is counted.
