# A source can drift against itself — within-document drift and the register rule
**Filed:** 2026-08-28 · Researcher, ratified by the Editor · Section 1, row 32 · **Ruling #38**
**Origin:** the OPM *Year 3 Evaluation of SLEIC* read for Ed05 piece 2 (Sierra Leone)

## The rule

**Vintage discipline applies inside a single document, not only across documents.** When
one document states the same fact twice and the statements disagree, the two instances
are two registers of one source: cite the register you used (body text vs. figure table
vs. acronym list vs. annex), pick by a stated hierarchy, and never average or silently
choose. The corollary of rows 13/21 (a figure carries its vintage) is that **a figure
also carries its page.**

## The two origin cases, both in one evaluator's report

1. **A number drifting against its own table.** The report's body text states the pooled
   maths effect as **0.278 SD** — four times, including the annex regression table. The
   SD-transform table under its own Figure 1 prints **0.280***. Two renderings of one
   estimate (almost certainly a rounding path difference), 264 pages apart from
   agreeing. Resolution hierarchy applied: **body text + annex agree → 0.278 is the
   figure of record; the table noted, not blended.** Had we quoted "0.28" as a
   compromise we would have printed a number that appears nowhere in the source.

2. **An institution drifting against its own name.** The same report expands NYAF as
   "National **Forum for Youth Awareness**" (p. 1) and "National **Youth Awareness
   Forum**" (§ programme description) — and its own acronym list *omits NYAF entirely*,
   so the document provides no internal arbitration. Resolution: **when the
   within-document hierarchy fails, drop to an external register** (EOF's programme
   page uses the bare acronym) — the piece therefore says "NYAF" and never expands it.
   Under our standing rule that an institution keeps its own name, an institution whose
   custodian document disagrees with itself keeps its *acronym*.

## Why this matters for the desk

- The seven-axis figure discipline (#19–#22 family) checks a figure against other
  *sources*; this row closes the case where the collision is **intra-source** and no
  amount of cross-source tracing would surface it — both readings are "confirmed by
  the primary."
- The Arabic side inherits it directly: an AR composition must transliterate the
  register of record, and an unexpandable acronym (NYAF) stays Latin per the
  institution-name rule rather than acquiring an invented Arabic expansion.
- Verifier instruction: when a draft cites a figure present in both prose and a table,
  the verdict names **which register** was cited. "Matches the source" is not yet a
  verification if the source disagrees with itself.

## The hierarchy, stated once

Body text and annex in agreement → body text. Body vs. table with no third witness →
name both, cite neither as sole. Acronym/definition list → authoritative for names,
unless absent, in which case external register. Any within-document drift worth using
is worth *showing* — the drift itself is often evidence (compare the participants
count, audited down across registers, in the same piece).
