## Part 1 — Section 1 source index: Chile / Simce (the census assessment)

Chile's assessment corpus splits cleanly across **two institutions with distinct jobs**, and the split is the sourcing map:

- **Mineduc (Ministerio de Educación)** — the *political* register: results-delivery press releases (each carries the year's headline figures verbatim — the 6 Mar 2026 delivery release holds II medio 250/263, 4° básico 276/262, 8° básico's first post-pandemic 238/258, the 15-pt 4° maths gender gap 269 vs 254, the +23-pt anxiety-strategy association, 83% II-medio teacher-use, and **Gino Cortez's** three-part quote). The word "historic" ("histórico avance") is a *government's* framing of its own good news — attribute as such per the Editor rubric, never as the agency's neutral read.
- **Agencia de Calidad de la Educación** — the *instrument's* register: what Simce **is** (census-not-sample, applied under the Plan de Evaluaciones Nacionales e Internacionales 2021–2026, suspended 2020, census resumed Nov 2022, delivered at the start of the school year since the 2023 cycle alongside the IDPS + context questionnaires), the national evaluations calendar, and the school-level results portal (`resultadossimce.agenciaeducacion.cl` — where the equity/school splits live, not in the ministry release).

**Register-by-load, banked:**
- The **instrument's own definitional claims** (census, cadence, the IDPS strand) come from the Agencia, not the ministry — carry them per #19 (the instrument's own rules are a primary about itself).
- The **year's figures** come from the dated Mineduc delivery release; the **prior-year comparison** figures (2024 4° básico 278/264, 6° básico 249/245, II medio 249/259, lowest-SES ~+15) come from the separate 2024 "histórico avance" release — two releases, two vintages, do not conflate (#21).
- The **CNED episode** (May 2026) needs two registers: Emol carries the *rejection* (the "brecha crítica" warning, the questioned loss of 6° básico + 8° English/natural sciences), La Tercera carries the *approved revised plan* (6° restored). The council's own site **cned.cl is redirect-walled to datacenter egress** (observation per #20) — its decisions corroborate on **curriculumnacional.cl**, a Mineduc register (banked at the 07-15 verification; second-register path for Chilean council decisions).
- The **standing critique** (Alto al SIMCE; the El Mostrador column; the USACH academic) is carried, not adjudicated — a census this consequential earns a permanent dissent, and the piece's job is to hold it, not settle it.

**Figure-axis traps already caught on this piece** (from the 07-15 verdict, ruling #22): the 8° básico "not significant" qualifier governs **both** falls (reading −3 and maths −5), not maths alone — *a qualifier keeps its scope*. Any future Chile piece re-using these numbers must re-attach the qualifier to the same list the register gives.

## Part 2 — the Spanish-vs-Italian-origin routing for a Chilean byline (extends #28b)

The Singapore increment (2026-08-04) gave three name-origins for an ASEAN byline: **restore** (Malay/Muslim → Arabic root), **convention** (attested romanization), **phonetic** (English given name). Chile adds the Latin-American case, where the trap is that **one byline can carry two origin systems at once** — a Spanish surname and an Italian (or German, or Croatian) given name, both naturalised.

Worked example — **Gino Cortez**, secretario ejecutivo of the Agencia:
- **Cortez → كورتيس** — CONFIRMED. Standard Latin-American Spanish: the terminal *‑z* is /s/ (not the peninsular /θ/), so كورتيس, not كورتيث. This is the *convention* route and it is safe for any Spanish surname.
- **Gino → جينو** — CONFIRMED-by-convention, but note *why the default Spanish-orthography reading is wrong*. Applying Spanish spelling rules, *Gi‑* would voice as /x/ ("HEE-no" → خينو). But **Gino is an Italian-origin given name**, ubiquitous in Chile's large Italian-descended population, and pronounced /ˈdʒino/ ("JEE-no"). The attested Arabic-media rendering is **جينو**. Routing the given name through the *surname's* language would have produced خينو — wrong.

**The rule:** in Latin America, **identify the origin of each name-part separately before routing.** A Spanish surname routes through Spanish convention; an Italian/German/Croatian/Levantine given name routes through *its own* origin, not the surname's. Same confidence class as India's Bhaduri (2026-08-03) and Singapore's Ashiq — an attested standard form is CONFIRMED-by-convention, **not** PROVISIONAL (#28b reserves PROVISIONAL for a name clearing *no* route, like Australia's Gniel).

**Why it matters at scale:** Latin America is under-represented in the corpus (2 pieces) and Ed05's slate touches it again; a byline-routing habit that doesn't silently Hispanicise an Italian given name removes the single most common Latin-American transliteration error before it can harden into a shipped name.
