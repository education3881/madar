## The routing rule — `ph` in Bantu orthography is an aspirated /p/, never /f/

The single most common surname in Zambia and Malawi is **Phiri**. An Arabic composer reading it through English or French orthographic habit renders it **فيري** — and that is wrong. In Chewa, Nyanja, Tumbuka and the related orthographies, the digraph `ph` is **an aspirated bilabial stop /pʰ/**, not a labiodental fricative. The `h` marks aspiration, exactly as it does in `th` (/tʰ/) and `kh` (/kʰ/) in the same writing systems. Phiri is *pee-ree*, not *fee-ree*.

**Convention:** route `ph` in Bantu-language names to **ب** (or **پ** where a piece already uses it), never to **ف**. So **Melesiana Phiri → ميليسيانا بيري**. The same rule governs `Phiri`, `Mphande`, `Chiphaka`, `Phika`.

**Why this is a rule and not a preference.** Transliteration encodes a *sound* (#28b). Latin-script orthography is a false friend here in a specific, predictable way: the same two letters mean /f/ in Greek-derived English vocabulary and /pʰ/ in Bantu orthography, and the reader of the Arabic has no way to recover the error. It is also not confirmable from a served text, so without a rule every such name would be flagged PROVISIONAL forever — the rule is what converts an unresolvable per-name question into a resolvable per-language one.

**Sibling to watch in the same family:** `ch` in Bantu orthography is /tʃ/ (**Chilala → شيلالا**, **Chibesakunda**), which coincides with English habit and so causes no trouble — worth stating only so the *contrast* with `ph` is explicit and nobody generalises "trust the English reading".

## Terms and institutions (gloss + Latin tag on first mention, per #28a)

- **kwacha** → **كواتشا**; amounts kept in full digits as the register prints them (1,994,817,600), never rounded into "roughly two billion" — the precision *is* the provenance.
- **penalty unit** → **وحدة عقوبة** — a Zambian statutory unit of account, glossed not converted; converting to kwacha would require a rate the Act does not state.
- **ZEEP / ZEEL / ZICTA / ZANIS** → Latin tags retained beside an Arabic gloss; none is naturalised, because none reads as an Arabic word and all appear once or twice.
- **Education Statistics Bulletin** → **نشرة إحصاءات التعليم** — glossed, and the piece cites it only through the Ministry's admission of its two-year lag, never for a figure (the `edu.gov.zm` wall still holds).
- **Grade 7 / grades 1–6** → **الصف السابع / الصفوف من الأول إلى السادس**. Note for the edition: Zambian "form one to form six" for secondary is rendered **السنوات من الأولى إلى السادسة**, deliberately *not* الصفوف, so the Act's two distinct ladders stay distinguishable in Arabic as they are in English.
