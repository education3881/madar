# Ruling #30 — a lineage claim is a figure

## The rule

**A claim that instrument B *is*, *descends from*, *continues*, or *is a phase of* instrument A is a citable figure, and it carries its own provenance axes.** It must be traced verbatim to a source that states the relationship — exactly as a number is traced to a source that states the value. Absent that trace, the honest draft carries the two instruments side by side, joined by idea and geography, with the connection **unasserted**.

The axes a lineage claim travels with — the drafter names which one the source supports:

| Axis | The question |
|---|---|
| **Same programme** | Is this literally the same instrument, still running under the same name and mandate? |
| **Formal successor** | Did an institution of record declare B the successor of A (a compact, a launch document, a budget line)? |
| **Rebrand** | Same instrument, new name — and who renamed it, when? |
| **Independent parallel** | Same idea, same place, same technology, *different build* — the most common truth, and the one resemblance hides. |

**Same place + same technology + same look ≠ same instrument.** Resemblance is a lead (the #26 family: an observation that a link may exist), never the citation.

## Why it needs its own number

The figure-integrity checklist (§0 of the INDEX, axes 1–7, rulings #12/#12b, #14, #21–#25) and the scoped-instrument family (Section-1 rows 15/19/20/21) both scope a **number** — its coverage, stage, vintage, scope, direction, position, condition, base, denominator, ruler. None of them scopes an **identity**. A draft can carry every figure perfectly and still assert, with no source at all, that the figures belong to the same programme — and that single unsourced verb ("continues", "is the successor to", "the same platform that…") is load-bearing for the whole spine.

This is not a hypothetical. It caught a live draft spine this week.

## The origin case (Sudan, 2026-08-13)

The tempting Ed05 Sudan spine was: *"Sudan's wartime digital learning spaces run the platform Sudan invented."* It is supported by **place** (Kassala), **technology** (tablets, solar, facilitator-led game learning), and **idea** (the no-school classroom). It is supported by **no served-text source stating the identity** — and one register points the other way: War Child's Annual Report 2025 lists Can't Wait To Learn's 2025 reach as Ukraine, Uganda and Lebanon, with **Sudan not on the list**.

Had the draft asserted the lineage, every individual figure in it would have verified clean, and the piece would still have been wrong — wrong in its spine, which is the expensive kind. The recon carried the two builds side by side instead, with the connection open pending a trace.

## Where it binds next (the Ed05 slate)

- **Egypt** — is the 2025 hiring drive the *same programme* as the earlier one, a *formal successor*, or a new instrument with an inherited target? (Row 20's claim series runs Nov 2024 → Dec 2025 across ministries; the register does not, on its face, settle the identity.)
- **Rwanda** — CBC's descent from the 2016 reform: successor or continuation?
- **Sierra Leone** — the shipped TSC deployment piece and the SLEIC outcomes fund are *deliberately fenced* as different instruments (row 21). That fence is this ruling, applied before the ruling existed.
- **Any "Phase II"**, any programme that changes funder or implementing partner mid-life, and every "building on the success of…" sentence in a press release — which is where lineage claims are manufactured.

## The verification move

Same shape as the rest of the method family: **the relationship gets its own trace-list row.** The Verifier's list carries `lineage claim traced to a source stating the relationship: ☐` whenever the draft's spine depends on B-descends-from-A. A lineage row failed is a spine failed, not a sentence failed — it goes back to the Editor, not to the copy pass.
