# A headline has more than one served form, and a quotation mark promises the one the document prints

**Ruling #119.** Filed 2026-10-28 from the standing queue's own head — **item 33**, raised 2026-10-22
from item 18's close, **6 days**, the oldest row in the DEFECT bucket and the **third served DEFECT ever
to take a head** under #116. Family: **reachability / identity** (#20, #26, #11, #27, #54, #100, #111,
**#112**), which keeps no running member count, so four counts moved and not five. Second family, by the
half of this ruling that is about an instrument rather than about a register: **enumeration** (*a green
check is scoped to what it enumerates*), which also keeps no count.

---

## What the item said, and what was actually there

Item 33 named **three published citations that misquote their own registers, and a fourth that is
soft-dead**. For the twenty-second consecutive closure the specification was not the scope. There were
**six misquotations on two pieces**, the fourth was not dead and never had been, and **one of the six
was already sitting in the instrument's own register as `absent` on 2026-10-22** — the run that raised
the item read three rows and stopped.

Every one of the six is a string the register *serves*. Not one of them is the string the register
**prints as its headline**. That is the ruling.

| # | Piece | What we printed inside quotation marks | What the document prints | Where our string came from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | `2026-05-28-iraq-mosul-library` (EN + AR) | `Restoring the Mosul University Library` | **Restoring and modernizing the Mosul University Library** | the title with a conjunct dropped |
| b | `2026-07-07-us-naep-honesty-gap` (EN) | `Oklahoma board raises testing rigor to restore truth and transparency to student testing` | **Oklahoma board raises rigor to restore ‘truth and transparency’ to student testing** | **the URL slug, de-hyphenated** |
| c | `2026-06-17-korea-aidt-reversal` (EN + AR) | `AI textbooks targeted for phaseout under South Korea's new administration` | **AI textbooks targeted for phaseout under new government** | **the URL slug, de-hyphenated** |
| d | `2026-06-17-korea-aidt-reversal` (EN + AR) | `South Korea's AI textbooks fail after rushed rollout` | **AI-powered textbooks fail to make the grade in South Korea** | **the `<title>` element** — which the page also serves, and which disagrees with its own `<h1>` |
| e | `2026-06-17-korea-aidt-reversal` (EN + AR) | `Teachers Are Leading an AI Revolution in Korean Classrooms` | **Teachers are leading an AI revolution in Korean classrooms** | the headline **re-capitalised** into Title Case |
| f | `2026-06-17-korea-aidt-reversal` (EN + AR) | `South Korea pulls plug on AI textbooks, leaving schools, companies without funding for them` | **South Korea pulls plug on AI textbooks** | a form served **by nobody** — absent from the page today and from every snapshot of it we can read, including one taken the day it published |

Four distinct provenances, and the common property is the whole finding: **a headline is not one
string.** One document serves it as an `<h1>`, a `<title>`, an `og:title`, a JSON-LD `headline` and a
URL slug, and those five routinely differ — on (d) the same page serves two of them that share not one
word beyond *South Korea* and *textbooks*. A drafter's hand reaches for whichever is easiest to copy:
the browser tab (that is the `<title>`), the address bar (that is the slug), the search result (that is
(f), and it is **#41 performed inside quotation marks** — *read the register; never cite from a result
list*). **The `<h1>` is the only one of the five that requires reading the page, and it is the only one
that was never used.**

**(b) is the sharpest, because the slug explains the punctuation.** The register's headline puts *truth
and transparency* in single quotation marks — the newspaper quoting the commission. Our version has no
inner quotation marks at all, and that is not an editing decision anybody made: **a URL slug cannot
carry punctuation.** De-hyphenating a slug produces a string that looks exactly like a verbatim
quotation and has had every mark of attribution inside it stripped by the web server. #112 found this
instance and described it as *a word inserted and the register's own inner quotation marks dropped*. The
mechanism is one step back from that: nothing was inserted and nothing was dropped. A different string
was copied.

**(e) is the one the Arabic edition cannot have.** Arabic has no letter case, so the re-capitalisation
defect does not exist on that side — the Arabic annotation's rendering of this headline was correct
throughout, as it was for (b), where the Arabic edition quotes only the inner phrase «الصدق والشفافية»
and never claimed the headline at all. This is #86 / #101 / #112's asymmetry a fourth time, and it now
runs in both directions at once: the edition **forced** back to the register is immune to this class,
and it is also the edition from which no instrument of ours can read the evidence (#112's 128 of 134
declined tokens; #113's 0 of 44 anchors).

## The figure underneath the URL

(e)'s repair was a one-line URL change — the annotation pointed at `blogs.worldbank.org/en/education`,
the blog **index**, not the article. Opening the article to confirm the headline found what no citation
instrument was looking at. The annotation and the body both attributed to the World Bank a **three-year
commitment of about ₩1 trillion, near $736 million, for teacher training and infrastructure across 2024
to 2026**. The World Bank register prints **no won figure and no infrastructure line**: its sentence is
*"has allocated approximately $0.74 billion for three years (2024–2026) specifically for teacher
training."* The won figure is real and belongs to **the Korea Times** — *"An estimated 1 trillion won
($736 million) has already been invested in teacher training and infrastructure"* — and the piece's
other money figure belongs to **the Korea Herald**: *"At least 533.3 billion won ($385 million) was
allocated to the project last year alone"*, whose **at least** our body had dropped.

So two registers' figures had been merged and the pair attributed to one of them, and **the near-agreement
is what hid the merge**: $0.74 billion and $736 million are close enough that `~$0.74bn / ₩1tn` reads as
one figure printed in two currencies rather than as two figures from two documents. It is not a
conversion. It is a composite — the thing the Edition 05 commissions prohibit under *no composite of
rulers*, and the thing `CLAUDE.md` prohibits under *never averaged across registers*, arriving in a form
neither sentence names: **not averaged, paired.** Every numeric instrument this operation owns passed on
it, correctly: ₩1 trillion appears in the annotation and in the body, so #114's comparison agrees; the
numerals are present, so #38's multiset agrees. **The defect is in the attribution, and the attribution
is prose.**

The body now gives each figure its own register, its own hedge and its own scope, and says the thing the
old sentence was trying to say with one register's name on three numbers: *three registers, three
scopes; none of the three is a part of another.*

## The fourth item was not dead

(d) in item 33's own lettering — `2026-06-25-brazil-crianca-alfabetizada` — was raised as a 200 serving
`EBC - Página temporariamente indisponível`, to be disposed under one of **#100's four dispositions**.
None of the four applies. Re-asked today, the register serves its full page, 6,494 characters, and the
quotation we attributed to it is **found**. The 10-22 reading was a transient, and **#100's four
dispositions are all dispositions of a document; this is a disposition of a moment.** No byte of the
publication changed, and the only reason anybody knows is that a second reading was taken six days
later. The same is true in the other direction on (a): `core.unesco.org` answers **403** to a browser
user-agent from this runner today and **200** to the instrument's own, which is #70 (*walled by
rendering, not dead*) restated as a property of the client rather than of the document.

**Binding, limb 1:** a state read once from a third-party register is an observation with a date on it,
never a property of the document. A row that says `absent`, `walled`, `dead` or `too-short` is a claim
about one request, and a repair queued off such a row **re-reads the register first.** Two of today's six
would have been repaired wrongly by trusting a six-day-old row.

## The instrument could not see two of the six, and it said nothing

`qa_citation_identity` extracted **no token at all** from (c) and (d). Both annotations quote an English
headline containing a possessive apostrophe — *South Korea's*, *Korea's* — and the straight-single
pattern is `(?<![A-Za-z])'([^'‘’]{10,260})'(?![A-Za-z])`. The character class excludes `'`, so the match
cannot cross an apostrophe, and the apostrophe itself fails both guards. **The result is not a declined
token. It is no token**, and the register row then reports fewer quoted entries for the piece without
reporting that it could not read them. #112's own header enumerates five quotation conventions
*measured rather than assumed*; it measured the delimiters and never measured what the delimited span
is allowed to contain.

Measured over all 88 approved pieces this morning: the tool extracts **257** straight-single spans; a
reading that treats `'` as an apostrophe when a letter stands on **both** sides of it extracts **290**.
**33 spans across 18 pieces — 11.4% of the straight-single population — are invisible, every one of them
English**, because Arabic uses guillemets and has no possessive apostrophe. And the blind spot is not
neutral: an English possessive is what a headline about an institution, a country or a minister almost
always contains, so the dropped spans are **systematically the headlines**. Two of the 33 are confirmed
misquotations found by hand today.

A looser reading — `'` absorbed whenever a letter follows it — recovers 34 and **mis-splits two nested
quotations** (`on the 'capillarity' of the state's literacy policy`; `so 'the date is the index's, not
the article's' is a complete statement`). The strict rule, a letter on both sides, recovers 33 with
**zero regressions against the 257**. That is the repair, and it is **item 48** rather than a line
changed this afternoon, because the 33 new tokens must each be asked before the register can report on
them and because at least one of them (`the date is the index's, not the article's`) is an annotation's
internal note rather than a claim about anybody's bytes — a new extraction rule needs its false-positive
class named before it ships, which is this operation's own rule about floors (#93).

**Binding, limb 2:** an extractor's *delimiters* and an extractor's *span* are two separate
measurements, and a tool that measured one has not measured the other. Where a check derives its
population from a pattern, the count of what the pattern **skipped** is part of the check's output —
`N extracted, M skipped and why` — because a span that matches nothing is reported as absent and a span
that was never extracted is reported as nothing at all.

## Binding, limb 3 — the register's own words have an address

#101 clause 4 says a correction is written **in the register's own words**. Today shows that the clause
is incomplete: the register has several sets of words about one document, and the clause does not say
which. It does now.

1. **A quoted headline is the `<h1>` the document prints**, read on the page. Where the page's `<title>`
   or `og:title` disagrees with it, the annotation names the disagreement rather than choosing silently
   — as `2026-06-17-korea-aidt-reversal` now does for Rest of World.
2. **A URL slug is never a quotation.** It is a normalised, punctuation-free, sometimes pre-edit copy,
   and de-hyphenating it produces a counterfeit with no tell.
3. **Capitalisation is inside the quotation marks.** A sentence-case headline is not re-cased to Title
   Case, and a Title Case headline is not lowered.
4. **A search result is not a register** (#41) — and the one form of this defect that no amount of
   care at the moment of drafting would have caught is (f), which matches no snapshot of the document
   that exists. A string nobody serves is the only one of the six that could not have been verified by
   looking at the right place, because there was no right place.
5. **The hand-off to the Editor carries `quoted headlines: N, each read from the page's own heading: ☐`**
   — same teeth as the dek/title cap (07-27), `annotation == sentence` (09-13), the named-humans gender
   line (#86), the acronym line (#101) and the editorial-futures line (#117). A hand-off missing it is
   returned unread.

## General form

**#112 established that a quotation mark is a claim about somebody else's bytes. This adds that the
bytes are plural.** A document does not serve one authoritative string for its own title; it serves
several, for several machines, and they drift apart in exactly the ways a publisher never has to
reconcile. So *verbatim* is not a property a quotation can have on its own — it is a relation between a
quotation and a **named form** of the source, and a citation discipline that does not name the form has
left the choice to whichever surface the drafter's hand was nearest.

And the half that generalises past citation: **the easiest copy is the one furthest from the document.**
The tab title, the address bar and the result list are all one keystroke away and all describe the page
from outside it; the heading requires reading the page. Every one of today's six took the cheap route,
across five pieces, four drafters and five months, and not one of them was carelessness — each is the
shortest path from a screen to a text editor.

**Forward surface, named rather than carried:** this operation has four instruments that compare a
quoted string against a register, and all four ask *is this string in that document?* **None asks
whether the string we chose is the string the document leads with** — and (d) proves the question is not
rhetorical, because both of Rest of World's headlines are *in* the document and only one of them is its
heading. A check that searches the whole body for a token will confirm a `<title>`-derived quotation
forever.
