# Ruling #112 — A quotation mark is a claim about somebody else's bytes, and the forced channel for a name is the blind channel for a quotation

**Filed 2026-10-22** · daily run, from the standing queue's own head — **item 18 at 7 days and six
displacements**, the oldest item past the three-displacement threshold, tied with item 17 on age and
taken on displacement count, 6 against 4. Sixteenth selection in seventeen daily runs made by the rule
rather than by judgment.

**Standing assertion 36, `qa_citation_identity`.** Register: 36 assertions, 21 `postbuild`, **33 of 36
gate the deploy**, all three derived by `qa_patch_queue` from its three homes rather than copied into a

## The question, and the half of it item 18 had not noticed

Item 18 asked: *every instrument we own classifies an outbound promise by **status code**, and nothing
asks whether the bytes at that URL are still the register the annotation describes.* It specified the
instrument too: *a per-citation identity token — a distinctive string from the annotation — asserted
against the served text.*

The specification was right about the gap and **wrong about where the token would have to come from.**
An annotation is *our* prose. A token chosen out of it is a token we composed, and asserting our own
paraphrase against a ministry's server is #85's error on the input side. **Nothing needed inventing.**
This publication's annotations already carry, inside quotation marks, strings it asserts the register
*prints*:

> `'Source: School census, Ministry of Education, 2025'` · `'declined only marginally'` ·
> `« Le PASEC 2024 sera la dernière évaluation internationale »`

**Those are claims about somebody else's bytes, written by us, dated by us, and never once checked
against those bytes.** Measured over the 682 source entries of the 88 approved pieces: **386 carry at
least one such fragment, 529 fragments over 218 distinct registers** — 362 straight-quoted and 21
guillemetted in the English edition, 335 guillemetted in the Arabic. The quotation mark is the only
identity token with evidentiary standing available, because it is the only one where we are not asking
whether a document still *resembles* what we said.

## The reading, with its denominator, because the denominator is the whole lesson of #111

**170 of the 218 registers asked** (the emit carries a request budget and tops up across runs). What
came back:

| state | n | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **confirmed** | **59** | every quotation we attributed is still in the served text |
| walled | 34 | 401/403/429 — unreadable by us, no statement possible (#70) |
| **absent** | **22** | answers 200, serves readable text, and **none** of our quotations is in it |
| script-mismatch | 20 | the document is not written in the token's script — declined, not failed |
| unreadable-format | 13 | PDF or binary; a distribution caption is never built and a PDF is never text |
| **partial** | **9** | some of our quotations are there and some are not |
| unreached | 7 | no answer from here on this reading |
| dead | 6 | 4xx/5xx — five of them `moe.gov.sg`, which is `qa_sources_alive`'s lane |

**The comparison was possible on 90 of the 170.** Of those 90, **31 — 34.4% — no longer serve a
quotation this publication put inside quotation marks.** At the token level: **275 tokens reached a
verdict, 110 found, 31 absent, 134 declined.**

**And the number has three defensible denominators, which is #111's lesson arriving one day later and
one instrument over.** 31 of 218 is **14.2%**. 31 of 170 asked is **18.2%**. 31 of the 90 the
instrument can actually *compare* is **34.4%**. Only the third is an answer to the question; the first
two are answers about the instrument's reach, wearing the units of the world. *A rate whose
denominator includes the rows nobody could read is a measurement of us.*

## What the four causes are, read by hand, because only a human read separates them

A missing quotation has four causes and **only two of them are ours.** The tool prints the bucket and
fails on none of it. Four registers were opened by hand on the day of filing, one per cause:

1. **Their document died softly, at 200 — item 18's own specified defect, found on first contact.**
   `agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/…/brasil-atinge-592-de-criancas-alfabetizadas-em-2024` answers **HTTP
   200** and serves **`EBC - Página temporariamente indisponível`** — 16 KB of error page, none of the
   article. `qa_sources_alive` reports it alive. Both editions of
   `2026-06-25-brazil-crianca-alfabetizada` lean on it.
2. **Our quotation marks around a name the register shortens — #101 in a quotation.** Our annotation
   reads `'Restoring the Mosul University Library' project page (Project ID 534IRQ1002)`. UNESCO's own
   title is **`Restoring and modernizing the Mosul University Library`**. The project is the right
   project; the quoted name is not the register's name. #101 said *an acronym is a name with its
   evidence removed*; this is the same removal performed by a **truncation inside quotation marks**,
   which is worse, because quotation marks are the one punctuation that promises there was no editing.
3. **Our quotation marks around a headline we tightened, with the register's own quotation marks
   dropped.** Ours: `'Oklahoma board raises testing rigor to restore truth and transparency to student
   testing'`. Theirs: **`Oklahoma board raises rigor to restore 'truth and transparency' to student
   testing`**. Two edits in one string — a word inserted, and *the register's own inner quotation marks
   removed*, which silently converts a phrase the board was being quoted as using into the newspaper's
   own description. That second edit is #43's register hierarchy destroyed by punctuation.
4. **The quotation is right and the URL is the wrong document.** Our annotation quotes the World Bank
   article `'Teachers Are Leading an AI Revolution in Korean Classrooms'`; the cited URL is
   `blogs.worldbank.org/en/education`, which is the **blog index** (`<title>Education for Global
   Development</title>`). Nothing is misquoted and the citation still points nobody at the document.
   **This is item 18's premise arrived at from our own side** — the bytes at that URL are not the
   register the annotation describes, and it was us who put them apart.

**Classes 2, 3 and 4 are ours, they are in served pages, and they are not this instrument's to
repair** — a fix that changes served bytes is the Web Developer's even when an assertion found it
(RUNBOOK, the 09-27 split). Queued, with the register beside the claim.

## THE SHARP HALF — the forced channel for a name is the blind channel for a quotation

**128 of the 134 declined tokens are Arabic**, and that is not a quirk of the sweep. Madār *composes*
the Arabic (#86), so when a register prints a sentence in English, French or Portuguese, the Arabic
annotation carries **Madār's rendering of it** inside guillemets. A rendering is not a quotation. The
string was never in the served bytes and never could be.

Set that against **#101**, which is three days old in this register and says the opposite about the
same two editions: *Arabic cannot carry a Latin-script acronym as a name, so the Arabic composer was
forced back to the register; the English composer had the option of the token and took it.* There,
Arabic is the **control**, and #101 clause 3 makes the forced rendering settle a disagreement.

**It is one property producing both results.** Arabic cannot hold Latin script. For a **name**, that
forces a read of the register and the Arabic edition becomes the second channel. For a **quotation**,
the same incapacity forces a *composition*, and the Arabic edition becomes **structurally incapable of
carrying the evidence at all.** The channel that is forced to be right about a name is forced to be
unverifiable about a sentence.

**So #86's rule needs its second clause.** #86 says: *when two editions check each other, first ask
which questions only one of them can answer.* That is necessary and it is not sufficient. **Also ask
which answers one edition is incapable of giving in the register's own terms** — because those answers
will arrive, in quotation marks, looking exactly like the other edition's.

**Measured consequence, stated rather than implied: the identity of this publication's quotations is a
single-channel measurement in the English edition, over 213 of 341 entries, and the Arabic edition
cannot second it.** The 33 pieces touched by an `absent` or `partial` register split 16 EN and 17 AR
only because the two editions cite the same URLs — not because anything compared them.

## The instrument, and the four false positives it produced before the code was right

Every one was found by opening a register by hand, and every one had a confident face. **A comparison
check's error rate is set by how much of the comparison it declines to make** — #109's lesson about
intended assembly, on an input.

1. **A language-switcher label is not an Arabic document.** `decree.om/2026/rd20260012/` serves an
   English decree summary with **14** Arabic characters in 11,775 of text. The first licence rule —
   *does the document contain any glyph of the token's script* — called an Arabic token `absent` there.
2. **A quotation mark inside a quotation is punctuation, not identity.** The UN's Arabic interview
   serves `يطلق عليها اسم "مكانّا (مكاننا)" باللغة العربية`; our annotation quotes the clause without
   the inner pair. Reported `absent` on a register carrying our sentence verbatim.
3. **U+2011 non-breaking hyphen.** Ghana News Agency serves `out‑of‑school`; we carry the ASCII hyphen.
4. **An English rendering of a Cyrillic programme name.** `osvita.ua` serves a Ukrainian instrument
   whose Latin navigation clears any character floor, so *School Offline* read `absent` rather than
   declined — **the mirror of (1), and the pair is what forced the rule to be rewritten properly.**

The licence is now **dominant script, measured** — a token is compared only against a document written
in the token's own script, with a floor of 200 characters, the two populations three orders of
magnitude apart (14 against 8,874) and both printed in the code rather than remembered. It is
**deliberately conservative**: a bilingual register — Bahrain's BQA prints Arabic and English in the
same row — has one dominant script, so the other edition's token is declined rather than compared.
**That trades a missed comparison for never making a confident wrong one**, which is the right
direction for an instrument whose output is a candidate list a human reads.

## The two halves, and why only one gates — the #89 / #111 design, reused a third time

`--emit` (network) fetches each quotation-bearing register and records a dated verdict per token into
`agents/stats/citation-identity.json`. **It gates nothing**: somebody else's rewrite at 09:00 must not
stop this publication deploying, which is the reason `qa_sources_alive` is one of the three
non-gating assertions, written in `astro-pages.yml` where the others are listed.

`--check` (offline, deterministic, **gated from `postbuild`**) asserts that the register **covers the
corpus** — every URL supplying an identity token in an approved piece has a row — and refuses the
silent-pass trap. If the emit quietly stops describing registers, or a piece ships a quotation nobody
has ever compared against its source, the build fails instead of the measurement narrowing in silence.

**Proved both ways, bite first, on `/tmp` copies, every injection asserted to have changed the artefact
before the check ran on it.** Five bites red with controls green on either side: a quoted register with
no row; a row that stops describing its subject; no register on disk at all; an approved corpus with no
quotation in it; and **the corpus keeping all 682 citations while losing every quotation mark** — the
trap this instrument is most exposed to, since a style change that stopped annotations quoting their
registers would leave coverage perfect and the instrument blind. *That fifth bite did not bite on its
first run*: it stripped four of the five quotation conventions and left `\"` alone, so the check exited
0 and read exactly like a passing control. Eleven readings of the comparison itself against synthetic
served bytes — three controls, one bite, two licence limbs, two format boundaries and the four false
positives above.
