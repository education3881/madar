# Ruling #87 — a quantifier's SCOPE is not preserved by composition, and it is the one claim parity cannot see

**Filed:** 2026-10-05 · **Lane:** Arabic Editor · **Family:** non-numeric (tenth member)
**Earned on:** Edition 05 row 4, `2026-09-14-africa-best-system-ruler`, held
**Full judgment:** `content-drafts/verdicts/2026-10-05-ed05-ruler-arabic-register-judgment.md`

## The rule, in one line

**A quantifier is a claim, and its strength lives in word order — so when two editions are
*composed* rather than translated, the quantifier is the first thing to diverge and the last
thing any gate can see.**

## What happened

The piece's spine sentence, in English:

> …there are four defensible ways to measure it, that they crown four different countries,
> and that **no two of them can be added together**.

The Arabic, composed independently and carried for twenty days through four gates:

> …**وأنَّ اثنتين منها لا تُجمَعان**.

The composition was leaning — correctly in spirit — on **النكرة في سياق النفي تفيد العموم**,
*an indefinite in the context of negation yields generality*. But that principle has a
condition: the indefinite must fall **inside the scope of the negator**. Here the indefinite
dual `اثنتين` is the **ism of `أنَّ`** and the negation sits in the **predicate**, so the
indefinite is the topic and the negation is asserted *of* it. Generality never triggers.
`منها` is partitive and pulls the same way. The Arabic therefore reads **"there are two of
them that cannot be combined"** — which *implies the remaining pairs can be*.

The English states a universal. The Arabic stated an existential. **At the sentence the whole
piece turns on**, and against the Arabic edition's own closing paragraph (`ولا تُتوسَّط` —
*"They cannot be averaged"*), which says the universal plainly four screens later.

Fixed by moving the indefinite inside the negation and changing nothing else:
**وأنَّه لا تُجمَعُ اثنتان منها.**

## Why no gate could see it

This is the part worth carrying, and it is a *different* blindness from the three before it.

- `qa_pair_frontmatter` compares **frontmatter**. This is **body prose**, which in a composed
  edition is *supposed* to differ. The check is right to be silent.
- The **Editor's** five-test verdict and the **Verifier's** verdict read the piece for what it
  claims — through instruments that are, as #76's family established, overwhelmingly numeric.
  **A quantifier contains no numeral.** `no two` is not a figure; it is a scope.
- The **Arabic gate** reads the Arabic for fluency and fidelity, and
  `اثنتين منها لا تُجمَعان` is **perfectly fluent** and *locally* faithful. It is wrong only
  against the other edition, and only at the quantifier.

**Each gate was correct within its own enumeration, and the defect lived in the gap between
them.** The enumeration family, #50's general form, pointed at a *claim* instead of a check.

## Its place beside #86, which is the sharp part

**#86** (2026-10-04) found that the Arabic edition makes determinations the English edition
**cannot make** — grammatical gender on a role noun — so those claims have exactly one
channel while appearing to have two.

**#87 is the inverse and completes the pair.** Here both editions make **the same claim**, and
Arabic cannot carry its *quantifier scope* with English's word order. So the claim's
**strength** is a thing each edition states by a different mechanism — English by word order
(`no two`), Arabic by the position of an indefinite relative to a negator — and **two correct
mechanisms can produce two different strengths from one intention.**

Together: **a second edition is a second channel for *facts* and a single channel for
*grammar*.** Names, numbers and dates cross; scope, gender and agreement do not.

> **The transferable form, which outlives Arabic:** when two renderings of one claim are
> compared, the comparison covers what both can express. **Anything one of them expresses
> structurally — by word order, by inflection, by agreement — is invisible to the comparison
> and must be checked inside that language, against the claim, not against the other edition.**

## Binding

1. **A load-bearing quantifier is read in both editions as a quantifier**, not as prose: the
   drafter states, per sentence, whether the claim is universal, existential or partitive, and
   both editions are checked to make **the same one**.
2. **In Arabic, an indefinite intended to carry generality under negation is placed inside the
   negator's scope** — after it, never as the ism of a preceding `أنَّ`. Where the structure
   cannot be rearranged, `ما مِن` + indefinite is the unambiguous universal negative and is
   preferred over relying on an unstated principle.
3. **The pair's spine sentence is read last, in both languages, side by side, for strength
   alone** — not for figures, names, hedges or fluency, all of which were already green here.
4. **Parity is a floor and the floor is frontmatter** (#42, #78, #84, #86, and now this).
