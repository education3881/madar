# Ruling #21 — A recount is not a revision (same cohort, two vintages)
**Date:** 2026-07-13 · **Filed by:** Verifier (first verdict, NZ NCEA co-requisite) · **Series:** figure discipline (#4 → #9 → #10 → #11 → #12/#12b → #14 → #19)

## The case
The NZ draft carried: "the 2024 Level 1 pass rate, first reported at 71.5 per cent, **was later revised to** 79.6." The 79.6 is real — the ministerial release of 14 July 2025 says verbatim that late co-requisite clearances "take the pass rate for NCEA level 1 in 2024 from 71.5 per cent to 79.6 per cent." But the live re-fetch of NZQA's 27 March 2026 attainment release — **the same document the draft cites two sentences earlier** — still prints "71.5% in 2024" as the standing comparator against 73.5% in 2025. The qualifications authority's annual series never adopted the recount. The draft asserted a restatement the system of record never made; recon-trace alone could not catch it (the draft matched its recon exactly) — only the live register could.

## The rule
Two true figures can describe the **same cohort at different vintages**: the year-end snapshot the system of record keeps, and a later recount an actor announces. Before writing "revised," "corrected," or "restated," check **whether the system of record adopted the number**. If it did not:
1. Attribute the recount to its author and vintage **in the sentence** ("by mid-2025 the ministry was counting the same cohort at 79.6") — never state it as a property of the record.
2. Carry the register's standing figure alongside when the piece also cites that register ("a recount, not a restatement: the authority's year-end series still records 2024 at 71.5").
3. Treat "which count does the keeper keep?" as part of the figure trace, not an optional flourish — a hostile competent reader reads with the register open.

## Why it recurs
Announcing bodies (ministers, agencies under reform pressure) have an interest in the more favourable vintage; record-keeping bodies (qualifications authorities, statistics offices) have an interest in series consistency. Both are honest; the *blend* is not. Sibling of **#12/#12b** (administration and coverage are axes of a figure — vintage is a third), of **#14** (name the stage before using the number — name the *count* too), and of **#19** (the fence lives in the sentence that carries the risk). Verification-side lesson: this is the first defect class caught **only** by live re-fetch of a register the recon had already cleared — the recon was faithful, the draft was faithful to the recon, and the claim was still wrong about the record. Trace-to-recon plus live-spot-check is the minimum method (Verifier persona §philosophy); this ruling is why.
