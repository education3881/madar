# Row 35 / Ruling #40 — A mirror can misname the instrument: a program name is a figure, traced to the owner's register

**Filed:** 2026-08-31 · Researcher, from the Ed05 Sudan re-verification · Extends #38 (a source can drift against itself) outward: a *mirror* can drift against the *owner*, and the thing that drifts can be the instrument's own name.

## The rule
**A program's name is a figure.** It carries provenance axes exactly like a number — who coined it, which register serves it, whether the form cited is the owner's or a reporter's — and it is traced to the *instrument's owner*, never taken from a mirror's headline. When a news mirror and a first-party register disagree on a name, the owner's register wins without arbitration, and every downstream artefact (headline, body, AR transliteration, tags) carries the owner's form.

## Origin case
The 08-13 Sudan recon carried the new GPE-funded schools programme as **"BRIDGE,"** from the Sudan Tribune's 4 May 2026 headline — the only register read that day. At re-verification (08-31), the first-party register — UNICEF Sudan's own press release, same date — serves the name as **"BRIDGES"** (*Building Resilience to Improve Development and Growth in Education in Sudan*), with the acronym's expansion printed beside it. The mirror's headline had carried a wrong instrument name for eighteen days in our recon index, and every figure around it was correct — which is exactly why nothing caught it: **every axis we trace (values, vintages, scopes) was clean; the name was not an axis.** Now it is.

## The second finding in the same case: recon freshness is register-specific
In the same eighteen days, **two of seven registers changed serving state** — Sudan Tribune went behind a bot-wall (served clean 08-13, walled 08-31), and the UNICEF USA adaptation became unreachable — while **zero confirmed figures went false**. Corollaries: (1) a recon index records a *fetch path*, and a fetch path has a shelf life independent of the facts it reached — the draft re-fetches everything (the Editor's #16-applied-to-recon-vintage instruction, now proven warranted on first use); (2) **a wall going up is itself a finding** — it changes the register of record, and the re-anchor hunt can surface a *better* register than the one lost, as here: the walled mirror was replaced by the first party, which corrected the name, added a third named voice, and carried an official Arabic edition.

## Applications
- Every recon index names the *owner* of each instrument it indexes, and marks any name taken from a mirror as PROVISIONAL until the owner's register serves it.
- Re-verification after a gap checks serving *state* per register, not just figure truth — walled/unreachable/moved are recorded outcomes, not errors.
- AR side: a mirror-sourced name must not be transliterated before the owner's form is confirmed — a transliteration of a wrong name is two defects wearing one word.
