# Ruling #32 — a credit line is not an output line; an attribution is a figure

**Filed:** 2026-08-18 · Section 1, row 25 · opened by Ed05 recon 8 (Rwanda teacher certification, the East Africa slot)
**Status:** binding on every piece, every brief, and on Madār's own copy

---

## Why this one takes a number

The seven figure axes (#12–#24) interrogate a measurement: who administered it, over what population, at which funnel stage, of what vintage, at what scope, in which direction, at which point in a series. Every one of them asks **what the number counts**.

None of them asks **whose number it is**.

An attribution — *"under the X project, the country built Y"* — is not a claim about what was counted. It is a claim about **who caused it**, and it can be false while every underlying figure is correct. The value is right, the population is right, the vintage is right, the scope of the *measurement* is right, and the sentence is still wrong, because the credit has been moved. It passes every check we own. That is what earns a number.

Kin, deliberately not merged: **#29** rejects a number whose *ruler* is unpublished. **#31** qualifies a number whose *alternative* is unexamined. **#32** qualifies a number whose *agent* is asserted. Three different things a figure can be silently missing.

---

## The rule

**When a source credits an actor with an output, the attribution is a load-bearing claim and is traced like a figure.** Before any sentence of the form *"under [programme / project / partnership / initiative], [actor] did [quantity]"* ships, three things are established:

1. **Whose ledger is this figure from** — the financier's, the implementer's, or the national programme's? These are three different books and they routinely carry three different totals for the same build.
2. **What share did the named actor finance or deliver**, and is that share published anywhere? If it is, use it. If it is not, the attribution is written loosely on purpose and our sentence does not tighten it.
3. **Does another register from the same body publish a different number for the same thing?** If yes, both are reported with their scopes named, and the seam is stated — never averaged, never picked.

**Default form.** National figures attach to national programmes; project figures attach to projects. Where only the credited framing exists, write *"a national programme that [funder] part-financed"* — never *"[funder]'s programme."* The safest sentence names the money and the output separately: *"a US$200m credit alongside Rwf86bn of government funding; the project's own progress note records over 11,000 of the roughly 22,000 classrooms."*

**Binding on our own copy first.** Madār does not write "the World Bank built 22,000 classrooms," and equally does not write "Rwanda built 22,000 classrooms with its own money." Both are attribution claims and both need their ledger.

## The origin case

Rwanda, 2020–2026. Two sentences, same partnership, same build, published by the two parties to it:

- **World Bank feature, 27 March 2026** — *"Under the Quality Basic Education for Human Capital Development (QBE) project, the Ministry of Education (MINEDUC) had recently completed construction of over **22,000 classrooms**."*
- **MINEDUC × World Bank joint progress note** — *"Over **11,000 classrooms** and 14,680 latrines have been constructed across the country"* under the same project.

Neither is a lie, and no figure in either is wrong. The **national accelerated-construction programme** built roughly **22,505** classrooms, financed by a **Rwf 86bn government contribution alongside a US$200m World Bank credit**, of which roughly **US$126m** covered **11,000 classrooms and 14,680 latrines**. So: 11,000 is the project's output; ~22,000 is the country's. The feature carries the country's number under the project's heading, and a draft quoting that sentence would credit a loan with **twice** what it built.

The tell was available and cheap: the same partnership publishes both numbers, and they differ by a factor of two for the same object. **Two figures for one build, from bodies with an interest in the build, is a scope question every time** — not a discrepancy to resolve, a seam to report.

## Why this will recur, and why now

Edition 05 is Africa, and the founder's posture is **positive** — accomplishments, not laments. Those two facts compound into exactly this failure mode:

**Nobody claims credit for a failure.** In a lament, attribution is contested downward and everybody's number shrinks. In an accomplishment, attribution is contested *upward*: the ministry, the financier, the implementing agency, the bilateral donor and the foundation all have a legitimate share and a publication budget, and each writes the largest true sentence available to it. The result is not fabrication — it is **a set of correct figures orbiting an unstated denominator of credit.**

Every story on the Ed05 slate is co-financed. Sierra Leone's outcomes fund (EOF / FCDO / five providers), Sudan's learning spaces (UNICEF, GPE, KfW, War Child), Rwanda's classrooms and certification (World Bank / MINEDUC / REB). Row 21 already banked the participant drift on Sierra Leone — **134k design → 130k+ funder → 100k+ completion** — which is the same defect one axis over: the number moved as it passed between ledgers. #32 names the general case.

**The positive posture does not relax this; it is the reason for it.** A verified accomplishment attributed to the wrong actor is worth less than an unverified one, because the error is invisible to a reader who checks the figure and finds it correct.

## The check

The Verifier's checklist gains one question, asked of every sentence that credits an actor with an output:

> **Whose ledger is this number from, and does the sentence say so?**

Two acceptable answers: the ledger is named in the sentence, or the source publishes only one ledger and the sentence uses the source's own framing verbatim with attribution ("*in the World Bank's account of the programme*"). Per the 2026-08-16 silent-pass trap, an assertion that finds nothing to check has **failed**, not passed: a piece about a donor-financed programme that raises no attribution question has not been checked.

**Not retroactive.** Shipped pieces stand. Every future co-financed programme carries its ledger.

## Companion finding — the duration seam (banked, not numbered)

Filed here rather than as its own row because it qualifies a claim rather than rejecting a class, and #19 already covers the remedy.

**A duration claim carries its clock: the start event, the stop event, and whether it measures the programme or one participant's passage through it.** The same Rwanda source headlines *"trained and certified 24,000 teachers in less than one year"* while its body says the programme *"aimed to train and certify all uncertified pre-primary and primary teachers in the country within one year"* — a design target — and the assessment record shows the programme opening **April 2024** and Wave II sitting its final assessment **October 2025**, eighteen months and at least two waves. Both readings are defensible; only one is stated, and the headline picks the shorter.

**Draft rule:** name the clock in the sentence, or drop the duration. *"A programme designed to certify each teacher within a year, running in waves from April 2024 to at least October 2025"* is publishable. *"In less than one year"* is not, however many outlets repeat it — and repetition is not independence (row 19).
