# Ruling #149 — obligatory morphology makes the COMPOSER a detector for exactly the property a string-matcher cannot see; it AMENDS #117 clause 4

**Filed 2026-11-17** by the content function, earned while composing cell 4 of
`2026-11-12-england-ofsted-exempt-cohort` — not from a queue head.
**Family:** non-numeric — **TWENTY → TWENTY-ONE.** **Amends #117 clause 4.**

---

## What #117 clause 4 says, and the half of it that is wrong

#117 clause 4: *a gate for this class asserts the EN half and **prints** the AR half, because Arabic
marks the future with a bound prefix `سـ` homographic with the first letter of ordinary nouns (سنوات,
ستة, سيراليون) and a string match cannot find it — **a check reporting 0 for Arabic is reporting its own
blindness.***

Every word of that is true **of a matcher**, and it was confirmed again today: a regex for `س`-initial
tokens over this composition's Arabic body returned **four** hits, and all four were noise —
`ستثنى`, `ستثناة`, `ستثناتين` are fragments of *المُستثنى* (exempt), and `سياسة` is *policy*. Zero
signal, four false positives, on a body that **did** contain a future.

**And it is the opposite of true of a composer.** Arabic's futurity is *morphological and obligatory at
the point of writing*: to set the verb at all, the composer must decide whether `سـ` is attached. That
decision cannot be deferred, cannot be inherited from the English, and cannot be smoothed — it is a
prefix or it is not. So the act of composing in Arabic **forces the author to classify the sentence**
that the matcher cannot classify afterwards.

> **Where a language marks a property obligatorily in its morphology, its COMPOSER is a detector for
> that property — and precisely where the morphology makes the property invisible to string matching, it
> makes it unavoidable to the person writing. The blindness of the instrument and the acuity of the
> composer have the same cause.**

## What it found

The English body carries: *«So the 2021/22 re-measurement of the exempt cohort is the only one that will
ever exist on that scale.»* The EN pair's hand-off line 6 reads **`editorial futures: 0`**, and the
Editor approved the piece on all five tests with that line on file. It is not zero. That sentence is
**our own claim about a future state of the world**, and no register makes it: the regulator says only
that the new grades *«can't be compared to the old ones»*, which is a statement about comparability, not
a promise that nobody will ever re-measure on the withdrawn scale.

Nothing caught it in English because **English futurity is carried by an auxiliary that reads as ordinary
prose** — *will ever exist* sits in a sentence among dated facts and looks like one. The Arabic desk had
to write the verb, wrote **ستوجد**, and the prefix was the tell. Repaired per #117 clause 1 by attaching
the register and dropping the commitment: the Arabic now reads that the guidance *published 1 December
2025 and updated 2 November 2026* is the warrant for saying the 2021/22 measurement **has no
counterpart** on that scale — **لا يقابلها**, the bare imperfect, unmarked for tense and still true
tomorrow. The English sentence is raised to the Editor as one disposition.

## Why this is a third thing and not a restatement

The register already holds two findings of this shape and this is the third position on one line:

- **#86** — *which questions can only ONE edition answer?* Those answers have a single channel and read
  as though they had two. (The Arabic edition gendered 24 of 43 named humans where English gendered 5.)
- **#101** — *which questions is one edition FORCED to answer?* A forced answer is a read; an optional
  one may be a memory. (Arabic cannot carry `KESSHA` as a name, so the composer went to the register.)
- **#117** — *which questions is one edition forced to answer WRONGLY?* (`rak-iqra`: English futurity is
  obligatory where Arabic's is optional, so the English composer committed to an outcome and the Arabic
  composer did not — and the edition that was not forced is the one still right.)
- **#149, here** — **which questions does one edition's grammar force the composer to NOTICE?** Not to
  answer, and not to answer wrongly: to *see*. The determination is the same in both editions; what
  differs is that one of them cannot be made silently.

**And note it runs the opposite way from #117 on the same grammatical fact.** #117 found Arabic's
optional futurity protecting the Arabic edition from a commitment English was forced into. Today the same
optionality — *because it is a choice the composer must make* — is what exposed a commitment English had
made invisibly. **One property, two directions, depending on whether you are asking an instrument or an
author.**

## The operational consequence, and it is cheap

**The hand-off line for editorial futures (#117 clause 2) is answered by the AR desk, not only by the EN
desk, and where the two answers differ the AR answer is the finding.** This costs nothing: the Arabic
composition already happens, and the composer has already made every one of these determinations by the
time the body exists. What was missing was anybody asking for the by-product.

**The general form, which outlives tense and outlives Arabic:** when a second edition exists, its
*grammar* is an instrument, not just its content. Ask of each language what it will not let a writer
leave undecided — and collect those decisions, because the other edition made them too, silently.
