## The routing rule this piece adds

**The West African labial-velar stop /k͡p/ (written `kp`) is ONE co-articulated consonant, not a cluster — and Arabic, which has neither the sound nor a single letter for it, renders BOTH closures in sequence: `kp` → كب.**

- **Kpakra → كباكرا.** Chief Education Officer Edward Kpakra (MBSSE). The `kp` onset is the voiceless labial-velar plosive found across the Mel and Mande languages of Sierra Leone (and West Africa generally — cf. Kpelle → كبيلي in Africanist Arabic usage). Dropping either closure loses the name: قاكرا invents a uvular that is not there, باكرا deletes the velar half. كباكرا is the established two-letter approximation and the reader recovers the name.
- The sibling contrast that keeps the rule honest (same shape as row 30's `ch`): Sierra Leonean `s`/`k` orthography reads as English habit suggests — **Sackey → ساكي** (Ga origin, heard *SAH-kee*, heard-vowel rule; the terminal `-ey` is /i/, not a diphthong, so no ساكاي).
