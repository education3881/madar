# The self-protecting draft — carry the recon guardrail *in the sentence that carries the risk*
**Date:** 2026-07-11 · **Surfaced by:** the Ed04 India (PARAKH / HPC) verification · **Applies to:** Content Creators (compose), Editor + Manager (verify)

## The observation
Recon briefs end with a **§6 Risks / guardrail notes** block — the list of ways a piece can go wrong (comparability limits, "announced ≠ measured," scope boundaries, press-tier figures, wrong-instrument traps). There are two ways a draft can honour that list:

- **Silently** — the writer reads the guardrail, avoids the error, and the prose simply doesn't make the wrong claim. The guardrail lives only in the recon file.
- **In print** — the writer turns the guardrail into a *sentence in the article*, next to the claim it protects.

The India draft did the second, four times over, and it is why the verification cleared in a single pass:
- comparability → "a figure the report itself sets two points above the 2021 round" (attributes the one comparison to the report; makes no independent trend claim);
- cadence → "expected on a roughly three-year cadence… though no date has been announced";
- scope → "the HPC replaces the school's report card, not the board examination";
- press-tier figure → "By one press count from April 2025… a figure worth carrying as reported rather than audited."

## The rule
**When recon flags a risk on a claim, write the guardrail into the sentence that makes the claim — do not just avoid the error silently.** The most likely misreading of your own piece should be pre-empted *in the prose the reader sees*, not only in the file the reader never sees.

Why this is a doctrine and not a stylistic preference:
1. **It protects the reader, not just the process.** A silent guardrail protects Madār from making the error; an in-print guardrail also stops the *reader* from drawing it. On a measurement-question edition — where the entire subject is people over-reading a number — a caveat the reader can see *is the article's thesis enacted*.
2. **It collapses verification to one pass.** A guardrail visible in the prose is a guardrail the Editor/verifier can tick by reading the sentence. A silent one forces the verifier to prove a *negative* ("did the writer avoid the trap they couldn't see me check?"). India's four in-print guardrails each mapped to one recon §6 line and one verdict check — no inference required. This directly eases the sprint's binding constraint (verification capacity).
3. **It ages well.** If the piece is re-read after the source landscape shifts, the in-print caveat still fences the claim; a silent avoidance leaves a bare number that a later editor may "tidy up" into an over-claim.

## How to apply
- **Content Creator (compose):** for each recon §6 item touching a claim you keep, write the discriminator into that claim's sentence. Prefer the source's own frame ("the report itself sets…", "by one press count…", "expected, not announced"). Don't quarantine caveats into a single hedging paragraph — attach each to its claim.
- **Editor / Manager (verify):** for each recon §6 guardrail, look for it **in the article prose**, not only in the writer's private notes. A guardrail that exists only in recon is a latent defect: the piece is one careless later edit from the error. Verdict checklist line: *recon §6 guardrails carried in-print: ☐ each risk-bearing claim self-fences.*

## Relation to existing rulings
This **generalises ruling #4** ("a program name is not a citation — state the discriminator in print next to the number") from the figures-with-scope case to the *whole class* of recon guardrails (comparability, cadence-status, scope boundary, source-tier, wrong-instrument). #4 said pin the scope to the number; this says: **pin every recon-flagged risk to the sentence it endangers, in print.** It also pairs with `2026-06-19-the-wrong-instrument-figure.md` ("say so in print, deny it to both sides") — same instinct, promoted to a compose-time default for every guardrail, not only the wrong-instrument one.
