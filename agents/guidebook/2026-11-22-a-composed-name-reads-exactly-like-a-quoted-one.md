# Ruling #155 — a composed name reads exactly like a quoted one, and nothing in the served bytes distinguishes them

**Filed:** 2026-11-22 (weekly review consolidation, as the 2026-11-15 section recorded it owed).
**Family:** non-numeric (named 2026-09-13) — **twenty-fourth member**, and the first whose subject is the
**evidential status** of a string rather than anything the string says.
**Earned:** 2026-11-18, by the Arabic Editor's gate on `2026-11-12-england-ofsted-exempt-cohort`, in full
at `content-drafts/verdicts/2026-11-18-england-ofsted-exempt-cohort-arabic-gate.md` §2.
**Recorded as owed, not rushed:** the 11-15 consolidation wrote it into its own section rather than into a
review HTML, and declined to file it on a short envelope — *a ruling written in the last ten minutes of an
envelope is a ruling nobody re-reads* (#96's reasoning applied to prose). This is that filing.

## The statement

**WHERE THIS PUBLICATION COMPOSES A PROPER NAME ITS OWNER HAS NEVER PUBLISHED, THE COMPOSITION IS SERVED
IN THE SAME CHARACTERS A QUOTATION WOULD BE, SO A READER CANNOT TELL WHICH IT IS AND NO INSTRUMENT OF OURS
CAN EITHER.** A composed name carries no mark, no hedge and no register, and its evidential status is
therefore a fact about the piece's history rather than about its bytes — which is to say it is not served
at all.

## How it was found

Ofsted publishes nothing in Arabic. The Arabic edition of the England piece therefore prints **eleven
grade names that are this publication's own compositions** — there is no Arabic register to read them out
of, and #101 clause 1 (*either the annotation carries the full name as the register prints it, or the body
keeps the acronym*) has no register to point at.

**Two of the eleven are already in our served Arabic corpus, with the opposite status.** `غير ملائم` and
`جيد` both appear on `2026-10-23-bahrain-bqa-five-cycles`, where they are **the BQA's own published
Arabic grade names**, read out of that regulator's Arabic CSV in its served bytes on 2026-11-05 and cited
as such. So one string now stands in this corpus twice: **a quotation on one piece and a composition on
another, identical to the byte, and only one of the two is challengeable against anything.**

## And the population is wider than the finding that produced it, in a way that sharpens the ruling

Measured today across the served Arabic corpus rather than inferred from the verdict: `غير ملائم` on **two**
pieces, `جيد` on **five** — and **the string match cannot tell a grade name from the ordinary adjective
*good***, which is what it is on at least some of those five. That is not a defect in the measurement; it
is the ruling restating itself one level out. A composed name is indistinguishable in the served bytes
from a quoted one **and** from ordinary prose, so the class has no textual boundary at all — #113 exactly
(*a name's boundary is not a textual property in general; it is one only where the name carries an
acronym*), and here the name carries none.

## Why it is worse than #101 rather than an instance of it

#101: *an acronym is a name with its evidence removed.* An acronym at least **announces** that something
has been removed — a reader sees `KESSHA` and knows an expansion exists somewhere. **A composed grade name
announces nothing.** It reads as a quotation, which is the one thing it is not, so the defect arrives
wearing the costume of its own remedy. #101's repair route was *re-open the register*; here there is no
register to re-open, because the composition is the first time those characters have existed in that
language.

Three prior rulings each supply one limb and none of them reaches this:

- **#101** — the expansion and its evidence ship together or not at all. Silent where the owner publishes nothing.
- **#114** — the annotation block is **served**, and a figure printed only there is a published figure. Same for a name.
- **#153** — a quoted span promises that a named party served those exact code points. **This is #153's complement**: #153 polices a promise that *was* made; this one concerns a promise a reader *infers* from a form that makes no promise at all.
- **#86 clause 2** — where no register carries a determination, the construction cannot be declined, so the determination is written down **as this edition's own, with its date**. That is the repair, and it already exists.

## The repair, which is binding on the piece already and is not yet general

`_STANDING_LEDGER.md` cell 8's **condition A** required the eleven grade renderings to be declared in the
**served Arabic annotation** — not in the hand-off, which no reader receives (#114's whole point, applied
to provenance instead of to figures). That discharged on 2026-11-21 and the piece shipped with it.

**What is not settled, and is this ruling's forward surface rather than its clause:** *when does this
operation's own composition of a proper name need a provenance marker in the served bytes, and which
instrument could ever hold it?* An annotation sentence is a marker a human reads and no gate can parse; a
machine-readable marker is a schema change; and a corpus-wide rule would have to distinguish a composed
name from an ordinary adjective, which the measurement above shows cannot be done from the text. **So this
ruling binds the hand-off and the annotation and asserts nothing**, per #93: a gate here would either red
every Arabic piece that uses the word *good* or pass the defect, and there is no floor between those.

## Binding clauses

1. **Where an owner publishes no register in a language this publication composes in, every proper name
   that edition prints in that language is a COMPOSITION and is declared as one in the served annotation**,
   with its date and the register the underlying thing was read in — #86 clause 2's form, extended from a
   grammatical determination to a name.
2. **The hand-off to the Arabic Editor carries `composed proper names: N, each declared in the served
   annotation: ☐`**, and a hand-off missing it is returned unread — the same teeth as the dek/title cap
   (2026-07-27), `annotation == sentence` (2026-09-13), the named-humans gender line (#86) and the
   acronym line (#101).
3. **Where one string stands in this corpus as both a quotation and a composition, the quotation's piece
   is cited by the composition's annotation** — a reader who can reach the one register that does publish
   those characters is one link from the evidence, and nothing else in our bytes can give them that.
4. **A composed name is never "corrected" against a later-found register without re-reading the piece that
   composed it** (#41 — supersede, don't resurrect): the composition was true as an act on its date, and a
   register found afterwards makes the next piece's name, not this one's history.
