# Ruling #23 — an approximation has a direction

**Date:** 2026-07-17 · **Verifier, ratified by Editor** · figure-discipline series (#12 → #12b → #14 → #21 → #22 → #23)

## The defect

The Australia NAPLAN draft carried the 2024 testing volumes as "some 4.4 million tests sat by **more than** 1.3 million students." The 2024 register (ACARA release, 14 Aug 2024) says "**almost** 1.3 million students." It is the **2025** release that says "a record **4.5** million tests… by **over** 1.3 million students." The volume was 2024's; the direction word was 2025's. Two adjacent years, the hedge flipped between them — because the cohort crossed the 1.3-million line between the two cycles — and the flip travelled into the draft.

## The rule

**The direction word is part of the figure.** *Almost*, *over*, *more than*, *nearly*, *about* — a hedge is not filler; it encodes which side of the round number the measurement sits on, and that side can change between vintages of the same series. A draft may not inherit a hedge from one year and a value from another.

**The verification move:** when a figure carries a hedge, trace the *hedge* to the same sentence of the same register that gave the value — not just the value. If the hedge and the value come back from different documents, that is the finding.

## Why it matters here

Round-number boundaries are exactly where series cross (1.3m students, 90% participation, "highest since…"). A system that publishes annual registers will flip its own hedges routinely; a publication that quotes those registers across years will import stale directions unless the hedge is traced as cargo. Same family as #21 (vintage) and #22 (scope): the figure travels with axes — administration, coverage, funnel stage, vintage, scope, and now **direction** — and each axis is checked at its own word in the sentence.
