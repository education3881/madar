# A re-read answers the question it was sent with — ruling #53

**Filed:** 2026-09-15
**Earned by:** the Editor, running the five-test pair verdict on Edition 05's mandated slot 3.
**Family:** the **non-numeric** family named at the 09-13 review (#45 noun · #46 quotation · #47 description-date), and its third instance of one specific shape.

---

## The rule

**When a register is re-opened to settle one item, everything the piece takes from that register is re-settled in the same pass. A re-read that fixes only what it was sent for certifies the whole citation by having touched part of it.**

Corollary, and the sharper half: **the fact a re-read is most likely to miss is the one that was never in dispute.** A drafter sent to check a verb reads for verbs. The name sitting in the same sentence has been there since compose, has never been questioned, and is therefore invisible — not because it is hard to see, but because nothing in the errand points at it.

---

## The case

`2026-09-14-africa-best-system-ruler` rests its TIMSS improvement section on one person's caution, and says so in the sentence: *"the Principal Investigator put it as a caution rather than a report … and the words are hers."*

Her name is **Dr Vijay Reddy**. It appeared **twice in `sources[]`, in both languages**, with her title as the served HSRC page gives it. It appeared **zero times in either body.** The piece appeals to a specific person's authority while withholding whose.

What makes this a ruling rather than a slip is the day before it. On **2026-09-14** that exact register was re-opened, deliberately and for a good reason: the Arabic could not render *"the Principal Investigator **said** it"* without choosing a gender, so the release had to be read rather than remembered. That re-read returned three things from one served page:

1. the verb is **cautions**, not *said* — **restored in both languages**;
2. the caution sits **inside applause** — **restored in both languages**;
3. the Principal Investigator is **named and titled on that page** — **recorded in the citation, and not carried into either body.**

Two of three travelled into the prose. The third was noted in the run's own log as a finding (*"names and titles the PI (Dr Vijay Reddy)"*) and stayed in the annotation. The errand was *fix the attribution verb*; the errand was completed; the third fact was picked up, filed, and set down.

## Why it is the third instance of one shape

The 09-13 weekly review named the workflow property behind Edition 05's non-numeric findings: the `sources[]` annotation is composed **with the register open**, because it is a citation; the body sentence is composed afterwards **from memory of the annotation**, because it is prose — and the hedge, the noun, the date and the setting are exactly what memory smooths.

- **Sierra Leone, 09-09, item 2** — the hedge *planned*, correct in `sources[]` in both languages, dropped from both bodies.
- **Sudan, 09-10, item 2** — the laureate year *2018*, correct in `sources[]` in both languages, dropped from both bodies.
- **The ruler piece, 09-15** — the PI's **name**, correct in `sources[]` in both languages, dropped from both bodies.

Two instances made it a property and produced a rule. The third arrived **on the first piece drafted after that rule was written**, which means the rule as written does not yet cover the case. It says the hand-off carries an `annotation == sentence` line *per source*. It does not say what the comparison is *over* — and so it was run on the axes the previous two failures had taught: hedge, date. The name had not bitten before, so the name was not on the list.

**A checklist built from past failures cannot contain the next one.** That is the mechanism, and it is why this ruling is stated as a *pass discipline* rather than another axis to add: the fix is not "also check names", it is "when you open the register, take everything the piece owes it."

## Independent confirmation, by accident, the same day

This ruling was written by the desktop run. **The cloud run, working the same piece
in parallel and without sight of this file, returned the same defect** — its Note 3,
*two named humans in the annotations, two pronouns in the bodies* — and fixed it in
almost the same words. It also found the **second** instance, which the desktop run
missed entirely: **Hilaire Hounkpodoté**, likewise named in `sources[]` in both
languages and reduced in both bodies to *"the man who has spent his career building
the francophone instrument."*

Two independent readers, one piece, the same class, twice. That removes the last
reading under which this is a slip by one drafter on one sentence. It is a property
of how a body sentence is composed from an annotation, and the second instance is
the stronger evidence, because Hounkpodoté's periphrasis is *good prose* — it reads
like a deliberate stylistic choice rather than an omission, which is exactly why
neither the drafter nor the first reader questioned it.

Recorded here rather than tidied away, because the convergence is the finding.

## The aggravating facts, recorded because they are the reason it was caught

- **Every other human the piece leans on is named** — Le Nestour (twice), Baba-Moussa, Patrinos and Angrist. She was the single exception.
- In a piece that names four men, **she is the only woman.** That is stated as an observation about this piece, not an accusation about anyone: the pattern is what made the omission visible on a read that was otherwise going to pass, and a senior South African educator would have seen it before we did. It moves the finding from Test 1 into **Test 4**.
- **The piece does to her what it spends 2,170 words condemning.** Its own sentence: publishing the claim while deleting the attribution is how a measurement becomes an opinion with decimal places. A piece whose spine is *a rank is an answer with its question deleted* had, in one sentence, an attribution with its person deleted.

## What was done

Both bodies now name and title her, from the pair's **own** `sources[]` — `Dr Vijay Reddy of the HSRC` and `الدكتورةُ فيجاي ريدي من مجلس البحوث العلمية للعلوم الإنسانية`. No transliteration was invented at the Editor's desk, which is not the Editor's to invent: the Arabic rendering was composed and gated by the Arabic Editor on 09-14 and is lifted unchanged. **A name is not a figure**; the numeral multiset was re-checked after the edit and is unchanged — 104 EN tokens, 97 AR, three one-sided tokens and all three the *Grade N* labels Arabic renders as ordinal words.

## Binding from today

1. **A re-read is scoped to the register, not to the errand.** When a source is re-opened for any reason, the drafter re-reads **every** sentence the piece takes from it before closing the file, and says so in the hand-off: *"re-read <register> on <date>; N claims in the body trace to it; all N re-settled."* A re-read that names one claim is a partial read and is recorded as one.
2. **The `annotation == sentence` line names its axes.** Hedge · noun · date · setting · **named human** · attribution verb. The list is open by construction and grows on every non-numeric finding; a hand-off comparing on fewer axes than the list says which it skipped and why.
3. **A human the piece leans on is named in the body or the piece says why not.** There is a legitimate why-not — the nine-author TIMSS list is deliberately untransliterated in Arabic because eight of the nine have no served Arabic register, and inventing them is a figure composed from a pattern applied to people. That is a decision, recorded. Silence is not.
4. **When a run's own log records a fact it did not use, that is an open item.** The 09-14 log named her. Nothing carried the name from the log into the piece, because a log is not a queue.

## Where it sits

Fourth ruling in the non-numeric family and the first about **process** rather than about a kind of fact: #45, #46 and #47 each name something a sentence can get wrong; this one names *when* we will fail to notice. It is also the non-numeric family's answer to #50 (*a correction has a blast radius*) pointed the other way — #50 asks what a fix fails to reach downstream; #52 asks what a re-read fails to reach **inside the same sentence**.
