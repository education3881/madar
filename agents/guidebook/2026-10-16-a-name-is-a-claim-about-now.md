# Ruling #102 — A name is a claim about *now*, and the present tense re-asserts it every day the page is served

**Filed:** 2026-10-16 (daily run, content function — executing standing-queue item 17's open job)
**Family:** the **non-numeric** family (named 2026-09-13). **Twelfth member, and the first whose unit is a
name's *date*.**
**Kin:** #101 (an acronym is a name with its evidence removed — this is its successor and was found by
its instrument), #86 (which questions only one edition can answer), #47 (description-date), #62 (a date
that matches is not a date that agrees), #42/#78 (parity is a floor), #100 (a dead citation is the only
event that re-opens a register).

---

## The instance

`2026-06-20-oman-school-performance-ratings`, both editions, names the Omani inspectorate **in the present
tense**:

> The verdict comes from the National System for Evaluating School Performance, **run by the Oman
> Authority for Academic Accreditation and Quality Assurance of Education** — an external inspectorate
> built on the Sultanate's 2023 School Education Law…

That has not been the body's name since **12 January 2026**. **Royal Decree 12/2026** (22 Rajab 1447;
Official Gazette 1630a, 13 January 2026) renames it, in Article I, the **Oman Authority for Quality
Assurance of Education**, and in Articles III and IV moves professional licences and the national and
international studies to it from the Ministry of Education, with those divisions' officers.

The piece published **2026-06-20 — five months after the decree** — and served the retired name in two
languages for **118 days**.

## Nothing we own could have caught it, and the enumeration is short

* **No number.** The numeric toolkit was never in play; there is no figure in a name.
* **`qa_pair_frontmatter` passed, correctly.** Both editions carried the *same* name, so parity had
  nothing to compare against. **Parity is a floor, never a verdict** (#42, #78, #86).
* **`qa_sources_alive` passed, correctly.** Both URLs answer 200. *Alive* is not *accurate*, which is
  standing-queue item 18's subject reached from a third side.
* **The `annotation == sentence` rule (09-13) could not reach it either, and for a NEW reason.** #101's
  defect was the annotation's **silence** — an acronym with no expansion to compare. Here the annotation
  was not silent: it carried the **current** name while the body carried the **former** one. The check
  that would have caught that is a human discipline executed at hand-off, and this piece shipped three
  months before the discipline existed. *A rule written in September does not inspect June.*

**What did catch it is #101's own clause 3, run forward instead of backward.** Where two editions render
one institution, the edition that cannot carry the Latin acronym is the control. Arabic cannot, so the
Arabic annotations were read first — and they carry **both** names, the long one in two Times of Oman
citations and the short one in the gov.om and oaaaqa.gov.om entries. **The piece's own two editions
disagreed with each other in their annotations**, and that disagreement is what pointed at the register.

## Limb one — the recon is dated, the publication is dated, and the reading is neither

A name read correctly at recon can be false at publication and falser every day after. This one was
*right* when the system launched (December 2024), *right* in the Times of Oman coverage of the first
results (January 2026 — that headline still uses the old name), and wrong by the time the piece was
composed. **The present tense is not a tense of the draft. It is a tense of the read**, and a served page
re-asserts it every morning to every reader, indefinitely, with no mechanism that ever revisits it.

**Binding:** where a body names an institution **in the present tense**, that name is re-read against the
register **at the last gate before the flag moves**, not at recon — and the annotation records **the date
of that reading**, exactly as #41 requires of a fetched URL. A name carried from recon to the gate without
a second reading is a figure carried from a search summary: the same defect, with letters instead of
digits.

**And the correct repair is not a substitution.** The old name is not wrong about the past; it is wrong
about now. The body now uses the current name in the present tense, states the former name with the date
it stopped being the name, and says why the launch and the first evaluation phase keep the older name —
*because that is the name their own registers print* (#101 clause 4).

## Limb two — an annotation's parenthetical is a sentence, and this one was right by accident

Both editions carried this citation:

> `Gov.om — Oman Authority for Quality Assurance of Education (the issuing body and its mandate; renamed
> in 2026)`

**That page says nothing about a rename.** Read in served text on 2026-10-16: it prints the short name as
its title and the long, former name in its management listing, gives no date, names no instrument, and
never says the name changed. The annotation made a **dated factual claim** — *renamed in 2026* — that was
**true and entirely unsupported by the document it was attached to.**

This is the sharper half, because of where it sits. **Every instrument this operation owns measures the
body against the annotation.** `qa_sources_alive` asks whether the URL answers; item 18 asks whether the
bytes are still the document we cited; the 09-13 rule asks whether the body matches the annotation. All
three take **the annotation's own prose as given**. The annotation is the ruler, and nothing has ever
measured the ruler.

**Binding:** a parenthetical that states a **fact** — a date, a rename, a figure, a named human, a hedge
— is held to the standard of a body sentence. It is supported by the document at the URL it is attached
to, or it moves to a source that carries it. A parenthetical that merely *describes* the document (what
it is, who issued it, what it is cited for) carries no such burden; the test is whether removing the
document would make the clause unverifiable.

**Corollary, and it is the practical part.** *The register of record for a rename is the instrument that
performs it, not the renamed body's own page.* An organisation's website shows the **result** of a rename
and frequently shows **both names at once** — a new title over an old masthead, a current name in the
header and a former one in a staff listing — without ever stating the event, its date or its authority. A
rename is a legal act: it lives in a decree, a statute, a gazette. Both editions now cite Royal Decree
12/2026 in their own language, with its number, both dates, the Gazette issue and the two articles it is
cited for.

## What this does and does not generalise

It does **not** say every proper noun needs a second reading. The class is narrow and nameable:
**institutions, programmes and offices named in the present tense**, which are exactly the nouns a state
can change by decree between our recon and our reader. A named human's name does not change by decree; a
statute's title does not change after it is promulgated; a figure has #41 and the eight axes already.

The forward question it leaves is already on the standing queue as **item 19** and is the one this ruling
cannot answer by itself: **which of our annotations assert something their own URL does not support?**
Population: **682 citations, 342 distinct URLs**, a large share carrying a parenthetical that states a
fact. One was read today and it was unsupported. That is a sample of one, and it is the only sample
anyone has ever taken.
