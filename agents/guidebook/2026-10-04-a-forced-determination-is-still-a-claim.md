## The ruling

> **Where one language forces a determination the other is free to decline, the edition that is forced
> makes a claim alone.** Arabic has no neutral agreement for a role noun: «مدير» and «مديرة», «وزير» and
> «وزيرة», «كبير» and «كبيرة» exhaust the options, and the composer must choose before the sentence will
> close. English has a neutral — *Development Director*, *the minister*, *Senior Advisor* — and takes it
> by default, without anybody deciding anything. **So the Arabic edition of this publication carries, for
> every named human it gives a role to, a determination its English twin never makes.** That
> determination is a claim about a real person. It must be read off the register like any other claim.

> **Corollary 1 — the English edition's silence is not corroboration.** A composer who checks the two
> editions against each other will find no disagreement, because there is nothing on the English side to
> disagree with. The absence of a contradiction in a language that cannot express the claim is not a
> second channel; it is the claim being unchecked in a way that looks exactly like the claim being
> checked twice. This is #84's corollary one turn further: *a reciprocity check has no arrow* — and here
> it has no second side at all.

> **Corollary 2 — the determination is made by the name when it is not made by the register.** Both
> confirmed errors below were produced the same way: the Arabic composer read a gender off how a name
> sounds. *Alex* sounded male and the register says **she**. *Les enseignements maternel et primaire*
> sounded like a woman's portfolio and the register says **le ministre**. A name does not carry a
> person's gender, and inferring one from a name misgenders a real person in the publication's own voice
> in a way the neutral default never does. **Where no register read by this operation attributes a
> pronoun or a gendered title, the Arabic records the determination as its own and says so** — the
> construction cannot be avoided, so the honesty has to be written down beside it.

## What was measured, and what it says

The 10-03 forward question asked the hard version of a real question: *in a composed bilingual pair,
which edition is the control — and how often has the other one been wrong?* It proposed the one class of
fact comparable across scripts without a parser: **every named entity each `sources[]` annotation
attributes a claim to.** All six held pairs were read by hand, both editions, annotation by annotation —
**56 English and 57 Arabic annotations, 684 source promises, 338 of them shared.**

**On institutions and publications the two editions agree.** Every institution, statute, report,
newspaper and programme name in the held set was compared and all of them resolve to the same body in
both languages, including the awkward ones: *Rising Academy Network* in one annotation and *Rising
Academies* in another, each matching its own register rather than each other; `ZICTA` and
`NYAF` and `KOICA` left in Latin on the Arabic side; the National Council for Literacy and Adult
Education carrying its full name in both editions since yesterday's fix. One loose rendering is
recorded and not changed: HSRC is «مجلسُ البحوث العلمية للعلوم الإنسانية», which parses the name
differently than *Human Sciences Research Council* does while naming the same council. **That is a
rendering, not an attribution, and nothing in the piece rests on it.**

**The errors were not in the entities. They were in the grammar attached to them.** The held set names
**43 individual humans**. The Arabic edition attaches a grammatically gendered role or predicate to
**24** of them. The English edition carries the same determination for **5** — *Mr* Kamboni and *Ms*
Phiri, *she* for Dr Vijay Reddy, *his* for Freddy Elbaiady and Mohamed Abdellatif. **So 19 named humans
are gendered in Arabic on the composer's judgment alone**, in a corpus where nothing compares the two
editions and nothing re-opens the register to check.

Ten of the nineteen were taken back to their registers today, in served text:

**Two wrong in ten, three right, five standing on nothing.** That answers the forward question, and it
answers it in the direction nobody expected. Yesterday the Arabic edition was the **right** channel and
caught the English one short by three words. Today the Arabic edition is the **wrong** channel, twice,
and the English edition is silently right both times — not because it was careful, but because English
let it say nothing. **So the answer to "which edition is the control" is: neither. The register is.** The
second language is a free second channel on everything the two editions *can* both say, and a **single**
unchecked channel on everything only one of them can say. The gap between those two facts is where both
of today's defects were living.

**And the family had already walked past this ruling once.** The 2026-09-20 register entry records that
the attribution verb was found on 09-14 *because* "Arabic cannot render *the Principal Investigator said
it* without choosing a gender, so the HSRC release was re-opened." The forced choice was used as a
**route to a finding** and never named as a **source of one**. An instrument that makes you re-open a
register is also an instrument that makes you fill a gap when you do not.

## What this does not say

It does not say the Arabic edition is less reliable. It says the Arabic edition is **more exposed**,
because it is asked more questions per sentence, and that the gates were built as though both editions
answered the same number. `qa_pair_frontmatter` passed on both defects today, on the 09-28 pair verdict's
clean 110/110 multiset, and on four earlier gates per piece, and **it was right to pass every time**:
composed prose is supposed to differ, and the ruling does not propose comparing it. Three days running
now the pair gate has been correctly blind to a real defect — 10-02's #84 (both editions agreeing on one
error), 10-03's National Council (the editions disagreeing), and today's (one edition speaking where the
other cannot) — and the three together bound what parity is for. **Parity is a floor. It was never a
verdict** (#42, #78, #84).

Nor does it propose a tool. The 10-01 attempt to compare annotation numerals across languages returned
**117 false positives out of 338** and was filed as a negative result. A gender-agreement comparator
would be worse, because there is no English token to compare against. **The instrument here is a
hand-off line, not an assertion** — see below.

## Binding, from today

1. **The Arabic composer reads a named human's gender off the register, never off the name.** A pronoun
   in the register, a gendered title in the register, or a first-party Arabic register naming the person
   — one of those three, or the determination is unsourced.
2. **An unsourced determination is written down as unsourced, in the annotation, with its date.** The
   construction cannot be declined, so the honesty goes beside it: *the masculine here is this edition's
   and not the register's, and it is recorded as such until a register is read that carries it.* Applied
   today to Edward Kpakra.
3. **The hand-off to the Arabic Editor carries a `named humans — gender read from register: N of M` line**,
   enumerating each named human and the clause the determination came from. A hand-off missing that line
   is returned unread — the same teeth as the dek/title cap (2026-07-27) and the `annotation == sentence`
   line (2026-09-13), and for the same reason: an unmeasured determination is an open item, exactly like
   an untraced figure.
4. **The five unsourced determinations in the held set are carried to each piece's confirmation read** and
   named in the edition ledger. Three of those pieces have confirmation reads already **closed** — which
   is its own finding, and the honest statement of it is that **a confirmation read closed before a defect
   class existed did not check for it**; the reads are not re-opened wholesale, only on this one class.
5. **The general form, which is the part worth carrying beyond gender and beyond Arabic:** when two
   editions, two formats or two instruments are used to check each other, **first ask which questions only
   one of them is capable of answering.** Those answers have one channel, and they will read as though
   they had two.
