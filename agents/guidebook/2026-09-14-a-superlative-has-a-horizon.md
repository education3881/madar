# A superlative has a horizon — ruling #49

**Filed:** 2026-09-14 · **Manager, from the drafting of Edition 05 slot 3** · Section 1, row 44
**Earned on:** a headline about the very instrument the ruler piece is built on.

---

## The rule

**A superlative — "the best", "the largest", "the last", "the first" — is a figure (#29), and like every figure it carries a scope. Its scope has two parts: the RULER it is measured on, and the HORIZON it is measured within. A superlative missing either one has not yet made a claim, and a superlative that arrives with its horizon in the *next* clause arrives without it.**

Corollaries:

1. **A headline is where the horizon goes missing.** Not through bad faith — through length. The clause that bounds the claim is almost always the one that gets cut, because it is the one that reads like qualification. Treat a superlative found in a title as unscoped until the body is read.
2. **Time-superlatives are the least examined.** "The best system in Africa" is obviously a ranking and gets challenged. "The last international evaluation" sounds like a fact about the world and gets repeated. Both are rankings; the second is ranked on a timeline.
3. **A superlative in a source's headline does not ride even when the source is first-party.** The owner wrote the sentence; the owner's sub-editor wrote the headline; these are two registers (cf. #43, the podium is not the document — this is the same split moved from the podium to the masthead).
4. **The test is one question: bounded how, and by when?** If the register does not answer both, either quote the register's full sentence including its bound, or do not carry the claim.

---

## How it was earned

Edition 05's slot 3 is a piece about rulers: it argues that a crown is meaningless without the instrument that awarded it, and that four defensible instruments crown four different African countries. Its spine rests on PASEC — the continent's widest comparable measurement — and on the fact that **PASEC 2019 is the latest *published* cycle while PASEC 2024's results are announced for the last quarter of 2026**. So the piece's date is part of its argument: a crown handed out in September is handed out knowing the ruler changes in December.

While confirming that date, a search returned a first-party register under the headline:

> **« Le PASEC 2024 sera la dernière évaluation internationale »**
> *("PASEC 2024 will be the last international evaluation")*

— an interview with **Abdel Rahamane Baba-Moussa**, secretary-general of CONFEMEN, which owns PASEC. If true as written, the piece's spine inverts: the ruler is not about to be re-read, it is about to be **retired**, and an argument about what happens when instruments change becomes an argument about what happens when they stop.

It is not true as written. Read in the body, the sentence continues:

> *"L'important de cette date de 2024, c'est que le PASEC 2024 sera la dernière évaluation internationale. **Avant 2030 qui est le point final de l'ODD4**, toutes les données que nous devons avoir en 2024 seront mobilisées pour faire le bilan en 2030."*

The horizon — **before 2030, the end point of SDG 4** — is in the following sentence, and it changes the claim entirely: PASEC 2024 is the last cycle whose results land in time for the 2030 stocktake. It is not the last cycle. The February 2026 workshop register, read on 2026-09-04, names a **PASEC 2028** cycle as the next step; the interview itself describes a programme running four-yearly since 1991.

Two further scopes the headline also drops, both recovered from the same body: the interview is dated **January 2023**, not 2026 — a plan register, three years before the piece that would have cited it — and the count it gives (23 participating countries, including two non-members, anglophone and lusophone as well as francophone) belongs to PASEC 2024 and to no other cycle.

**Nothing false was published, because the horizon was found before the sentence was written.** It was found for the ordinary reason: the claim was consequential enough that the register was opened rather than quoted from the result list (#41 — a figure is cited as fetched).

## Why it is a new rule and not an instance of #29

Ruling #29 (*a ranking is not a measurement; a superlative is a figure*) established that "best" requires its ruler. It was written against **aggregators** — third parties composing a rank out of measurements. This is the other half, and it bites in a place #29 does not look:

- #29 asks **on what instrument?** This asks **within what bound?**
- #29 was earned on a *secondary* compiling other people's numbers. This was earned on a **first-party owner talking about its own instrument**, where #29's suspicion does not fire at all.
- #29's failure is a composite. This failure is a **truncation** — the claim is the owner's own, correctly stated, and is made false by removing a clause. That is the shape of the non-numeric family (#45–#47): the words around the claim, not the claim.

It therefore sits at the junction of two families already in the register — the **scoped-instrument** family (#19 multiplier without base · row 20 deficit is a ratio · #44 a converted figure has two owners · #48 a trend line has a seam) and the **non-numeric** family named at the 09-13 review. The scoped-instrument family says *the unit is doing work the sentence is not showing*. The non-numeric family says *the failure is in the words, and no numeric check will see it*. A truncated superlative is both at once: **it has no number in it at all, and its unit is a date.**

---

## What changes in the workflow

- **Verifier's trace gains a row:** *does this sentence carry a superlative, and does the register bound it in the same sentence we are quoting?* Sits beside #43's register question and #48's seam question.
- **Drafting rule:** a superlative taken from a source is quoted **with the clause that bounds it**, or it is paraphrased with the bound restored. A superlative in our own voice names its ruler and its window in the same sentence — which slot 3's standing prohibition already required of *"best"* and now requires of *"last"*, *"first"* and *"largest"* too.
- **Recon rule:** when a search result's **headline** carries a superlative that would change a piece's argument, the body is read before the piece is drafted, not before it is verified. This one would have survived to the verdict; it would not have survived to publication. That is the wrong margin to be working on.
