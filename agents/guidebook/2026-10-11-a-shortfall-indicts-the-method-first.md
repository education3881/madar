# A shortfall indicts the method before it indicts the population

**Ruling #95.** Filed 2026-10-11 at the weekly review. **Family: assertion discipline — the
thirty-first member**, and the first whose subject is a *hand* derivation rather than a check.
Discharges the enumeration owed to this review by the 2026-10-04, 2026-10-07, 2026-10-08 and
2026-10-10 runs.

---

## The claim

**When a recovery method returns fewer members than the count it is auditing, the first
hypothesis is the method — not the population.** A method narrower than the document it reads
fails *silently*, and it fails in the one direction that reads as diligence: it produces a
deficit. A deficit looks like honesty. A run that finds one will write it down, decline to
invent the difference, and feel correct doing so — and the number it writes down is wrong.

**Corollary.** *Declining to invent members is right and is not the same as measuring.* The
decline protects the population from the method. It does nothing for the count, and the count
is what the next run reads as state.

## Why four runs measured it short

Two narrow methods, each failing silently.

1. **The §1-label regex was narrower than the labels.** The recovering run reported *"§1's
   `Assertion discipline — ruling #N` labels yield **fourteen**."* The true yield is **24**. The
   labels are not uniform: eleven of them read `Assertion discipline / build determinism`,
   `Assertion discipline / storage vs content`, and six further variants. A pattern anchored on
   the em-dash immediately after the family name cannot see a label that qualifies the family
   before the dash. **The family's own sub-classification was what hid its members.**

2. **The prose reading skipped the bullet's own lists.** The bullet carries **three**
   parenthetical member lists, deposited by three different weeks' consolidations at three
   different depths — the recent adds in the opening clause, `(#73 … #85)` and `(#59 … #70)`
   mid-bullet, and the founding enumeration `(#16 … #70)` at the end. The recovering runs read
   the opening clause and the closing one and missed the two in the middle. **An accreted
   document records its own membership in the order it was accreted, not in one place.**

Both failures are #70 exactly — *a bucket set is an enumeration too, and cannot report a
distinction it cannot hold* — turned on a reader instead of on a check. And both are #57 with
the sign flipped: #57 warns that a count agreeing with itself can disagree with its population
for a month. **This is a population agreeing with itself while the instrument reading it
disagreed, for nine days.**

## The direction is the whole finding

An over-recovering method produces a surplus, and a surplus is loud: it names a member the
population does not have, and the next reader can go look for it. An **under-recovering** method
produces a deficit, and a deficit names nothing. It is unfalsifiable in the small — you cannot
go look for a member that was never named — and it recruits the operation's best instinct,
which is not to invent. **Four runs in a row did the right thing with the wrong number.**

So the discipline is not *be more careful* and it is not *invent nothing*. It is:

- **State the method with the shortfall, at the same resolution.** *"Twenty-four against a count
  of twenty-seven"* is a measurement of the register. *"A regex anchored on `— ruling #` against
  a register that writes `/ build determinism — ruling #`"* is a measurement of the instrument,
  and it is the one that would have been checked.
- **Before reporting a deficit, run a second method that cannot fail the same way.** Here: grep
  the files for a self-declaration. It took one command and would have returned 18 members, all
  inside the 30, from a channel the §1 regex cannot touch.
- **A shortfall against a count whose increments you can trace is evidence about the
  shortfall.** The count's every move since 10-02 was recorded in the filing ruling's own file,
  with the running figure. A number with a legible provenance and a derivation with none
  disagreeing by three is not a tie.
