## 2 — Transliteration ruling: the "lakh" carry (Arabic path)

**Ruling:** *a magnitude word native to the source register keeps that register, glossed once into the reader's system — never silently converted away.*

India reports in **lakh** (1 lakh = 100,000). The measured sitting is "21.15 lakh." In Arabic composition the correct carry is **«٢١٫١٥ لكهًا — ٢٫١ مليون»**: transliterate the native unit (لكه), then gloss to the international magnitude (مليون) once, in-line. Do not drop "lakh" for a bare "2.1 million" (erases the source's own voice and makes the figure un-cross-checkable against the report), and do not leave "lakh" unglossed (an Arabic reader may not hold the 100,000 conversion). Same rule the EN piece follows ("21.15 lakh — 2.1 million"); the AR mirrors it. Generalises to crore (10 million), and to any source-native unit a reader won't automatically convert.

**Named-human transliteration (Arabic gate, this piece):** Prof. Indrani Bhaduri → **البروفيسورة إندراني بهادوري.** Both components are standard, well-attested Arabic-media forms (Indra→إندرا; Bhaduri, Bengali surname→بهادوري) — CONFIRMED-by-convention, not "unheard" (contrast the Australia gate's `Gniel → غنيل` PROVISIONAL, ruling #28b). One named human in the piece; no institutional-name Latin tags dropped (PARAKH/NCERT/HPC/NEP each keep the Latin tag + Arabic gloss per #28a).
