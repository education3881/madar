# A claim inherits the scope of its register — ruling #51

**Filed:** 2026-09-15 · **Editor, from the pair verdict on Edition 05 slot 3** · Section 1, row 46
**Earned on:** two sentences in one piece, and then — in the same run, by accident — on one of our own instruments.

---

## The rule

**Every claim is read somewhere, and where it was read is its boundary. A qualifier belongs to the cell it was printed in; a comparison belongs to the set that was actually opened. Carrying either one wider than its register is a new claim, and nobody made it.**

Corollaries:

1. **A significance mark belongs to its cell.** `***` is printed against a subject, a level and a cycle. *Significant at the end of primary* is not what a footnote on the mathematics column says; it is what a reader adds by taking the column for the table.
2. **A comparison is bounded by the set actually opened.** One country brief read against its own regional averages supports *clear of the region's pattern*. It does not support *the continent's outlier* — that requires the continent, and the continent was never read. The distance between those two sentences is the distance between what was fetched and what was implied.
3. **The wider claim is almost always the more natural sentence,** which is why this is a discipline and not an instinct. "In mathematics at that level" reads like throat-clearing; "at that level" reads like prose. The register's scope is the awkward one every time.
4. **A superlative asserted while a neighbour is deliberately unnamed is the same claim with the evidence withheld.** (#49 from the other side: there the horizon was cut by a sub-editor, here by us.)
5. **And it binds our instruments, not only our sentences.** An assertion's floor is scoped to the region it measures. A region where the defect cannot show up is not a measurement, however green it reads. Before a floor is set, the bite is measured *at that floor* — not merely shown to have changed the artefact (#35 + the 2026-09-14 addendum), but shown to cross the line the check will draw.

## How it was earned

### The two sentences

Slot 3 of Edition 05 argues that a rank published without its ruler is an opinion with decimal places. Its pair verdict returned four notes, and two of them were this rule, in both directions.

**One.** The Niger paragraph carried: *"at the end of primary, reading rose 67.5 and mathematics 56.0, and at that level Niger and Benin were the only two countries whose gains were statistically significant at all."* The recon's own row attaches *the only two significant gains* to **mathematics** (PASEC2019, p. 225). At end-of-primary **reading** the report also records Senegal at **+27.5\*\*\*** — which is where the piece's *fourth* ruler gets its crown four sections later. The sentence did not misread a number. It moved a footnote up one level, and in doing so contradicted the piece's own conclusion.

**Two.** The level section opened: *"On that ruler Mauritius was the continent's outlier."* What had been read was one World Bank country brief, against one comparison country, with the brief's own regional averages printed beside it. Nothing this piece read ranks African countries on the Human Capital Index. *The continent's outlier* is a rank — in the one piece whose whole argument is that ranks without rulers are opinions — and it appeared while the commission's own prohibition kept a neighbouring island state deliberately unnamed.

Neither was visible to any numeric instrument the operation owns. The numeral multiset was balanced in both languages; every figure traced; both sentences would have passed a figure-by-figure verification and did pass an Arabic composition gate that re-opened three registers. What failed was not a number but the **width** of the sentence that carried it.

### The instrument, the same morning

The rule then bit on our own work within the hour, which is why it is filed as a ruling rather than an editorial note.

The day's new standing assertion, `qa_render`, asserts among other things that the design system's kiln-orange actually reaches the rendered pixels — the 2026-09-14 defect class, where an undefined CSS token evaporates into the inherited ink and every structural check stays green. Proving it per #35 meant neutralising the accent token in the served CSS and confirming the check fires. It fired — on the two **article** pages, where the count fell 674 → 0 and 629 → 0.

On the two **home** pages it did not. Whole-page accent barely moved: 949 → 900, 849 → 817. The hero still carries its own kiln-orange stroke as an SVG literal, so it does not read the token, and it sat inside the region the check measured. A floor set there would have passed forever with the accent entirely dead.

That is corollary 2 with the words changed: the measurement was wider than the thing being measured, so it found the right colour for the wrong reason. The fix was to scope P3 per target — the chrome band on the home pages (49 → 0 and 32 → 0 under the same bite), the whole page on the article pages, where the chrome band carries no accent at all. Each scope is now the one that was *proved to be able to fail*.

## Why it needed its own number

Three rulings already sit next to this one and none of them covers it:

- **#45** (a number's noun) governs what a figure *is*. Both of today's sentences had the right noun.
- **#48** (a trend line can have a seam) governs a span that crosses a join. Neither sentence crossed one.
- **#49** (a superlative has a horizon) governs a bound the *source* dropped. Today the source printed its bound correctly on its own page, in both cases; we widened it afterwards.

The family is the non-numeric one named on 2026-09-13 — *whether the words around the number are true* — and this is its fifth member and the first whose error is a **quantifier of scope** rather than a noun, a date, a verb or a hedge. It is also the first member to bind an instrument and a sentence with the same words, which is the test of whether a rule is about writing or about care.

---

## What changes, operationally

1. **The Verifier's trace gains a ninth axis: `scope`.** Per load-bearing claim: *which cell, which set, which window was this read in, and is the sentence exactly that wide?* Failed like an untraced figure.
2. **A comparative claim names its comparison set in the hand-off.** "Best / largest / outlier / only" is returned unread unless the drafter states, per claim, what was opened to support it.
3. **Every new assertion records, beside its floor, the measured value of its own bite at that floor** — per target, not per check. A check with a target it cannot fail on lists that target with the reason, or drops it.
