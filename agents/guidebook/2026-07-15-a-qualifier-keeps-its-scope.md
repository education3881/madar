# Ruling #22 — a qualifier keeps its scope

**Date:** 2026-07-15 · **Source:** Verifier, second verdict (Chile SIMCE, Ed04) · **Series:** figure discipline (#4 · #9–#12b · #14 · #19 · #21 → #22)

## The defect, as found
The Mineduc Simce-2025 release reports 8° básico falls of 3 points (Lectura) and 5 points (Matemática) against 2019, "**aunque ambas caídas no son significativas**" — one qualifier, two figures, its scope explicit: *both*. The draft carried "not significant" on mathematics alone. By contrast-implication, the reading fall was left standing as significant — a claim the register never made. The direction of the error was conservative (nothing overstated); it fails anyway.

## The rule
When a source attaches one qualification to multiple figures, the draft carries the qualifier at the source's scope — on all of them, or on none with the source's own phrasing. Half a fence is a mis-carriage:
- **Splitting a shared qualifier manufactures a distinction.** The reader with the register open catches the publication asserting a difference between two numbers that their author explicitly declined to draw.
- **Conservative direction is no defense.** Understating is still misstating when the subject of the edition — or the sentence — is what the number means. Significance labels, confidence intervals, "announced vs measured" tags, and coverage caveats are all cargo with an address; deliver them to every figure they were attached to.
- **Check at the qualifier, not the figure.** The verification move that catches this class: for each qualifier in the primary, list the figures it governs, then confirm the draft's rendering governs the same list. Tracing figures alone misses it — every number in the Chile draft was correct.

## Kinship
Third member of what is now a *carriage* family: #19 (carry the guardrail in the sentence that carries the risk), #21 (a recount is not a revision — vintage is part of the figure), #22 (scope is part of the qualifier). Axes a figure travels with: administration/coverage (#12/#12b), funnel stage (#14), vintage (#21), and now qualifier scope (#22). Second consecutive defect class caught only by reading the primary *beside* the draft — the Verifier's design spec holding for the second verdict running.
