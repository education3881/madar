# Ruling #46 — A quotation is verbatim or it is paraphrase

**Filed:** 2026-09-09 · Verifier (under the Editor) · Section 1, row 41
**Produced by:** the Sierra Leone verification verdict
(`content-drafts/verdicts/2026-09-09-sierra-leone-sleic-outcomes-verification.md`)
**Family:** the register family (#38 · #40 · #42 · #43 · #45) — *which register speaks, and
in whose words*

---

## The rule

**Quotation marks are a promise about the register, not about the meaning.** They say
*these are the words on the page.* Therefore:

> Anything inside quotation marks is what the source served, character for character. An
> emendation — even one that corrects the source, even one that is obviously right — must
> be marked, or the quotation's boundary must be moved so the disputed word falls outside
> it. There is no third option, and "it was clearly a typo" is not one of them.

The reason is not pedantry about typography. It is that **an unmarked emendation is
indistinguishable, after the fact, from the source having changed underneath us** — and
the operation cannot tell the two apart on its own record.

## What bit

Edition 05's Sierra Leone piece carried, in quotation marks:

> the caution that the programme **"does not definitively prove"** outcomes-based
> financing is a better way to fund education

The Education Outcomes Fund's page, re-fetched as served on 09-09, says:

> SLEIC does not **definitely** prove that outcomes-based financing is a better way to
> fund education.

*Definitely* → *definitively*. It is almost certainly a correction: the same page also
serves "what is takes to help children learn," so the register is typo-prone and the
intended word is obvious. **That is what makes it an item and not a shrug.** The drafter
made a judgement about what the source *meant* and printed it as what the source *said*.

## The corollary that is the real lesson

**Capture the quotation verbatim at compose, or you cannot defend it later.**

The 08-28 compose-time read recorded this clause only in its already-emended form, inside
the `sources[]` title. So on 09-09 the operation could not establish which of these
happened:

- the drafter silently normalised the word; or
- EOF has edited the page in the eleven days since.

Both are ordinary events. Only one is our defect — and with no verbatim capture, the
evidence to tell them apart does not exist. #27 says a clean figure needs two independent
routes; this says **a clean quotation needs one route recorded exactly**, because the
object being checked is an orthography and a second reading of a changed page tells you
nothing about the first.

## Second corollary — a plan is only a plan until its date arrives

Filed with #46 because the same verdict produced it and it is the same shape: a claim that
was true when read and is not true when printed.

The same piece rendered a **January 2026** register's *"planned launch … in March"* as
*"In March, the same partnership takes the architecture earlier in the system"* — in a
piece dated **28 August 2026**. Two things went wrong at once, and neither is a sourcing
failure:

1. **The hedge was dropped from the prose only.** #43 already says *a schedule is a plan*,
   and the recon had carried the word *planned* in quotation marks. So did the piece's own
   `sources[]`, in **both** languages. **The bibliography was more careful than the body.**
   No register drifted, no research failed, and the fence still did not reach the reader.
2. **Nobody looked at the calendar.** March 2026 was five months in the past at compose.
   A plan whose date has passed is no longer a plan; it is an event or a slippage, and
   which one is a question with a served answer. Here it had one: the successor programme
   carries its own page, status **Running**, with the owner's own launch register dated
   **24 April 2026** — so per #41 the January plan is superseded by the living record.

**The rule:** *every dated forward-looking claim is re-read against the calendar at
compose, not only against its source.* A source is checked for what it says; a **date** is
checked for whether it has happened.

## What this adds to the trace

The Verifier's trace already carries eight axes (#45 made *type* the eighth). This adds
two checks that are not axes on a figure at all, because neither of the two failures it
was born from is a number:

- **Quotation:** does every character inside the marks appear inside the source's own
  marks? Recorded as a captured string at compose, not as a memory.
- **Clock:** is any forward-looking date in this piece already in the past on the day we
  publish? If so, the piece owes a read of what happened.

Both are cheap. Both would have caught their defect in under a minute. Neither had ever
been asked, on the edition's most figure-dense piece, which passed every numeric test we
own.

## Where it has been applied

- **Sierra Leone (Ed05 row 2), 2026-09-09** — both items, fixed in-run in the affected
  languages; the successor register added as `sources[]` entry 8 in both files, so a new
  claim arrived with its own register rather than on the strength of an old one.
- **A note for the Arabic Editor:** Item 1 was **English-only by the nature of the
  languages**. The Arabic renders the caution «لا يُثبِت إثباتًا قاطعًا», and both English
  readings collapse to that one adverb — so an orthographic emendation has no Arabic twin,
  and the pair stayed even without an Arabic change. First asymmetry in the edition that is
  a property of the languages rather than an error on one side (#42's mirror image).
