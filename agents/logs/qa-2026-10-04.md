## §1 — The 10-03 forward question, executed before anything new was added. **It bit, and it bit backwards.**

The question was the hard one and it asked for a measurement, not a tool: *in a composed bilingual pair,
which edition is the control — and how often has the other one been wrong?* Method prescribed: take the
six held pairs and compare by hand the one class of fact comparable across scripts without a parser —
**every named entity each `sources[]` annotation attributes a claim to.** The cheap instrument was
already known not to work (10-01: annotation numeral parity, **117 false positives out of 338**, filed as
a negative result).

Done. **56 EN and 57 AR annotations, 684 source promises, 338 shared, 43 named humans.** Full write-up in
`verdicts/2026-10-04-ed05-cross-edition-named-entity-sweep.md`; ruling **#86**.

**Institutions and publications: CLEAN.** Every institution, statute, report, newspaper, programme and
funder resolves to the same body in both editions, including the cases most likely to drift — *Rising
Academy Network* in one annotation against *Rising Academies* in another, each following its own register
rather than the other annotation, which looks like inconsistency and is the opposite of one.

**The errors were in the grammar attached to the entities, which is where nothing was looking.** Arabic
has no neutral agreement for a role noun. English has one and takes it by default. So:

| | count |
|---|---|
| Named humans in the held set | **43** |
| Gendered by a role or predicate in the **Arabic** edition | **24** |
| Gendered anywhere in the **English** edition | **5** |
| **Determinations made by one edition alone** | **19** |
| Taken back to their registers today, in served text | **10** |
| → wrong | **2** |
| → right | **3** |
| → standing on no register at all | **5** |

**Two confirmed defects, both fixed in-run, both in the Arabic, both annotation-only.**

- **Alex Maclean, FCDO Development Director** — printed «مديرُ», masculine. Her own register quotes her
  and uses the pronoun: *"The discussions I joined were incredibly informed and engaged," **she** said.*
- **Benin's minister of maternal and primary education** — printed «وزيرة», feminine. The register is in
  French and French makes exactly this marking: *« le directeur de cabinet du **ministre** béninois des
  enseignements maternel et primaire »*. **Masculine, one clause from the name we took.**

Both were read off **how the name sounds** — *Alex* reads male, a maternal-and-primary-education portfolio
read female. A third fix: the same human's one English title was rendered two ways in one Arabic edition,
and one of the two («كبيرُ مستشاري») claims the headship of a function rather than a grade inside it; the
register settles it (*"Senior Education Evidence and Learning Advisor **within** Save the Children UK's
Global Outcomes Department"*) and the two annotations are harmonised.

**The direction is the finding.** Yesterday the Arabic was the right channel and the English was short by
three words. Today the Arabic is the wrong channel twice and the English is silently right twice — **not
because it was careful, but because English let it say nothing.** So the answer to *which edition is the
control* is **neither; the register is.** The second language is a free second channel on everything both
editions *can* say and a **single, unexamined** channel on everything only one of them can.

**And `qa_pair_frontmatter` passed on all of it, correctly.** 44 pairs, 432 identity fields, 684 source
URLs, 338 shared annotations, 3 of 3 declared asymmetries used and unchanged. It does not compare composed
prose because composed prose is supposed to differ. **Three consecutive days of that gate being correctly
blind to a real defect now bound what parity is for:** 10-02's #84 — blind to an error both editions
**shared**; 10-03 — blind to a **disagreement**; today — blind to a claim only one edition is **capable of
making**. Parity is a floor and was never a verdict.
