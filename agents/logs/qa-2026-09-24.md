## 1. The red deploy — diagnosed, repaired, and the repair cannot be pushed

**Run 35846542997, `verify` job, step "Feed cache validators work for a polling reader".**
`build` green. `deploy` green. The byte-compare **inside the same job** green on all five
files. The failure is the step after it: `ar/rss.xml` conditional GET returned 200.

**Cause.** The step reads an ETag with one request and revalidates with a second. Those are
independent connections and land on independently chosen Fastly edges. GitHub Pages unpacks
each deploy onto more than one origin replica; nginx mints `ETag: "<hex-mtime>-<hex-size>"`;
the replicas unpacked the 09-23 artefact **one second apart**. So each feed URL carries two
permanent validators:

```
rss.xml      "6ab3a42c-7344"  "6ab3a42d-7344"
ar/rss.xml   "6ab3a42c-920e"  "6ab3a42d-920e"
                     ^ +1s            ^ size identical
```

**Measured before anything was changed** — 12 probe pairs, ~23 hours after the deploy:

- every **304**: the response's own ETag **equalled** the one sent
- every **200**: the response's own ETag **differed**, in the mtime limb only, never the size

The origin never once refused a validator it actually held. Both mtimes were still being
served a day later, so this is **permanent, not propagation settling** — the step is a coin
flip on every deploy, on either feed. Green on 09-21 and 09-22 **by luck**.

**Repair.** `agents/tools/qa_feed_validators.py`, **standing assertion 24**, ruling **#63**.
Do what a polling reader does: hold one connection and revalidate on it, because a TCP
connection terminates at one edge holding one validator. Live origin today: **16/16 honored,
one body per feed.** Strictly stronger than the step it replaces — *every* revalidation must
be 304, not merely one.

**The first draft of the repair was wrong, and proving the bite is what caught it.** It kept
the two requests and merely forgave the mismatch, leaving "at least one probe revalidated" —
a 1-in-64 coin flip on six probes. **The fanout control failed on its first run.** Per the
09-14 rule, that is the cheaper half of the lesson a failed bite teaches, and it rewrote the
check from a tolerance into the honest instrument.

**Proved seven ways** (harness assigns a replica **per connection**, as Fastly does):

| Shape | Expect | Got |
|---|---|---|
| honest origin, one validator | 0 | 0 |
| **FANOUT — two validators, identical bodies (the real 09-23)** | **0** | **0** |
| the live origin itself | 0 | 0 |
| validator ignored: 200 carrying back the SAME ETag | 1 | 1 (A3) |
| two validators **and two different bodies** | 1 | 1 (A2) |
| no ETag at all | 1 | 1 (A1) |
| never honored: a fresh validator on every response | 1 | 1 (A4) |

**The axis the old step could not have had.** It compared identifiers and reported on
content, and never fetched a body. If an origin can serve two validators for one URL, the
unasked question is whether it serves two **bodies**. The replacement hashes every response:
**many validators are tolerated, many bodies are not.**

**The repair cannot be pushed by this identity.** Re-probed today on a scratch branch:

Patch staged at `agents/tools/patches/2026-09-24-verify-feed-validators.md`; escalated on
