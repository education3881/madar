## 4. The head: item 10, and what was actually wrong

The item asked for one of two reductions — subset the faces, or drop a family — and the answer
was neither. Full diagnosis in **ruling #103**; the measurement is the growth note. In short,
read out of a browser rather than out of the stylesheet:

```
EN article   html: Times New Roman   body: Newsreader
AR article   html: Amiri             body: Newsreader     <-- 145 days
```

`[dir="rtl"] { font-family: var(--font-arabic) }` sits on `<html>` and was meant to hand Amiri
down the document. `body { font-family: var(--font-body) }` matches `<body>` **directly**, and
a declaration that matches an element always beats a value that element inherits, whatever the
specificities. So every Arabic page drew its unstyled Arabic from the system serif and fetched
**278,872 bytes** of Latin serif to render nothing. Three further Arabic rules in
`SiteFooter.astro` never matched at all: Astro attached the component's `data-astro-cid` to a
bare `[dir="rtl"]`, which is `<html>`, an element the component does not render.

**Measured before and after, over the whole population, 88 pages, zero failures both times:**
AR article median **574,500 → 295,628** (−48.5%); AR home −278,872; AR editions −278,872; AR
browse −259,328; **EN articles identical in min, median and max.** The Arabic edition was
heavier than the English and is now 43% lighter.

**Why nothing we own saw it.** Three standing assertions drive a browser over Arabic pages.
`qa_arabic_shaping` reads **computed style — and exists because of this exact defect class**,
its own note naming *inheritance from a `body` rule that names no component at all* — and it
reads computed `letter-spacing`. One property away, for 30 days. `qa_ar_language` asks which
*language* a string is in; every string here was correctly Arabic. `qa_render` counts accent
pixels; a different serif in the same colour is invisible to it.

**Proved on copies, and one hypothesis was refuted rather than shipped.** The first story —
that a single ASCII full stop at the end of the Arabic strapline pulled 146,872 bytes — was
testable and **false**: removing it changed nothing, and so did removing the middle dot. The
removal that did bite was the word `Madār` (−71,376, two Cormorant files). **A bite that
fails to bite is a result**; this one stopped a clean, wrong, memorable sentence from reaching
the brief.

**And the instrument was audited before it was trusted.** The probe shape inherited from 10-10
injects a `<script>` into the page — which for a *font* measurement is Latin text inheriting
the body's Latin stack, i.e. the instrument can pull the face it counts. Re-measured from the
loopback server's own access log with nothing injected: identical on all four control pages,
file for file. **Cleared.** One reading was then discarded: the English home read 646,408/9
once against 778,376/12 on seven other readings, having dropped exactly the three `latin-ext`
subsets — the last files discovered during layout. On a control that could not have changed,
under a fix claiming *bytes went down*, **it had the exact shape of a success.**
