## (1) THE HEAD — ruling #88, `qa_sources_alive` is now bounded and schedulable

**What was actually unbounded — measured, not asserted.** A per-URL `TIMEOUT = 20` and `flush=True` were
both already present, and **neither bounds a sweep**:

- 59 held-cited URLs × HEAD-then-GET, with the TLS path adding a second HEAD+GET inside `_still_served`
  → **4 × 20s × 59 = 79 minutes** of socket budget.
- And 79 minutes is the *optimistic* reading. `timeout=` is a **per-socket-operation** deadline, not a
  per-request one, so a host dribbling one byte every 19 seconds holds the connection forever. **The
  true worst case is unbounded.**

**Why that is a scheduling defect rather than a performance one:** the ledger names this sweep as the
**last step before the flip commit**, and a step whose duration cannot be stated **cannot be placed in a
sequence**. Six days from the gate, the only instrument standing between the wave and a dead source
link was the one step nobody could schedule.

**The design, and the half that is the lesson.** A ceiling checked *between* units bounds the loop and
inherits the unboundedness of whatever is in flight — which is the thing being bounded. So the **main
thread owns the deadline** against a monotonic clock and a **daemon worker owns the socket**; a daemon
does not hold interpreter exit, so a wedged read is *abandoned* rather than waited on. Per-attempt
timeouts are clamped to the remaining budget. **Still sequential** — one worker, same order, same
traffic; going parallel would have made it fast *and* rude.

### Proved both ways, and the bite proved before the ceiling was tested (#35, #74)

| Experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| **Is the bite real?** unbounded sweep, black-hole host (accepts TCP, answers nothing, never closes) | **killed at 45s, still running, 1 of 6 done** — exit 124 |
| **Bite** — `--budget 25`, same host | returned in **25.0s**, `checked 0 of 6 · TRUNCATED`, all six named `unchecked` |
| **Bite, exit limb** — `--require-complete` | **exit 2**, with the count on stderr |
| **Control** — healthy local host, ceiling 25s | `checked 6 of 6 in 0.0s · complete`, **exit 0**: a non-binding ceiling changes nothing |
| **Control, mixed** — 3 healthy + 3 hanging, ceiling 30s | the 3 that answered reported **as answered**; only the 3 unreached marked `unchecked`. Returned at 30.0s |
| **Control, regression** — no `--budget` | unchanged behaviour; the ceiling is opt-in and defaults to off |
| **Control, the gated half** — `--collect-only` | unchanged: 684 citations, 343 URLs, 59 held, 88 files |
| **The real population** | **59 of 59 held URLs in 153.2s under a 300s ceiling, complete, exit 0** |

**The first row is the one that matters and it is the row 09-14 cost a day to learn.** A ceiling tested
against a host that would have finished anyway passes for the wrong reason. The injection was proved to
change the artefact *before* the ceiling was run on it.

**Truncation is louder than failure, deliberately.** An unreached URL reads **exactly like a clean one**
in the only direction that costs a reader anything — it reports a held source as fine when it was never
opened. So `unchecked` is its own bucket, every member is named individually, and the note says in
words: *this is a finding about this run, not about the source.* And `--require-complete` gives the flip
**different exit codes** for *"everything answered"* and *"we ran out of clock"*, which until today were
the same code and the same silence.

**The flip step now has a command and a measured duration**, written into the ledger:

```bash
python3 agents/tools/qa_sources_alive.py --held-only --sample 0 \
        --budget 300 --require-complete --json agents/logs/flip-sources.json
```

## (2) CONTENT — the edition's oldest item, and both halves bit

**Item 3, routed 2026-09-15, closed today at 20 days.** The 10-04 review named it the week's single
escalation and escalated it *by putting it first rather than by making it louder* (the 08-30 rule). It

**¶48 — the spine. CONFIRMED DEFECT.** EN: *"no two of them can be added together."* AR as composed:
`وأنَّ اثنتين منها لا تُجمَعان`. The composition leaned on **النكرة في سياق النفي تفيد العموم** — true,
but conditional on the indefinite falling **inside the negator's scope**. Here `اثنتين` is the **ism of
`أنَّ`** and the negation sits in the predicate, so the indefinite is the *topic* and generality never
triggers; `منها` is partitive and pulls the same way. **The Arabic stated an existential where the
English states a universal** — *"there are two of them that cannot be combined"*, which implies the rest
can — at the sentence the entire piece turns on, and against the Arabic edition's **own** closing
paragraph four screens later (`ولا تُتوسَّط`). Fixed by moving the indefinite inside the negation:
**`وأنَّه لا تُجمَعُ اثنتان منها`**.

**¶60 — lexical, and simpler. CONFIRMED DEFECT.** `دعوتان` is the dual of **دعوة** (an invitation), not
of **دعوى** (a claim) — in `دعوى` the alif maqṣūra is the **fourth** letter, so it converts to yāʾ:
**دعويان**. The composed Arabic said *"they are two different invitations."* The noun was right; only
its dual was wrong.

**Applied to the held AR file. No numeral moved either way, no EN prose touched, no annotation touched.**
Zero served bytes until the flip commit.
