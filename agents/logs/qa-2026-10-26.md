## 4. The AR half, and the two wrong populations it returned first

The Arabic sweep was run twice and **neither pass found the subject on the first attempt.**

* **Pass one** matched a closed list of future *verbs* plus `بحلول`. It returned ten "expired" hits, and
  `بحلول` is a **past-completion** preposition in every one of them — *by April 2026 Darfuri candidates were
  registered* is not a forecast. It missed all three real instances: `سيجعلها`, `سيكون`, `سيطبِّق`.
* **Pass two** matched the bound future prefix `سـ` directly and returned 40 hits on 22 pieces, **most of
  them nouns**: `سنوات` (years), `ستة` (six), `ستريتس` (Streets), `سيارا` (Ceará), `سيمسه` (SIMCE),
  `سيراليون` (Sierra Leone).

**A closed list of words cannot detect a bound morpheme, and a single-letter morpheme cannot be told from a
word's first letter without a lexicon.** English marks futurity with a free word; Arabic marks it with a
prefix. So this defect class is assertable in EN and **not** assertable in AR by the same means — the fourth
month-running instance of the Arabic wall (#101 names, #112 quotations, #113 acronyms, now tense), and the
first where the obstacle is orthographic rather than lexical. Any gate built on #117 asserts the EN half and
**prints** the AR half; a check reporting 0 for Arabic would be reporting its own blindness (#93's reasoning).

**And the pair disagreed once, in the direction that matters.** `rak-iqra` EN said *Iqra **will run***; the
Arabic said *فمن سبتمبر 2026 **يعمل** «اقرأ»* — the bare imperfect, unmarked for tense, which reads as *runs*
and **has not expired.** One of the four pieces carried this defect in English only, because English made the
composer choose and Arabic did not. #86 asked which questions only one edition can answer; #101 which
questions one edition is **forced** to answer; #117 adds **which questions one edition is forced to answer
wrongly** — and the edition that was not forced is the one still right.
