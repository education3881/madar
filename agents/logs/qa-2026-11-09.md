## FORWARD QUESTION — what can today's sweep not structurally see?

**Nothing this operation owns can tell a Madār URL from anybody else's URL.**

Ruling #137 was earned twice over this morning — a composed title and a self-falsified byte length, in
the first self-citation this corpus has ever carried — and **both were caught by luck of scope.**
`qa_citation_identity` saw the title *only because a title happened to be a quoted identity token*, which
is a property of how that annotation was written and not of what it cites. **No instrument saw the byte
length at all**, and none could: to `qa_sources_alive`, `qa_claim_registers`, `qa_citation_identity` and
`qa_annotation_figures`, `education3881.github.io` is just another host in a `url:` field.

Every one of those four instruments is built on the premise that the register belongs to **someone
else** — their host answers or does not, their archive kept a copy or did not, their bytes still carry
our quotation or no longer do. **Turn the citation inward and all four premises become false at once**,
and not one of the four asks whether they are. The corpus now has two self-citations and the standing
ledger's singles make follow-ups cheaper, so the population grows from here.

**The test for tomorrow, before anything new is added:** enumerate every `sources[]` entry whose `url`
is a Madār URL, and ask of each — does any gate in this repository treat it differently from a
third-party register in any respect at all? If the answer is no in all respects, then #137 clauses 1 and
2 are **prose that nothing can fail**, which is exactly what #71's *every brief carries the previous
deploy's conclusion* turned out to be before `qa_prior_publication` was built.
