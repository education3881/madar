## 3. The instrument change, proved both ways against a LIVE defect

`qa_sources_alive` reported a host with an expired certificate serving every document, and a hostname
that does not exist in DNS, as the **identical string** `unreachable · URLError`. The check ran. It was
green-lit. Its vocabulary could not hold the distinction its reader needs.

`classify()` now names the layer. The *before* behaviour was captured **before** the edit — which is the
09-14 discipline applied to a refactor rather than an injection:

| Case | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **BITE 1** mineduc yearbook — cert expired, document served | `unreachable · URLError` | `tls · cert: certificate has expired · document still served (200)` |
| **BITE 2** mineduc news index, same host, HTML | `unreachable · URLError` | `tls · cert: certificate has expired · document still served (200)` |
| CONTROL 1 World Bank feature, valid cert | `ok · 200` | `ok · 200` |
| CONTROL 2 allAfrica story, valid cert | — | `ok · 200` |
| CONTROL 3 hostname absent from DNS | `unreachable · URLError` | `dns · name does not resolve` |
| CONTROL 4 resolves, nothing listening | — | `refused · connection refused at :443` |
| CONTROL 5 valid cert, genuine 404 | — | `error · HTTP 404` |

**The bite is proved as carefully as the control.** Bites 1 and 3 produced *the same string* before the
change — that identity **is** the defect, which is why the table has to show it rather than show a
green. Three distinct non-TLS failures landing in three distinct non-TLS buckets is what proves the new
bucket is naming something rather than swallowing everything.

Still deliberately **not** a deploy gate; the reason in the workflow file is unchanged and still right —
someone else's expired certificate is not our build's failure. It is now a finding our sweep can *say*.

## 7. Forward question for the next run, per the 08-23 rule

**Named surface: we now check that a source ANSWERS. Nothing checks that it answers with the document
we cited.**

Today closed the transport. What sits directly on top of it is worse and cheaper to state: a `200` says
a server sent bytes, not that the bytes are the ones a run read. Every failure mode the new buckets
name is a failure to *connect*. **None of them can see a host that connects perfectly and serves
something else** — a paywall interstitial at 200, a redirect to a section index at 200, a CMS migration
that re-slugs a story into a generic landing page at 200, or a document silently re-issued with a
figure changed. Sierra Leone's blog was re-slugged rather than deleted (#54) and we only found that out
because it had the decency to 404; had it 200'd on a landing page, the sweep would still be green.

**The probe, and it is one line of storage:** record, per source URL, a **content fingerprint** at the
moment a run reads it — a length and a hash of the fetched body, with the date. Then the sweep's
question changes from *did it answer* to *did it answer the same thing*. Bite and control are both
free and both live: the two yearbooks are stable and must fingerprint identically across days; any
news index must not. **This is the honest form of `fetched-on-date`** — we have been writing that
phrase into annotations for months while storing nothing that could ever check it, which is #37 (a
contract in prose is a declaration) pointed at our own citations.
