### 2. The attribution half cannot be gated, and the reason is ours rather than the language's

Measured with `agents/tools/annotation_claims.py` (new today; a **measurement, not an assertion** — no
`qa_` prefix, outside the counting glob, so **no count in `CLAUDE.md` moves**). Population: **714 served
annotations, 357 EN and 357 AR, over 361 distinct registers**; 64 of them carry no digit at all, so
assertion 38 says nothing whatever about those.

The obvious rule is #101 clause 1 moved from an expansion onto an utterance: *either the annotation
quotes the register, or it does not claim the register's speech.* It fails, and it fails four ways:

| Ground | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — the document-type noun occupies the verb | 184 → **76** | A wide attribution vocabulary flags 184 annotations; forms with no English noun reading flag 76. The 108 difference is `Annual Report 2024`, `Situation Report #203`, `Country Note`, `review reports`. A citation's whole job is to name documents. |
| 2 — **the house style occupies the verb** | **9 of 13** | The standing prohibition is *announced ≠ measured, labelled at the figure*. A correct annotation is **required** to print `announced`/`stated` beside a figure: *the announced scale: a target of about 23 lakh students*; *the 2025 tally as announced at the minister's press conference*. The token an instrument would key on is the token the style mandates. |
| 3 — the verb's subject is not the register | 2 of 13 | *roughly one in three Year 9 students **writes** at a level expected in primary school*; *the intermediate it publishes at the address **printed** inside that certificate*. The subjects are a schoolchild and a field in a TLS certificate. A string match cannot read a subject. |
| 4 — **an absence claim is unquotable by construction** | 1 of 13 | The Arabic edition of `2026-05-27-egypt-ekb-decade` had thought of this before we did. |

**Precision of the candidate rule: 0 true positives out of 13 flagged, over 714 annotations.** The
thirteenth row is a correct attribution evidenced by a dated read of the register under #101 clause 4.

Ground 4 is the one that settles it, and it is worth quoting because the corpus argued the point itself:

> وقد ذُكرت الصيغةُ هنا بِلا علامات اقتباس عن قصد، لأنَّ الاقتباسَ ادِّعاءُ أنَّ البايتات تَحملها، وهذا
> ادِّعاءُ أنَّها لا تَحملها.
> *(the form is given here without quotation marks deliberately, because a quotation is a claim that the
> bytes carry it, and this is a claim that they do not.)*

That annotation's claim is that the register titles a named human by his **deanship and not by a
doctorate** — a claim about what is **absent** from the bytes. A quotation asserts presence. So the rule
does not merely misfire there; it **inverts**, demanding presence as the evidence for absence. #140's
criterion read onto a candidate instead of onto an instrument: an instrument that returns **no answer**
is behaving correctly, and this one's answers would be **wrong** rather than absent.

**Verdict on the fork: the non-numeric half is a VERIFIER ROW, not a gate** — reached by measurement, and
it is one of the two answers the row itself offered.

### 3. What IS reachable is a class item 38 did not name

**39 of the 714 annotations carry a self-specifying provenance claim** — the annotation prints a **method
and its value together**: `sha256 d6b62bda…`, `HTTP 200`, `Last-Modified 2026-08-16`, a byte length, a
dated fetch, `read in served bytes <date>`. **86 instances**: http-status 53, byte-length 13, fetched-dated
6, sha256 6, last-modified 5, read-in-bytes 3.

That is **#113's criterion met by a different artefact**. An acronym states how many words and which
letter each begins with; a sha256 states which function over which bytes. The claim specifies its own
test, which is exactly what the attribution verb cannot do. Two tiers, and only one could ever gate:

- **OFFLINE** — the claim's own internal arithmetic: a read date that has not arrived, a `Last-Modified`
  later than the read that observed it, a sha256 that is not hex or truncated with no ellipsis, a read
  asserted behind a 4xx. **Measured over all 39 today: ZERO violations.**
- **NETWORK** — re-taking a sha256 or a status needs somebody else's server. Cannot gate, for #111's
  reason unchanged. Printed, never asserted.

**Proved both ways on a `/tmp` copy, injection asserted to have changed the file first (2026-09-14):
control 0 violations, bite 2 of 3 red.** And the third bite is the useful half of the proof:

> **BITE 3 DID NOT BITE.** Turning one `HTTP 200` into `HTTP 404` beside a surviving `HTTP 200` in the
> same annotation exits like a passing control, because the limb can only ask *all statuses ≥ 400* — and
> it **must**, since an annotation legitimately records a dead register beside a live one (yesterday's
> Ofsted read recorded a `gov.uk` person page that now 404s, in exactly that shape). **The annotation is
> not the claim's boundary**: one annotation carries many fetches, so a per-claim limb has nothing to
> attach to. That is item 38's own problem one level down, *inside the half that was supposed to be the
> reachable one.* Recorded rather than repaired by widening a limb into a false positive.

The tool is deliberately **not** wired into `postbuild`: the offline tier has zero bite today and the
class is young — all 39 written since 2026-10-23 — so a gate would be guarding a convention rather than
enforcing one. The gate is **handed over as a queue row with its population already measured**, which is
