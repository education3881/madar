### 14 · `qa_render` — two kernels, both in the arithmetic, both closed today

**EXTENT.** `--screenshot` under `--headless=new` captures the **viewport**, not the document. Old
headless captured the full page; the flag name did not change when the behaviour did. Measured:
**every raster this tool had ever read was 1200×900**, against documents **2,559 to 5,648 px** tall.

| target | document | raster was | the ruler held |
|---|---|---|---|
| EN home | 3,041 px | 900 px | **29.6%** |
| AR home | 2,559 px | 900 px | **35.2%** |
| EN article | 5,648 px | 900 px | **15.9%** |
| AR article | 5,310 px | 900 px | **16.9%** |

**Not a regression.** The floors were calibrated 2026-09-15 from the same crop and today's viewport
numbers reproduce that range to the digit (ink 0.0228–0.0506 against the recorded 0.0216–0.0506). The
instrument has read one screenful per page since the day it was written, while printing one line per
*page*. **And the crop is positioned where the properties cannot fail**: the masthead and the opening
of the article are the most reliably-inked part of any page.

**POSITION.** A pixel population count has no coordinates, so widening the raster **alone would have
reduced sensitivity** — the lower page is less ink-dense, and on the EN home the whole-page dark
fraction falls from 0.0095 to 0.0053 when the ruler is widened. The decisive number is the pair: the
EN home's whole-page dark is **0.0053 and passes the 0.003 floor**, while **its own last band reads
0.0006 — five times below that floor and invisible to the average.**

**Both closed in one change.** The shot is taken at the document's own height, and **P1 NOT BLANK is
asserted per 900px band**. The property is now *no screenful of any page is blank* — which is what the
2026-08-16 edge-to-edge defect actually was — rather than *the page as a whole drew something*, which
no page with a masthead can fail.

**Floors chosen from measurement before anything was asserted (#93).** Band ink across all 21 bands of
the five targets runs **0.0114 to 0.0612**, so `BAND_INK_FLOOR = 0.006`: below today's worst band and
twenty times above a blank one.

**The height is verified, not assumed.** A layout that depends on viewport height cannot be captured
in one pass, so the tool measures `scrollHeight` at a 900px viewport and then **re-measures it at the
height it is about to shoot**, refusing the page if the two disagree. None of the four targets carries
a `vh` declaration — checked — but that is a fact about today's templates, not a property of the tool.

**DECLINED, with the falsifier stated (#93, and #125 on how a decline must be written).** Per-band
*dark* and per-band *accent* are measured and printed and **not** asserted. The EN home's footer band
reads dark **0.0006** and the EN article's **0.0008** — both legible, both small muted type. A per-band
dark floor at the whole-raster 0.003 would red this build today; a floor low enough to pass the footer
(0.0005) sits too close to what a genuinely white-on-white band produces (≈0) to separate them. **The
ruler cannot hold per-band legibility because it is an ABSOLUTE count.** The decline expires the day
the ruler is made relative to the band's own ink — that is the falsifier, it is **item 73**, and per
#125 it names **no sibling instrument**, because there is none.

### 18 · `qa_arabic_joining` — the locality kernel, and the ruler's two requirements trade against each other

The SERVED half takes **the page's own longest real Arabic run** and compares its advance width against
its own ZWNJ-de-joined self, failing above **0.90**. Measured today by injecting N evenly-spaced ZWNJ
into the real run on each target — N broken joins and nothing else changed:

| target | eligible Arabic runs | **runs read** | longest run | clean ratio | 1 broken join | 25% broken | 79% broken |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR home | 37 | **1** | 191 ch / 162 joins | 0.642 | 0.642 | 0.696 | **0.863** (passes) |
| AR article | 49 | **1** | 609 ch / 511 joins | 0.689 | 0.691 | **0.742** | — |
| EN home | 11 | **1** | 323 ch / 241 joins | 0.620 | 0.620 | 0.747 *(27%)* | 0.821 *(53%)* |
| VALENCE | 1 | **1** | 14 ch / 12 joins | 0.682 | 0.682 | 0.742 *(17%)* | **0.907 RED** *(67%)* |

**One broken join is invisible on every target**, to three decimals on three of four. On the AR article
**128 broken joins out of 511 reads 0.742** against a 0.90 threshold. Only VALENCE's 14-character run
can cross the threshold at all, and it needs 8 of its 12 joins broken.

So the assertion is an instrument for a **global** shaping failure — a CSS rule, a font outage, which
is exactly what bite A proved when it landed — and it is structurally blind to a **local** one. **And
the two limits are not independent: they cause each other.** A ratio over a short run has no dynamic
range (`مدار`, 4 chars, measures 0.972 shaped — the tool's own header says no threshold can separate
it), so `SERVED_MIN_LEN = 12` and the *longest* run is selected — and the longest run is precisely the
one in which a single broken join is most diluted. **The ruler's dynamic range and its locality trade
against each other, and the tool resolved the trade in the direction that maximises dilution.** That
is not a mistake; it is the only resolution a single scalar admits. Raised as **item 74** and handed
over rather than fixed here: a per-join or per-word ruler changes served-byte expectations and the
instrument that found the defect must not also fix it.

### 26 · `qa_feed_direction` — the kernel is empty, and that is the template

`signature()` pushes `b.left` and **discards `b.top`**, so a line wrap and a direction flip are the
same symbol: in an LTR host the first character of a new line sits far to the left of its predecessor
and scores `-`, exactly as an RTL run does. The projection is one-dimensional over a two-dimensional
layout. **And it loses nothing, because the probe pins `white-space: pre; width: 60000px` so that no
field can wrap** — with the reason written in the comment at the point of construction: *"a very wide
box keeps every field on one line, so a visual-order comparison is unambiguous rather than a question
about where the browser chose to wrap."*

**This is the only one of the four whose author interrogated the ruler**, and the method generalises:
where you cannot widen a ruler, **construct the geometry in which its kernel is empty**. The residue is
real and is named rather than asserted: that geometry is **ours, not the consumer's** — a subscriber's
reader wraps — so the probe answers the direction question in a layout no reader is in. Whether a
*wrapped* field survives the base direction is a separate question needing a two-key `(line, x)`
signature, and it is **item 75**.
