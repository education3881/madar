### Half (a) — the publication has never been measured and it is CLEAN, at both widths

**Both flagged pages were adjudicated against the geometry rather than counted**, because *a bite wider
than the rule it targets manufactures the defect it was aimed at* (#115) and two raw hits on a corpus
nobody had ever measured is exactly the shape that reads like a discovery.

- **`/editions/index.html`** — three SVG `<text>` nodes, worst +104px. **Not overflowing.** The `<svg>`
  carries `viewBox="0 0 680 880"` and renders 1263.98px wide, a scale of 1.859. The flagged text's
  `getBBox().width` is 326.73 **user units**; 326.73 × 1.859 = **607.3px**, which is exactly the
  `scrollWidth` reported. Its `getBoundingClientRect().right` is **943.7** against the svg's own right edge
  of **1271.98** — comfortably inside. **For an SVG element `scrollWidth − clientWidth` is not a
  containment relation**; the containment relation is the rect against the nearest SVG viewport, and it
  holds for all three.
- **`/valence/index.html`** — two `<div>`s, worst +300px, with `clientWidth` **0** and **1** and
  `getBoundingClientRect().width` 0 and 1. **A zero-width box cannot contain anything, so the subtraction
  is meaningless.** `documentElement.scrollWidth − viewport` is **0** and `body.scrollWidth` is exactly the
  viewport on both pages, at both widths. Valence is the 540 KB runtime-JS instrument whose markup and
  classes are written by template literals, and #93 already holds that a static answer about it is an
  answer about the probe.

**So the publication could be gated at a floor of ZERO today, at both widths** — which is the condition
#93 names for a measurement to become a gate — provided the oracle carries two guards it does not have:
**skip the SVG namespace** (or compare rect against the nearest SVG viewport instead) and **skip boxes of
≈zero width**. Without those two guards a gate here would red the build on two pages that are correct.

## FORWARD QUESTION — what today's sweep cannot structurally see

**Every overflow number in this log was measured on a document loaded into an `<iframe>` of a fixed CSS
width, and a phone is not a 390px iframe.** The probe sets no `devicePixelRatio`, no
`<meta name="viewport">` emulation and no touch flags, so **`@media` queries that depend on anything but
`width` have never been evaluated at all** — and the artefacts' own stylesheets are copied forward from a
template written for a desktop column. The sharp version: **a document can be clean at a 390px iframe and
broken on a 390px phone**, because the phone applies the viewport meta tag, a 3× pixel ratio and a
different initial containing block, and **not one of the three is a width.** So today's 17 is a floor on
the real population and cannot be an estimate of it. Nothing we own can currently tell the difference, and
the cheapest instrument that could is Chrome's own device emulation over the same corpus — one flag, same
browser, same harness. Raised as item **145**.
