## ITEM 39 — the head, re-measured first (#142), and the row's own numbers split in two directions

### Instance 1 — `newsreader-italic` on English pages. REPRODUCES EXACTLY.

The row: *"reaches all 68 EN pages at 146,872 bytes and paints 196 characters on an article — 28.2% of
an English article's payload."* Measured today by `qa_font_payload`'s server-log probe on the current
build: **146,872 bytes, 196 painted characters, 749 b/char, on a 521,408-byte page = 28.2%.** Identical
to three significant figures after three weeks and one new published piece.

**And the probe says something the row does not.** The same file on the **English home page** paints
**982 characters** at **149 b/char**, and on the Editions page **235**. The family is idle on an
article and busy on an index.

### Instance 2 — `cormorant-garamond` on Arabic pages. REPRODUCES EXACTLY, TO THE BYTE.

The row: *"on an Arabic page it costs **71,376 bytes** to render the five characters of `Madār` in the
footer."* Measured today on both the Arabic home and an Arabic article:

```
  37,640  cormorant-garamond-normal-400-600var-latin.woff2        4 chars    9,410 b/char
  33,736  cormorant-garamond-normal-400-600var-latin-ext.woff2    1 char    33,736 b/char
  ------
  71,376                                                          5 chars
```

**The one character in the latin-ext subset is the macron-a of our own name.** `ā` is U+0101; Cormorant's
latin-ext cut opens at U+0100. So **33,736 bytes are fetched on every page that prints `Madār` in the
display face, to paint one diacritic** — on the English article and the English home as well, where the
same line reads `1 char`. That is 47% of this instance's cost and it was never stated.

### THE DEFECT THE RE-MEASUREMENT FOUND — 56 Latin runs on 10 served Arabic pages

`[dir="rtl"] em, [dir="rtl"] i, [dir="rtl"] cite { font-style: normal }` — the #115 rule, shipped
2026-10-25 — is written as a statement about a **page** and is enforcing a fact about a **script**. The
two coincide only while every emphasised run on an Arabic page is Arabic. **Fifty-six are not:**

```
  egypt-ekb-decade      10   Nature · Britannica · Cambridge · Elsevier · Scopus AI ·
                             ScienceDirect AI · Reaxys Predictive Retrosynthesis
  indonesia-permen-13    9   Kurikulum 2013 · Kurikulum Merdeka · "Bukanlah tentang
                             Kurikulum Baru." · pembelajaran mendalam · penyempurnaan
  rwanda-teacher-cert    9   uruguay-eduia-lab-ceibal 9 · qatar-humanity-spark 6
  uae-madrasa-tiktok     4   Common Sense Networks · Poynter
  vietnam 3 · bahrain-bqa-five-cycles 3 · zambia 2 · bo-teacher-chalk 1  (Lan fɔ lan)
```

Every one is a journal title, an institution, a programme name or a foreign-language quotation — the
cases where Latin italic is not decoration but **disambiguation**. All 56 have rendered upright and
indistinguishable from the surrounding Arabic since 2026-10-25, **21 days**, on pages a reader is
served. No gate can see it: `qa_font_payload` limb 1 asks whether a run *containing an Arabic code
point* is slanted, and these contain none, so the limb is silent **correctly** — it is answering the
question it was built for.

**It is not fixed in this run, and the reason is a finding rather than a shortage of time.** Every
CSS-only repair sets the computed `font-style` of the element to `italic`, and an `<em>` holding
Arabic is the same element as an `<em>` holding Latin — so any such repair turns **limb 1 of standing
assertion 39 red on 139 Arabic runs**, and limb 1 would be right to red: it reads the computed style,
which is the only thing CSS can be asked about. **A correctness rule about a script cannot be enforced
by a selector about a page**, because CSS selects elements and a script lives inside an element's text.
The repair therefore belongs in the markup — the Arabic composer marks a foreign-language run with
`lang`, which the accessibility family (`qa_a11y_lang`, 2026-09-13) wanted anyway, and the stylesheet
keys `:lang()` off it. That is content work across ten **published** pieces under the Arabic Editor's
gate, not a stylesheet line, and it is raised as **item 107, the board's only DEFECT**, rather than
improvised at the end of a run.

## THE JUDGMENT ITEM 39 ASKED FOR. Both halves, in writing, and the row CLOSES at 21 days.

The row's own last line: *"Neither (a) nor (b) is a byte question and that is why they are here: the
numbers are now settled and the judgment is not."* Here is the judgment.

### (a) Drop a family — **NO, AND THE UNIT IS WRONG.**

Neither family is droppable and the measurement says so in its own numbers. **Newsreader Italic paints
982 characters on the English home page. Cormorant Garamond paints 1,507 on the Editions page.** A
family that carries four figures of text on an index page is not dead weight; it is a face doing its
job on the pages that need it and riding along on the pages that do not.

**What the numbers actually select is not a family. It is a `(page, family)` pair** — and the gate
already prints the selector, on every build, in the column nobody has read as a rule: **bytes per
painted character.** Below ~500 b/char the face is earning its transfer. Above ~5,000 it is being
fetched for a handful of glyphs. Today's probe:

| pair | bytes | chars | b/char | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EN article · newsreader-normal | 132,000 | 9,813 | **13** | earning |
| AR article · amiri-normal-arabic | 108,560 | 7,889 | **13** | earning |
| EN home · newsreader-italic | 146,872 | 982 | **149** | earning |
| EN article · newsreader-italic | 146,872 | 196 | **749** | thin, and it is the dek |
| AR home · cormorant-latin | 37,640 | 4 | **9,410** | **not earning** |
| EN+AR, every page · cormorant-latin-ext | 33,736 | 1 | **33,736** | **not earning** |

So **"drop a family" is replaced by a rule the operation can apply without a face judgment every time:
a family is scoped off a page where it paints fewer characters than it costs kilobytes.** That is a
number the build already produces, and it converts a standing design argument into a per-page question
with an answer.

The two pairs the rule selects, handed to the Designer and the Web Developer **with their numbers
rather than as an opinion** — queue item 108:

1. **Cormorant on the 68 Arabic pages — 71,376 bytes for the five characters of `Madār` in the
   footer, ≈ 4.85 MB across the Arabic edition.** The Arabic edition's typographic identity is carried
   by `مدار` beside it and by Amiri throughout; the romanisation in the footer is a secondary lockup.
   **This is a face judgment and it is the Designer's**, which is why it is handed over and not taken
   here — but it is now handed over with a number and a page count instead of a feeling.
2. **Cormorant latin-ext, corpus-wide — 33,736 bytes for one macron.** Not a face judgment at all:
   the `ā` of `Madār` falls one code point past the end of Cormorant's latin cut. This is a subsetting
   question for the bundle generator, and it is the cheapest 3.8 MB on the board.

### (b) What replaces italic emphasis in Arabic — **NOTHING, AND THAT IS A RULE RATHER THAN A GAP.**

Arabic typography has no italic convention. The house has now said so three times in three places
(`[dir="rtl"] .pullquote`, the 10-17 strapline, #115) and each time as a point fix, which is why the
question keeps coming back as though it were unresolved. It is resolved: **emphasis in the Arabic
edition is carried by syntax and by the register's own quotation marks «  », which the Arabic Content
Creator already uses, and no visual substitute is introduced.** A slant, a tracking change or a weight
shift would each be an import from a script that is not this one, and the operation's own standing rule
about tracking (2026-08-18 clause 4) is the same sentence in a different costume.

Written into the standing ledger's prohibitions in this commit so it is a term every future Arabic
composition is written under, rather than a decision a fourth run has to make again.

**But the question's premise was wrong, and that is the half worth keeping.** It assumed the Arabic
edition had lost a mark it should get back. What it had lost is **56 Latin runs' worth of a mark that
was never Arabic's to begin with** — see the defect above. *A question about what to add found
something that should never have been taken away.*

## The forward question — what this sweep cannot structurally see

**Every instrument we own reads the artefact as a SINGLE document, and the Latin-inside-Arabic defect
is a property of a BOUNDARY INSIDE ONE ELEMENT.** Today's finding was not that a page was wrong or that
an element was wrong — it was that one `<em>` holds two scripts and the stylesheet can only address the
whole of it. Nothing in the set can express that: `qa_arabic_shaping` and `qa_arabic_joining` read
Arabic-bearing elements, `qa_a11y_lang` reads a declared `lang` against the string under it,
`qa_font_payload` partitions runs by whether they contain an Arabic code point — **all four ask a
yes/no question of an element, and none can report that an element is MIXED.**

So: **how many served elements in this corpus contain more than one script, and in how many of them is
a per-script property — font, style, direction, digits, `lang` — being decided for all of the text by a
rule that is correct for only part of it?** The 56 runs found today are the instances that happen to be
inside an `<em>`. The question is the population: every element in both editions, partitioned by the
set of scripts its own text actually carries. **Test it before adding anything new.** It enters the
board at OPEN's head as **item 109**.
