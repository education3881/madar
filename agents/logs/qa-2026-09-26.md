## 6. Forward question for the next run, per the 08-23 rule

**Named surface: the probe that found today's hole can only be run against defects we have already
found — so what is the equivalent probe for the defects nobody has reported yet?**

#66 turns our own history into a regression suite, and it worked: four of six caught, one hole. But its
material is **closed defects**, and every one of them was found by a human reading rather than by a
check. That means the probe measures how well we remember, not how well we cover. The two blind results
today were both **negative space** — a pointer at nothing (H4) and a figure written as a word (H2) — and
negative space is exactly what an enumeration cannot reach, which is the ninth statement of *a green
check is scoped to what it enumerates.*

**The probe, and it is the inverse of today's:** instead of injecting defects we have seen, **enumerate
the surfaces the build emits and ask, per surface, which assertion would fail if it were wrong.** Take
the 804 distinct targets, the 19 census numbers, the frontmatter fields in the schema, the chrome set,
the feed elements — and produce a **coverage table with named gaps**, not a list of green checks. A
surface with no assertion against it is today's H4 before anyone re-injected it. Do it as a written
audit, not a tool, because the output is a map and not a verdict.

**Second, and sharpened rather than carried:** ruling #69 says a recorded **absence** ages worse than a
recorded figure, and that re-verification must re-test the absences. **Nothing in this operation
enumerates its own recorded absences.** Every recon and verdict on disk contains sentences of the form
*not published*, *not found*, *does not exist*, *unreachable* — and the 09-24 entry proves one of them
was false for five months. That is a one-afternoon grep over `content-drafts/**` producing a dated list
of every claimed absence, oldest first, and it is the cheapest instrument named in this log.
