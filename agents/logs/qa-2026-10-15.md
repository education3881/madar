## 2. The head: item 8 closed, and what the refusal is worth in numbers

The remedy is `agents/tools/lastmod_tree_guard.py` — a **precondition**, deliberately not a `qa_*`
assertion, shared by the only two instruments that compare a `<lastmod>` to anything. It parses
`CHROME_GLOBS` and `CONTENT_DIRS` out of `sitemapLastmod.mjs` on every run and copies neither, classifies
the working tree against those paths, excludes dirty **held** drafts by the resolver's own semantics, and
refuses with **exit 4** — a third outcome, distinct from drift (1) and from an unreachable origin (2).

**Proved 13 ways, bites first.** Eleven in the harness
(`agents/tools/proofs/lastmod_tree_guard_proof.sh`), on clones under `/tmp` — because the subject of this
guard is the working tree, so a harness that dirtied the real one would be injecting into the thing it
measures. **The harness caught itself twice:** bite 8 first deleted a clone's `.git`, failed on read-only
objects and left a *partial* `.git`, refusing for a reason the case did not claim (rebuilt by `git archive`,
so the condition is now definitional); and every control would have passed for the wrong reason had
`fresh()` not copied the uncommitted instruments into each clone — *a guard that is not there cannot refuse.*

**The remaining two proofs are the ones that matter, and they ran on the real tree in the real state**, not
on an injection — today's content repair left two approved article files dirty, which is exactly the
condition that produced the 10-14 false negative:

| | `qa_served_manifest --check` | `qa_live_drift` |

| **Pre-commit** (tree dirty in 2 resolver paths) | **REFUSED(4)**, before the origin was even fetched | **PARTIAL(4)** — URL set and 4 sampled heads match across 134 URLs; **the date axis makes no claim** |

## Forward question — what today's sweep cannot structurally see

**A 200 is not the document we cited.** Every instrument this operation owns that touches an outbound
promise classifies it by **status code** — alive, dead, walled, timeout, bad certificate — and **nothing
anywhere asks whether the bytes at that URL are still the register the annotation describes.** Today's
`?p=508411` is the proof that the gap is reachable: 200, served, healthy by every test we have, and not the
article. The consequence is sharper than it first looks, because ruling #100 says a citation's death is the
only event that ever makes anyone re-read a register — so a citation that dies **softly**, by being replaced
at its own URL with a listing, a paywall notice, a redirect to a section front or a rewritten article, never
re-opens anything and reports healthy forever. Of the 341 promises, **283 returned 200 yesterday**; the
number of those whose served text still contains the headline, the figure or the quotation we attributed to
it is **unknown, and has never been asked**.
