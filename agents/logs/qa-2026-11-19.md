## ITEM 41 — WHICH OF OUR ASSERTIONS READ A SAMPLE, AND WHAT LIVES OUTSIDE IT

beside its verdict, the way `qa_bridge_coverage` prints 2.3% instead of implying 100%.* Its second, the
hard one: *for each sample, run the same check over the population once, and record how many instances
were outside.*

### The first cut — `agents/tools/sample_coverage.py`, a MEASUREMENT and not an assertion

No `qa_` prefix, outside the counting glob, **so no count in `CLAUDE.md` moves.** It could not honestly
gate: a floor on coverage would red the build on every instrument this operation deliberately built as a
sample, which is #93's reasoning, and the right response to a sample is to know its fraction rather than
to forbid it.

**It classifies by the property, never by the header** — ruling #150 is one day old and this is its first
application. Several of our tools state their own coverage in prose; those sentences were true the day
they were written and standing-queue item **67** is a whole row about that class. So nothing here reads a
header. The property that actually matters is: **does the set of documents this instrument reads grow
when the corpus grows?** That is decidable from the source, and it is the difference between a gate that
will see the next page we publish and one that will not.

**The MIXED bucket exists because a three-bucket version would have hidden the thing item 41 asked for.**

### The second cut — the sample's own check, run over the population

Taken on the **widest** sample, `qa_arabic_shaping` at 7 of 139, by rewriting only its `TARGETS` literal
to all 139 served routes and changing nothing else.

**The denominator first, because it is the finding.** Counted over the served bytes of all 139 documents:
**5,125 Arabic-bearing text runs, on 139 of 139 documents.** Not 70, not "the Arabic edition" — **every
page this publication serves carries Arabic**, because the wordmark and the strapline are in the chrome
of all 69 English pages too. So the gate's sample is **401 of 5,125 served runs, 7.8%**, and
`qa_arabic_joining`'s is **147 of 5,125, 2.9%**. The denominator nobody had written down was never "the
Arabic pages."

**And then the population run, which is the half item 41 was actually owed:**

```
pages probed ................ 139 of 139
painted Arabic elements ..... 4,709
elements with tracking ...... 0
pages with a finding ........ 0
```

**4,308 painted Arabic elements live outside the gate's sample — 91.5% of them — and the defect count out
there is ZERO.** That is a negative result and it is stated as one: *the sample is narrow and the
population is clean.* Until this morning nobody knew either half, and the gate's green was a statement
about seven pages that read like a statement about the publication.

**The two figures are in different units and are not reconciled**: 5,125 is Arabic-bearing text runs in
the **served bytes**, 4,709 is Arabic-bearing elements **painted** by a browser. A run and an element are
not the same object and nothing here pretends otherwise (#150's neighbouring trap).

**The cost was measured, and it is the sharpest thing in this section.** The whole-population run — 139
documents, one browser launch each — completed in **under two minutes**, start to final write. The
assumption underneath every sample in this operation is that the population is expensive; for this
instrument it is **not**, and that converts item 41 from a question about coverage into a question about
a choice nobody has had to defend. It is handed to the Assertions Engineer as a queue row rather than
done here, because widening a gate changes what reds the deploy and that is not the Manager's to decide
on the afternoon it is measured (the 09-27 split's own rule).

## Named forward question

**Every sample in this operation is defended by a cost nobody has priced — so for each of the five, what
does the population actually cost, and which of them is a sample by necessity rather than by habit?**

Today priced exactly one: `qa_arabic_shaping`'s population run is **under two minutes** for 139 documents
against a sample of seven, which means its narrowness buys almost nothing. The other four are unpriced
and the assumption behind all five is identical and untested. `qa_render` rasterises and compares pixels
and is plainly the expensive one; `qa_font_payload` needs a server-log window per page and may be
*genuinely* a sample, because its subject is a race (item 124); `qa_arabic_joining` measures run widths;
`qa_live_drift` crosses the network to someone else's origin, which is the one cost that is not ours to
spend. **The sharp version: a sample is a decision about cost, and not one of the five has its cost
written down anywhere — so every one of them is currently defended by a belief.** The first cut needs no
new tool: run each of the four at its full population once, with a clock on it, and write the number
beside its coverage fraction. *A coverage percentage without a price beside it tells a reader how little
an instrument sees and nothing about whether that was the right call.*
