## 2. The forward question, tested — and it bit

**Asked on 09-21:** *does any surface — the sitemap's `lastmod`, the feeds' `pubDate`, the
rendered `<time>`, the ISO-week buckets — disagree with the frontmatter about which day a
piece was published?*

**Answer: yes. The rendered date, on every page, on any runner behind UTC.**

`z.coerce.date()` parses a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` as **UTC midnight**. Eight
`Intl.DateTimeFormat` instances — one per route that prints a dateline, four English and
four Arabic — then formatted that instant with **no `timeZone` option**, so each asked the
build machine what day it was.

**Proved against the served shape, not the source** (the 09-14 rule): the same commit,
built once under `TZ=UTC` and once under `TZ=America/Los_Angeles`, produced **116 of 121
pages different**, and `2026-07-07-singapore-psle-sbb` printed **July 6, 2026** in English
and the matching shift in Arabic-Indic digits.

Our CI is UTC. The operation's calendar is Asia/Dubai, UTC+4. Both land on the correct side
of midnight. **Four months of correct datelines were luck.**

**Fixed:** `timeZone: 'UTC'` named at all eight sites, with the reason at the point of
change. **Control after the fix: a Los Angeles build is byte-identical to a UTC build
across all 121 pages** — #58's property restored on a second axis. Filed as **ruling #60,
a date is not a moment.**

### Why no existing check could see it

Three instruments touch dates, and all three compare a date to something other than the
field that produced it:

- `qa_lastmod` asserts sitemap dates against a **git** ceiling — file provenance.
- `qa_ar_language` asserts the Arabic listing date is in Arabic-Indic **digits** — script.
- `qa_feed_enclosures` never reads `pubDate` at all.

A shifted date is well-formed, correctly scripted and plausibly provenanced. It is simply
the wrong day. **Nothing had ever compared a rendered date to its own frontmatter**, which
is the whole of #60's binding clause.

### Two traps caught while proving it, and the second is the better finding

**(a) The first draft failed its own control, 46 findings on a known-good build.** Its
carrier regex closed on a fixed `</div>|</p>`, so one `<span class="piece__date">` on the
editions index swallowed every list item after it and paired nine dates with the first slug
on the page. The 09-14 trap on the input side: *an assertion scoped wider than the thing it
checks*. Fixed by capturing the element's own tag name.

**(b) Bite 4 did not bite, and the tool printed PASS.** A date carrier whose contents the
language's date pattern could not match was being **skipped**. So the Latin-digits
injection — *the real 09-21 defect, nine Arabic browse pages for seven days* — walked
straight through, while the listing total quietly fell from 232 to 231 and the sentence
still read PASS. A carrier that yields nothing readable is now a finding, not a skip.
**A total that moves is a check that stopped checking something**, and nothing in a PASS
line tells you which way its own number moved. That generalises past this tool and belongs
beside #52.

**(c) One declared exemption, named rather than skipped.** The English editions index
carries one date with no frontmatter behind it — the VALENCE standing row, served from
`public/` with a hand-written *August 2026* (08-17 orphan audit). It is declared by URL in
the tool, and the tool **fails if the count of such rows is ever anything but one**, so a
second unfrontmattered date cannot arrive behind the exemption. The Arabic editions index
still has no VALENCE row; that remains an open editorial question from 08-17, not a defect.
