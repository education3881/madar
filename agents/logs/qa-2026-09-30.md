## 1. The 09-29 forward question, tested first: **which of the twenty-six could have its bite frozen into a fixture, and which is cheapest?**

**Audited all 27.** `qa_patch_queue` is the only one with fixtures; the other 26 each prove themselves
once, by a bite that is then discarded.

**The distinction this is really about, stated because it is not the one `qa_census` already covers.**
`qa_census` asserts that each instrument enumerated the population it names — it would catch a check
that suddenly reads zero pages. It cannot catch a check that reads **all** the pages and has lost the
ability to **recognise** the defect, because every scope decision inside a check (a class name, a tag
set, an exemption regex) is a guess about served markup, and a template change can move a string out
of scope silently. **`qa_census` guards the population; only a fixture guards the judgment.**

**Cheapest first: `qa_a11y_lang`**, on three grounds — its judgment (`LangAudit`) is a pure function of
an HTML string, so a fixture needs no fake `dist`; **four distinct historical wrong-readings are already
written down in its own docstring as prose**; and the two real bites that proved it on 2026-09-13 are
one line each. *An instrument that documented its own mistakes has already paid for its fixtures; it
just never cashed them.* That is the selection rule for the next conversion, and it is better than
"simplest check".

`web/.astro` is gitignored, so the deploy never sees it.

cache, not to argue about the warning.*

## THE NAMED FORWARD QUESTION FOR TOMORROW

Today's three findings on the Rwanda pair all live in `sources[]`, not in prose — and the reason is
structural. The 09-29 pair verdict rewrote **twelve** annotations on that pair for their serving-state
clause, and **nothing re-read the rest of what those annotations assert.** A correction pass is scoped
to the defect that prompted it; the annotation around it inherits a presumption of innocence it never
earned.

So: **an annotation is the only prose in this publication that no instrument reads.** The body has the
numeral multiset, the eight axes, the four non-numeric rows and `qa_packet_figures` downstream of it.
`sources[]` has a URL check (`qa_sources_alive`, third-party reachability only) and nothing else. Every
claim an annotation makes *about the document* — its date, where that date is served, its byte count,
its extraction method, its serving state, what it does and does not say — is checked by a human reading
it once, at compose, and then never again. **Two of today's six items were exactly that: an annotation
that was true when written and a claim about a page that was never true.**

**The question, and it is meant to be cheap to answer:** *which claims in `sources[]` are machine-
checkable against the document itself, rather than against the URL's status code?* Two are obvious
today — **a byte count** (we assert 4,023,048 and 5,224,340 in prose; a fetch can compare) and **a
declared fetch date against the newest date in the annotation's own text**. A third is harder and more
valuable: **a claim of the form "this page carries no date"**, which is a property of served HTML and
which we got wrong today on a page we had got right one annotation earlier.

**And the sharper one underneath it, which no check can answer and should therefore be a rule:** when a
verdict corrects one clause of an annotation, is the rest of that annotation re-read, or does the
correction's scope become the re-read's scope? Today says the second. Tomorrow should test it on the
five pairs whose annotations were never re-read at all.
