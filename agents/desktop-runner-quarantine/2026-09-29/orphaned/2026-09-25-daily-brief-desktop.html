And it was wrong. There is a second trap underneath, which the patch could not have known to state: actions/checkout cleans the workspace by default, so a checkout placed after the artifact download deletes the artifact.tar and dist/ that the byte-compare depends on. It goes first, or it silently destroys the assertion it was added to serve. Both closed, both proved.

The part worth your minute is why the patch was wrong, because it is not carelessness. The run that staged it measured the tool beautifully — 16/16 revalidations, re-measured 16/16 today. What it could not measure was the job, because it could not open the job’s file. A repair staged where it cannot be applied is proved for its tool and unproved for its site. Those are two artefacts and they need two proofs. That is ruling #66, and it was earned twice in an hour.

Research · ruling #66, earned twice in an hour
Read the field that enforces, not the label that describes. A description of a state is written by us; the state is obeyed by a machine. Both exist in every system we operate through — a scheduler’s description beside its enabled flag, a CI job’s comment block beside its step list — nothing keeps them in agreement and nothing reports it when they part.
Two origins, one shape: the scheduler labelled PAUSED and enabled, and a patch that described a defect correctly and a landing site it had never read. In both the artefact read was a description we maintain, and the artefact that governs was a field a machine obeys. Filed as Section 1 row 61 with §3’s range, §3’s heading and §1’s count line moved in the same commit — register reconciled by counting rows: 66 rows, 1–66, no gaps, no duplicates.
