| Piece | Tags | Gate |

- **The four country exonyms are the standard Arabic forms**, and `السودان` correctly carries the
  definite article where the other three correctly do not.
- **`#المعلمون` is nominative plural, which is right** — a hashtag occupies no syntactic position, so
  there is no case to govern it, and the nominative is the citation form.
- **One note, recorded and not blocking.** `#التعليم_في_الطوارئ` compresses the fuller register
  `التعليم في حالات الطوارئ`, which is the form UN Arabic uses. Compression is idiomatic in a tag and
  is what Arabic-language education accounts write; **it would be returned if it appeared in prose**,
  and this gate is not prose. Stated so the next composer knows the line rather than guessing it.
- **No named human appears in any tag**, so no transliteration is owed and **no gender determination
  arises** — which is worth one line, because #86's whole subject is determinations made by one
  edition alone.
- **Checked against Egypt's pre-existing set rather than against itself.** Egypt's caption already
  carried `#مصر #سياسات_التعليم #أفريقيا`. The five composed sets use **`أفريقيا`, not `إفريقيا`**, and
  Zambia reuses `#سياسات_التعليم` verbatim — so the pattern the 10-13 note said it was following, it
  actually follows. *A consistency claim checked against the thing it claims consistency with, which is
  #57 applied to prose.*
