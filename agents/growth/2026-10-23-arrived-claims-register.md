## Two detectors, two numbers, and the difference is the finding

Item 25 reports **78 future-dated mentions across 22 of 44 EN pieces**. Today's detector reports
**91 forward-dated mentions across 37 of 44**, of which **11 have arrived**.

**Neither number is wrong and they are not measuring the same set.** This detector counts a mention
only if its date is **after the piece's own publication date** — a *forward* claim in the sense that
matters, rather than any date in the future of some fixed epoch — and it reads day, month and bare-
year precision separately. That is a different population, and the honest statement is the one #113
made this morning about acronyms and #85 made about names: **two recognisers over one corpus
returning different populations is the ordinary condition, and the number is only meaningful beside
the rule that produced it.** The rule here is written down so the next run can reproduce it:

> a `D Month YYYY`, `Month YYYY` or bare `20NN` token in a served EN body, whose resolved date is
> strictly later than the piece's own `date:` frontmatter. Month precision resolves to the 28th, bare
> year to 31 December, and a bare year before 2026 is discarded.
