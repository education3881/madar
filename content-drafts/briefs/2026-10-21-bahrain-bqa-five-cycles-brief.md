## The finding that decides this commission, and it was not in the recon

**This publication already has a Bahrain BQA piece, and it ends by asking for this one.**
`2026-05-26-bahrain-bqa-public-grades` — *"Bahrain grades its schools, by name, in public"* — closes
on two open questions, the second of which reads, verbatim and in the served text today:

> *"The second harder question is whether the four-grade scale flatters or punishes the median
> school, and whether the BQA's distribution across the four grades reflects the school system or
> reflects the inspectorate's calibration. **The 2024–2025 announcement does not give the
> cross-tabulation; the question is the kind that earns a follow-up piece, not a parenthetical
> here.**"*

The recon's register **is** that cross-tabulation: 882 numbered reviews across five cycles, every
grade the Authority has ever issued to a named government school, on the Kingdom's own open-data
portal, and it was there the whole time. So this is not a second piece on one document; it is **the
follow-up the first piece promised, 148 days later, answered from a register the predecessor did not
know existed.** That is the strongest *why now* available and both ends of it are first-party.

**Binding consequences of the overlap, and they are not optional:**

1. **The predecessor is named in the body, with its date and its question quoted.** A follow-up that
   does not say what it is following reads as a repeat. #41 — *supersede, don't resurrect* — applies
   in its dated-follow-up form: this is a new reading of a new register, not a rewrite.
2. **Nothing the predecessor already said is re-reported as news.** The 76 government / 23 private /
   22 kindergarten / 19 vocational counts, the 24-improved / 45-held movement, Dr. Maryam Hasan
   Mustafa's quotation and the publish-by-name posture are all **in the published piece**. They may
   be referred to as established; they may not carry a paragraph.
3. **`related:` carries the predecessor**, both editions.

## The spine, and it is one sentence

> A regulator that has graded its own government schools by name, in public, in two languages, for
> seventeen years — whose most common verdict on itself across 882 reviews is **`(3) Satisfactory`
> (350)**, with **`(4) Inadequate` (245) ahead of `(2) Good` (202)** and **`(1) Outstanding` at 85** —
> awarded 22 of those schools a Gold Seal in 2025 *"for outstanding performance."*

No superlative of ours. Both halves first-party. The tension sits **inside one authority's own
documents**, which is where this publication prefers it, and the predecessor's question — *does the
scale flatter or punish the median school* — is answered by the shape of that distribution rather
than by our opinion of it.

**AMENDED 2026-10-22 — the recount corrected the recon twice, and both corrections are population

mention monitoring visits at all.

The batch percentages and the cycle percentages are **both first-party and they disagree** — 26%
Outstanding against 17% — because one is a year's cohort and the other is an unfinished cycle to
date. **That disagreement is not an error in either register, it is the single easiest sentence in
this piece to get wrong, and the draft states both clocks wherever either number appears.** 26% of 76
is ≈20 schools rated Outstanding in 2024-25; 22 government schools received a Gold Seal in 2025.
**Those two are never subtracted, never divided, and never set in one sentence without both clocks.**

Also carried from the Edition 05 commissions, because they outlive that edition: no "best" without
its ruler in the same sentence; no composite of rulers; improvement never without its floor;
**announced ≠ measured, labelled at the figure**; a pair carries only what both languages share (#42).

**C5 is OPEN.** Every sentence that uses a C5 figure says so. A cycle in progress is not a result.

## Five-test expectations

* **Test 1 — sourcing reality.** All five registers re-opened at verdict, dates recorded. The CSV
  counted again from the bytes, not carried from the recon's table.
* **Test 2 — specificity.** The piece must be impossible to relocate by find-and-replace. It has
  seventeen years of one authority's own scale, in that authority's two languages, with a named
  instrument (the Gold Seal) and a named framework (Schools Reviews Framework 2024).
* **Test 3 — why now.** The predecessor's own question, quoted and dated, plus a C5 cycle that closes
  in April 2026. **This test is the strongest part of the commission and the draft should lead with
  it, not bury it.**
* **Test 4 — dignity.** The BQA publishes verdicts most states would not. A distribution with 245
  `Inadequate` rows in it is not a scandal and must not be written as one; it is what a scale looks
  like when a regulator uses the whole of it. Schools are named in the register; **this piece names no
  school by its grade.**
* **Test 5 — long arc.** Slow, specific, GCC-anchored, one register read properly. Close on what the
  distribution cannot say, not on a moral.
