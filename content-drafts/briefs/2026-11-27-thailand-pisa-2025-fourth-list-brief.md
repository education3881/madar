## The finding

**Thailand's national PISA centre printed a list that the OECD's own three registers do not.** The
institute's PISA 2025 press notice, published on 8 September 2026, the day of the global launch, states that
**eight** countries and economies raised their average in **all three** subjects between PISA 2022 and
PISA 2025 with statistical significance, and names them: Georgia, Cambodia, Jordan, Montenegro, the United
Arab Emirates, Thailand, Türkiye and the Philippines.

Piece 1 of this edition set out the release's three improver lists and showed that each runs on its own
clock. **All three are about science alone.** This is a fourth list, on List A's three-year clock but across
all three subjects, and every one of its eight names is already on List A's sixteen. So the fourth list is
not a rival to the first three; it is a **strict subset** of one of them, and the half of List A it keeps is
exactly half.

Thailand's own numbers are the second half of the piece, and they do two things at once. Measured against
2022, every one of them is up: science by 23 points, mathematics and reading by 13 each. Measured against
Thailand's own nine rounds since 2000 — the institute's own published series — its 2025 science average is
its **second-highest ever** and its 2025 mathematics and reading averages are its **second-lowest ever**.
One year, three rises, two of them from the system's own floor. The register says so itself: the trend since
2000 in mathematics and reading is downward, and in science it is statistically unchanged.

## Prohibitions

- **Thailand's averages are never set beside the OECD averages in a sentence that computes the distance**,
  except where **R5 itself** does it — and R5 does it twice, for the proficiency shares and for the equity
  gap. Where R5 states a comparison, quote R5's comparison. Where it does not, print the two figures and
  let them stand apart.
- **The OECD averages in this piece are IPST's reporting of them, and are attributed to IPST**, not to the
  OECD, wherever R1 and R2 do not carry the same figure. R1 and R2 do carry 482 in science, 463 in
  mathematics and 461 in reading for 2025; they do **not** carry the 2022-to-2025 falls of 14 in reading and
  3 in science, nor the 500-point computational-problem-solving average. Those are R5's, said as R5's.
- **"Statistically significant" is R5's own hedge on the eight-name list and it travels with the claim.**
  The list is of systems whose rise met that test, not of systems that rose.
- **Nothing about why any of the eight rose.** No register in hand says. Thailand's own notice gives
  recommendations, not causes, and they are printed as recommendations.
- **Nothing about Thailand's rank.** R5 prints no rank for Thailand and the piece prints none (#29 — a
  ranking is not a measurement).
- **The subset claim is arithmetic on two quoted lists and is shown, not asserted.** Name the eight, name
  List A's sixteen, and let the reader see that eight of sixteen is the overlap. Do not say the OECD
  "omitted" or "suppressed" a list: the OECD's three lists answer three questions about science, and a
  question nobody asked is not a question avoided.
- **The seven other names on the fourth list are named once and not characterised.** Türkiye, Montenegro and
  Georgia are out of scope as subjects; they appear here as the register prints them, in a list, which
  `docs/10_geo_scope.md` permits. The piece's subject stays Thailand.
- **Jordan is the one exception, and it is a corroboration and not a comparison.** Jordan's own national
  centre said on the same day that its three averages rose with statistical significance; Thailand's centre
  independently names Jordan among the eight. Two first-party registers, in two languages, agreeing on one
  fact about a third party is worth one paragraph and no more, and it carries a link to this edition's
  Jordan piece rather than restating its figures.
- **The school-group figures are IPST's categories and keep IPST's names.** "Opportunity-expansion schools",
  "science-focused schools" and "demonstration schools" are administrative groups under named authorities,
  not quality tiers, and nothing here ranks them.
- **No Buddhist-era year reaches the reader unconverted, and no converted year is printed without being
  right.** R5 dates itself 8 กันยายน 2569 and dates the test administration สิงหาคม 2568; those are
  8 September 2026 and August 2025. The self-regulated-learning results are promised for 2570, which is 2027.
- Every figure carries its scale, its two years and whose average it is, in the sentence that makes the
  claim.
- No editorial future without its dated register attached (#117).

สถาบันส่งเสริมการสอนวิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี and its Thai abbreviation is สสวท. Both forms are in R5's served
text and the annotation records which is which. **สสวท. is not an acronym of the English name**, so the
piece does not present it as one.
