## Why this piece, and why it could not have been commissioned yesterday

The cell-3 verdict on the Uzbekistan piece put a condition in writing: **the seventh piece of this edition
may not be commissioned without a named human from a reachable non-OECD register**, or the Editor would
rather Edition 06 stop at six. Three of this edition's pieces carry Mathias Cormann and two carry no human
at all; a fourth Cormann would have been decoration, and the edition would have ended having quoted one
man and nobody else about the schooling of fifteen-year-olds in seven countries.

**The condition is met, and not narrowly.** `pisa.anep.edu.uy` is Uruguay's national PISA centre, it
answers this runner **200**, it is first-party, it is not the OECD, and its team page names **eight
people** with their functions — **Laura Noboa, Coordinadora nacional**, and seven others. See the recon.

**And it is met by a method rather than by luck**, which is the part to keep: `anep.edu.uy` was the only
ministry front door of eight tested that answered, and it carries exactly one `pisa` link. *A register is
a document, not a page* — the rule that unblocked the Qatar piece — pointed at a ministry instead of at a
report.

## The frame

**Uruguay is the first system in Edition 06 that has published its own reading of PISA 2025.** Every piece
this edition has shipped ends with a paragraph saying no first-party register was reachable: the UAE's,
Saudi Arabia's, Uzbekistan's all say it in almost the same words. Uruguay has published **four volumes and
a technical report**, dated **Setiembre de 2026**, contemporaneous with the OECD's 8 September launch.

So this piece does the one thing the edition has not been able to do once: **set the OECD's account of a
country beside that country's own account of itself, both read in served text, and report where they
agree and where they do not.**

**The spine is the clock habit this edition was built on, inverted.** Edition 06 has asked of four systems
*which clock is this list running on*. For Uruguay **the two clocks agree** — science positive on both
(+9 over three years, +11 fitted across the decade), reading negative on both (−7, −10), mathematics
negative on both (−4, −14). Nothing changes sign with the table. **And Uruguay is still on both of the
release's decade science lists**, because both of them are about science alone. That is the sharper
version of this edition's lesson: *the clocks were never the only thing a riser list hides — the subject
is the other one, and a system can be on a list of improvers for a decade while two of its three subjects
fall on every instrument the report prints.*

## Parking conditions — any ONE of these parks the piece at cell 2 or cell 3

1. **If the ANEP registers go dark before compose**, the piece parks. It does **not** fall back to an
   archive snapshot of them without saying so in the annotation, and it does not fall back to the OECD
   alone — a piece whose whole reason is the first-party account cannot be written without it.
2. **If no figure in Uruguay's own volumes can be traced to a page**, the piece parks rather than
   characterising the volumes in general terms. *An account we could not read is not an account we may
   describe.*
3. **If the only disagreement found between the two accounts is a rounding difference**, the piece does
   not report it as a disagreement. Say they agree, and say it plainly.
4. **The named human must appear with a function the register states**, not as decoration. If the piece
   has nothing for Laura Noboa or any colleague to be named *for*, the Test 2 debt is not discharged by
   putting the name in a sentence, and the Editor would rather read why.

## Specific instructions

- **Gender determinations are LICENSED here and must be carried in both annotations (#134).** The register
  prints `Coordinadora`, feminine where `Coordinador` exists. This is the first piece in the edition whose
  named humans come with the register's own gendered tokens, and it is the clean counter-case to the two
  corrections made on 2026-11-28, where English printed an ungendered title, no register carried a
  pronoun, and the Arabic edition was forced to determine with nothing behind it. **Read the token off the
  register and carry it verbatim; never off the name.**
- **The `Mtra. Daysi Iglesias` / `CONSEJERO` mismatch in R4 is an extraction artefact until proven
  otherwise** and must be settled against the rendered page before any R4 name is used. Do not resolve it
  from the text layer.
- **`aproximadamente 6.500` keeps its hedge** in the sentence that uses it, in both editions.
- **Percentage points and score points stay apart**, as in every piece of this edition. I.2.8's `−4.7` and
  `0.0` are percentage points; `9`, `−7`, `−4`, `11`, `−10`, `−14` are score points. **Uruguay's `0.0` is
  a change of zero to one decimal place and not an absence** — it is printed where Uzbekistan's row prints
  `0.5` — and a sentence that reads it as "no top performers" would be the worst error available here.
- **The three-year and decennial figures are two instruments and are never subtracted**, exactly as the
  Saudi and UAE pieces establish. That the signs agree makes the temptation worse, not better: agreement
  is not commensurability.
- **Spanish is read in the original** and quoted in Spanish in both editions, per the plan's rule for
  Edition 08 applied early. By #160 as restated on 2026-11-27, a third-language register makes **Arabic
  the forced and better channel** — the Arabic edition quotes each Spanish span in the register's own
  script, and the English edition must do the same rather than translating.
- **Scope.** The subject is Uruguay, an in-scope geography on the Latin America open list. The OECD is a
  source. **Ceibal is out of scope for this piece** — it is an Edition 08 subject and reaching for it here
  would be the institution-not-geography error in a new coat.
- **No cause is offered for any figure unless a register offers one**, and if Uruguay's own volumes offer
  one, it is reported as *their* explanation with their hedges, never adopted as the piece's.
