## The angle, in one sentence

Abu Dhabi's regulator reports that **23 private schools rose from Acceptable to Good in one academic
year** under an inspection programme its own page says is **conducted biennially** — so the piece's whole
job is to establish, from the regulator's own registers, what the 23 is a count of: schools that were
measured twice inside a year, or a year's worth of publishing from a ruler that moves every two.

## Why this piece, why now

Two Bahrain pieces asked how much of the movement in a published distribution belongs to the schools and
how much to the instrument. `2026-11-12-england-ofsted-exempt-cohort` answered that in the **scale's**
unit — a ruler withdrawn after one use. This register asks it in the **cadence's** unit, which this corpus
has never used, and it asks it on a Gulf regulator with a full Arabic face, which the England piece did not
have. The question is not whether ADEK is doing something wrong; a biennial programme reporting annual
movement is ordinary practice. The question is what a parent reads off the number, and the answer is
currently undecidable from the page that prints it.

## The spine, every figure quoted from a register read in served text

**Load-bearing, and composed with the register open (#101, 2026-09-13): every sentence below is written
with the register in front of the drafter, never from this brief and never from the annotation.** Both
language faces of register 1 were read in served text on 2026-11-22 by the Researcher; the drafter re-opens
both.

**Register 1 — ADEK, "Abu Dhabi Private Schools Demonstrate Steady Improvement, Strengthening Parent
Confidence in Quality Education", dated on its own page 01 Oct 2025.**
EN: `https://www.adek.gov.ae/Media-Centre/News/Strengthening-Parent-Confidence-in-Quality-Education`
AR: `https://www.adek.gov.ae/ar-AE/Media-Centre/News/Strengthening-Parent-Confidence-in-Quality-Education`

1. **The movement claim.** *"23 private schools have advanced in Irtiqa'a inspection results from
   Acceptable to Good in one academic year"* — and, in the body, *"23 schools advanced to Good within a
   single year according to the latest Irtiqa'a results."* The Arabic: *"ارتفع تصنيف 23 مدرسة خاصة من
   «مقبول» إلى «جيد» خلال عام واحد"*. **Two English sentences on one page, and they do not say the same
   thing**: *in one academic year* names an interval, *within a single year according to the latest
   results* names a publication. The piece quotes both and says so.
2. **The distribution.** 13 Outstanding · 51 Very Good · 93 Good · 42 Acceptable · 5 Weak. **Sum 204.**
   Identical figures in both faces.
3. **The scale.** *"Schools are assessed on a 6-point scale: Outstanding, Very Good, Good, Acceptable,
   Weak, and Very Weak."* **Very Weak is defined and carries no row in the distribution.** The piece may
   state that and may NOT state that no school is Very Weak — a band with no row is not a band with a zero
   (#151: an absence in a register is a claim about a search, not about the world).
4. **The cadence.** *"Irtiqa'a inspections are conducted biennially and evaluate schools across six core
   standards and 17 performance indicators."*
5. **The bar.** *"In line with national expectations, Good is the minimum standard for every school in the
   UAE."* And the consequence the same page attaches: schools at Acceptable or lower were under
   restrictions on capacity growth and grade expansion, lifted "only for schools demonstrating verified
   improvement", and *"Schools remain restricted from enrolling new Emirati students until further
   regulatory requirements are met."*
6. **The framework's owner.** *"Irtiqa'a, operating under the UAE Unified School Inspection Framework,
   remains the core regulatory tool to drive school improvement."*

## The two language faces disagree about a CATEGORY, and that is a finding, not a translation note

The English separates the **scale** (six grades) from the **standards** (six graded domains). The Arabic
calls both **معايير**: *"تُقيّم المدارس على ستة معايير رئيسية للأداء هي: متميز، جيد جداً، جيد، مقبول،
ضعيف، ضعيف جداً"* for the grades, and *"وفق ستة معايير و17 مؤشراً للأداء"* for the domains. Both sets
number six. **An Arabic reader meets "six معايير" twice, with two different referents, and the page
distinguishes them nowhere.** Separately, the English heads the five rows "Sector-wide Performance
Results" while the Arabic heads them *"نتائج تحسن الأداء على مستوى الإمارة"* — performance **improvement**
results, which is a claim the five static rows do not carry.

This is standing-queue item 81 arriving as editorial material: *a register that publishes in two languages
is two documents that can disagree.* **The Arabic Content Creator composes from the Arabic face and the
English drafter from the English face, and neither may silently harmonise them** — where they differ, both
editions say that the register differs, in that edition's own words.

## The three guardrails this commission is granted under

1. **No composite.** The piece may not build a single index out of the scale and the standards, or out of
   Irtiqa'a and the separately-published National Identity Mark. Two instruments grading one school are
   two measurements.
2. **Announced ≠ measured** (the Edition 05 standing prohibition, and #117). *23 advanced* is a claim the
   regulator published on a stated date; it is written with that date attached or it is not written.
3. **No proportion the register does not print.** 204 is a sum this brief computed from five printed rows
   and it may be stated as such. **Percentages of 204 are forbidden until owed read 3 establishes whether
   204 is the population**, because a share of a subset read as a share of a sector is #111's defect.

## The inconvenient half (mandatory, and it cuts at our own frame)

The cadence tension may dissolve. A biennial cycle plus mandatory follow-up inspections for schools at
Acceptable or lower is a perfectly coherent design under which 23 schools genuinely can move inside one
year — and if the register says so anywhere, **the piece's spine becomes the reporting rather than the
instrument**, and the honest version is shorter and less interesting. The drafter looks for that sentence
first and hardest. If it exists, the piece is re-briefed, not bent.

Second, this operation has published two Bahrain pieces and one England piece on inspection instruments.
**A third piece about a regulator's own ruler risks being a house formula rather than a finding**, and the
Editor is asked to weigh exactly that at the five-test verdict. The defence, if there is one, is that the
unit is new (cadence, not scale) and the bilingual register is a channel the earlier three did not have.

## The four reads owed, in order, and the first one gates the draft

1. **The UAE Unified School Inspection Framework, first-party from the Ministry of Education.** If the six
   bands and the biennial cadence are **national**, the subject is a national instrument applied in one
   emirate and the piece says so from the first paragraph. **Cell 2 does not open until this is read.**
2. **A prior-year ADEK distribution**, so the yield has two endpoints.
3. **ADEK's own count of private schools it regulates**, to settle whether 204 is the population. Until
   then, no percentages (guardrail 3).
4. **Whether per-school ratings are published** (School Performance Plaques, the "Rayah" platform), which
   decides whether the distribution's members are checkable or only assertable.
