## The angle, in one sentence

England's schools regulator spent eight years not inspecting its best-graded schools, went back in 2021–22
and graded them with the current instrument, and published the yield beside the stock in one release —
which is the only direct measurement this publication has found anywhere of **how much of a published
distribution belongs to the schools and how much to the instrument** — and then, in two further steps, it
removed the overall grade altogether and told parents in writing that *"the new grades can't be compared
to the old ones."*

## Why this piece, why now

`2026-10-23-bahrain-bqa-five-cycles` shipped on 2026-11-09 and closed on a sharpened question it said
could not be answered: *how much of the movement in a published distribution belongs to the schools, and
how much to the instrument — which no amount of the Authority's admirable openness can settle, because
the register that would settle it is one nobody publishes anywhere.* **That last clause is ours, it is
published, and it is wrong.** One regulator does publish it, because one regulator ran the experiment on
itself by accident of policy. The honest place for that correction is a piece.

The *now* is not ours either: Ofsted published `State-funded schools inspections and outcomes as at 31
August 2026` on **10 November 2026**, two days before this brief, and it is the first release of a
framework that came into effect on **10 November 2025**. The piece is read in the reader's present
(#117), and the reader's present is the first full year of an instrument that does not produce the number
the old series was about.

## The spine, every figure quoted from a register read in served text

**Load-bearing, and composed with the register open (#101, 2026-09-13): every sentence below is written
from the quoted document, not from this brief.** The drafter re-opens both registers at compose time
(#41).

1. **The freeze, dated in the register's own words.** *"Between May 2012 and November 2020, outstanding
   primary and secondary schools were exempt from routine inspection."* *"In November 2020, the government
   lifted its exemption."* — `…as at 31 August 2022`, main findings.
2. **The stock at the moment the frozen cohort was re-graded.** *"Eighty-eight per cent of all schools are
   good or outstanding"*, and the same release's Figure 1 table gives 21,725 schools at **18% outstanding,
   70% good, 9% requires improvement, 3% inadequate** as at 31 August 2022; the 88% *"has increased from
   86% in August 2021, and had been stable at 86% since 2019."*
3. **The yield.** *"Only 17% of previously exempt schools that had a graded inspection this year remained
   outstanding."* *"Of these, 17% retained their outstanding grade, while 21% were judged requires
   improvement or inadequate. The remainder were judged to be good."*
4. **The split, which is the piece's one genuinely surprising figure.** *"Fourteen per cent of previously
   exempt primary schools inspected this year remained outstanding"*, *"compared with 25% of secondary
   schools"* — Figure 3's table: primary **263 schools** (14% outstanding / 65% good / 18% RI / 3%
   inadequate), secondary **104 schools** (25% / 54% / 14% / 7%).
5. **The register's own statement that its stock is stale.** *"Sixty-four per cent of all schools have not
   had a graded inspection in the last five academic years."*
6. **Then the instrument changed twice.** From September 2024 the overall grade stopped being awarded
   (**owed read — open gate 2**); from **10 November 2025** a renewed education inspection framework (EIF)
   grades six core evaluation areas on a five-point scale — *"'exceptional' · 'strong standard' ·
   'expected standard' · 'needs attention' · 'urgent improvement'"* — with safeguarding reported
   separately as *"'met' or 'not met'"*.
7. **And the regulator says the series does not join.** *"The main change is that we no longer give one
   overall judgement, such as 'good', 'outstanding' or 'requires improvement'."* *"It's important to
   understand that the new grades can't be compared to the old ones: this is a different approach to
   inspection and a new way of reporting to you."* — Ofsted guidance for parents, published 1 December
   2025, last updated 2 November 2026.
8. **The first year of the new instrument, in its own units.** *"There are currently 21,957 state-funded
   schools."* *"Between 10 November 2025 and 31 August 2026, we have carried out 3,262 inspections, of
   which 3,188 were full inspections."* Graded *'expected standard' or better*: 90% inclusion, 78%
   curriculum and teaching, 72% achievement, 84% attendance and behaviour, 95% personal development and
   wellbeing, 85% leadership and governance. *"These statistics are published as official statistics in
   development."*

## The three guardrails this commission is granted under

Standing prohibitions in `_STANDING_LEDGER.md` bind as written. These three bind harder here, and a draft
that breaks any of them is returned rather than noted:

1. **The grade profile is not a stock figure, and the register says so.** *"The 3,188 full inspections
   carried out represent only 15% of all state-funded schools, so the grade profile is not representative
   of all schools."* *"As we inspect lower-performing schools more often than high-performing schools, the
   mix of schools inspected in any single year is not typically representative of all schools."* Any
   sentence putting a 2026 evaluation-area percentage next to the 2022 stock percentages must carry that
   disqualification in the same sentence, in the register's own grammar. **This is #29/#30 on a ruler
   rather than on a superlative: there is no composite of the two scales, and the piece may not build
   one.**
2. **THE 17% COLLISION.** Two different 17%s, both Ofsted, both about inspected schools, meaning opposite
   things: 2022 — *previously exempt schools that remained outstanding*; 2026 — *this year's inspected
   schools that had been RI or inadequate at their previous full inspection*. **Every "17%" in this piece
   carries its year, its direction and its denominator.** A numeral check cannot catch this, because both
   figures are correctly cited; the error would be in the reader's head, and it is the Editor's to refuse.
3. **A comparison the register declines to make may not be attributed to it.** The 2022 release's
   framework changes appear only in chart notes, and Figure 7's note says *"We have not compared the data
   with the 2020/21 academic year"* because *"we only carried out a small number of inspections during
   this year."*

## The inconvenient half (mandatory, and it cuts at our own frame)

This piece is structurally a story about measurement, so the inconvenient half is **the case for the
regulator**, not another grievance against it:

1. **The frozen cohort's 17% is not proof that the instrument is the whole story.** Eight years passed. A
   school that was outstanding in 2013 and good in 2022 may have changed; so may its intake, its
   leadership and its area. The release measures a cohort whose grade was held constant by rule, which
   removes *one* confound and not the others — and the piece must say which one. **This is the
   improvement-never-without-its-floor prohibition applied to a decline.**
2. **Replacing the single grade is defensible on its own terms**, and the guidance page's stated reason is
   about what parents can use. A piece that treats the removal as the destruction of a time series is
   making an argument the regulator never undertook to serve: a parent choosing a school is not a
   statistician, and the register is not kept for us.
3. **The old series was already unreliable as a stock, by the register's own 64% sentence** — so some of
   what looks like a break caused by the new framework was already a break caused by the inspection
   interval.
4. **And our own piece carries the cost.** The Bahrain single's closing clause was wrong. It is corrected
   in the body of this piece, in our own name, with the date it was published.

## The two open gates — **BOTH RESOLVED 2026-11-13.** The bars are left standing below, with what cleared each

**Gate 1 CLEARED.** Ofsted's own release *Hundreds of formerly outstanding schools reinspected*, 22
November 2022, prints *"Ofsted's Chief Inspector, Amanda Spielman, said:"* and then puts her on the
record about this cohort and this policy — *"The exemption was a policy founded on the hope that high
standards, once achieved, would never drop, and that freedom from inspection might drive them even
higher"* and *"These outcomes show that removing a school from scrutiny does not make it better."* The
operator of the instrument the piece is about, inside the 2021–26 dateline. The union voices on the same
story are **representative, not operational**, and are not carried.

**Gate 2 CLEARED, and it corrected this brief.** The removal was announced by the **Department for
Education** on **2 September 2024** — *"Single headline grades for schools will be scrapped with
immediate effect"* — not by the regulator. The body may now print the date, **attributed to the
Department**, and **may not attribute the removal to Ofsted**. The 10 November 2025 framework is the
regulator's own act; the 2 September 2024 grade removal is the Department's. Two instrument changes,
two owners, fourteen months apart.

**Gender read CLEARED and this brief's assumption about it was wrong.** The sentence below says English
registers carry pronouns. The release the voice comes from carries **none** for her. It is settled on a
second first-party register, Ofsted's own speech page, whose standfirst prints *"Ofsted's Chief Inspector
reflects on **her** first speech at the Festival of Education"* — so the hand-off line is `named humans —
gender read from register: 1 of 1`, a read under #86 clause 1 and not a declaration under clause 2. The
trap, named: her own text says *"His Majesty's Chief Inspector"*, and that **His** is the monarch's.

### The bars as they were set on 2026-11-12, unaltered

1. **The operational voice.** The Saudi bar holds without exception: **no named, operational, recent voice,
   no draft.** Nothing in either register read so far is one — the 2026 release names a statistician

   parents' guidance. Until it is, the body may say *"Ofsted no longer gives one overall judgement"* in
   the register's own present tense and **may not print a removal date**.
