## Why this candidate, and why it is not a fifth variation on one trick

Edition 06's method is *a list is only as good as its clock*. Four pieces have now filled four cells of that
frame, and the Editor's own verdict on the UAE row this evening set them out as a table. **This candidate is
not a fifth cell of the same table. It changes the axis from the clock to the UNIT.**

| Piece | Three years' both-ends list | The decade's both-ends list |
|---|---|---|
| Qatar | absent | on it |
| Saudi Arabia | on it | absent |
| UAE | on it | on it |
| **Uzbekistan** | **on it** | **cannot be on it** |

The last row is the one no piece has written. **Uzbekistan is named in the chapter's prose among the seven
systems that cut their low performers and grew their top performers between 2022 and 2025, and it has the
largest reduction of low performers in the entire release — −46.1 percentage points, where the next largest
is Cambodia at −22.1 and the OECD average-35 is +1.2.** And it appears in **neither** of the two tables that
print a change in score points. Not in Table I.2.6, not in Table I.2.9.

**So the release says Uzbekistan moved more than almost any system in it, and never once says by how many
score points.** The front-report page goes further: it names Uzbekistan in the list of systems whose
**"scores also went up"**, which is a claim about score points, printed on a page whose own report prints no
score-point change for this system anywhere. That is the spine. It is not a complaint about the OECD; it is
the thing a reader has to know before quoting any of it.

**The reason is in the register and must be in the piece.** Table I.2.9's note shows only systems with
**"at least two previous assessments among PISA 2015, PISA 2018 and PISA 2022"**, and Table I.2.6 only those
comparable in all three subjects across 2022 and 2025. **A system new to the instrument cannot be measured by
an instrument that works by subtraction.** Uzbekistan's absence is a missing instrument, not a verdict — the
distinction the UAE piece introduced on Jordan, here as a whole piece.

## Prohibitions, binding at commission

- **Never state or imply a score-point change for Uzbekistan.** The whole piece is that the register prints
  none. Not an estimate, not a range, not "roughly in line with". **This is the one prohibition whose breach
  parks the piece outright** rather than returning it.
- **−46.1 is percentage points of students and is never set against any score-point figure**, anyone's.
- **No significance claim on the chapter's table colour.** The both-ends membership is quoted **as prose**,
  from the passage that is itself about significance counts, exactly as the Saudi and UAE pieces do.
- **No cause for any figure, and in particular no account of why the share fell.** The register offers none.
  A reduction of 46.1 percentage points invites an explanation and the piece supplies none; if the drafter
  cannot resist, the piece is not ready.
- **Coverage is the one caution that must be IN the sentence** (#19). The chapter's own passage on PISA
  coverage says the expansion of enrolment **"makes it more difficult to interpret how mean scores in PISA
  have changed over time"**, and names systems whose coverage rose. **Read that passage in served text and
  establish whether Uzbekistan is named in it before writing the paragraph** — if it is named, that fact
  governs the whole piece and goes high; if it is not, say that it is not rather than implying either way.
- Every figure carries its unit, its years, its subject and whose average it is.
- **No editorial future without a dated register** (#117). *(The launch speech does carry one —
  *"We will publish the full results, and what they mean for policy, in May 2027."* — available if needed,
  and attributed.)*
