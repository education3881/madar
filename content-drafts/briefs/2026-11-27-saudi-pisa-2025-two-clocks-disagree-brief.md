## Why this candidate, and why it is not a repeat

Edition 06's method is *a list is only as good as its clock*. Three pieces have now applied it to three
different shapes, and this is the fourth and sharpest, because **it is the first where the two clocks point
in opposite directions in the same subject.**

| Piece | Three years (Table I.2.6) | The decade (Table I.2.9, fitted) |
|---|---|---|
| Jordan (shipped 11-26) | +32 / +20 / +26 | — |
| Thailand (shipped 11-27) | +23 / +13 / +13 | 4 / −20 / −16 |
| Qatar (shipped 11-27) | +2 / −1 / +2 | 19 / 18 / 12 — **decade up, three years flat** |
| **Saudi Arabia** | **+23 / +5 / +1** | **37 / −18 / 25** — **reading up over three years, DOWN over the decade** |

**The spine:** Saudi Arabia's science is up on both clocks and strongly. Its **reading** is +5 against 2022
and **−18** on the fitted decennial trend. Its **mathematics** is +25 over the decade and **+1** over three
years. One system, one instrument, one release — and the answer to *is reading improving in Saudi Arabia?*
depends entirely on which of the OECD's own two tables you open. That is this edition's thesis stated at its
strongest, and it is the first piece that can state it **inside one country and one subject** rather than by
setting two countries side by side.

  that is a statement the OECD makes in prose — quote it as prose.
