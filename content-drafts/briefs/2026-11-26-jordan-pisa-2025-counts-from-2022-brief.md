## The finding
Jordan's national PISA centre announced Jordan's PISA 2025 results at a press conference on
8 September 2026, the same day as the OECD's global launch, and reported its three domain averages rising
against **2022**: science 375 to 407, mathematics 361 to 388, reading 342 to 363, described as statistically
significant in all three. The OECD's own release names Jordan **once**, on the list of systems whose science
score rose **since 2022**, and on neither of its two ten-year lists. Both registers are measuring the same
three-year interval. Neither register read here gives Jordan a figure for any earlier round, so the span
Jordan's recovery is measured across is the whole of what can be said about it — which is piece 1's finding
arriving at a single country.

## Prohibitions
- **No comparison between Jordan's domain averages and the OECD averages.** R2 prints OECD averages for 2025;
  R4 prints Jordan's. Putting them in one sentence invites a gap figure neither register states, and the
  scales, the samples and the participation sets are not established here as comparable.
- **Nothing about Jordan's 2015 level, or its position before 2022, in any domain.** No register in hand
  carries it. Jordan's absence from the OECD's 2015-to-2025 list is a fact about **that list**, and it is not
  a statement that Jordan is below its 2015 level.
- **No claim that the OECD does or does not confirm Jordan's mathematics and reading rises.** The OECD's
  three lists are about science only. Silence on a domain is not a contradiction of it.
- **"Among the few participating countries that recorded improvement in all three domains" is R4's claim,
  attributed to Dr Abdullah Ababneh by name**, never restated as a finding of the OECD's or as a count. Same
  for "the largest among participating Arab countries that have comparable data."
- **The rank claim carries its hedge.** R4 says Jordan's position advanced among the 91 participants and
  prints no rank. Do not print or imply one (#29 — a ranking is not a measurement).
- **The narrowed gaps are not attributed a direction.** R4 reports the boys'/girls' and rural/badia school
  gaps narrowing and does not say whether it was the lower group rising or the higher falling. The OECD's own
  narrowed gap is explicitly the second of those. Set them side by side; do not merge them.
- **King Abdullah II Schools for Excellence "exceeding the international average and the OECD average" is
  R4's statement about a school category**, attributed, and it is not a statement about Jordan.
- No editorial future without its dated register attached (#117).
- Every figure carries its scale, its two years and whose average it is, in the sentence that makes the claim.
