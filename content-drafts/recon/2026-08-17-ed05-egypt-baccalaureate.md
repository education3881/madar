## 1. Why this story, under this frame

On **18 August 2025** the President ratified amendments to the Education Law creating the **Egyptian Baccalaureate (بكالوريا مصر)** — a new secondary-leaving certificate standing beside, not on top of, the 120-year-old **Thanaweya Amma**. It is the most consequential change to Egyptian secondary schooling in a generation, and it is a *build*: a state that has been told for decades that its exam culture is unfixable designed a replacement, legislated it, and opened it to a cohort.

The frame's honest version is not "Egypt fixed its exams." It is **an offer, and what happened when it was made.** Roughly a year on — the first cohort has now completed the preparatory year — the reported answer is that **almost everyone took the offer**. The reported opt-in for the 2026-27 intake is **99%**, on the order of **832,000 students**; the first year's figure is reported at about **95%**.

That number is the piece. A 99% take-up of a *voluntary* alternative is either the most emphatic verdict a school system has ever received from its own families, or it is not a verdict at all — because a choice that 99% make is worth reading only against what the other 1% were actually offered. Both readings are live in Egypt's own public record, and the piece can hold them without deciding for the reader. **That is exactly the shape Madār does best, and it lets the positive posture be earned rather than asserted.**

**Edition-arc fit.** Ed04 asked whether the number on a child's certificate means the same thing twice. This asks a prior question: *what if you replace the certificate?* Sits naturally beside recon 6 (the ruler question) and gives the Africa edition its North-African, native-Arabic, instrument-reform piece.

## 3. The instrument, in one paragraph (for the drafter)

Thanaweya Amma is a single terminal year in which one sitting of examinations, in a large number of subjects, produces one aggregate percentage that determines university placement through a national coordination office (التنسيق). The Egyptian Baccalaureate re-cuts that: a **preparatory year** (grade 10) with a broad core that includes second foreign language, programming and computer science **not counted toward the total**; then a **two-year main stage** in which the student carries a short core (Arabic, Egyptian history, first foreign language; religious education graded in the final year) plus a **track** — Medicine & Life Sciences, Engineering & Computer Sciences, Business, or Arts & Humanities — each with its own specialised subjects at standard or advanced level. Assessment is spread across the two years with **two sittings per year** and **retakes permitted**, the highest attempt counting, on a **700-point** scale. The first attempt in each subject is free; each further attempt costs **EGP 500**.

## 4. The findings this run produced (the reportable ones)

**(a) The announced instrument is not the enacted instrument — and the drift is in the load-bearing word.** In January 2025 the state's own English register described a system "**designed to replace** the current Thanaweya Amma education system" (Ahram, 9 Jan 2025, in served text). By July 2025 the bill that reached the floor introduced the certificate "**without abolishing** the existing Thanaweya Amma structure" (Al Manassa, 7 Jul 2025, in served text), and the enacted law carries **Article 37bis** guaranteeing the track is optional. Replacement became coexistence somewhere between the cabinet and the chamber. **Both statements are true of their own date** — this is a vintage finding (#21), not a contradiction to catch anyone out in, and the piece should date each rather than pick one.

**(b) The 99% is the story, and it is the figure most in need of its counterfactual.** See §5 — this run's ruling.

**(c) The doors were published before the exams existed.** The 25 July 2025 joint-ministry release mapped every track to its faculties and majors, explicitly engineering "parity" with the old streams. Announcing the *admissions consequence* a full two years before the first baccalaureate cohort sits its final exams is the single most persuasive evidence that this is an implemented instrument rather than an announced one — and it is the kind of accomplishment the founder's positive posture is for. It is also, quietly, what makes the 99% legible: families were choosing between two certificates whose university consequences had been published side by side.

**(d) The reform is the second layer, not the first.** August 2024 had already cut Thanaweya Amma's examined subjects to five per stream, in force from 2024-25. The baccalaureate lands on top of a system already one year into a change. Any before/after read on tutoring, attendance or density must say **which** reform it is measuring — a scope trap (#22) with two candidate treatments a year apart.

**(e) A candid fee to price.** "Free" and "EGP 500 per retake" are both accurate: the first attempt in every subject is free. But the instrument's headline benefit — *you get another chance* — is the part that is priced, and it is priced per subject per attempt. The reform's generosity and its cost are the same feature. EIPR's objection and the ministry's selling point describe one mechanism. **The piece should say this once, plainly, and not moralise it.**

## 5. The ruling this run earns — **#31: a voluntary-uptake figure carries its counterfactual**

**Statement.** A participation, opt-in or take-up rate is evidence of *preference* only if the unchosen option remained genuinely available on comparable terms. Where the alternative was administratively disfavoured — not offered at the local school, staffed thinly, reached only by transfer, or discouraged by officials with enrolment targets — the same percentage measures **availability**, not preference. Before a take-up figure rides, the drafter states **what the alternative was, who could still get it, and on what terms**; absent that, the figure is reported as *"N% enrolled"* and never as *"N% chose."*

**Why it needed a number of its own.** The seven figure axes (#12–#24) all interrogate a measurement: who administered it, over what coverage, at what stage, of what vintage, at what scope, in what direction, at what series position. A take-up rate is not a measurement of children — it is a **record of an administrative process**, and its validity depends on a *counterfactual* rather than on an instrument. Nearest kin is #14 (the funnel: target ≠ sat ≠ passed ≠ appointed), which polices stages within one pipeline; #31 polices the **choice set** the pipeline was offered against. It is also the mirror image of #29 (a ranking is not a measurement): #29 rejects a number whose ruler is unpublished; #31 qualifies a number whose *alternative* is unexamined.

**Egypt is the case, not the verdict.** Egypt's own public record supplies both halves — Article 37bis guarantees optionality; an MP and a parliamentary party allege the guarantee was hollowed by transfers and enrolment targets; the ministry says it instructed directors to inform and counsel. Madār does not adjudicate that from Cairo. It reports the 99% **with** the guarantee, the allegation and the ministry's answer in the same breath, and lets the reader hold all three.

**Binding on our own copy.** Madār has shipped take-up and coverage figures before (Vietnam's fee waiver, Morocco's 626→4,626 pioneer schools, Poland's enrolment gain). None is retro-corrected on this ruling, but every future one states its counterfactual, and the Verifier adds the question to the figure checklist: *what was the alternative, and to whom was it actually available?*

## 6. Draft-blocking homework (the trace list)

1. **The 99% / ~832,000, from the ministry's own count.** Currently a single business-newsletter register, body unread. Per #27 two independently-routed channels are required before it rides; per #29 the source class matters. Anchor to the MoE's own enrolment statement or CAPMAS. **State the denominator**: 832k of what — all grade-10 entrants, all general-secondary entrants, or all who submitted a preference form? The three differ, and the piece's spine sits on this number.
2. **The 2025/26 figure (~95%) and the 2026-27 figure (99%) side by side**, each with its own vintage and denominator. Two points make a trend only if they count the same thing.
3. **Article 37bis, verbatim, from the Official Gazette.** The optionality guarantee is the pivot of both readings; it must be quoted, not summarised.
4. **EIPR in its own words** (the EN press release is at a clean URL, unlike the percent-encoded Arabic slugs that hit the **URL-length wall** in recon 3 — see row 20). Quote the 22× decile figure and the 4%/2% constitutional floors from EIPR's own text, and give its own vintage (2019-20) in-sentence (#21).
5. **A voice that is not an official, an MP, or an NGO.** The story currently has ministers, a parliamentarian and a rights group — no teacher, no school director, no parent, no student. The minister met ~4,000 secondary directors on 31 Aug 2025; the director-level voice is the one that would carry both the build and the pressure. **Video-only quotes do not ride (#26).**
6. **The tutoring question, if it is to be raised at all.** EnterpriseAM's own standfirst names it as unproven. Egypt's private-tutoring economy is the thing every one of these reforms is ultimately aimed at; the piece may state the aim, but may not imply a result. If no measurement exists, say that no measurement exists — that sentence is itself reportable.
7. **The minister's before-figures** (density 120 → under 50; attendance 9% → 85%+; 32 subjects). These are the minister's claims in the minister's own register and carry a scope trap: attendance "not exceeding 9%" is implausible system-wide and almost certainly describes the terminal Thanaweya year, where attendance had collapsed into tutoring centres. **Do not print without the grade scope.** Two-channel or drop.
