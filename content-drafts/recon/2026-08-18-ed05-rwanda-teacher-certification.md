# Recon — Rwanda: the quality debt of an access expansion, and the programme that paid it

**Date:** 2026-08-18 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Ed05 recon bank, entry 8** · **Status: PROCEED — with three draft-blocking gaps named in §7**
**Slot:** East Africa (the Ed05 slate's first; the bank was West / North / continental before today)
**Posture:** positive, per the founder's 2026-08-11 frame. The accomplishment here is not the building — it is the *bookkeeping*: a government that opened a quality gap deliberately, wrote it down, and then closed it.

---

## 1. The instrument — and why it is two instruments, not one

**The emergency instrument.** In **April 2024** the **Rwanda Basic Education Board (REB)** opened a *Special Programme for the training and certification of uncertified pre-primary and primary teachers*, delivered **on weekends and public holidays** so no classroom time was lost. Four condensed modules drawn from the standard Teacher Training College (TTC) curriculum — **Educational Psychology, Pedagogy and Instruction, Inclusive Education, Subject Matter Education**. Delivery chain: **36 experienced facilitators → 600 trainers** (TTC instructors + education specialists) → sessions **across all 30 districts**. Method: **micro-teaching** — trainees study content independently, then teach short lessons to peers against structured observation checklists. Assessment: written tests, a comprehensive final exam, and a **practical micro-teaching evaluation conducted by NESA**; passing earns a **Professional Teaching Certificate** and official workforce status. Onsite **childcare** was provided so teachers with young children could attend — a design detail worth a sentence, because it is the kind of thing that decides who actually completes.

**The permanent instrument.** On **10 August 2026** MINEDUC announced that from **September 2026** TTC entrants take a **five-year programme instead of three** — explicitly to lift Rwanda's primary-teacher qualification toward the regional norm of a diploma or degree. Same ministry, same problem, opposite time horizon.

**Why the two belong in one piece:** the emergency programme certified the teachers the system had already hired; the five-year TTC decides who it hires next. A piece that carries only the first reads as a rescue; carrying both makes it a policy sequence — *expand access, incur a quality debt, name it, retire it, then raise the entry bar so the debt does not recur.* That is the argument, and it is the reason this is an Ed05 accomplishment rather than a press release.

**Origin of the debt (stated plainly, because the positive posture does not mean omitting it):** under the **Quality Basic Education for Human Capital Development (QBE) project (P168551)**, a national classroom build meant tens of thousands of new rooms needed teachers at once. Hiring criteria were **temporarily relaxed**; secondary-school graduates with subject specialisation but no TTC training entered classrooms and were carried in the system as **"uncertified teachers"** with training "underway." Rwanda created this category on purpose, on the record, with a plan attached. That is the story.

## 3. Figures, with axes flagged

**MEASURED / REPORTED BY THE IMPLEMENTER**
- **24,000 teachers trained and certified** — World Bank + REB, Mar 2026. Axes open: *is 24,000 the number trained, the number certified, or both?* The headline says "trained and certified"; no split is published. **See §7.1.**
- **~25,000 graduates** entered teaching under relaxed criteria (from 2021). The gap to 24,000 is ~1,000 and **unexplained** — attrition, failure, deferral, or rounding are all live.
- **Over 11,000 classrooms and 14,680 latrines** — MINEDUC × World Bank, the **project's** output.
- **~22,000 / 22,505 classrooms** — the **national** accelerated-construction programme total (government contribution reported at **Rwf 86bn** alongside the **US$200m** World Bank credit, of which ~**US$126m** was for the 11,000 classrooms + 14,680 latrines).
- **13 of 16 model schools complete (98% overall)**; **9 of 16 TTCs complete, 7 in progress (91%)**; **621 retaining walls (100%)**; **14 inspection vehicles**.
- **Salary series:** **+10% (2019)**, then **+40% to +88% (2022)** depending on qualification tier — a range, not a single figure, and the 88% is the **A2 primary** tier only.
- **Class-size colour, single district:** Kayonza mayor Nyemazi John Bosco — "more than 80 students in one class; now closer to 46." One district, one speaker, no series. Carry as testimony, never as a national ratio.

**ANNOUNCED / FORWARD**
- **TTC 3 → 5 years, September 2026.** Not yet in force; write it in the future tense.
- **Parental contributions, January 2027** — announced, with exact figures.

**THE SCOPE SEAM — this run's finding**
The World Bank feature states that "under the QBE project, MINEDUC had recently completed construction of **over 22,000 classrooms**." The **same partnership's own progress note** credits the project with "**over 11,000 classrooms**." Both are true of different things: 11,000 is what the project financed and built; ~22,000 is what the *country* built, project plus its own Rwf 86bn. The funder's feature carries the national total under a project heading. Nobody is lying; the denominator moved silently, and a draft that quotes the feature's sentence would credit a loan with twice its output. **Our copy uses the national figure for the national programme and the project figure for the project, and says which is which in the sentence.** Filed as **ruling #32** (`agents/guidebook/2026-08-18-a-credit-line-is-not-an-output-line.md`).

**THE DURATION SEAM — carried as a guardrail, candidate for a future number**
"**In less than one year**" is the headline. The programme opened **April 2024**; **Wave II's** final assessment ran **October 2025** — eighteen months, and at least two waves. The feature's own body says the programme "*aimed* to train and certify all uncertified pre-primary and primary teachers in the country **within one year**" — i.e. one year is the **design target**, and may describe an individual teacher's passage through the modules rather than the programme's elapsed span. Both readings are defensible; only one is stated. **Draft rule: name the clock in the sentence** — "a programme designed to certify each teacher within a year, running in waves from April 2024 to at least October 2025" — or drop the duration entirely. Do not repeat "in less than one year" unqualified, however many outlets do. (Ruling #19 applies: the guardrail goes in the sentence that makes the claim.)

## 6. Proposed angle

**Working title (EN):** *The teachers Rwanda hired before it trained them*
**Alternative:** *Rwanda opened 22,000 classrooms, then went back for the teachers*

Rwanda did something most systems only do by accident, and did it on purpose: it put unqualified people in front of children. Having built classrooms at a scale that outran its teacher pipeline, it relaxed the hiring bar, filled the rooms, and entered roughly 25,000 people into the payroll under a category it invented for them — *uncertified* — with the training obligation written down beside the job. Three years later it discharged the obligation: four condensed TTC modules taught on weekends and public holidays across all 30 districts, peers watching peers teach with checklists in hand, childcare at the venue so mothers could attend, and a NESA examination at the end that a teacher could fail. Twenty-four thousand certificates. And now the permanent correction — from September 2026, becoming a primary teacher in Rwanda takes five years of college instead of three.

The piece reads that sequence as one instrument with three moments — **incur, disclose, retire** — and asks the question the sequence poses to every system expanding access faster than it can staff it: *not whether to take on quality debt, which is often unavoidable, but whether you write it down where someone can see it.* Rwanda's answer is legible because the category has a name, the cohort has a number, and the discharge has an exam. The counter-texture is equally legible and belongs in the piece: the accounts are the funder's and the ministry's own, the pass rate is unpublished, and the parents' contribution goes up in January.

**Why it clears the five tests (Editor's provisional read, for the verdict file):** a specific instrument with dates and figures ✓; a mechanism a reader elsewhere can act on ✓; primary-register sourcing ✓ (with §7 gaps closed); a named operational voice with something at stake ✓; and it does not repeat a shipped piece — the corpus's Rwanda entry is the exam/CBC story, this is the workforce, and by **ruling #30** they do not merge.

**Instrument variety across the Ed05 slate:** Sierra Leone = a financing instrument; Sudan = a delivery instrument; best-in-Africa = a measurement instrument; Egypt = a certificate; **Rwanda = a workforce instrument.** No two are the same shape. Regional spread now West / North / East / continental — **Southern Africa remains the one open gap.**

## 7. Draft-blocking gaps (close these before commission)

1. **The funnel.** Per ruling #14: *entered ≠ trained ≠ sat ≠ passed ≠ certified.* We have one number (24,000) standing in for five. Required before print: how many were enrolled in each wave, how many sat the NESA assessment, how many passed, how many remain uncertified today. Route: NESA's own site (assessment reports / announcements), REB's SPIU pages, the 2024/25 Statistical Yearbook.
2. **The denominator.** 24,000 out of *what* pre-primary + primary workforce? Without it the number is impressive but unreadable. Route: Education Statistical Yearbook 2024/25 (MINEDUC publications).
3. **The QBE progress note's date.** The page as served shows none; it is load-bearing for the 11,000-classroom figure that anchors ruling #32. Route: MINEDUC news index ordering, or the project's own status page.

**Secondary, close if cheap:** NESA's own Wave II announcement (currently mirrored via an aggregator); the 2022 salary communiqué in served text; the Umwalimu SACCO membership figure at source.

## 8. Risks / guardrail notes (to be carried in-sentence, ruling #19)

- **Attribution.** Never write "the World Bank built 22,000 classrooms," and never let "under the QBE project" attach to the national total. Ruling #32.
- **Duration.** Never repeat "in less than one year" without its clock. §3.
- **Register.** The lead source is funder+implementer co-authored. Say so once, early.
- **Relaxed criteria are not a scandal, and not nothing.** The piece neither buries the fact that unqualified adults taught children for up to three years, nor writes it as an exposé. It is the debt; the programme is the repayment; both are the story.
- **Single-school and single-district claims** (Rukara's 100%, Kayonza's 80→46) are testimony, not statistics.
- **Rwandan press** (New Times, KT Press, Taarifa) is government-adjacent: quotes and colour only, never load-bearing figures — the 07-06 recon's rule stands.
- **Arabic side.** All names here route by **Kinyarwanda phonology**, not French or English spelling conventions, even where the orthography looks French (`Murasira`, `Mutezigaju`, `Nyemazi`, `Umuhoza`, `Kamugisha`, `Muyengwa`, `Abayikunda`, `Bahati`, `Twagirimukiza`). Kinyarwanda is consistently vowel-final and syllable-timed, which maps cleanly to Arabic; the trap is **French-influenced readings of `u` and `e`** (Kinyarwanda `u` = /u/ → و, not /y/). Extends **#28b**; the Arabic Editor confirms each name at compose, per standing rule. Institution names take their attested Arabic forms where they exist (البنك الدولي), transliteration otherwise (NESA, REB carried Latin-tagged on first use).
- **The corpus already holds Rwanda.** Cross-link the CBC/exam story; do not re-narrate it.
