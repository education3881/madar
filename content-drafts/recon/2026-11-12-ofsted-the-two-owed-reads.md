# Recon addendum — the two owed reads on the Ofsted candidate, and the frame they changed

**Researcher · 2026-11-12 · appended to `2026-11-11-instrument-vs-schools-candidate-recon.md`**

Yesterday's recon named **three** things owed before an Editor's brief could go to a drafter. Two are
reads and one is an Editor's call. **Both reads were taken today, in served text, from the regulator's
own pages** — and the first of them was published **two days ago**, on 2026-11-10.

**They did not top up the frame. They replaced it, and in the direction that makes the piece sharper.**
Yesterday's recon had the regulator answering the Bahrain single's closing question with a cohort frozen
by rule. That is still true. What the reads add is that **the regulator has since dismantled the
instrument that answered it, and says so to parents in writing.**

## Read 1 — the current release. `State-funded schools inspections and outcomes as at 31 August 2026`

**Published 10 November 2026** (Ofsted, Official Statistics, applies to England). Read in served text
today at
`https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/state-funded-schools-inspections-and-outcomes-as-at-31-august-2026/main-findings-state-funded-schools-inspections-and-outcomes-as-at-31-august-2026`.

Quoted as printed:

- **The instrument changed again, and this release is the first year of the new one.** *"This official
  statistics release covers the first year of the renewed education inspection framework (EIF), which
  came into effect on 10 November 2025."* And: *"These statistics are published as official statistics in
  development."*
- **The stock, in the only unit this release carries it in:** *"There are currently 21,957 state-funded
  schools. Forty-six per cent of these are maintained by local authorities and 54% are academies and free
  schools."*
- **The volume:** *"Between 10 November 2025 and 31 August 2026, we have carried out 3,262 inspections, of
  which 3,188 were full inspections."*
- **The register disqualifies its own grade profile as a stock figure, in its own words — twice:** *"The
  3,188 full inspections carried out represent only 15% of all state-funded schools, so the grade profile
  is not representative of all schools."* And: *"As we inspect lower-performing schools more often than
  high-performing schools, the mix of schools inspected in any single year is not typically representative
  of all schools."*
- **The only bridge it builds to the old series, and it is a bridge about the inspected cohort rather than
  about the stock:** *"Of the 3,187 schools that received a full inspection this year, 17% were graded
  'requires improvement' or 'inadequate' for overall effectiveness at their previous full inspection. This
  17% compares with 10% of all 21,879 schools being graded 'requires improvement' or 'inadequate' at their
  most recent inspection in August 2024, and an estimate of 8% in August 2025."*
- **What a grade now is:** *"The proportions of schools graded 'expected standard' or better for each
  evaluation area at a full inspection were: 90% for 'inclusion'; 78% for 'curriculum and teaching'; 72%
  for 'achievement'; 84% for 'attendance and behaviour'; 95% for 'personal development and wellbeing';
  85% for 'leadership and governance'."* And: *"The most common grade was 'expected standard': between 53%
  and 62% of schools received this grade across the 8 evaluation areas."*
- **The old series is not deleted, it is parked:** *"Data for the previous version of the framework remain
  available. Statistical releases for inspections between 2019 and 2025 are available on the Ofsted
  statistics website."*

**There is no `good or outstanding` percentage in this release, and there cannot be one.** The 88%
figure the 2022 release printed is a statement in a unit this register has stopped producing.

## Read 2 — the September 2024 removal, first-party, and the sentence that is the whole piece

Read in served text today at `https://www.gov.uk/guidance/understanding-ofsted-report-cards-and-grades`
— Ofsted's own guidance page for parents, **published 1 December 2025, last updated 2 November 2026.**

- *"The main change is that we no longer give one overall judgement, such as 'good', 'outstanding' or
  'requires improvement'."*
- The new scale, as printed: *"'exceptional' · 'strong standard' · 'expected standard' · 'needs attention'
  · 'urgent improvement'"*, plus *"We also tell you whether safeguarding responsibilities are 'met' or
  'not met'."*
- **And the sentence this candidate exists to carry:** *"It's important to understand that the new grades
  can't be compared to the old ones: this is a different approach to inspection and a new way of
  reporting to you."*

**A regulator telling parents in its own voice that its new numbers cannot be compared with its old ones
is the Bahrain question answered from the other end.** Bahrain's archive could not separate the schools
from the instrument because it publishes only verdicts. England can — and the moment it could, it
replaced the instrument and said the series does not join.

**What these reads do NOT say, recorded now so no draft invents it (#49, #117).** Neither page states
*why* the overall grade was removed; the policy reasoning is the Department for Education's and is not in
either register read here, so a draft may not attribute a motive to Ofsted from these two documents. The
guidance page does not date the September 2024 removal — it describes the current state, in the present
tense, as at 2 November 2026. **A draft needs the dated first-party announcement of the removal itself
before it may print "September 2024"**, and that read is owed (see the brief's open gate 2). Neither page
mentions the previously exempt cohort at all; that finding belongs to the 31 August 2022 release and must
be labelled with that release's own date wherever it appears.

## THE TRAP THIS CANDIDATE CARRIES, AND IT IS A NUMBER COLLISION

**Two different 17%s, four years apart, both about Ofsted, both about inspected schools, and they mean
opposite things.**

| | What the 17% is | Register |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | *"Only 17% of previously exempt schools that had a graded inspection this year **remained outstanding**."* | `…as at 31 August 2022` |
| 2026 | *"…17% **were graded 'requires improvement' or 'inadequate'** for overall effectiveness at their previous full inspection."* | `…as at 31 August 2026` |

One is a survival rate at the top of the old scale. The other is the share of this year's inspected
schools that sat at the bottom of it. **Any sentence that prints "17%" in this piece carries its year,
its direction and its denominator, or it does not go in.** Flagged here rather than left for the
Verifier, because a collision between two true figures is not caught by a numeral comparison: both
numbers are correctly cited, and the error would be in the reader's head.

## Read 3 — the Editor's call, taken

**The question:** does an England candidate need a Gulf register to carry it, or is
`2026-10-23-bahrain-bqa-five-cycles` a sufficient anchor inside our own corpus?

**Taken: the Bahrain single is sufficient, and the piece is commissioned as its answer.** Two reasons,
both about this publication rather than about England. First, the Bahrain piece ends by stating that the
register which would settle its question *is one nobody publishes anywhere* — a claim about the world
that this recon has now falsified with the world's own documents, and the honest place to publish a
correction to our own sentence is a piece that carries it. Second, the geography is not the subject: the
subject is **what a published distribution is a measurement of**, and England is in the brief because it
is the only system read so far that froze a cohort by rule and printed the yield beside the stock.

**The cost is stated rather than waved past:** this is the first single to lean on a published predecessor
as its anchor, and it will be the publication's second consecutive piece on school-inspection registers.
If the Editor's five-test rubric finds the piece is readable only by someone who has read the Bahrain
single, it is returned with notes — an anchor inside our own corpus is a rail, never a prerequisite.
