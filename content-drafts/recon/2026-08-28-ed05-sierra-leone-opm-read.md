## Blocker 1 — the OPM evaluation report (the payment-bearing register) ✅ CLEARED

**Document of record:** Oxford Policy Management, *Year 3 Evaluation of SLEIC*
(`SLEIC OPM Evaluation Report 2025.pdf`, linked as "Evaluation results" from
educationoutcomesfund.org/insight/challenge-accepted). RCT of record; per the report,
"These estimates of impact form the basis for the outcome-based payments to providers."

### The lot map (report's own words, two registers — see drift note below)
Lots 1–5 respectively: **EducAid · Street Child · Rising Academy Network ("Rising") ·
Save the Children · NYAF**. ("The service providers are EducAid, Street Child, Rising
Academy, Save the Children, and National Youth Awareness Forum (NYAF), operating in
Lots 1 to 5 respectively.")

### The pooled effects (Year 3, all lots combined)
- **Maths: 0.278 SD**, statistically significant (body text, stated four times).
  The SD-transform table beneath Figure 1 prints **0.280***** — a within-document
  table-vs-text drift; cite the body text and note the table (guidebook row 31).
- **English: 0.102 SD**, significant but smaller.
- Gender: maths girls 0.300 / boys 0.260 (difference n.s.); English girls 0.120 /
  boys 0.085 (difference n.s.).

### The per-lot table (what the payments were computed on)
- Maths positive in **all lots and both genders except boys in Lot 5 (NYAF)**;
  lot-level effects range **0.130–0.384 SD**.
- Largest maths effects: **Lot 2 (Street Child) 0.351 SD** and **Lot 3 (Rising) 0.384 SD**.
- English significant at lot level **only in Lots 2 and 3** — Lot 2 driven by boys
  (girls n.s.); **Lot 3 significant for both girls and boys**, girls larger.
- Save the Children = **Lot 4**: boys 0.056 SD n.s. in Y2 → larger and significant in Y3
  (their blog's "boys improving" texture, confirmed in the evaluator's register).

### The green provider, identified
The StC blog's unnamed provider "marked in green" (sustained Y2→Y3 maths + significant
English) is **Lot 3 — Rising Academy Network**: the report's year-on-year figure shows
impact peaked in Y2 in most lots, "with Lots 2 and 3 seeing even higher effects this
year" (maths), and Lot 3 is the only lot with significant English across both genders
in Y3. State it in the piece by lot with the provider name attached, per the report's
own lot map — not as the blog's colour code.

### Year-on-year (the honesty texture)
"With the exception of maths in Lots 2 and 3, effect sizes tend to be smaller in Year 3
relative to Year 2." Y2 peak attributed speculatively (intensity, novelty, motivation);
report says further investigation needed. Maths scores declined Y2→Y3 in treatment AND
control; treatment stayed above. English declined in both since baseline, more in
control — "SLEIC's impact on English outcomes … has been primarily in mitigating the
extent of this decline."

### Minimum competency (the second payment metric)
- P3/P4 maths minimum competency **up in all lots: +6.2 pp (Lot 1) to +24.4 pp (Lot 3)**;
  larger increases amongst girls in most lots.
- English minimum competency **declined in most lots**, except boys in Lots 2 and 5
  (flat vs baseline) — read against the national English decline.
- Cut-points defined by **MBSSE** (with EOF) — the competency bar is the ministry's own.

### Distribution texture (keep if space allows)
- Maths: bottom-25% pupils gain more (0.263 SD) than top-25% (0.198 SD).
  English: reversed — top quartile gains more (bottom quartile 0.113 SD).
- 95% of performance payments allocated to the learning-gains metric (programme document,
  as cited by OPM); activity-based payment Year 1, outcomes-based Years 2 and 3.
- Participants: the report's own register is "more than 134,000 pupils across 325
  primary schools" (design/target register — matches the launch-era count; the
  programme-page "100,000+" remains the completion register; keep the three-register
  drift visible per the commission).

## Blocker 2 — a named MBSSE voice from served text ✅ CLEARED

**Document of record:** "Learning What Works: Closing SLEIC", EOF news, 16 January 2026
(educationoutcomesfund.org/insight/learning-what-works-closing-sleic), reporting the
15 January close-out at the British High Commissioner's Residence, Freetown.

- **Hon. Conrad Sackey, Minister of Basic and Senior Secondary Education (MBSSE)**,
  keynote, verbatim: "Expanding access is necessary, but it is no longer sufficient.
  The next phase of reform must be decisively focused on learning outcomes." And:
  "SLEIC shifted us from inputs to outcomes, from assumptions to evidence. The
  responsibility now is to act on that evidence." Also on the record: one finding
  "made me pause" — English literacy not improving at the pace of mathematics —
  named by the minister as a priority for further government action.
- **Mr. Edward Kpakra, Chief Education Officer, MBSSE**: frequent breaks and
  extracurricular time often leave most subjects **under one hour of teaching per day**.
- Supporting named voices, same text: Josephine Gauld (British High Commissioner,
  host — "where we find the money"), Alex Maclean (FCDO Development Director),
  Miléna Castellnou (EOF CPO — "Paying for results that matter rather than activities
  that may or may not lead to impact").
- Successor signal, served text: the early-childhood outcomes partnership "planned
  launch … in March" — consistent with the recon's warning to keep the separate
  Sierra Leone Early Childhood programme un-conflated.
