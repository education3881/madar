# Recon — New Zealand: the NCEA literacy & numeracy co-requisite
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument (official name, issuing body, date, link)
- **NCEA literacy and numeracy co-requisite** — 20 credits (10 literacy or te reo matatini + 10 numeracy or pāngarau) that every student must hold before ANY level of NCEA can be awarded; **mandatory from 2024**. Standalone standards sat as national digital Common Assessment Activities (CAAs), two rounds a year (May, Sept/Oct). Issuing bodies: NZ Ministry of Education (policy) and NZQA (assessment). Overview: https://ncea.education.govt.nz/overview-NCEA-corequisite-standards · results data: https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/subjects/litnum/literacy-and-numeracy-data/
- Transition pathway (approved literacy/numeracy-rich achievement standards) extended to **end-2027**: https://ncea.education.govt.nz/standards-approved-ncea-co-requisite-during-transition-period-2024-2027

## 2. Figures
- **May 2025 CAA round vs May 2024** (all students): numeracy **57% (from 45%)**; reading **61% (from 58%)**; writing **55% (steady)** — **MEASURED** — ministerial release, 14 July 2025: https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/surge-ncea-numeracy-literacy-results (canonical per-round tables: NZQA data page above)
- Equity gap, same release: lower-decile schools' numeracy **34% (from 19.8%)**; reading **41% (from 34%)** — i.e. roughly 20–23 points under the national rate even after improvement — **MEASURED**.
- Year 10 early sitters, May 2025: numeracy 68%, reading 72%, writing 66% (~95% first-time) — **MEASURED** — same release.
- **Co-requisite attainment by end of Year 11: 73.8% in 2025, up from 69.5% in 2024** — **MEASURED** — NZQA, 27 Mar 2026: https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/about-us/news/ncea-and-university-entrance-attainment-in-2025/
- Knock-on: 2024 NCEA Level 1 pass rate revised **71.5% → 79.6%** after Year-12 students cleared the co-requisite a year late — **MEASURED** — Beehive release, 14 July 2025.
- NZQA equity characterisation (27 Mar 2026): "Significant disparity in attainment associated with socio-economic barriers to attainment (school equity index group) and ethnicity remains." — **MEASURED** finding, same NZQA URL.
- Support package: NZ$2.2m targeted at students in 141 lower-decile schools (2024) — **ANNOUNCED** (spend figure, ministerial) — Beehive release above.

- **March 2026**: replacement confirmed. **16 May 2026**: details confirmed — a literacy/numeracy **Foundational Award** benchmarked at Year 11 from **2028**; **NZ Certificate of Education** (Year 12) from **2029**; **NZ Advanced Certificate of Education** (Year 13) from **2030**; A+–E grading; the Foundational Award is required to gain the new certificates — the co-requisite's logic survives the qualification it was bolted onto: https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/details-ncea-replacement-confirmed
- Transition (achievement-standard alternative pathway) runs to end-2027; from 2028 the standalone floor becomes the Foundational Award.

## 5. Proposed angle (the mechanism story)
New Zealand bolted a fixed external floor onto the most flexible school qualification in the OECD, and the floor ended up outliving the building. NCEA let schools assemble credits many ways; from 2024 none of it counted unless a student separately passed standalone reading, writing and numeracy assessments set nationally. The first mandatory-year numbers were brutal — numeracy at 45% — and, more politically potent, unequal: under 20% of students in the poorest schools cleared numeracy in May 2024. The system responded the way a measured system does: targeted money at 141 low-decile schools, structured literacy and an hour a day of the basics, and the needle moved (numeracy 45→57; lowest-decile 19.8→34; Year-11 co-requisite attainment 69.5→73.8) while the gap stayed wide enough that NZQA still calls it significant. Then the ruler consumed the qualification: within two years of the co-requisite exposing what NCEA's flexibility had hidden, the government confirmed NCEA itself will be replaced — and the one component carried forward, renamed the Foundational Award, is the measurement floor. The mechanism lesson for the edition: a fixed bar doesn't just measure students; it makes a system's gaps undeniable, and that visibility is both its virtue and its political cost.

## 6. Risks / guardrail notes
- The 14 July 2025 Beehive release contains an internal typo (a "writing" bullet says "reading"); and several press summaries swap the reading/numeracy series. Figures above were taken from the release text directly — numeracy 57 (from 45), reading 61 (from 58) — and must be reconciled against the NZQA data page tables at draft time.
- "Lower decile" is legacy terminology (system now uses the Equity Index); mirror each source's own wording when quoting.
- Keep ministerial (Stanford) framing as ANNOUNCED policy intent; use NZQA (Marshall) for measurement claims.
- Attainment stats mix bases: Level 1 is participation-based (about a third of Year 11s skip Level 1), co-requisite is cohort-based by end of Year 11 — don't blend them in one sentence.
- 2026 round-1 results likely land mid-July 2026 — if published before our run date, refresh Section 2 and the angle's last numbers.
- Replacement dates (2028/2029/2030) are ANNOUNCED implementation, not yet operating — label accordingly in-piece.
