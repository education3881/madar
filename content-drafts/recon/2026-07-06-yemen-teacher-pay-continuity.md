# Recon — Yemen: teacher-pay continuity mechanisms
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

The mechanism: with most public teacher salaries frozen or eroded since 2016, teachers were kept in classrooms by a layered payment machine — World Bank–financed, UNICEF-administered incentives; rural-female salary top-ups; governorate-level allowances; community/farmer in-kind support. The machine's measured output exists on primary sources, and its main financing line just changed (2025) — which is the news.

## 1. The instrument (official name, issuing body, date, link)
- **Yemen Restoring Education and Learning Emergency Project (REAL, P175036)** — World Bank (IDA + GPE trust funds), implemented by UNICEF/WFP/Save the Children with the Ministry of Education. **Implementation Status & Results Report Seq 9, archived 30-Jun-2025 (project close)**: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063025153091285/txt/P175036-162a07a4-674d-464b-84e2-e7bbf6926acd.txt
- Successor: **Sustaining Education and Learning Project (SEAL)** — US$10m grant approved 17-Jun-2025, UNICEF-implemented (World Bank press release: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/17/world-bank-approves-us-30-million-to-support-financial-inclusion-and-education-in-yemen).
- Program page: UNICEF Yemen REAL: https://www.unicef.org/yemen/restoring-education-and-learning-project
- Ministry/exam-board payroll documents (Aden or Sanaa) were not found usable as primary surfaces; per task terms, UNICEF/World Bank/OCHA operational data carry the figures.

## 4. Status as of 2026-07 (latest developments, reversals, disputes)
- **The financing line changed in mid-2025:** REAL — the vehicle that paid ~39,000 teachers — closed 30-Jun-2025. Its announced successor (SEAL) is one-tenth the size and does not list teacher payments in scope. Whether any donor-financed incentive line continues at scale is the open question the draft must answer from the SEAL project documents (appraisal PDF: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099052925162535429/pdf/BOSIB-ec2238b8-a67e-4db1-9d40-ebc97908e783.pdf) before asserting either way.
- OCHA's March 2026 plan counts 193,668 teachers unsalaried; AJE (Apr 2026) documents teacher protests/strikes in government-held areas, riyal collapse (~215/$ pre-war; ~2,900/$ mid-2025 in government areas before partial recovery to ~1,560/$), and governorate-level allowances as the operating patch.
- Northern (Sanaa-authority) areas: public salaries unpaid since the central bank moved to Aden in late 2016 (WB/OCHA-consistent framing). Reports that the Sanaa authority stopped, then restarted, its own volunteer-teacher stipends circulate only in partisan Yemeni outlets — not verifiable on primary sources; keep out or flag explicitly.

## 5. Proposed angle (the mechanism story, no politics)
When a state stops paying its teachers, what exactly keeps a school open? Yemen is the fullest answer on record. Layer one: an internationally audited incentive machine — the World Bank–financed REAL project wired performance-based payments to 39,047 teachers and full salary allowances to 2,135 rural female teachers, with results verified in implementation reports rather than press claims. Layer two: governorates topping up pay by decree, at 25,000–50,000 riyals a month depending on the governor's budget. Layer three: the informal economy of continuity — teachers stacking two and three jobs, farmers in Marib paying in tomatoes and potatoes, principals staffing rooms with paid volunteers. The piece walks the payment stack from a Washington results table down to a Mukalla classroom, on named operators at each layer. The hinge is 30 June 2025: the audited layer closed, its successor is smaller and differently scoped, and OCHA now counts 193,668 teachers receiving no salary. The question the mechanism now faces — who pays layer one next — is answerable with documents, not blame.
