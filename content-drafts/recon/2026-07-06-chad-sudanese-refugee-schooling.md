# Recon — Chad: the host system that absorbed Sudan's students
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: CONDITIONAL (route-2)**

## 1. The instrument
- **Chad's refugee-inclusion policy line**: 2014 MENPC (Ministère de l'Éducation Nationale et de la Promotion Civique) decision to integrate refugee students into the national system and curriculum; 2018 education sector plan formally included refugees and **converted camp schools into Chadian public schools**; **Stratégie Éducation 2030** (validated 2020) codifies full inclusion. Sources: World Bank blog, 08.06.2026: https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/integrating-refugee-students-into-chad-s-national-education-system · UNHCR case study (Aug 2025): https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-08/teachers-in-refugee-and-displacement-settings-chad-case-study.pdf · Stratégie 2030 text: https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/download/83328
- **For the exam question**: a Chadian **interministerial ad hoc committee** (Higher Education; National Education and Civic Promotion; Social Action) organised Sudan's Grade-12 certificate exams on Chadian soil with the Sudanese Embassy/Consulate, UNICEF, UNHCR and NGOs — 25.08–02.09.2025, seven centres in Ouaddaï, Sila, Wadi Fira. UNICEF press release, 02.09.2025: https://www.unicef.org/chad/press-releases/close-5000-refugee-students-sudan-finalize-their-baccalaureate-exams-chad

## 2. Figures
- **876,000+** Sudanese refugees into eastern Chad since April 2023; **~61% children** — MEASURED (UNICEF, 02.09.2025, URL above).
- **163,000+** Sudanese refugee children/youth with access to education, **enrolled in public schools following the Chadian curriculum** — MEASURED-operational (UNICEF, 02.09.2025).
- **~5,000** Sudanese refugee students sat the Sudanese baccalaureate in Chad, Aug–Sept 2025, 7 centres, 3 provinces — MEASURED (UNICEF, 02.09.2025).
- Since 2015, **12,348** Sudanese refugees have sat the **Chadian** baccalauréat; **5,919 passed** — MEASURED (UNICEF notes for editors, same URL). Host-exam pathway predates this war.
- **218,564** refugee children enrolled pre-school→secondary for the 2025-26 rentrée, 52% girls — MEASURED (UNHCR Tchad, Tableau de bord de la rentrée scolaire 2025-2026: https://reliefweb.int/report/chad/unhcr-tchad-tableau-de-bord-de-la-rentree-scolaire-2025-2026 — **fetch blocked in recon; re-verify against the PDF before print**).
- **~38,000 community teachers ≈ 65% of Chad's teaching workforce**; salaries often months late — MEASURED (Annuaire statistique de l'éducation du Tchad 2025, INSEED, via World Bank blog; annuaire: https://www.inseed.td/statistiques/694694a9e91c8b9ce12a07ca).
- **1,700+** refugee teachers trained to teach the Chadian curriculum; 100+ camp schools converted to official Chadian schools across 19 camps/sites — MEASURED-operational (UNHCR).
- ~2/3 of school-age refugee children in Chad still out of school — MEASURED-operational (UNHCR, 2025: https://www.unhcr.org/us/news/stories/sudanese-refugees-chad-face-challenges-deliver-education). Scale honesty: inclusion is real AND coverage is partial.

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- Inclusion machinery holding but strained: 2025-26 rentrée dashboard shows enrolment growth; Education Cannot Wait added US$2M (Oct 2025) on top of a $20M multi-year programme; World Bank/UNHCR INSPIRE programme (June 2026 blog) is financing the shift from parallel humanitarian schools to national-system inclusion, incl. plans to bring refugee-area teachers into sector budgeting.
- The Sudanese-certificate exam round in Chad (Aug–Sept 2025) was explicitly a **one-time initiative**; recurrence is unresolved — a live question for the piece.
- Government held an inclusion workshop with partners, N'Djamena, 5–6 Nov 2025 (ONAMA: https://www.onama.td/education-le-tchad-renforce-son-engagement-pour-une-education-inclusive-des-refugies/).
- Funding risk is the standing threat: humanitarian education appeals persistently underfunded.

## 5. Proposed angle
One of the world's poorest school systems is running one of its most complete refugee-inclusion policies, and the mechanism deserves the spotlight: since 2014 Chad has not built a parallel system for refugees — it converted camp schools into Chadian public schools, put arriving children on the Chadian curriculum, and trained refugee adults (1,700+) to teach it. When 876,000 people crossed from Sudan after April 2023, that machinery is what absorbed the school-age wave: 163,000+ Sudanese children now sit in Chadian public schools, and 218,564 refugee children enrolled at the 2025-26 rentrée. The exam layer shows both directions of the mechanism: Sudanese refugees have been sitting the Chadian baccalauréat for a decade (12,348 since 2015), and in August 2025 Chad went the other way — an interministerial committee hosted Sudan's own certificate exams in seven centres so 5,000 stranded finalists could graduate. The honest counterweight: two-thirds of refugee children are still out of school, classes exceed 100, and the teacher corps holding it together is 65% community teachers paid little and late. The mechanism is inclusion by default; the constraint is money, not policy.
