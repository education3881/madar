## 5. Proposed angle
In 2020 Bangladesh made an unusual system-design decision: the ~400,000 school-age children in the Cox's Bazar camps would be taught Myanmar's national curriculum, in Burmese, by a workforce of Rohingya and Bangladeshi teachers — schooling engineered for a return that has not come. The mechanism scaled: a 10,000-child pilot in 2021 became ~300,000 enrolled across all grades by 2023/24 in ~3,400 learning centres. Then the funders left: in June 2025 the system's operators suspended the early grades entirely, ended over a thousand teacher contracts, cut English and science from the youngest classes and stopped printing textbooks — a controlled shrinkage of a curriculum, grade band by grade band, decided by budget line. Classes have resumed, thinner. And in March 2026 the community answered the system's open question — what is this education FOR? — by building its own examination board and holding matriculation exams whose certificates no state recognizes. The piece follows the mechanism end to end: who chose the curriculum, how it rolled out, what a funding cut removes first, and what a certificate means when no ministry will stamp it.
