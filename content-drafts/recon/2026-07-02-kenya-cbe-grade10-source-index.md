# Recon — Kenya: the CBE senior-school transition (Grade 10, January 2026)

**Date:** 2026-07-02 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED (banked; Edition-02 slot 9)**
**Working frame:** *"The pathway, assigned at fourteen"* — Kenya just ran, at national scale, the thing every tracking debate is about: a single algorithmic exercise that read three assessments, took 1.13 million fourteen-year-olds' pathway choices, and placed each of them into STEM, Social Sciences, or Arts & Sports. The seam is that the same cohort now carries **four different STEM numbers** — the target, the potential, the choice, and the placement — and the state has published its own correction ledger.

## Mandatory counter-textures (all four, per the rubric)

1. **Capacity vs distribution.** 2.2M slots comfortably hold 1.13M learners *in aggregate* — and 20,000 of them wanted 500 seats at three schools. Both are true; print both.
2. **Placed ≠ taught.** STEM learners in some schools went without specialised teachers from January (Nation: *"Crisis as Grade 10 STEM learners go without teachers"*; TSC's own 58,590 figure). Placement is not provision.
3. **The blended score.** 20% of the placement number is scored by the learner's own school (SBA) — the #12 axis, inside a national algorithm. Name it; don't editorialise it.
4. **The review ledger read honestly.** 355K review requests is nearly 1 in 3 placements contested — *and* the state published the number itself and ran a second window. The correction mechanism is as much the finding as the error rate.
