## 1. The instrument (official name, issuing body, date, link)
- **Sudan School Certificate Examination** (امتحانات الشهادة السودانية), supervised by the **General Directorate for Evaluation and Examinations, Federal Ministry of (General) Education**; results validated by the **Sudan Examination Board** (machinery named in INEE practitioner account, 2026-01-14: https://inee.org/blog/resilience-crisis-sudan-national-secondary-school-exams-amidst-conflict).
- War-time sittings to date: **28 Dec 2024** (deferred 2022/23 cohort); **March 2025** catch-up round for hard-to-reach areas; **29 Jun–10 Jul 2025** (2024 batch, results 20 Oct 2025); **13 Apr 2026** (third full sitting, includes backlog batches).
- Official results announcement (Oct 2025): National Information Centre, https://www.nic.gov.sd/news/details/13 · official results portal: https://result.sudani.sd/

## 2. Figures (each with primary-source URL; MEASURED or ANNOUNCED)
- **343,644 registered** for the first war-time sitting (Dec 2024), of whom **120,724 from war-affected states**; ~2,300 centres inside Sudan — ANNOUNCED (ministry registration tally, carried by Al Jazeera net 2024-12-28: https://www.ajnet.me/politics/2024/12/28/انطلاق-أول-امتحان-للشهادة-الثانوية ; corroborated by INEE account above).
- **42,000+ candidates abroad in 46 centres across 15 countries, incl. ~28,000 in Egypt (25 centres)** — ANNOUNCED (ministry via Al Jazeera net, same URL). Egypt = largest host-state operation.
- **2025 sitting results (20 Oct 2025): 222,393 registered / 193,809 sat / 132,817 passed / 68.5% pass rate / 2,018 centres inside and outside Sudan** — MEASURED (ministry ex-post tally, minister press conference; official page https://www.nic.gov.sd/news/details/13 ; carried by Youm7 2025-10-20: https://www.youm7.com/story/2025/10/20/-/7165289). Top-ranked student sat at the **Sudanese Consulate School in Aswan, Egypt** (97%).
- **2026 sitting (from 13 Apr): ~564,000 registered across 3,333 centres inside/outside Sudan**, incl. **226,684 from war-delayed batches + 222,404 from the 2024 class**; **Khartoum state 148,842 registered vs ~30,000 the year before**; **38,000 examinees in Egypt, ~3,000 in Uganda** — ANNOUNCED (ministry figures + official report seen by Al Jazeera net 2026-04-10: https://www.aljazeera.net/politics/2026/4/10/القفز-بمناطق-والتعثر-بأخرى-امتحانات ; Sudan Tribune 2026-04-12: https://sudantribune.com/article/312732). Pre-war baseline in same ministry series: 480,519 sat (2021), 518,712 (2022).
- **~280,000 students in RSF-held Darfur/Kordofan unable to sit for a third year** — ANNOUNCED (estimate by the National Initiative to Support Sudanese Exams, not a ministry count; via Sudan Tribune/Al Jazeera above; context only, not load-bearing).

## 4. Status as of 2026-07 (latest developments, reversals, disputes)
- Third sitting ran from 13 Apr 2026; 2026 results not yet announced as of today. Backlog explicitly being cleared through combined batches.
- **Reversal to report:** Chad refused to host sittings in Dec 2024 (~10,000 refugee students affected, ministry statement via Al Jazeera net 2024-12-28); by April 2026 Darfuri students **were registered at centres in Chad**, alongside Egypt, South Sudan and Uganda (Al Jazeera net 2026-04-10). A 2024 fact that changed — must be reported as changed.
- UAE: Sharjah centre postponed to May 2026 days before start (in-person schooling restricted nationally); 1,200+ students affected; exam fee 450 AED per student (Sudan Tribune 2026-04-12).
- Dispute: RSF-aligned civil authority announced parallel exams in Darfur for June 2026; the government side says results would gain no recognition; EU mission head warned in Feb 2026 against parallel exams. Report as a certification-recognition fact only (see §6).
- Sudan Tribune (2026-04-12) also reports 76+ Gezira teachers excluded from exam duties after demanding unpaid wages — mechanism-integrity detail, single-sourced, verify before use.

## 5. Proposed angle (the mechanism story, no politics)
Since December 2024, Sudan's exam directorate has run a national school-leaving exam three times without a functioning country around it. The mechanics are the story: registration opened to any student regardless of location; sittings concentrated in safer states with transport, meals and fee waivers for displaced candidates; overseas centres run through consulates in 15 countries, with Egypt alone examining tens of thousands; the exam clock shifted to 2:30 PM so a student in Aswan and a student in Kassala sit the same paper at the same hour. Each sitting has produced an audited results tally — registered, sat, passed — announced by the minister and posted to an official portal. The numbers trace the system's recovery arc: 343,644 registered in the first emergency sitting, 222,393 in the second, 564,000 in 2026 as Khartoum's registrations quintupled and host states came online. The certificate still converts into university admission, which is why students crossed rivers and borders to reach a desk. The unresolved tail — roughly 280,000 students in areas the system cannot reach — is reported as the mechanism's stated limit, in the initiative's own numbers, not as a war narrative.
