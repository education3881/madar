## The story, one line
The UAE is nationalizing its teaching workforce through three simultaneous, differently-scoped targets — a federal count (4,000 Emiratis into education over four years), an emirate count (Dubai: 3,000 Emirati educators in private schools by 2033), and a subject mandate (social studies taught by Emiratis in all schools within three years) — and each ruler measures a different thing. The piece is the anatomy of a nationalization count: what is a "teacher" target counting — people, posts, subjects, or sectors — and what baseline does each start from?

## Why this piece fits Ed05 second
- **The method travels intact from recon 1:** England's pledge needed its Feb-2026 scope read beside its 70% claim; the UAE's three targets are three *different* scopes for one national direction. Row 15's rule — *a pledge is a scoped instrument* — was built for exactly this.
- **The slate's first-class Arabic story:** every primary exists in official Arabic as well as English (WAM, Abu Dhabi Media Office AR mirrors, u.ae AR). Arabic composition can quote the state's own Arabic — for once, transliteration risk nearly vanishes for program names (كن معلماً, نافس are *native*, not rendered).
- **Not a lament:** this is a build story — pipelines (Kon Moallim postgraduate diploma via ECAE), incentives (Nafis alignment), and a first-of-its-kind public-private recruitment compact. The scrutiny is measurement-shaped, not motive-shaped.

## Load-bearing figures — axes flagged (rulings #12–#28)
**Served-text confirmed (source 1, quoted verbatim this run):**
- "**9 fold increase** in UAE Nationals representation across key roles in Aldar operated schools" — **a multiplier with no disclosed base.** The release never states the current headcount. This is the scope axis (#22) in its purest form: ninefold *of what?* The piece cannot print "ninefold" without saying the base is undisclosed — and that undisclosed base may itself be the story's sharpest sentence.
- "the **national direction** to have **social studies teaching roles occupied by Emiratis across all schools within the next three years**" — a subject-scoped, all-schools, three-year mandate. Note the register: the release calls it a *national direction*, not a decree — find whether a federal instrument exists (MoE circular? Cabinet decision?) or whether the direction lives only in speeches (#18 announced-vs-measured).
- Kon Moallim = **postgraduate diploma in education** with **ECAE**; priority areas beyond social studies: **MOE subjects, SEN, Foundation Stage, KG**.
- "introducing new-to-sector Emirati educators into the workforce **by the 2030/2031 academic year**"; **up to 30 internships + up to 40 Classroom Assistant** opportunities annually — small, precise, *scoped* commitments sitting beside the large undisclosed-base one. The contrast is usable.
- First-of-its-nature agreement between ADEK and a private education provider; joint steering committee tracking "recruitment, retention and progression" + candidate readiness — i.e., **the compact names its own metrics** (#19: carry the instrument's own rules in-sentence).
- Named humans (for AR routing, below): Mohamed Al Qadi / Mohamed Taj Eddine Ahmed Alqadi (ADEK Chairman — the release itself uses both forms; reconcile at draft), Mohamed Khalifa Al Mubarak (Aldar Chairman), Mariam Alhallami (ADEK Private Education & Charter Schools Sector ED), Sahar Cooper (CEO, Aldar Education).

**NOT in source 1 (misattribution trap, flagged now):** the widely-reported "**over 300 UAE Nationals, including 100+ new-to-sector teachers**" figures do **not** appear in the Media Office release. They live in secondary coverage, presumably from a WAM wire or Aldar's own release. Until traced to a served-text primary they are leads, not citables (#26 family). Do not let coverage's numbers ride on the Media Office citation.

**Search-stage (leads to chase — NOT citable until served-text traced at draft):**
- Federal: **1,000/year from 2024 → 4,000 over four years** (Teaching Specialists Programme) — with its *own* subject scope (Arabic, Islamic, social studies, national identity, KG, primary, SEN) *and* an admin-roles component: an "education sector" count is not a "teachers" count (#22 — same trap as England's secondary+special+FE).
- Dubai: **3,000 by 2033** — baseline unknown. How many Emirati educators work in Dubai private schools *today*? (The Emirati Educator Forum gathered "around 100.") Without the baseline the target is unreadable; finding it (KHDA data, DSC?) is draft-blocking homework.
- The whole-workforce denominators: how many teachers in UAE private schools total; Emirati share of public-school teaching. The nationalization story needs its −1,900-equivalent: the denominator that shows what share the targets move.
