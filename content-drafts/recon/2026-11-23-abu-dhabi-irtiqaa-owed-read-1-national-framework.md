## The read's own premise was wrong, and that is the first finding

The brief commissioned owed read 1 as *"the UAE Unified School Inspection Framework, **first-party from
the Ministry of Education**."* It is not a Ministry document. ADEK's own **School Quality Assurance
Policy** cites it in its References as:

> "Dubai Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA), Abu Dhabi Centre for Technical and Vocational
> Education and Training (ACTVET), Abu Dhabi Department of Education and Knowledge (ADEK), and Ministry of
> Education (MoE). (latest version). United Arab Emirates School Inspection Framework."

**Four authorities, jointly.** The Ministry is one of four named owners, not the publisher. So the piece
may not write *the Ministry's framework* and may not write *a national instrument applied in one emirate*
without that joint authorship attached — the framework is **unified across the authorities that wrote it**,
which is a different claim from *national* and the one the register supports.

The framework's own standing is statutory, and the policy states the instruments:

> "UAE School Inspection Framework — The unified school inspection framework for the UAE, which sets out
> common criteria that inspectors apply to evaluate the quality of education provided by a school, as per
> **Law No. 9 of 2018** Establishing the Department of Education and Knowledge and **Federal Decree-Law No.
> (18) of 2020** on Private Education and its amendments."

## The three sentences that move the spine

**1. The cadence carries a hedge the news page drops.**

> "Th[e] t[ypical] i[nspection] c[ycle] for a School is **2 years**. **ADEK reserves the right to conduct
> any type of inspection or visit at its discretion.**"

The word is **typical**, and the very next sentence reserves discretion. The 01 Oct 2025 news page states
it flatly — *"Irtiqa'a inspections are conducted biennially"* — with no hedge and no discretion clause.
**Same owner, two registers, and the stronger claim is on the page a parent reads.** That is ruling #49's
shape (a claim quoted without its bounding clause) pointed at a **cadence** instead of a superlative, and
it is the piece's new spine: not *biennial versus one year*, but *typical versus reserved*.

**2. The entry cadence is stated exactly, and it is not "every two years" from year one.**

> "ADEK Inspections: Schools shall be subject to ADEK inspections in their **third year of operations and
> every 2 years thereafter**."

So a school's inspection history is a function of its founding year. **A distribution of 204 schools is a
snapshot of schools standing at different points in their own cycles**, which is a sentence the piece can
write from this register and could not write from the news page.

**3. There are FOUR visit types, and only ONE of them carries a rating.**

> "These comprise **monitoring visits, inspections, follow-through visits, and thematic visits**."

Each is defined, and the three that are not inspections are defined *away* from rating:

- "**Monitoring Visits** — Visits conducted by ADEK inspectors to determine a school's **readiness for ADEK
  Inspections**." And: "New schools shall be subject to monitoring visits in their second year of
  operation… Established schools shall be subject to monitoring visits **if ADEK believes the visit will
  support the school in its next ADEK inspection**."
- "**Follow-Through Visit** — A visit conducted by ADEK inspectors, **at ADEK's discretion**, for the
  purpose of **following up with schools in addressing inspection recommendations**."
- "**Thematic Visits** — Visits conducted at ADEK's discretion to gather data on specific aspects of the
  education system and **inform policymaking**. Such visits usually focus on one particular aspect of the
  school."

And the rating instrument is named separately: "**ADEK Inspections** — Regular inspections conducted by
ADEK to assess the quality of education provided by a school against the UAE School Inspection Framework."
The report that follows one is "a finalized **School Performance Report specifying their inspection
rating**."

## What this does to the brief's "inconvenient half" — HALF-CONFIRMED, HALF-FALSIFIED

The brief instructed the drafter to look first and hardest for the sentence that would dissolve the spine:
*"a biennial cycle plus **mandatory follow-up inspections for schools at Acceptable or lower** is a
perfectly coherent design under which 23 schools genuinely can move inside one year — and if the register
says so anywhere, the piece's spine becomes the reporting rather than the instrument."*

**That sentence is not in this register.** The follow-through visit is *at ADEK's discretion*, is keyed to
*recommendations* rather than to a rating band, and is nowhere made mandatory for schools at Acceptable or
lower. Nothing here lets a school's **grade** move outside an ADEK Inspection.

**What is there instead is weaker and more interesting: a discretion clause.** ADEK reserves the right to
inspect at any time, so 23 schools *can* have been re-inspected inside a year — but by a reservation, not
by a published rule, and the number of discretionary inspections conducted is not in any register read so
far. **So the piece's spine survives, in a third unit.** The question is no longer *how can a biennial
ruler yield annual movement* but **what a parent can know about why their own school was measured again** —
and the answer, from the regulator's own policy, is *at ADEK's discretion*.

The brief is **re-briefed, not bent** (its own instruction). The angle sentence changes; the commission
holds.

## What is now FORBIDDEN to the drafter, added by this read

1. **"Biennially" may not be written unhedged.** The policy's word is *typical* and the discretion clause
   is one sentence away. Where the piece quotes the news page's flat *conducted biennially*, it quotes the
   policy's *typical* in the same paragraph, both attributed.
2. **No visit type may be called an inspection.** Three of the four are defined as not producing a rating.
   A sentence that lets a monitoring or thematic visit move a grade is a composite (guardrail 1).
3. **"National" is replaced by the joint authorship.** The framework is unified across four named
   authorities; *national* is our word and not theirs.
4. **No claim that 23 schools were re-inspected.** Nothing read says how the 23 were measured twice. The
   register permits it by discretion and reports it nowhere.
