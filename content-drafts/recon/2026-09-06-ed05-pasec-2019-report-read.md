## Three findings the Editor must see before the draft

1. **Ruler 4 has a crown, and it is the report's own sentence.** The 08-16 recon said *"Crown: nobody"* on the blog's *"inequality rose in all countries."* The report says the opposite of Senegal, twice, in its own words (pp. 225, 227), and its rural–urban maths gap fell 38 points (p. 232). This is positive-posture material of exactly the founder's kind — a build (a 5% repetition cap; early entry for pre-schooled five-year-olds; the report lists them, p. 237) with the report's own reservation (footnote 47) attached. **Recommendation:** ruler 4 crowns **Senegal, at the end of primary, on the report's sentence**; the rail keeps its teeth because the same tables show the top decile capturing the gains in most of the other nine, and because Senegal's start-of-primary inequality did not move.
2. **The Niger story now has its own disclaimer, printed by the owner on the table it rests on.** The policy list and the arrows sit in the same table, and the owner says in a footnote that the arrows do not imply cause. The piece may set the list beside the gain — that is what the report does — but *"Niger bought learning with quality of teaching"* is the analyst's inference and rides as his. Ruling #44 filed today (row 39).
3. **The comparison set is ten, with two exclusions, and the report's own hedge is the vintage clause.** "Fourteen countries" is the 2019 cross-section; the trend is ten; Niger's national-language pupils are outside it. And the sentence the vintage clause needs is on p. 224: the gains *must be confirmed by the next PASEC study* — which is PASEC 2024, results announced for the last quarter of 2026 (09-04 register). The report names its own sequel; the piece can quote it doing so.

## Smaller things worth a line each

- **The document drifts against itself in its own running footer (#38):** the odd-page footer through Chapter 5 reads *"ÉVALUATION PASEC2020"* (pp. 221–239) against *PASEC2019* on the cover, the even pages and the title. Cite by title; note it in the Verifier's trace so the footer is not "corrected" into a wrong citation.
- **A first-party written voice with a byline exists inside the primary:** the *Avant-propos* is signed by **Professeur Abdel Rahamane Baba-Moussa, Secrétaire général de la CONFEMEN** (pp. VIII–IX; the five-year cycle stated as the programme's own design), and the *Liste des rédacteurs* (p. V) names **Hilaire Guy Hounkpodoté, Coordonnateur** as the report's lead author. Both are 2020-vintage written registers — better than reported speech for the closing voice; the 2026 voice is still sought (trace item 4 stands).
- **Burundi** is the counter-story the piece does not need but must not contradict: −35.5 reading, −47.6 maths at end of primary (pp. 224–226), with the report tying it to the crisis since April 2015 and donor sanctions (p. 235). If the piece says "six of ten improved in reading," it says "two fell" in the same sentence (p. 227: *"le Burundi est le seul pays qui a connu une régression aussi bien en lecture qu'en mathématiques"*).
- **Rural–urban:** the report **rejects** the hypothesis that the rise in between-school inequality is a rural–urban widening (pp. 232–233) — the piece must not explain the ICC rise that way.
- **Gender:** differences under 10 points at every level and no significant trend at the ten-country level (p. 233); Burundi flipped from +33 for girls to +22.9 for boys (p. 233). Not for this piece; noted so the Arabic composition does not add a gender line the report does not support.
