# Ed05 recon 4 — Sierra Leone: the country that paid for what actually happened
**Filed:** 2026-08-12 · **Researcher** (under the Editor) · Edition 05 (AFRICA) **mandated slot #1** — the NEW Sierra Leone piece · **First recon of the Africa slate** (founder frame set 2026-08-11) · positive posture: an accomplishment, verified

## The story, one line
Sierra Leone ran what was at launch the world's largest education outcomes partnership — $18m, 325 government primary schools, five providers, each measured against its own randomised control group, payment released only for independently verified learning — and when the three-year results landed **under target**, the system did the rarest thing in education reform: **it paid exactly what the measurement said, published the evaluator's report, and one provider published its own miss** ("Our results are not what we would have hoped them to be"). The accomplishment is not a miracle number. The accomplishment is a low-income country that built an instrument honest enough to be worth believing — and real math gains, in schools that improved while the national tide was going out.

## The hard figures (each with its axis, before any draft)
**Served-text confirmed this run:**
- **$18m · 325 schools · 5 providers · 2022–2025** — stable across all registers read.
- **Participants drift: "134,000 children aged 6–12" (design register, launch-era) vs "more than 130,000 learners" (funder register, Jun 2026) vs "more than 100,000 learners" (completion register, programme page).** Targeted vs reached vs verified-reached — three scopes, likely three vintages (#13/#21). The OPM report arbitrates; until then the piece says *which register* any count comes from. The drift itself is usable: an outcomes program is one of the few places where the reached number is *audited down*, not inflated up.
- **"Outperform roughly 70% of comparable programmes globally"** (program register) vs Save the Children's own math result at "somewhere between the 80th and 90th percentile" of the Evans & Yuan distribution (provider register, math only, vs median effect .11 SD) — **two percentile claims, two scopes, one ruler family.** Never blend (#22); name the ruler both times.
- **The gift of a detail — the ruler that shrinks your own number:** Save the Children computed learning gains two ways and *chose the one that made its result smaller*: its own baseline-derived Grade Effect (.359 SD per grade, local, numeracy-specific) yields "two-thirds of a year ahead"; the standard LMIC ruler (0.15–0.21 SD/year, Evans & Yuan) would have let it claim "1.1 to 1.6 years." Stated reason on the record: the local ruler is (a) numeracy-specific, (b) specific to the schools they worked in. **This is the scoped-instrument discipline practised by the measured party itself** — the spine sentence of the piece.
- **English: gains small and not statistically significant** (provider register, <90% confidence) — carried as *no demonstrated gain*, never as a small one (#12 family). National backdrop: English declining in treatment AND control, WASSCE-validated; SLEIC schools' math finished above baseline while control math finished unchanged.
- **The contract paid as designed:** behind target for girls and boys in Y3 → "only a portion of our final payment… will not recoup all of the budget that was invested." The instrument's integrity is the *payment* event, not the press release.
- **Provider spread:** 3 of 5 providers outgained Save the Children; one (unnamed in the blog, "marked in green") sustained Y2→Y3 math gains AND achieved significant English gains — **identify from the OPM report at draft**; that provider is the edition's "what worked" thread.
- **Gender texture, honestly:** the Y2 girls-ahead gap narrowed by Y3 — partly boys improving (math), partly girls' gains *falling* (math). Both directions named or neither.
