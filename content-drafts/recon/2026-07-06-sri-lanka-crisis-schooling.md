# Recon — Sri Lanka: keeping school running through economic collapse
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: CONDITIONAL (route-2)**

## 1. The instrument
Not one instrument but a continuity toolkit operated by two bodies:
- **Department of Examinations (Commissioner General of Examinations)** — statutory power to postpone/reschedule national exams. Used in March 2022 (term tests indefinitely postponed when printers could not get forex for paper and ink) and again in Nov 2025–Jan 2026 (Cyclone Ditwah: A/L halted, resumed 12–20 Jan 2026). Dept: https://www.doenets.lk/
- **Ministry of Education — National School Meal Programme (NSMP)** — retention mechanism, expanded nationally from 25.03.2024 to all grade 1–5 students (announced: 1.6M children, 9,134 schools, Rs 16.6bn: https://www.newswire.lk/2024/03/25/sri-lanka-begins-free-school-meal-program-for-all-primary-students/), scaled in Budget 2025 (~1.5M coverage; preschool meal rate Rs 60→100: https://economynext.com/sri-lankas-budget-for-2025-speech-205680/).

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- Exam calendar normalised through 2024–2025, then the postponement machinery was reused for weather (Ditwah, Nov 2025) — resumed on schedule Jan 2026; results cycle ran to April 2026 (gazette.lk tracker). The 2022-built muscle is now standing procedure.
- Meals programme is now the flagship retention mechanism: universal primary coverage targeted by 2026, secondary extension mooted by 2030 (School Meals Coalition: https://schoolmealscoalition.org/member/sri-lanka); parliament's Committee on Ways and Means demanded a progress report from MoE (parliament.lk: https://www.parliament.lk/en/home/parliament-news/view/4052) — oversight friction worth one line.

## 5. Proposed angle
When Sri Lanka's foreign currency ran out in 2022, the school system's failure mode was oddly specific: no dollars → no imported paper → no printed question papers → term tests postponed for two-thirds of 4.5 million students. The mechanism story is what the system did next, in two moves. First, triage by calendar: exams were postponed rather than cancelled, preserving certification integrity at the cost of time — a lever the same office pulled again, cleanly, when a cyclone hit the 2025 A/L, resuming within seven weeks. Second, retention by lunch: with 54.9% of school-age children's education measurably hit and household food budgets collapsing, the state scaled the school meal from welfare add-on to core continuity infrastructure — free meals for every primary child from March 2024, ~1.5 million covered in 2025, with one Colombo school's attendance register jumping from 60–70% to 85–95% once meals arrived. The third, quieter finding: the crisis broke the measurement system itself — no school census for 2022–23 — so the state was flying blind exactly when dropout risk peaked. Recovery meant restarting the counting as much as the feeding.
