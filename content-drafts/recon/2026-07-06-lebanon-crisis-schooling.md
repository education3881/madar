## 5. Proposed angle
How does a public school system with no operating budget keep opening every September? Lebanon's answer is a lattice of workarounds, and each one is documented. Teachers' base pay collapsed with the currency, so a donor-funded productivity allowance — paid directly in dollars, outside the treasury — became the de facto salary system. The same schools run twice a day: a morning shift for Lebanese children and a registration-gated afternoon shift for refugees. When the March 2026 escalation closed every school in the country, the ministry pushed 40,000 teachers and 390,000 students onto remote platforms within weeks, then reopened school-by-school using one operational rule: a building being used as a shelter cannot also be a classroom. Now MEHE has counted its damaged schools — 340 — and the summer of 2026 is a race to repair them before September. The mechanism story is that continuity in Lebanon is bought term-by-term, with someone else's money and the staff's patience — and both are measurably running out (strikes; $350–600 take-home).
