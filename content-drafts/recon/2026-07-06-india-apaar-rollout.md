# Recon — India: APAAR student-ID rollout ("One Nation, One Student ID")
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- **Name:** APAAR — Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry, a lifelong 12-digit student ID under NEP 2020's "One Nation, One Student ID".
- **Issuing body:** Ministry of Education, Department of School Education & Literacy; generated via the UDISE+ portal against a student's PEN, pushed to DigiLocker; linked to the Academic Bank of Credits. Rollout from late 2023.
- **Portal (primary):** https://apaar.education.gov.in/ — states the flow explicitly: "Parents fill up consent form voluntarily → UDISE system generates APAAR ID against PEN → pushed to DigiLocker." Prerequisite (portal FAQ): student name in UDISE+ **must match Aadhaar**.
- Key compliance instrument: CBSE circular of 24 Jan 2025 directing implementation in affiliated schools: https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Circular_Implementation_APAAR_ID_24012025.pdf

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- Counter at 33.74 crore and still climbing; state drives continue.
- The consent architecture lost in court: Odisha HC (12 Dec 2025) forced the refusal option; MoE issued a revised consent form (in circulation by Apr 2026); NCERT clarified (Apr 2026) no admission can be denied without APAAR; CBSE had already conceded REFUSED/NOGEN for boards (Sept 2025).
- Civil-society pressure continues (Rethink Aadhaar, 28 Oct 2025: https://rethinkaadhaar.in/blog/2025/10/28/re-post-apaar-id-raises-concerns-worsening-exclusion). Aadhaar name-mismatch remains the top operational failure mode.
- No pause or rollback of the scheme itself; what changed in 2025-26 is that "voluntary" acquired enforceable paperwork.

## 5. Proposed angle
A national ID for schoolchildren scaled to a third of a billion registrations on a consent form that, for two years, had no box for saying no. The deployment mechanism was never a law — it was targets cascading through UDISE+: ministry to state, state to district, district to principal, principal to parent-teacher meeting, with CBSE's board-exam paperwork as the enforcement point. The measurable arc: 10 crore by Jan 2025, 33.74 crore by mid-2026. The counter-arc is what makes it a story: a single parent in Odisha litigated the missing checkbox, and by spring 2026 the ministry had reprinted the form, CBSE had built "REFUSED" into its exam lists, and NCERT had put in writing that no child can be turned away without the ID. Principals like Pratibha Kohli describe the ground truth — help desks, WhatsApp blasts, Aadhaar-mismatch triage — deployment as a thousand small administrative acts. The mechanism lesson for the region: when an ID gates exams, "voluntary" is a property of the paperwork, not the press release.
