# Recon — Syria: certificate recognition and student reintegration after December 2024
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

The mechanism: how a school system re-admits hundreds of thousands of returning students whose paperwork comes from a dozen foreign systems — or doesn't exist. Post-December-2024 Syria has built a documented pipeline: conditional registration, equivalency waivers, placement ("sabr") testing, and one unified exam session. All 2025–2026 developments, verified on state and UN operational surfaces.

## 1. The instrument (official name, issuing body, date, link)
- **Ministry of Education Decision No. 943/950** — suspends the complementary examination required for equivalency of foreign grade-9 and grade-12 certificates held by Syrian students, for academic years 2025-26 and 2026-27 only. Issued on the basis of Law 19/2002 (art. 1) and Decision 6853/2001. Reported with decision copy by SANA, 2026-03-13: https://sana.sy/education/2425127/ ; UNHCR "Syria is Home" announcement, 2026-04-05: https://syriaishome.org/en/2026/announcement-new-decision-by-the-syrian-ministry-of-education/
- **MoE instruction of 11-Sep-2025**: conditional school registration for children returning from abroad for 2025-26 — registration on available documents or a written parental pledge, documents due before semester two. Documented on UNHCR Syria's operational help page (current as of 2026-07-06): https://help.unhcr.org/syria/where-to-find-help/education/
- Standing machinery (same UNHCR page): **Certificate Equivalency Department (MoE Damascus)**; **School Documents Committee of the Examinations Directorate**; school-level **Knowledge Testing Panels** administering placement tests (Arabic, mathematics, foreign language, biology) for undocumented students; conditional admission for two months, extendable.

## 2. Figures (each with primary-source URL; MEASURED or ANNOUNCED)
- **354,075 candidates sat the 2025 general secondary certificate (all branches): scientific 211,473; literary 115,801; religious 2,139; vocational 24,662**; pass rates 75.5% (scientific, regular) / 54.9% (literary, regular) — MEASURED (minister's results announcement, SANA 2025-08-30: https://sana.sy/education/2275016/). First unified post-December-2024 session.
- **2025-26 session (June 2026): 832,000+ registered nationwide across general certificates**; secondary exams began 6-Jun-2026 in **1,570 centres** across all governorates — ANNOUNCED (SANA 2026-05-31: https://sana.sy/education/2489845/ ; SANA exam-start report: https://sana.sy/education/2494168/).
- **Damascus governorate 2025-26: 65,111 registered (28,559 basic education; 29,287 general secondary; rest sharia/commercial/industrial/women's branches) across 192 centres** — ANNOUNCED (named directorate source, SANA 2026-05-31, URL above).
- Equivalency fees: **SP 25,000** (secondary certificate equivalency) + **SP 50,000** (supplementary-subjects exam, now suspended for two years); equivalency windows Feb/Aug/Nov — ANNOUNCED (UNHCR Syria help page, URL in §1).

- Equivalency-exam suspension (Decision 943/950) in force for 2025-26 and 2026-27 — a two-year window, explicitly temporary; reassess at expiry (SANA 2026-03-13).
- Conditional-registration instruction operating through 2025-26; UNHCR's help page (checked 2026-07-06) still lists the pledge mechanism, the two-month conditional admission, and placement testing — the pipeline is live, not just announced.

## 5. Proposed angle (the mechanism story, no politics)
A student who left Aleppo in 2014 and finished grade 9 in Gaziantep, Beirut or Amman returns in 2025 holding paperwork Syria's system has never seen — or nothing at all. The piece follows the document, not the politics: the September 2025 instruction that lets a parent's signed pledge stand in for a birth record so the child starts school on day one; the school-level Knowledge Testing Panel that places an undocumented child by testing Arabic, maths, a foreign language and biology; the two-month conditional admission that buys families time to chase stamps; and the March 2026 decision that simply switched off the equivalency exam for two years because the queue of returnees made it a bottleneck. At the top of the funnel sits one unified exam session — 354,075 candidates in 2025, 832,000+ registered for 2026 — that converts all of this back into a single national credential. The Damascus education directorate, which runs 192 exam centres, supplies the operator's view of the machine under load. Fees, deadlines and exam windows give the story its concrete texture; every step is on a state or UN operational surface.
