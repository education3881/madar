# Ed05 recon 1 — England: the pledge that counts its own teachers
**Filed:** 2026-08-09 · **Researcher** (under the Editor) · Edition 05 candidate #1 (UK, from the `_EDITION_05_PLAN.md` slate) · **First recon of the Ed05 bank** (frame: "the teaching question" — recommended, founder nod pending; this candidate survives a re-point, see below)

## The story, one line
Two years into a manifesto pledge of 6,500 more teachers, England's government announced it is "over 70% of the way" — in the same season its own census showed the total teacher workforce *fell* — and both numbers are true, because in February 2026 the pledge was given a scope: secondary + special schools + colleges, against a 2023/24 baseline, counting net growth. The piece is not a gotcha; it is the anatomy of a workforce count — who counts as "a teacher," and who sets the ruler that counts them.

## Why this piece opens Ed05 cleanly
- **Sequel logic made literal:** Ed04 asked what a child's score measures; this asks what a *teacher count* measures. Same measurement discipline, one level up — the bridge piece between editions.
- **It is the slate's "counter" piece too:** the underlying labour-market news is genuinely improving (leaver rate lowest since 2010/11 outside the pandemic; maths recruitment met target for the first time in over a decade), so the edition opens by informing, not lamenting.
- **Frame-robust:** if the founder re-points Ed05 away from "the teaching question," this recon still serves — the piece reads equally as a measurement story (Ed04's family) or a teacher-supply story.

## Load-bearing figures — axes flagged (rulings #12–#28)
**Served-text confirmed (source 1, quoted verbatim this run):**
- **4,654** additional teachers "of the 6,500 pledge" = **3,008** more in secondary and special schools + **1,646** more in further education. *(4,654/6,500 = 71.6% — "over 70%" checks.)* → **Scope axis #22 is the piece:** the count is real *inside the Feb-2026 scope* (secondary + special + FE, baseline 2023/24, net growth not "new recruits").
- **1,100** more teachers in special schools (subset of the 3,008 — do not double-carry).
- "**2,100 fewer teachers** [leaving] than last year" — a **count**, from the government; NFER's "9% left 2023/24→2024/25" is a **rate**. Count ≠ rate (#25 family): never chain them into one trend line.
- Median pay **£51,048**, "+4% from last year" — the release says "average median pay," a redundancy worth keeping *out* of our sentence; attribute the figure to the census at draft (#26).
- Bursaries "up to **£31,000**" (chemistry, maths, physics, computing); early-career payments "up to **£6,000 after tax**" in disadvantaged schools; pay "boosted by almost 10% over two years."
- "**13% rise** in people training to teach"; "**STEM recruitment target surpassed for the first time** since it was introduced" — announced in the political register; the census (#3) is where the measured version lives (#18 family: measured-vs-announced).

**Search-stage (leads to chase — NOT citable until served-text traced at draft, #26):**
- Total workforce **466,372 FTE** Nov 2025 = **−1,900** vs Nov 2024, **−2,322** vs Nov 2023 (Full Fact reading of source 2). *This is the piece's other half: the same-season, whole-workforce number.* → same-event-two-scopes pair with 4,654; the story lives in the join, and both must be traced in the census's own served text.
- **Feb 2026**: government confirms pledge = "at least 6,500 more … secondary, special schools and colleges at end of Parliament," baseline **2023/24**, net growth (Full Fact / Schools Week timeline; find the primary — likely a DfE statement or PQ answer — at draft).
- ITT census 2025/26: secondary PGITT **89%** of target vs **61%** in 2024/25 (unconfirmed; verify on #3).
- NFER 2026: secondary recruitment **11% below** target 2025/26 ("best in four years"), forecast **14% below** 2026/27 (**forecast ≠ measured — carry the word "forecast" in-sentence**, #21 vintage + #17 direction discipline); primary met target; **maths met target 2025/26 for the first time in more than a decade**, forecast 22% *above* 2026/27; chemistry above; business studies + drama below; leavers **9%** (vs **10.6%** a decade ago), "lowest since 2010/11 outside the pandemic."
- **The attribution axis (the piece's honest spine):** NFER explicitly credits **weaker job prospects in the wider labour market** as a likely driver of improved recruitment — so the government's policy list (pay, bursaries, retention payments) and the macro cycle are *rival explanations for the same curve*. Carry both; adjudicate neither.
