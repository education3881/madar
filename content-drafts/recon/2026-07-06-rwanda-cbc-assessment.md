# Recon — Rwanda: comprehensive assessment under the competence-based curriculum

**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- **National examinations (PLE/P6, O-Level/S3, A-Level/S6, TVET/TTC) plus comprehensive assessment**, administered by **NESA — National Examination and School Inspection Authority** (est. 2020), aligned to the competence-based curriculum (CBC, introduced 2015/16). https://www.nesa.gov.rw/
- Assessment architecture: NESA's *Simplified Guide to Classroom-Based and National Assessment in Rwanda* (https://www.nesa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/NESA/Ressources/Simplified_Guide_to_Classroom_Based_and_National_Assessment_in_Rwanda/simplified_guide_to_classroom_based_and_national_assessment_in_Rwanda.pdf); NESA now issues **signed national assessment standards for end-of-term school assessments, P1–S6** (e.g. 2025–2026 End of Term II standards: https://www.nesa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/NESA/Ressources/Assessment_Standards_and_Guidelines/2025-2026_End_of_Term_II_Assessment_Standards_P1_-_S6_GE_PE_TSS/A_L_GE_AS_Term_II_2025-2026_Signed.pdf); classroom marks are captured in the **CA-MIS** comprehensive-assessment platform (context: https://www.globalpartnership.org/blog/lesson-rwanda-comprehensive-assessment-management-software-scores-highly-teachers).
- Latest results release: **2024/2025 PLE + O-Level, 2025-08-19** — https://www.mineduc.gov.rw/news-detail/the-ministry-of-education-announces-2024-2025-primary-leaving-and-olevel-national-examination-results

## 2. Figures
All from MINEDUC/NESA primary pages unless noted.
- **PLE 2024/2025 — MEASURED**: 220,927 registered; 219,926 sat (exams 8–10 July 2025); 219,900 graded; **166,334 passed = 75.64%**. Girls 53.2% of passers. https://www.mineduc.gov.rw/news-detail/the-ministry-of-education-announces-2024-2025-primary-leaving-and-olevel-national-examination-results
- **O-Level 2024/2025 — MEASURED**: 149,206 registered; 148,702 sat (24 July–3 Aug 2025); 148,676 graded; **95,674 passed = 64.35%**. Same URL.
- **A-Level 2024/2025 — MEASURED**: 106,418 registered; 105,997 graded; **94,409 passed = 89.1%, up from 78.6%** the previous year; Kayonza district top at 96.9%; TVET pass rate 98%. https://www.mineduc.gov.rw/news-detail/a-level-national-exam-pass-rate-rises-by-10-as-kayonza-district-tops-performance
- **The ruler change — MEASURED (policy in force)**: a **50% pass mark** introduced from the prior cycle; results now published as raw scores *and* percentages (Minister Nsengimana, on the MINEDUC release above).
- **Distribution shift behind the lower pass rates — MEASURED (NESA analysis)**: candidates scoring 30–40% fell **45,148 → 10,916** year-on-year; those scoring 50–60% rose **24,925 → 41,269** (NESA DG, via Africa-Press 2025-08-19: https://www.africa-press.net/rwanda/policy/national-exams-show-75-pass-in-ple-64-in-o-level)
- **Per-school transparency — MEASURED**: NESA publishes per-school pass-rate tables (e.g. S3 2024/25: https://www.nesa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/NESA/Announcements/Performance_of_schools_in_2024_2025_PLE_and_O-Level_national_examinations/S3_Performance_of_Schools_in_the_2024-2025_Ordinary_Level_National_Examinations_Final.pdf)
- **2025/2026 cycle — ANNOUNCED**: registration guidelines signed and published (https://www.nesa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/NESA/Announcements/2025_2026_National_Examinations_Registration_Guidelines/2025-2026_National_Examinations_Registration_Guidelines_Signed.pdf); TSS exams from ~15 July 2026 per exam-timetable coverage (secondary).

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- **2025/2026 examination cycle is live now**: comprehensive/continuous assessments ran May–June 2026; national exams underway/beginning July 2026 (TSS from mid-July). Results expected ~Aug–Sep 2026 — a piece published mid-cycle should say so.
- Remedial programme extended to all students from the new academic year (Nsengimana, Aug 2025 release).
- NESA continues publishing per-school performance PDFs and per-term assessment standards — the school-based leg of the comprehensive-assessment model is now nationally standardized.

## 5. Proposed angle
Rwanda is the sprint's cleanest case of a country changing the ruler on purpose and accepting the political cost. To align examinations with the competence-based curriculum, NESA didn't just rewrite exam papers: it raised the pass mark to 50%, made results legible as raw scores and percentages, standardized end-of-term school assessments nationally, and wired classroom marks into a management system (CA-MIS). The visible consequence was pass rates that *look* worse — 75.6% at P6, 64.4% at S3 — and the mechanism story is how the assessment authority defended the new ruler: by publishing the score distribution, showing the failing band collapsing from 45,148 to 10,916 while the middle band nearly doubled. A-Level's 89.1% (up 10.5 points) rode a targeted remedial programme, now extended system-wide. The piece reads the whole loop — curriculum → classroom assessment → national exam → remediation — as one measurement system, with the 2025/26 exams sitting right now as the first full cycle under the settled rules.

## 6. Risks / guardrail notes
- **Comparability trap**: never quote pass-rate declines across the 50%-pass-mark change without stating the rule change; that's the whole point of the piece, not a gotcha.
- Define "pass" per level (P6 pass = placement eligibility) — check NESA's grading annex at draft time.
- Rwandan press (New Times, Taarifa, KT Press) is government-adjacent; keep all load-bearing numbers on mineduc.gov.rw / nesa.gov.rw, use press only for quotes and colour.
- The Bahati distribution figures were stated at the release and carried by press; they match the NESA framing on its own site ("quality gains over pass rates") — cite as NESA analysis via Africa-Press.
- A-Level release date (July 2025) not pinned on the MINEDUC page — verify before print if used.
- No independent critique voice found (no Rwandan equivalent of Alto al SIMCE in sources); note the absence rather than manufacturing balance.
