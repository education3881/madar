## 1. The recommended piece — Zambia

### The load-bearing primary document (READ IN FULL, served as text)

**The Education (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — N.A.B. 6 of 2026**, National Assembly of Zambia.
- Bill page: `https://www.parliament.gov.zm/node/12930` — all five stages recorded (Presentation, First Reading, Second Reading, Committee Stage, Third Reading).
- Full text: `https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/bills/N.%20A.%20B.%206%20of%202026.pdf`
- Memorandum dated **19 March 2026**, signed **M.D. Kabesha, Attorney-General**.

This is the statute text, not a press release, and it is more interesting than the coverage of it.

**New section 15(1), verbatim:** *"A child who is a citizen shall have the right to free education."*

**The four clauses that are not in the coverage:**

1. **Citizenship gate** — s.15(3): a parent or guardian *"shall, at the time of enrolment, provide proof of citizenship of that child, as prescribed."* Free education is a **citizen's** right, not a child's. Zambia hosts refugee settlements at Meheba and Mayukwayukwa.
2. **A criminal penalty for helping a non-citizen child** — s.15(4): anyone who *"aids, abets, counsels, procures or solicits"* access to free education for a non-citizen child commits an offence: up to **300,000 penalty units** or **3 years' imprisonment**, or both.
3. **Accommodation is now inside the fee ban, and defined** — s.119(1) bars public and community institutions from charging admission, tuition **and accommodation**; s.2 defines accommodation as *"the provision of bed space, a bed and mattress to a learner residing at an educational institution."* This is a real expansion beyond the 2022 policy: boarding cost was the residual barrier.
4. **The escape hatch** — s.119(2): the Minister *"may, by statutory instrument, prescribe the fees payable… in respect of services or programmes **not covered under free education**."* The category is undefined in the Act. Any future re-charging happens here.

### The structural reform buried in the same Act
- "primary education" redefined as **grade one to grade six** (was one to seven).
- "secondary education" redefined as **form one to form six**.
- basic school / high school categories **deleted**.
- Schedule para 2: conversion of basic/high schools runs **30 May 2026 → 30 May 2031**.
- A new eight-key-stage framework writes **day care (0–3), nursery (3–4) and reception (4–5)** into statute — directly relevant to our ECE remit.

**This is the angle.** Zambia's headline access metric for two decades has been **Grade 7 completion**. The same Act that guarantees free education stops Grade 7 being the end of primary. *When a country rewrites the law that defines "primary school," what happens to the statistics that measured whether primary school was working?* That question is not in any coverage and comes straight out of the served text.

### The 2022 baseline (READ IN FULL)
**Ministerial Statement, "Update on Recruitment of 30,000 Teachers," Hon. Douglas Syakalima MP, 9 June 2022**
`https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/images/publication_docs/Ministerial%20Statement%20-%20Update%20on%20Recruitment%20of%2030%2C000%20Teachers.pdf`
- **K1,994,817,600** budgeted in the 2022 National Budget for the recruitment.
- **100,877 applications against 30,000 posts**, with a full provincial breakdown (Central 17,513 for 4,150; Lusaka 5,318 for 740; Western 7,153 for 3,600).
- Pre-recruitment ratios **1:58 primary, 1:38 secondary** against standards of 1:45 and 1:35 — **announced by the Minister, vintage 2022, underlying source unstated** (#12 family: flag, do not launder).
- **Funnel discipline (#14):** this statement was delivered *before* the recruitment closed. It reports **30,000 allocated**, not 30,496 achieved. The two are different stages and may not be used interchangeably.

### The measured outcome (READ)
**2025 Grade 12 results**, ZANIS (government news agency), announced by Minister Syakalima, January 2026 — `https://www.zanis.gov.zm/?p=1779`
- **136,434** obtained full Grade 12 School Certificates; **54,771** received statements of results; out of **194,148** candidates who sat.
- Pass rate **70.26%**, up from **68.19%** in 2024 — first time above 70%.
- Absenteeism **1.98% → 1.83%**.
- ECZ Executive Director **Michael Chilala**: in-house results processing built for **K4.7m**, saving ~**K15m** against external procurement; processing cut from up to 8 days to **48 hours**.

**Scoped-instrument warning (rows 15/19/20).** "70.26%" counts **full certificates among candidates who sat**. 28.22% of those who sat received a *partial statement*, which is not a certificate. The denominator is entrants, not the age cohort — and the cohort has just been swollen by the free-education intake. **A rising numerator over a self-selected denominator is not more young Zambians finishing school.** Syakalima attributed the gain to free education, teacher recruitment, monitoring, grants and school feeding: an **attribution (#32)**, not a finding.

**Ledger drift on the same event (#32):** ZANIS gives 136,434 / 194,148; a secondary gives 136,357 / 194,069 — ~80 candidates apart. **Use ZANIS.**

### The honest counterweight (READ)
**UNESCO IICBA Zambia country brief**, vintage January 2024 — `https://www.iicba.unesco.org/en/zambia`
- **Learning poverty 99%**; HCI expected years **8.8**, harmonized learning outcome **358**, learning-adjusted years **5**; a child born today reaches **40%** of potential.
- From ESB 2020: Grade 7 completion **97% (2019) → 86.4% (2020)**; Grade 9 pass **53%**; Grade 12 **64%**.

This is the piece's spine under a positive posture: **a government converted a fee-abolition policy into an enforceable statutory right, and widened it to cover boarding — while its measured learning floor remains among the world's lowest.** The accomplishment is institutional and real; the counterweight makes it honest rather than promotional. *What a law can and cannot do.*

### Figures the draft MAY NOT USE
- ❌ **"2.3 / 2.5 / 2.6 million children returned to school."** Three government ledgers, three numbers (Ministry via Mail & Guardian, Feb 2026; Open Zambia, 6 May 2026; Open Zambia relaying State House, 3 June 2026), **no disclosed base year and no counting rule**. Textbook #32 + row 21 participant drift.
- ❌ **Teacher totals** — 30,496 (2022) / "4,500 more" (2023) / 46,236 (2022–24) / "over 40,000" / "over 41,000." Nothing reconciles these publicly. Use only the traced 2022 ministerial-statement figures.
- ⚠️ **ESB enrolment series is SEARCH-STAGE ONLY and untraced**: 5,538,681 (2022) → 5,936,505 (2023) → 6,527,980 (2024); teachers 154,304 → 158,504. Do not use until the wall falls.

**One number in that set is worth the trace on its own:** 2023 **Gross Intake Rate 112.8% against Net Intake Rate 55.2%**. That gap means a large share of Grade 1 entrants are over- or under-age — precisely what a surge-enrolment policy produces, and a far better description of what free education *did* than any "millions returned" aggregate.

### Political timing — must be named
Zambia holds a **general election in August 2026**. The Act was assented **two months before it**, and free education is the campaign's central issue. **Posture: positive about the statute, sceptical about the aggregates** — and say so in the piece rather than around it.

## 2. Banked, with reasons

**Namibia — bank until 2026 first-semester enrolment publishes.** President **Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah**'s maiden SONA (April 2025) announced 100%-subsidised tertiary from 2026. Finance Minister **Ericah Shafudah**: *"N$663 million is to cater for registration and tuition fees for the first quarter (January to March 2026)"* plus **N$151m for 665 teachers**.
**The figure discipline point, and it is a good one: N$663m funds ONE QUARTER.** The widely-repeated "N$5 billion annually" is an announced running cost, not an appropriated one — a draft saying "Namibia funded free tertiary education with N$663 million" is wrong by a factor of the year.
Then January 2026 (The Namibian, **Chris Kaukemua**, 20 Jan 2026): *"the ministry has yet to disburse the funds, prompting Nust to suspend registrations."* Minister **Sanet Steenkamp** ordered refunds; eligibility was narrowed the preceding Friday (not retrospective; students who failed the prior year or carry balances must pay; first undergraduate qualification only); accommodation, meals and transport remain means-tested NSFAF loans.
**Verdict:** current, well-sourced, honest-making — and the verifiable content is *announced policy meets disbursement failure*. That is a good Madār piece and a bad fit for this edition's posture. No measured outcome exists yet.

**South Africa — the premise does not survive its own register.** DBE, *South African 2023 TIMSS Highlights Report* (2024), read in sections as served text — `https://www.education.gov.za/Portals/0/Documents/Reports/2024/TIMSS%202024.pdf?ver=2024-12-03-192400-000`
- Grade 5 maths **376 (2015) → 374 (2019) → 362 (2023)**; science **324 → 308**.
- Grade 9 maths **389 → 397**, science **370 → 362** — and the report states these are **not statistically significant**, framing the result as *"some resilience,"* not improvement.
- Grade 5 2023: **40% below Low** in maths, **47%** in science, with a formal reliability caveat that grade-5 science *"not reliably measured"* because the share too low to estimate exceeds 25%.
- Inequality: Grade 5 maths Q1 **307** vs Q5 **492** (185 points); science Q1 **227** vs Q5 **493** (266 points).
**Verdict:** South Africa has two registers pointing opposite ways in twelve months — a record **88% matric pass** (Jan 2026, search-stage) against a falling Grade 5 TIMSS score. **That contradiction is a methodology piece, not an accomplishments piece**, and it cannot be told positively without misleading. Bank it for the ruler-choice frame — it is a strong future piece.
**Image wall:** all sample sizes and all trend charts are **JPEGs inside the PDF**; the 2011/2015 Grade 9 trend points are not available as text. Do not assert them from memory (cf. the 08-17 image wall).

**Botswana — decline.** Free basic education for the first ten years, 96%+ primary enrolment (search-stage, secondary only). But the current register runs the other way: Botswana's own news agency (BOPA, **Gontle Merafhe**, 25 March 2025, `https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/85433`) reports President **Duma Boko** describing the education system as being in **"shambles"** and calling for radical revamp. The STEAM / General Education Curriculum and Assessment Framework targeted at January 2026 is **announced, not measured** — no served confirmation the rollout began. **You cannot build a verified positive piece on a system whose head of state called it "in shambles" fourteen months ago.**
