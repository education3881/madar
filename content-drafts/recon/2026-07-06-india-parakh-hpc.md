# Recon — India: PARAKH and the Holistic Progress Card
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument (official name, issuing body, date, link)
- **PARAKH** (Performance Assessment, Review and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic Development) — India's national assessment regulator, constituted 2023 under NCERT as the National Assessment Centre mandated by NEP 2020. Portal: https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/
- **PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan (PRS) 2024** — successor to the National Achievement Survey; sat 4 Dec 2024 in Classes 3, 6, 9; national report released early July 2025 (coverage dated 7–10 July 2025). Primary report PDF: https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-07/REPORT_India_IND.pdf · hub: https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs · state reports: https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs-reports-2024
- **Holistic Progress Card (HPC)** — PARAKH-designed 360° report card replacing marks-only cards; stage cards published for foundational, preparatory, middle and secondary stages: https://ncert.nic.in/parakh/hpc.php · secondary-stage card PDF: https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/themes/parakh/hpc-files/cards-pdf/Holistic-Progress-Card-(Secondary-Stage).pdf

## 4. Status as of 2026-07 (latest developments, reversals, disputes)
- Report, state reports and district dashboard live since July 2025; PARAKH ran state/regional implementation workshops through H2 2025 (e.g., DIET Kargil HPC workshop with PARAKH, Aug 2025: https://ladakh.gov.in/diet-kargil-and-parakh-ncert-collaborate-to-empower-educators-with-holistic-progress-card-implementation/).
- HPC now spans all four school stages; adoption is state-by-state adaptation (translations, teacher training), not a single national switch-on — the story's live edge is uneven state uptake.
- Adjacent NEP assessment reform operating in 2026: CBSE's second Class-10 board sitting (Bhaduri piece references the twice-a-year boards) — context only.
- No national re-measure yet; next PRS expected on a roughly 3-year cadence (~2027) — treat cadence as expected, not announced.

## 5. Proposed angle (the mechanism story)
India changed the ruler at both ends of the system in the same reform. At the top, PARAKH replaced the old National Achievement Survey with a stage-aligned census-scale survey and, in July 2025, published the uncomfortable arithmetic: averages that start at 64% in Class 3 language and fall to 37% in Class 9 maths. Instead of stopping at a national headline, it pushed the results down into 781 public district report cards — accountability aimed at the administrative unit that can actually act. At the bottom, the same regulator redesigned the artefact a parent holds: the marks-only report card gives way to the Holistic Progress Card, which records socio-emotional and psychomotor development alongside marks, with self- and peer-assessment built in. The mechanism question the piece walks through: what happens when a system that used to measure children one way starts measuring them two new ways at once — does the district report card change state behaviour, and does the HPC change classroom behaviour, or do both become paperwork? The honest answer as of mid-2026 is that the measuring is real and public while the acting is still uneven and state-by-state — which is itself the story, told with the dashboard as evidence.
