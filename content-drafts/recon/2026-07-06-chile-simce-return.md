# Recon — Chile: SIMCE's return and what it now measures

**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- **Simce** (Sistema de Medición de la Calidad de la Educación), census standardized assessment, administered by the **Agencia de Calidad de la Educación** (results delivery) under Mineduc's Plan de Evaluaciones Nacionales e Internacionales 2021–2026.
- Suspended 2020 (pandemic); census application resumed **November 2022**. Since the 2023 cycle, results are delivered at the **start of the school year** (early March) together with the **Indicadores de Desarrollo Personal y Social (IDPS)** — the socioemotional/wellbeing strand — and Cuestionarios de Calidad y Contexto.
- Latest delivery: **Simce 2025 results, 2026-03-06** — https://www.agenciaeducacion.cl/simce/ · results portal: https://resultadossimce.agenciaeducacion.cl/

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- **The ruler survived a budget knife.** The incoming administration's Mineduc proposed cutting the 2026 plan to 4°b + II medio only (plus 2°b reading), citing fiscal restrictions. **CNED rejected it (May 2026)**, warning of a "brecha crítica" in monitoring learning trajectories and questioning the loss of 6°b, 8°b English and natural sciences — Emol/El Mercurio, 2026-05-20: https://www.emol.com/noticias/Nacional/2026/05/20/1200492/simce-ajuste-mineduc.html
- CNED subsequently approved a revised proposal restoring 6° básico (La Tercera, above). The Agencia had meanwhile advanced a minimum financeable scenario with Contraloría.
- Standing critique alive: Alto al SIMCE current; recent technical critiques — El Mostrador column 2026-03-02 (https://www.elmostrador.cl/noticias/opinion/columnas/2026/03/02/del-pragmatismo-a-la-renuncia-el-debate-pendiente-sobre-el-simce/); USACH academic on over-valuation of standardized testing (https://www.usach.cl/news/experto-critica-excesiva-valoracion-pruebas-estandarizadas-medicion-simce). Treat as dispute-not-verdict.

## 5. Proposed angle
Chile's national ruler was stress-tested twice in four years — once by a pandemic that suspended it, once by a budget that tried to shrink it — and both times the system chose to keep measuring. The return story is a mechanism story: the 2022 resumption produced the fall on record, 2023–24 produced the recovery, and 2025 produced the hardest thing to narrate — a plateau the Agencia itself calls stabilisation. What Simce now measures has quietly broadened: scores arrive bundled with wellbeing indicators (IDPS) and context questionnaires, and delivery moved to March so schools can act on the data in the same year — measurement retooled as a pedagogic instrument, not a ranking. The 2026 CNED episode shows the counterweight: when the ministry proposed measuring less, the independent council forced the census back to three grades. Meanwhile Alto al SIMCE keeps the opposite question open — whether the instrument distorts more than it reveals. The piece holds both: what the ruler shows (recovery, then plateau; a widening maths gender gap), and the live fight over how much ruler a country can afford.
