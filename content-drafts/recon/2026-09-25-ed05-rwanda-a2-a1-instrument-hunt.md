## 2. The finding the hunt was not looking for — **the word *A1* is press-supplied too**

The 10 August register is the one first-party document that carries the TTC change. Read in
full today, its TTC passage is three paragraphs. The first is the sentence the recon already
has. The third is the one that matters here, verbatim as served:

> "The extended programme will allow trainees to deepen their subject knowledge, strengthen
> their teaching skills and gain more hands-on experience in schools. It will also help bring
> Rwanda's qualification requirements for primary teachers closer to regional and international
> standards, where **a diploma or bachelor's degree is increasingly required** to enter the
> profession."

**The ministry does not say the five-year programme awards an A1.** It names no tier at all. It
gestures at *a diploma or bachelor's degree* and attributes that standard to the region, not to
itself. The page also says **nothing whatever about teachers already in service** — confirmed by
a second read asking that question directly.

The New Times supplies both missing pieces: that the extra two years "will enable graduates to
qualify at **A1** level," and that serving A2 holders have five years to reach the same tier,
in a two-phase plan that then moves A1 holders to A0. **Every occurrence of the A-tier
vocabulary in this instrument reaches us through one newspaper.**

That is a sharper constraint than §5 recorded, and it is a **drafting** constraint rather than
an ending one:

> **GUARDRAIL (new, binding).** No sentence may attribute the *A1 outcome of the five-year
> TTC* to MINEDUC. The ministry's own claim is *longer training, closer to a regional standard
> that increasingly expects a diploma or degree.* The tier labels — A2, A1, A0 — are The New
> Times', and any sentence using them carries the paper and the date in the same clause.

It is #43's hierarchy arriving as a vocabulary problem rather than a figure problem: the
register we trust least is the only one fluent in the terms the piece most wants to use.

## 3. What is confirmed, and confirmed clean

**The #62 licence holds on a second read.** The article dates the *speech*, not merely itself:
*"The announcement was made on Thursday, August 20, by State Minister for Education Claudette
Irere during the release of the results of the 2025/26 national examinations."* The paper's own
publication date is **24 August 2026**, four days later, and the two are not the same digits —
which is the condition under which #62's defect cannot hide. Recorded as licensed.

**The quote is unchanged as served:** *"We have put in place a five-year period to give teachers
an opportunity to upgrade their qualifications."*

**And what the article does not contain**, asked directly and answered: it cites **no written
ministry document**, and it states **no number of teachers affected**. The second matters — the
piece's own denominator work (~27,322 untrained, 2023/24 Yearbook) is the only quantity anyone
has attached to this instrument, and it was attached by this operation, not by the register.
**It may not be presented as the instrument's scope.** Nothing read says the five-year window
applies to that set; it applies to A2 holders, and *untrained* is not *A2-qualified* — a fourth
definition beside the three the seam already names.
