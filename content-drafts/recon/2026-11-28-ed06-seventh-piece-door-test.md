## Why IPST was declined even though it answered

`ipst.ac.th/news/126897/climate-literacy-pisa2029.html` is a first-party, dated, reachable register from a
national PISA centre in a priority-interest country, and it is tempting. **It is declined because its
subject is the instrument, not a geography.** `docs/10_geo_scope.md`: *international orgs are fine as a
source, but the piece must be about an in-scope geography, not about the institution.* A piece on what PISA
will measure in 2029, anchored on Thailand only by who announced it, is a piece about PISA. Thailand already
has this edition's fourth piece on its own 2025 results, which is the geography-anchored version of the
same door.

**Recorded rather than dropped, because it is a real candidate for a different slot:** a piece on what a
national PISA centre tells its own teachers about a domain that does not exist yet would be about Thailand
if it were built on what IPST is asking Thai schools to do. That needs a Researcher pass, not a door test.

## The honest conclusion, and the single next step

**No candidate on this list clears the Editor's condition today without a document being read**, and reading
a document is a Researcher pass rather than a reachability check. The condition exists precisely so a
seventh piece is not commissioned on a thin register, and it did its job: **it stopped a commission rather
than slowed one.**

**The next content run's first content act, in this order:**

1. **Walk `eec.mn`'s link set** — its news, reports and assessment indices — for a dated PISA 2025 item and
   a named official. Mongolia is non-OECD, in scope, and on the report's own risers list. One open door, one
   unwalked link set: that is exactly the shape that opened Qatar and Uruguay.
2. If that returns nothing, **walk `kemendikdasmen.go.id`**, same method.
3. If both return nothing, **the edition stops at six and that is a correct outcome**, not a failure: six is
   the floor and it is met, two pieces are one cell from the flag, and the plan says explicitly that a short
   edition is named in the brief rather than padded.

The next run does not pay for that again.*
