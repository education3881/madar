# Recon — United States: NAEP vs state proficiency — the honesty gap
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- **NAEP — The Nation's Report Card** (National Assessment of Educational Progress): administered by **NCES**; policy and achievement levels set by the **National Assessment Governing Board (NAGB)**. Latest national read: **NAEP 2024** (administered Jan–Mar 2024, released 29 Jan 2025) — https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/
- The comparison instrument: **"Mapping State Proficiency Standards Onto the NAEP Scales" (NCES 2026-014, published Feb 2026)** — places every state's own "proficient" cut score onto the NAEP scale using 2021-22 state data + NAEP 2022. https://ies.ed.gov/nces/2026/02/mapping-state-proficiency-standards-naep-scales-results-2022-naep-reading-and-mathematics

## 2. Figures
- **NAEP 2024 reading (MEASURED, NCES/NAGB, 29 Jan 2025):** grade 4: **31% at/above NAEP Proficient** (−2 pts vs 2022, −4 vs 2019) https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/reports/reading/2024/g4_8/ ; **~40% of grade 4 below NAEP Basic — largest share since 2002**; about a third of grade 8 below NAEP Basic — **largest ever recorded**; average scores −2 pts at both grades vs 2022; no state gained in reading. https://www.nagb.gov/news-and-events/news-releases/2025/nations-report-card-decline-in-reading-progress-in-math.html
- **NAEP 2024 math (MEASURED, same release):** grade 4 +2 pts vs 2022, nearly 40% at/above NAEP Proficient; grade 8 flat, "a little more than a quarter" at/above Proficient and nearly 40% below NAEP Basic. Only Louisiana (G4 reading) and Alabama (G4 math) have surpassed their 2019 scores.
- **The gap itself (MEASURED, NCES 2026-014, Feb 2026):** in 2022, **most states' own "proficient" standards mapped at the NAEP *Basic* level** in both grades and subjects. Grade 4 reading: only **6 of 50 states** (AK, DC, IL, MA, OK, RI) set their bar at NAEP Proficient; 2 states (IA, VA) below NAEP Basic; the spread between the toughest and easiest state bars = **49 NAEP points — wider than the 30-point distance between NAEP Basic and NAEP Proficient**. So identical students flip from "proficient" to "not" by crossing a state line. (URL in §1.)
- **State responses, 2025-26 (status):** Oklahoma — after the state education department lowered cut scores in 2024, the Commission for Educational Quality and Accountability voted **unanimously (21 May 2025)** to restore the 2017–2023 NAEP-aligned cut scores ("truth and transparency"). https://oklahomavoice.com/2025/05/21/oklahoma-board-raises-testing-rigor-to-restore-truth-and-transparency-to-student-testing/ (context: https://oklahoma.gov/oeqa/about-oeqa/faqs.html). Counter-direction: several states have lowered/recalibrated post-pandemic benchmarks (Wisconsin 2024 among them) — The 74: https://www.the74million.org/article/responding-to-post-pandemic-norms-more-states-are-lowering-testing-standards/ (ANNOUNCED/reported; verify any named state on its own DOE page before use).

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- **Schedule/budget turbulence 2025 (all status, report as institutional events):** long-term-trend age-17 assessment cancelled, field operations ended 19 Feb 2025 (https://www.the74million.org/article/after-declaring-naep-off-limits-education-department-cancels-upcoming-test/); NCES Commissioner Peggy Carr placed on paid leave days later; April 2025 scope reductions (https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/fewer-subjects-students-data-points-feds-to-scale-back-naep/2025/04); NAGB votes trimmed the schedule through 2033 — grade 4 science 2028 cut, no grade 12 science through 2032, 2030 grade 12 U.S. history cut, 2032 writing cancelled (https://www.k12dive.com/news/NAEP-nations-report-card-assessment-changes-2033-nagb-vote/746821/).
- **NAEP 2026 went ahead:** ED statement confirming the 2026 schedule (https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-releases-statement-2026-naep-schedule); administered **26 Jan–20 Mar 2026**, grades 4/8 reading + math on updated frameworks, plus national G8 civics/U.S. history (https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/subject/participating/pdfs/2026_naep_in_your_school_math_reading_naep_device.pdf). **Results due early 2027** — NAEP 2024 stays the latest MEASURED national read through our window; the Feb 2026 mapping study is the freshest federal comparison.

## 5. Proposed angle
This is a story about a word, not a war: "proficient" is a policy choice, and the U.S. runs fifty-plus definitions of it in parallel. NAEP is the fixed ruler — same test, same cut scores since the 1990s — and the February 2026 NCES mapping shows what happens when you lay every state's bar against it: most states call "proficient" what NAEP calls Basic, and the distance between the strictest and loosest state definitions (49 points) is wider than NAEP's own distance between Basic and Proficient. The seam becomes visible every NAEP year: January 2025's release put grade 4 reading at 31% proficient nationally while typical state report cards tell parents two or three times that share are on track. The mechanism to explain: cut scores are set by committees, moved by boards, and every move manufactures or erases "proficiency" without a single child learning more or less. Then the 2025-26 twist in both directions — Oklahoma re-pegging its bar to NAEP after a one-year dip, other states recalibrating downward post-pandemic — while the fixed ruler itself wobbled for the first time (cancelled assessments, a commissioner on leave, a slimmed schedule to 2033). Close on the quiet dependency: every state's honesty about its own numbers is only checkable because the federal ruler exists.

## 6. Risks / guardrail notes
- **Zero partisan framing** (founder rule for this piece): report the 2025 cancellations/leave/schedule cuts as dated institutional events; name no parties; ascribe no motives; both cut-score directions (raising and lowering) get the same mechanical treatment with each state's stated rationale.
- **NAEP Proficient ≠ grade level.** NCES/NAGB's own caution — "NAEP Proficient does not represent grade-level proficiency as determined by other assessment standards." Must appear in-piece; otherwise we commit the same error the piece describes.
- The mapping study says explicitly it is **"not an evaluation" of state assessments** and states may legitimately set different bars — carry that line; "honesty gap" is an advocacy coinage (Collaborative for Student Success), attribute it as such if used.
- NAEP achievement levels are by law "trial basis, interpret with caution" — one-line note suffices.
- Wisconsin (or any second state example) must be verified on the state DOE's own documents before drafting; The 74 alone is not load-bearing.
