# Recon — Poland: Ukrainian students under the September 2024 school obligation
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- Amendment to the Act on Assistance to Citizens of Ukraine (nowelizacja ustawy o pomocy obywatelom Ukrainy), adopted May 2024, effective **1 September 2024**: Ukrainian refugee children became subject to Poland's roczne przygotowanie przedszkolne + obowiązek szkolny + obowiązek nauki, same as Polish pupils; the 800+ child benefit was made conditional on the child attending a school in the Polish system. Online study in the Ukrainian system allowed only for the final-year cohort in 2024/25.
- Issuing body: Sejm/MEN (Ministerstwo Edukacji Narodowej). Ministry announcement, 12.06.2024: https://www.gov.pl/web/edukacja/uczniowie-z-ukrainy-w-polskich-szkolach--nowe-rozwiazania-prawne
- Enforcement tightened from **1 June 2025**: ZUS now grants 800+/Dobry Start to UKR-status holders only if the child fulfils the school obligation in a Polish institution (plus residence/economic-activity checks); re-application cycle from 1 Feb 2026. ZUS: https://www.zus.pl/en/-/zmiany-w-800-dla-obywateli-ukrainy-1 · explainer: https://www.pomocprawna.org/nowe-zasady-800-dla-obywateli-ukrainy-od-1-czerwca-2025
- Supporting rozporządzenie (Aug 2024): class-size limits raised to 29 in grades I–III to absorb the influx; asystent międzykulturowy (intercultural assistant) created as a formal school role.

## 4. Status as of 2026-07
- Mechanism is in steady state: 2025/26 enrolment slightly above 2024/25; conditionality enforcement moved to ZUS with the June 2025 rules; next re-application checkpoint passed Feb 2026 (Zielona Linia: https://zielonalinia.gov.pl/800-dla-obywateli-ukrainy-zmiany-od-lutego-2026-r).
- NIK audit (June 2026) validates the machinery: no refusals of admission found in audited schools; criticisms are operational (preparatory-class rules not always followed, Polish-language hours under-delivered, teacher-training gaps) — usable, non-political texture.
- ~20,000 Ukrainian children reportedly lost 800+ for non-attendance (OKO.press investigation of ZUS data: https://oko.press/800-plus-dzieci-z-ukrainy-polska-szkola) — reconcile with NIK's 101 mln zł figure at production; use NIK as spine.
- Government support programme "Szkoła dla wszystkich"/"Przyjazna szkoła" 2025–2027 running (MEN).

## 5. Proposed angle
Poland ran a live experiment in what closes the refugee-enrolment gap: two years of open-door voluntary access left tens of thousands of children in Ukrainian online schooling, so in September 2024 the state flipped the default — compulsory schooling extended to refugee children, with the 800+ child benefit wired to attendance. The mechanism story is the machinery built around that switch: raised class-size caps, preparatory classes, a new intercultural-assistant role, 36 months of free Polish lessons, and an exam waiver for eighth-graders. The measurable result was smaller than the ministry feared and the system absorbed it: ~13,000 net additional pupils rather than 80,000, enrolment up 9%, and by 2025/26 refugee pupils sit in 57.8% of Polish schools. Enforcement then migrated from schools to the benefits agency: since June 2025 ZUS verifies attendance before paying. The state audit published last month closes the loop — the right to education was delivered, and the remaining faults are operational, not legal. Kicker: the conditionality lever cost non-compliant families real money (101 mln zł withheld), which is the uncomfortable core of how the mechanism works.

## 6. Risks / guardrail notes
- Keep the 800+ conditionality framed as mechanism design, not as a judgment on Ukrainian families; avoid Polish domestic politics (2025 presidential veto fights over the special act) entirely — not needed for this story.
- Figure hygiene: school-only counts (NIK series) vs counts incl. preschools (195,288) differ — label denominators explicitly.
- MEN's 50–80k estimate vs ~13k actual: present as forecasting-under-uncertainty, not ministry failure.
