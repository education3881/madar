## GATE 1 — the named, operational, recent voice. **CLEARED.**

**Register:** Ofsted, *Hundreds of formerly outstanding schools reinspected*, **published 22 November
2022**, read in served text 2026-11-13. First-party: the publishing organisation is printed as **Ofsted**.

**The voice, as the register prints it:** *"Ofsted's Chief Inspector, Amanda Spielman, said:"* — and what
follows is on the record about **this cohort and this policy**, which is why it clears the Saudi bar
rather than merely being a quotable name:

- *"Exempting outstanding schools deprived parents of up-to-date information."*
- *"It also left a lot of schools without the constructive challenge that regular inspection provides."*
- *"The exemption was a policy founded on the hope that high standards, once achieved, would never drop,
  and that freedom from inspection might drive them even higher."*
- *"These outcomes show that removing a school from scrutiny does not make it better."*

**Why this is operational and not representative.** The brief's bar asks for someone who ran an
inspection or was inspected. The Chief Inspector of the regulator that was *forbidden to inspect this
cohort for eight years*, speaking about the cohort after inspecting it, is the operator of the instrument
the piece is about. The union voices on the same story — Paul Whiteman of the NAHT, Jason Elsom of
Parentkind, both quoted in the Department's 2024 release — are **representative** voices and the brief's
bar does not accept them. They are not carried.

**The figures this register prints, with their own period, for the drafter:**
- *"over 80% (308) of these schools that had a graded inspection last year did not retain the outstanding
  grade"* — the period is printed as *last year*, which the page ties to **2021/22**.
- *"Some 1,400 schools remained outstanding throughout the period"* — and the page says these were **not
  inspected at all**.
- about **1,900** schools *"ceased to be outstanding"* during the exemption period, usually after an
  inspection triggered by a risk assessment, with no separate date range printed.

**Carry the 80% with its own period and do NOT reconcile it against the recon's 17%.** They are different
releases over different windows and the piece must print each with its own "as at" date. *Announced ≠
measured*, and 2021/22 ≠ 2021–24.

## The gender read — **CLEARED, and the brief's own assumption about it was wrong**

The 11-12 brief said *English registers carry pronouns, so this is a cheap line here*. On the register the
voice comes from, **it is not there at all**: the 22 November 2022 release carries **no third-person
pronoun and no gendered title** for Amanda Spielman anywhere — only *"Ofsted's Chief Inspector, Amanda
Spielman, said:"*. Read directly, asking the question #86 clause 1 asks, rather than assumed.

**Settled on a second first-party register**, Ofsted's own speech page *Amanda Spielman's speech to the
Festival of Education, 2023* (gov.uk, Ofsted), whose standfirst prints: **"Ofsted's Chief Inspector
reflects on her first speech at the Festival of Education."** That is the register's own third-person
feminine pronoun, so **the determination is a READ and not this edition's own** — #86 clause 1, not
clause 2, and the Arabic composition may use the feminine role noun on that evidence.

**One trap on that page, named so the Arabic composer does not walk into it.** The only other gendered
word on it is in her own first-person text — *"this is my last speech as His Majesty's Chief Inspector"* —
and the **His** is the monarch's, attached to the office. Arabic must gender the role noun and cannot
decline to; a composer reading the office phrase rather than the standfirst would gender her from a word
that is not about her. *A gendered morpheme in a title is a fact about the title.*

**Hand-off line this supplies:** `named humans — gender read from register: 1 of 1` — Amanda Spielman,
feminine, from *"reflects on her first speech"*, Ofsted's own standfirst, read 2026-11-13.

**Also noted, not chased:** her gov.uk person page (`/government/people/amanda-spielman`) now returns

## GATE 2 — the dated removal of single-word overall judgements. **CLEARED, and the owner is not who the brief assumed.**

**Register:** *Single headline grades scrapped in landmark school reform*, **published 2 September 2024**,
read in served text 2026-11-13. Publishing organisation as printed: **Department for Education**, with
*The Rt Hon Bridget Phillipson MP* on the From line.

- The removal, verbatim: **"Single headline grades for schools will be scrapped with immediate effect"**.
- What replaces it this academic year, verbatim: **"For inspections this academic year, parents will see
  four grades across the existing sub-categories"** — *quality of education, behaviour and attitudes,
  personal development and leadership & management*.
- The named decision-maker, quoted: **Bridget Phillipson, Education Secretary** — *"Single headline grades
  are low information for parents and high stakes for schools."*

**The correction this read makes to the brief.** Gate 2 was written as *Ofsted's first-party account of the
September 2024 removal*. The removal is **not Ofsted's act**: it was announced by the **Department for
Education**, by the Secretary of State, with immediate effect, and the regulator implemented it. The body
must attribute it that way. This matters to the piece's spine rather than being a pedantry — the piece's
frame is *the instrument has been replaced by its own author*, and on the single-word judgement the author
was the Department and not the regulator. The **framework** change of 10 November 2025 is the regulator's
own; the **grade removal** of 2 September 2024 is the Department's. Two instrument changes, two different
owners, fourteen months apart, and the body says which is which.

**The brief's prohibition is therefore LIFTED with a condition:** the body may now print the removal date,
**attributed to the Department's dated announcement**, and may not attribute the removal to Ofsted.

**Not read and not needed:** the page says nothing about schools keeping an existing grade until their
next inspection, so the piece does not claim it. If the drafter wants that, it is a separate first-party
read and a separate annotation.
