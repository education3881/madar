## 2. THE CLOCK HAS MOVED UNDER THE RECON, AND IT MOVED THROUGH ITS DRAFTING INSTRUCTION

The recon's §5 reads: *"TTC five-year programme starts September 2026 — three weeks from
publication. A piece running now sits directly on the transition, which is the strongest
possible publication timing and must be said in the piece: **the first five-year cohort has not
yet enrolled**."* Its §3 adds the drafting rule: *"Not yet in force; **write it in the future
tense**."*

**Today is 24 September 2026. September has arrived.** MINEDUC's own register says *"Beginning
in September 2026, learners enrolling in Teacher Training Colleges (TTCs) will undertake a
five-year programme instead of the current three-year programme."*

A drafter following this recon's explicit instruction would compose a false tense into the
piece, **and the instruction would be the reason.** This is **#46's corollary 2** — *a plan is
only a plan until its date arrives* — and it is the edition's third instance, after Zambia's
August election (¶103) and slot 3's December (¶94). **It is also the first one this operation
has caught BEFORE drafting rather than after banking**, which is the entire purpose of the #40
corollary and the first evidence it pays for itself.

**But the tense is not simply to be flipped to the past, and this is the finding under the
finding.** What is on the record is an *announcement* that the programme begins in September.
Whether the first five-year cohort actually enrolled is **not established by any register read
today** — and a government-adjacent report of 24 August (§5 below) says discussions were then
still open on whether trainees would spend all five years in TTCs or finish at the University
of Rwanda's College of Education, three weeks before the programme was due to start. *Announced
≠ commenced*, which is this edition's own standing prohibition wearing a calendar.

**Binding for the drafter:** the sentence is written as *announced for September 2026*, with
the announcement's register and date attached, **until a register is read that says the cohort
enrolled.** Do not write "began"; do not write "will begin."

## 3. DRAFT-BLOCKER 2 (the denominator) — **CLOSES**, from a register one year older than the one the recon named

**The route the recon named does not exist.** §7.2 sends the drafter to the *Education
Statistical Yearbook 2024/25*. The most recent published is **2023/24** (`Final_Education_
Statistical_Yearbook_23_24.pdf`, mineduc.gov.rw, source line *"School Census, Ministry of
Education, 2024"*). Named rather than skipped, per #54's discipline: the absence is the
publication schedule, not a dead link.

Read in that register, **Tables 3.2.1–3.2.3, printed page 87–88:**

| Figure, 2023/24 | As read |
|---|---|
| Total teaching staff, all levels | **113,548** (the register's own prose: *"increased by 3,088 persons from 110,460 in 2022/23 to 113,548 in 2023/24"*) |
| **Pre-primary teaching staff** | **10,898** (prose: *"increased by 1,618, rising from 9,280 in the 2022/2023 school year to 10,898 in the 2023/2024"*) |
| **Primary teaching staff** | **68,326** (male 29,601 + female 38,725) |

**Pre-primary + primary = 79,224.** That is the denominator the 24,000 is a share of, and it
makes the number legible for the first time.

**How it was read, and why the method is stated (#52).** The yearbook is a 5 MB PDF that no
tool on this runner renders. It was extracted with a stdlib zlib/`Tj` decoder written for the
purpose. **A mangled table is exactly where a composed figure comes from**, so nothing above is
carried on the extraction alone — every figure is confirmed at least twice:

- **68,326** — the sex split sums to it, the register's own `%Male 43.3%` recomputes it
  (29,601 ÷ 68,326 = 43.32%), and the World Bank independently reports 68,326 primary teachers
  for 2024.
- **10,898** — the sex split sums to it *and* the register's prose states it in words.
- **113,548** — stated in the register's prose in words.

**And what is deliberately NOT carried:** the *trained*-teacher trend in Table 3.6.2. The
register's prose says trained primary teachers **decreased** from 45,875, while the columns as
extracted sum to a larger 2023/24 figure — which means the sparse rows' column alignment is
unreliable and a trend read off them would be invented. **The untrained figures below survive
this because they are confirmed by prose and by percentage, and the trend is not.** Flagged so
nobody later reads the omission as an oversight.

## 4. DRAFT-BLOCKER 1 (the funnel) — **PARTLY CLOSES**, and the half that closes is the half that matters

The recon asked for enrolled / sat / passed / still-uncertified, and routed to NESA and REB.
**None of those four is published, and today's hunt did not find them.** That limb stays open
and the route-2 precedent (ruling #11) applies: publish what can be read, state in print that
the pass rate is not published.

**But the ministry counts the debt itself, which nobody had looked for.** Yearbook Tables 3.6.1
and 3.6.2, printed pages 104–105, count teaching staff as *trained* or *untrained*:

| 2023/24 | Untrained | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-primary | **5,162** | **47.4%** |
| Primary | **22,160** | **32.4%** |
| **Combined** | **~27,322** | — |

Both confirmed twice: the register's prose states *"from 4,382 untrained teachers in 2022/23 to
5,162 untrained teachers in 2023/24, representing a share of 47.4% untrained pre-primary
teachers in 2023/24"* in words; and 22,160 ÷ 68,326 = 32.4%, the register's own printed share.

**The chronology is what makes this valuable.** The programme opened **April 2024**; the
2023/24 school year **ended in July 2024**. So this count is the debt as it stood essentially
at the programme's opening, in the ministry's own voice, on a definition the ministry chose —
and the 24,000 is the discharge claimed against it. That is precisely what draft-blocker 2 was
for, and it arrives from a register the recon sent the drafter to for something else.

**A third number, and a three-way seam — ruling #14 exactly.** The piece now has:

- **~25,000** entered under relaxed criteria from 2021 — *World Bank feature*
- **~27,322** counted **untrained** in 2023/24 — *MINEDUC Statistical Yearbook*
- **24,000** trained **and certified** by Mar 2026 — *World Bank + REB*

Three registers, three definitions, three vintages. *Entered* is not *untrained* is not
*certified*, and the residual against the ministry's own count is **larger** than the ~1,000 the
recon computed against the funder's. **No sentence may put two of these three in a subtraction
without naming both registers and both dates in the same sentence.** The recon's §3 guardrail is
extended accordingly.

**And the register carries the debt's cause in its own voice**, which the positive posture
should quote rather than paraphrase — the yearbook attributes the decline in trained primary
teachers to *"newly recruited primary teachers who may not have received training."* The
ministry wrote the debt down in its own statistical annual. That is the piece's thesis, stated
by the subject, and it is a better citation than anything in the funder's feature.

## 5. NEW DRAFT-BLOCKER — an instrument that changes the piece's ENDING, and it is reported speech

On **20 August 2026**, at the release of the 2025/26 national examination results, **State
Minister for Education Claudette Irere** announced a **five-year period for teachers holding
secondary-level (A2) qualifications to upgrade to an A1 diploma**, quoted as: *"We have put in
place a five-year period to give teachers an opportunity to upgrade their qualifications."*

**If it holds, the piece's spine changes** — and improves. The recon's argument is *incur,
disclose, retire*. This makes it **incur, disclose, retire, and immediately re-open at a higher
bar**: the Professional Teaching Certificate the 24,000 just earned is an A2-tier credential,
and the same ministry has now put those holders on a five-year clock to exceed it. The
accomplishment is not diminished by that; it is *sharpened*, because it shows a system treating
a qualification floor as something that moves.

**But it cannot carry an ending yet.** The claim reaches us **only through The New Times
(Kigali)**, via allAfrica. The recon's own guardrail says Rwandan press is *"government-adjacent:
quotes and colour only, never load-bearing figures,"* and **#43's hierarchy** puts reported
speech below a first-party written register. An ending is load-bearing by definition.

- **Owed before commission:** MINEDUC's or REB's own register for the A2→A1 five-year period —
  a communiqué, a policy page, or the ministerial instruction. If none is published, the
  instrument is carried as *reported remarks by a named minister on a named date*, in-sentence,
  and it may not be the closing argument.
- **Licensed, and recorded as such (#62):** the date **20 August 2026** attaches to the
  *announcement event* — the article dates the speech itself, *"on Thursday, August 20 …
  during the release of the results"* — not to a publication date read as an event date. This
  one is clean, and it is noted because the edition has just spent a ruling on the cases that
  are not.
