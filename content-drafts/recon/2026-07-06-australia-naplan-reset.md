# Recon — Australia: the NAPLAN proficiency reset
**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Researcher → Editor** · **Status: PROCEED**

## 1. The instrument
- **NAPLAN** (National Assessment Program — Literacy and Numeracy), years 3/5/7/9, run by **ACARA** (Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority).
- The reset: announced 10 Feb 2023 — four proficiency levels (Exceeding / Strong / Developing / Needs additional support) replaced the 10-band scale and the 2008 national minimum standard; new time series starts 2023; testing moved to March; standards set at "challenging but reasonable" and validated by teacher expert panels. https://www.acara.edu.au/docs/default-source/media-releases/naplan-proficiency-standards-media-release-2023-02-10.pdf
- Results on the new scale: 23 Aug 2023, 14 Aug 2024, 30 Jul 2025 national releases (links below).

## 2. Figures
- **2023, first year on the new ruler (MEASURED):** ~65% of students at Strong/Exceeding, ~23% Developing, ~10% Needs additional support — i.e. roughly **1 in 3 below the "meets expectations" bar** the moment it moved. By year level: Y3 65%, Y5 68%, Y7 67%, Y9 62% meeting. Year 9 writing: 58.0% meeting. On average ~1 in 3 Indigenous students at Needs additional support. https://www.acara.edu.au/docs/default-source/media-releases/naplan-national-results-release-media-release-23.08.23.pdf
- **2024 (MEASURED):** broadly stable vs 2023; ~two-thirds meeting expectations; 4.4m tests, ~1.3m students. https://www.acara.edu.au/docs/default-source/media-releases/media-release-2024-naplan-national-results-release-14-8-24.pdf
- **2025 (MEASURED):** reading Strong+Exceeding **68.2%** (2024: 67.0%); numeracy **66.8%** (65.5%); Needs additional support: reading **9.3%** (10.3%), numeracy **9.2%** (9.5%). Participation **93.8%** — highest since 2017. Numeracy gains in Y5/7/9 "particularly among stronger students" = **+20,000 students at Exceeding vs 2024**. https://www.acara.edu.au/docs/default-source/media-releases/2025-naplan-national-results-broadly-stable-as-participation-rates-bounce-back-30-7-25.pdf
- **What the new scale makes visible (MEASURED, 2025, same URL):** Indigenous students at Needs additional support in reading **30.8%** vs non-Indigenous **7.6%**; very remote schools Strong+Exceeding in reading **22.8%** vs major cities **71.9%**; Year 9 writing Strong+Exceeding: girls **69.1%** vs boys **53.9%**.
- **Writing-gap debate (secondary analyses of NAPLAN data — not ACARA releases):** AERO decade-of-NAPLAN writing study (~1 in 3 Year 9 students writing at primary level) — summary: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED673992.pdf and coverage https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/onethird-of-year-9-students-writing-at-primary-level-report-finds/287598 ; 2025 media analysis claiming 52.6% of Qld Year 9 boys "failing" writing https://www.literacyforkids.com.au/53pc-of-year-9-boys-are-failing-writing/ ; academic counter-analysis (AARE) that such claims distort AERO/ACARA data https://blog.aare.edu.au/naplan-results-havent-collapsed-but-media-interpretations-have/

## 3. Voice

Also: "These may be small percentage changes, but the increases represent an additional 20,000 Australian students performing at the highest proficiency level – 'Exceeding' – in 2025 compared to 2024."

## 5. Proposed angle
The 2023 reset is the cleanest case going of a country deliberately changing the ruler rather than the students. Under the old 2008 national minimum standard, ~90% of students routinely cleared a bar widely criticised as too low to mean anything; ministers replaced it with a "challenging but reasonable" expectations bar, and overnight roughly one in three students stood below the line — with ACARA saying explicitly that this was the point. The mechanism story writes itself in three moves: what the reset was designed to reveal (a baseline benchmark for support need, teacher-validated), what three cycles on the new scale actually show (stable topline: 65%→67%→68% in reading; needs-support steady at ~1 in 10), and what the sharper ruler now makes undeniable (a 4x Indigenous support gap, a 49-point city/remote seam, a widening boys' writing problem). The quiet second finding is that the reset survived: no state walked it back, participation recovered to pre-COVID highs, and the first growth cohorts (two assessments on one scale) arrived in 2025. The tension to carry: a higher bar produces better information and worse headlines, and the writing-gap fight shows how quickly "below Strong" gets misread as "failing." Ends on the ruler holding still — the thing the old NAPLAN never managed.

## 6. Risks / guardrail notes
- **Never compare post-2023 numbers to pre-2023 bands** — ACARA's own rule; the time series restarts in 2023.
- "Roughly 1 in 3 below the bar" is a derivation (Developing + Needs additional support); ACARA's framing is "2 in 3 meeting." State both; do not render Developing as "failing" — it is defined as "working towards expectations."
- Writing-gap figures (52.6% Qld boys etc.) are third-party analyses of ACARA data, not ACARA-published proficiency stats — attribute as analysis, keep the AARE counter in-piece if used.
- 2026 national results due early Aug 2026: re-check ACARA newsroom at publish gate; do not let the piece read as if 2025 is current-year data without saying so.
- Terminology for AR rendering: fix one gloss each for "proficiency level," "Strong," "Needs additional support" and reuse.
