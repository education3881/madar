## The commission, one line

Answer the founder's question the only honest way research can: **there is no African
system that wins on every published ruler, and the four defensible rulers crown four
different countries** — level (Mauritius), learning-relative-to-income (Senegal and Benin),
improvement (Niger, and South Africa on the global instrument), and shared gains (nobody).
The piece names each ruler, says what it can and cannot see, crowns Mauritius as the most
defensible single answer with Niger as the counter-crown, and takes the aggregator's answer
away from the reader in the first paragraph (#29: a ranking is not a measurement).

## The spine

**Choosing the ruler is choosing what school is for.** Runs dek to close. The edition's
bridge from Ed04: Ed04 asked whether the number on a child's certificate means the same
thing twice; this asks it of the number on a country's. And the frame that makes it a
2026 piece rather than a 2021 one: **the continent's widest ruler is about to be re-read** —
PASEC 2024's results are announced for the last quarter of 2026 — so a crown handed out in
September is handed out knowing the ruler changes in December. That is not a caveat to
bury; it is the argument, demonstrated.

## Positive posture, honestly earned (founder's Ed05 frame)

The accomplishments are builds and every one is verified in served text: Mauritius
converting 12.4 expected years into 9.4 learned ones against a region that loses far more;
Niger's contract-teacher audit, remedial-maths programme and director accountability, with
one of the two largest PASEC gains and *no change in class size*; Benin's union settlement
and school-feeding programme behind the other; South Africa's 2011→2019 climb at Grade 9.
The equity ruler rides in-frame at full weight — Burkina Faso's bottom decile down 45 while
the average rose, between-school concentration up in all fourteen, maths down 41→39, the
45-year line — because a piece that reports velocity without the floor is the aggregator's
trick in the other direction.

## Standing prohibitions for this piece

- No "best" without its ruler in the same sentence. No composite of rulers, ever.
- No figure from a country brief compared across countries without both vintages stated
  (Mauritius 2018/2006 vs Kenya 2019/2006 in the HCI brief).
- "Punching above its weight" is a residual claim — the regression (HLO vs GDP per capita,
  Patrinos & Angrist) is named where it is used.
- Improvement is never reported without its floor; the floor is never reported without
  its improvement.
- The piece does not say Niger "sacked" anyone in our voice — it carries the report's own
  description (evaluated all contract teachers; dismissed those below the required
  proficiency) attributed to the report.
