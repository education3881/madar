## The four returned notes

### Note 1 — a significance qualifier carried one subject too wide. **Test 1. Closed.**

The Niger paragraph read: *"at the end of primary, reading rose 67.5 and mathematics 56.0,
and at that level Niger and Benin were the only two countries whose gains were
statistically significant at all."*

The recon's row 11 attaches *"the only two significant gains"* to **mathematics** at the end
of primary (p. 225). It does not hold for reading, and the piece's own ruler four depends on
its not holding: Senegal's end-of-primary reading mean rose **+27.5\*\*\*** (Table 5.6,
p. 225), which is where ruler four's crown comes from. The piece would have contradicted
itself four sections apart.

**Fixed in both languages** — *"and in mathematics at that level…"* / «وفي الرياضيات عند ذلك
المستوى…». No figure added, no figure removed. This is #45's family on the *scope* of a
qualifier rather than the noun of a figure: the significance was real, the level it was
attached to was ours.

### Note 2 — a continental superlative with no continental register. **Test 1. Closed.**

The level section read: *"On that ruler Mauritius was the continent's outlier."*

Nothing read for this piece ranks African countries on the Human Capital Index. What was
read is **one country brief** (Mauritius), against **one comparison** (Kenya, in the 08-16
recon), with the brief's own regional averages printed beside it. *The continent's outlier*
is a rank claim, and it appeared in the one piece whose entire spine is that a rank
published without its ruler is an opinion with decimal places. The commission also forbids
naming Seychelles — the piece honours that (verified by grep, zero hits in both files) — and
a continental superlative asserted while a known neighbour is deliberately unnamed is the
same claim made with the evidence withheld.

**Fixed in both languages:** *"On that ruler the crown is Mauritius's — handed out on a
single country brief read against the regional averages the brief itself prints, not on a
continental table."* The crown survives, stated as the piece's own act, which is what it
always was. #29 and #49 applied to our own sentence rather than to a source's.

### Note 3 — two named humans in the annotations, two pronouns in the bodies. **Test 1 + commission blocker 5. Closed.**

Both bodies carried the TIMSS launch caution as *"the Principal Investigator … she"*, and
both closed on *"the man who has spent his career building the francophone instrument"*.
**Dr Vijay Reddy** and **Hilaire Hounkpodoté** were named in `sources[]` in both languages
and in neither body.

This is the 09-13 review's shape one layer further out. The annotation was right; both
bodies smoothed — and what they smoothed this time was not a hedge or a date but a **name**.
The Arabic gate of 09-14 recorded the decision honestly (*"the name now rides in the
`sources[]` annotation on both sides"*), so this is a disagreement with a stated choice, not
the discovery of an oversight: a citation is where a reader checks a claim, not where they
meet the person making it. The commission's draft-blocker 5 asked for **a named human
voice**; the piece's closing sentence — its rhetorical peak — was attributed to an unnamed
man, and *"has spent his career building"* is itself a biographical description no register
read for this piece carries (#47).

**Fixed in both languages.** Reddy is named and titled at the launch sentence; Hounkpodoté
is named at the close with the affiliation the annotation already carries and nothing more
(*of CONFEMEN, which runs the francophone instrument*), which removes the unsourced career
claim in the same stroke. No figure added.

### Note 4 — the crowned improver's own floor was missing. **Test 2, a standing prohibition of this commission. Closed.**

*"Improvement is never reported without its floor; the floor is never reported without its
improvement"* (commission, 09-04). The piece honours it for South Africa in-paragraph — the
deceleration and the narrowing 320→252 range sit beside the +104 — and it honours it
generally in ruler four. It did **not** honour it for **Niger**, the country ruler three
crowns: the report prints Niger's distribution on the same page as Benin's, and its shape is
the same one ruler four calls out (P10 **+51.6** against P90 **+139.3**, start of primary,
language, p. 222). The reader met the crown without the distribution it sits on.

**Fixed in both languages**, one sentence beside the gain, with the two figures added to the
PASEC annotation on both sides so the sentence and its citation stay written from the same
open register. Numeral multiset re-checked after the edit — see test 1.
