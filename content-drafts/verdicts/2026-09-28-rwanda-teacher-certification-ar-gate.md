This is the edition's sixth and last pair, and the **first in this edition whose registers are not
available in Arabic at all.** Every one of the eight is an English-language document: two ministry
yearbooks that are English PDFs, four English pages on `mineduc.gov.rw`, a World Bank feature, and a
Kigali newspaper. Egypt's Arabic was composed *from Arabic registers* — the statute in Arabic, Youm7's
reproduction — which is why its quoted clauses are the register's own words. Nothing of that kind exists
here. **That single fact drove every decision below**, and it is worth recording as the condition rather
than discovering it again on the next non-Arabophone piece.

## 2. Numeral multiset, EN ↔ AR

```

distinct tokens          52
Arabic-only figures       0

```

**Zero one-sided figures in either direction — the edition's second clean-both-ways pair**, after
Egypt (row 5). Two things made it reachable and both are decisions, not luck:

- **Where the English writes a figure as a word, so does the Arabic.** *Thirty-six facilitators*,
  *thirty districts*, *four modules*, *five-year*, *three-year* → «ستّةٌ وثلاثون ميسِّرًا»،
  «المقاطعات الثلاثين»، «أربعُ وحداتٍ»، «خمس سنوات»، «ثلاث». Writing any of those in digits on the
  Arabic side would have produced an Arabic-only token for a figure the pair genuinely shares. The
  house digit split (Western digits in the body, Arabic-Indic in the caption) is about *which digits*,
  never about *whether* — and a word is not a digit set.
- **One divergence was found and closed at compose, and its mechanism is new to this register.**
  The first composition read `2026` **14 times against the English's 13**. Cause: the Arabic carries
  each load-bearing quotation in the register's own English *and* glosses it in Arabic (§4), so the
  date inside *«Beginning in September 2026…»* was stated twice — once in the quotation and once in
  the gloss. **A verbatim quotation plus a gloss doubles every figure inside the quotation.** The
  gloss now points at the quoted date («من ذلك الشهر») instead of restating it. Recorded because the
  next bilingual-quotation pair will hit it on its first composition too, and the numeral multiset is
  the only instrument that sees it.

## 4. Composition is composition — and the quotation policy is UNIFORM, on purpose

Structure follows the English: three opening movements, seven subheads, same order, same spine (the
amended four moments). Every sentence is composed at register; **no sentence is a back-translation of
ours**, and the body was written with the registers open, per the 09-13 rule.

**The decision this pair turned on.** With no Arabic register anywhere, a quotation in the Arabic body
can only be *our translation of an English sentence*. The corpus precedent (Sierra Leone 08-29, Sudan
09-03) renders such a quotation in Arabic alone. This pair does not, and the reason is not fidelity in
the abstract — it is that **this piece's argument is made of specific words**. *«declined only
marginally»* is the ministry's own adverb and the whole pivot of §5; *«may not have received training»*
is a modal the piece then spends a paragraph reading; *«aimed to»* against the headline's *«in less than
one year»* is a contrast between two clauses of one document. An Arabic paraphrase of a hedge is a hedge
**we** chose. So each load-bearing quotation is carried in the register's own English inside the
quotation marks and immediately glossed in Arabic.

**And it is applied to EVERY quotation, including the teacher's — which is the part worth arguing.**
The first composition considered rendering Pelagie Abayikunda's two testimonial quotes in Arabic alone,
since neither carries a hedge or a scope, and keeping the bilingual treatment for the ministry's words
where the wording is the evidence. That was **rejected**, and not on grounds of consistency. The piece's
own §*ما لا يمكن قراءته* observes that every voice in the record reaches the reader through the funder's
channel or the ministry's — the one teacher in the corpus speaks inside her funder's feature. A policy
that carried the ministry's words in their own language and translated the teacher's would have printed
that hierarchy into the Arabic typography, in the section that exists to name it. **A translation policy
applied unevenly across a piece's voices stops being a policy and becomes an editorial claim about
whose words count.** Filed as today's ruling (#72).

The cost is real and accepted: the Arabic body carries more Latin than any pair this publication has
shipped, and it is heavier reading than Egypt's. The alternative was lighter prose that quietly
re-authored five hedges.
