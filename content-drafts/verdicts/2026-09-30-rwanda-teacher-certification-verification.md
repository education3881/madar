## ITEM 1 — the ministry's *"declined only marginally"* is the register's sentence about **primary**. The piece attaches it to the combined count that **rose**. CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.

**What the piece says.** ¶81 closes: *"Combined, across the two levels, 27,409 in 2024/25 against
27,322 in 2023/24. **The stock went up.**"* ¶83 opens: *"The ministry's own words **for this**, in the
register's own prose: the number of untrained teachers **'declined only marginally'**; and 'the
persistence of a relatively high number of untrained teachers highlights the urgent need to scale up
targeted training and induction programs, particularly for newly recruited primary school
teachers.'"*

**What the register says**, read by this desk out of the 2024/25 yearbook's own content stream and
located by its neighbours rather than by a page number:

> `[…Table 3.6.2, Trained teaching staff in Primary education…]`
> **"However, the number of untrained teachers declined only marginally, from 22,160 in** *[2023/24
> to 21,717 in 2024/25 … Despite this]* **overall progress, the persistence of a relatively high
> number of untrained teachers highlights the urgent need to scale up targeted training and induction
> programs, particularly for newly recruited primary school teachers, to ensure quality teaching and
> learning outcomes."**
> `[…Table 3.6.3, Trained teaching staff in Gen. and prof. Secondary education…]`

The sentence sits **inside the primary-education commentary** — after the primary table, before the
secondary table — and it proves its own scope twice over: it names **22,160**, which is the primary
column, and the second half says *particularly for newly recruited **primary** school teachers*. The
quantities it is about are the two the piece itself gives in ¶81 as *a fall of 443*.

**Why this is a failure and not a quibble.** The register is describing the one level where the stock
**fell**. The piece places those words immediately after the sentence in which the **combined** stock
**rose**, and introduces them with *for this*. A reader who is paying attention — which is the reader
this piece is written for — meets an apparent self-contradiction (*went up* / *declined only
marginally*) whose resolution is a scope the piece removed. The register's own bounding clause,
*from 22,160 in 2023/24 to 21,717*, is exactly the clause that was cut.

**The class, named precisely, because it is one the operation has been hunting for ten days.** The
09-20 ledger named the week's forward surface as ***does a hedge survive its own paragraph?*** — and
both clock items it was written about are hedges that die in the second half of their own sentence.
This is the **mirror image**: the hedge does not die, it **travels**. It survives its paragraph
perfectly, in quotation marks, verbatim, and lands attached to a quantity it was never about. #49's
binding already says a superlative is quoted *with its bounding clause or paraphrased with the bound
restored*; the same is true of a hedge, and this is the first time we have caught the omission of a
bound rather than the omission of a hedge. **A hedge with its bound removed is not a weaker claim
than the register's — it is a wider one.**

**Closed as follows, both languages, +2 words.**

- EN: *"The ministry's own words for **the primary column**, in the register's own prose: …"*
- AR: «وكلماتُ الوزارة نفسِها **لعمود الابتدائي**، في نثر السجلِّ نفسِه: …»

The two quoted fragments are untouched and remain verbatim. No figure moved. The reader already has
22,160 → 21,717 one paragraph up, so the bound is restored by attribution rather than by repeating
the numbers — which is the cheaper of the two honest fixes and the only one the cap allows.

**Editor's note recorded for the record:** the second quotation could have stayed where it was, since
it names primary teachers in its own text. It is kept in the same sentence as the first because
splitting them would imply the register said them in two places. It said them in one.

## ITEM 3 — no MINEDUC news-detail page dates its own article. Source 5 says one does, and that claim is the control source 4's date rests on. CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.

**What source 5 claims.** *"MINEDUC — 'The Ministry of education introduces new reforms…', **dated on
its own page Monday, 10 August, 2026**, read in served text 2026-09-24…"*

**What source 4 claims, one annotation earlier, and correctly.** *"the article page itself carries no
publication date, byline date or timestamp of any kind — confirmed again on 2026-09-27, when every
date string on the page belonged to the sidebar's latest-news rail and none to the article."* Source
4 then rests its date on the index and **proves the index's pairing on a control**: *"the index dates
the 10 August 2026 reforms release to Monday, 10 August, 2026, **which is the date that release
prints on its own page.**"*

**Measured, three pages, same host, today.** Each MINEDUC `news-detail` page serves **exactly nine**
`<time itemprop="datePublished">` elements, and they are **the same nine on every page** — the
latest-news rail, in identical order, all after the article region's `Topics` block:

| page | `<time>` elements | earliest offset | the nine dates |
|---|---|---|---|
| QBE progress note (source 4) | 9 | 22,852 | 10 Aug 2026 · 14 Jul 2026 · 16 Jun 2026 · 4 May 2026 · 17 Apr 2026 · 6 Mar 2026 · 26 Feb 2026 · 25 Feb 2026 · 21 Feb 2026 |
| reforms release (source 5) | 9 | 23,651 | *identical* |
| salary communiqué (source 6) | 9 | 19,473 | *identical* |

The reforms release's "own page" date is **its own entry in that rail** — the rail's newest item is
the reforms release, so on that one page the rail's first row links to the page it is printed on.
That is why it reads as a page date and is not one. **The string was found for the right reason and
in the wrong place**, and the page it was found on is the single page in the corpus where the rail
and the article coincide.

**So the control does not exist.** What actually agrees is the index's rendering of a date and the
rail's rendering of the same CMS field — two templates over one record, which is a consistency check
and not an independent confirmation. **The body is unaffected and was right all along:** ¶51 says the
QBE note's date *"this piece takes from the ministry's news index rather than from the page."* The
*proof* behind that sentence was the thing that was wrong.

**The class, and it is one this operation wrote down sixteen days ago.** The 09-14 trap: *"the field
the assertion exists to supplement was masking the assertion"* — an instrument scoped wider than the
thing it checks finds the right string for the wrong reason. Here the instrument is a citation and
the wider scope is *the page* instead of *the article*. And the sharp part is that the same drafter
asked the correct narrower question one annotation earlier and answered it correctly. **A finding
about a template is a finding about every page that template serves, including the page you then use
as your control.**

**Closed as follows, both languages, no body change and no cap cost.**

- **Source 5's** location claim is replaced by what is true and by where the date does live: MINEDUC's
  news-detail template serves no date for its own article; the **10 August 2026** date is the
  ministry's own, carried as `datetime="2026-08-10"` in the latest-news rail's entry for this release
  (present on every news page on the host) and as prose on the news index. Re-verified 2026-09-30.
- **Source 4's** control clause is replaced by the control that **does** exist, and by a positive
  enumeration that makes *the date is the index's* a complete statement rather than a hedge. Measured
  today: the index is strictly reverse-chronological and this note sits on **page 3 of 35 between a
  17 November 2025 item and a 30 October 2025 item**, dated **Wednesday, 05 November, 2025**; and the
  date is served **nowhere else on the host** — no `article:published_time`, no date in the page's
  JSON-LD (breadcrumbs only), `sitemap.xml` **404**, and both TYPO3 news-feed routes **500**.

**Method note, because it matters to whether the confirmation is independent.** Page 3 was reached by
parsing the index's own pagination hrefs — the `cHash` is required and a guessed `currentPage`
parameter silently returns page 1. A verification that had guessed the URL would have re-read page 1
and reported agreement with itself.

## ITEM 4 — the ~25,000 is the register's count **in** 2021, not a flow **from** 2021. CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.

**The piece.** ¶53: *"Hiring criteria were relaxed, and **from 2021** roughly 25,000
secondary-school graduates … entered classrooms."* Annotation 3: *"roughly 25,000 graduates entering
teaching under relaxed criteria **from 2021**."* The hand-off's seam table types it as a **flow**,
*from 2021, reported Mar 2026.*

**The register, re-read today.** *"**In 2021**, a secondary school graduate named Pelagie Abayikunda
began teaching primary school French in Kigali… To ensure all classrooms had teachers, the hiring
criteria were temporarily relaxed… **At that time, approximately 25,000 graduates** like Pelagie, who
had subject specialization but had not attended TTCs, **became teachers**."*

*At that time* is anchored to 2021 and to nothing later. *From 2021* is an open interval, and the
piece's own argument makes the difference live: §*The second count* turns on arrivals **continuing**
after 2024, and a reader who takes the 25,000 as accumulating from 2021 onward will read it as the
same population. It is not: the 25,000 is a 2021 cohort, and the continuing arrivals are the
ministry's *"newly recruited primary school teachers"*, counted by a different body on a different
instrument. **Keeping the two apart is the whole of the five-definition seam**, and the preposition
was quietly joining them.

**Closed, both languages, word-neutral.** EN *"in 2021 roughly 25,000…"*; AR «وفي 2021 دخل نحو
25,000…». Annotation 3 in both languages likewise. The figure's **kind** is unchanged — it remains a
flow, the seam table's definition 1 — and the flow is now dated where the register dates it.
