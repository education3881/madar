## Summary

**Three confirmed defects, and they are the same defect three times.** In each case the piece has
taken a **date that belongs to a document** and attached it to **the event the document reports**.
In each case the `sources[]` annotation was *correct* — it dated the document — and in each case
the body printed **the same digits** while meaning something else. That is why neither the numeral
multiset, nor the eight figure axes, nor the `annotation == sentence` hand-off line could see
them: **the strings matched and the referents did not.**

This is the edition's **seventh consecutive verification verdict in which not one finding is a
wrong number.** Every digit in all three defective sentences is correct about something.

Filed as **ruling #62 — a date that matches is not a date that agrees.**

**The piece makes this move six times, and it gets it right three.** Set out once, because the
pattern is the finding and the controls are what make it one:

| # | the piece's date | the register | licensed? |

| 1 | *"On 25 July 2025 … jointly published"* | Ain Shams mirror, dated 2025-07-25, says the ministries announced **"today"** | **YES** — the document dates itself to the act |

## ITEM 1 — the EIPR statement is dated **7 July 2025**. The piece dated it **21 July 2025**, which is the Wayback capture timestamp. **CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.**

The piece read, in both languages:

> *"On **21 July 2025** — while the bill was in parliament, weeks before it became law — the
> Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights published its objection to the amendments as
> submitted."*

The archive capture was re-opened today and read as served. The statement's own dateline,
sitting directly under the headline and the section labels, reads:

> Economic and Social Justice · Press Release · متوفر بالعربية · **7 July 2025**

**21 July 2025 is the capture, not the publication.** It is the timestamp in the Wayback URL —
`/web/20250721184835/` — the moment a crawler saw the page. The two are **fourteen days
apart**, and the piece printed the crawler's day as the publisher's.

**Where the defect came from is the whole point.** The annotation has always been right: it says
*"read in served text 2026-09-19 from the Internet Archive capture of 21 July 2025"* — which is
an exact and careful statement about a **capture**. The body then took the only date in the
annotation and made it the date of the **act**. An `annotation == sentence` comparison passes
here: both say *21 July 2025*. The annotation means the crawl. The sentence means the
publishing.

**What survives the correction, checked rather than assumed.** EIPR's own text says the
amendments *"were referred to parliament just days before the end of the current parliamentary
term"* — so *"while the bill was in parliament"* is the register's own claim and stands. The law
was issued **13 August 2025**; from 7 July that is five weeks, so *"weeks before it became law"*
stands. **Only the date changed.** No clause was added and none was removed.

**Deliberately NOT written:** the piece does not say the statement was published *the day
parliament approved the bill*, even though source 6 is headlined *'Parliament approves new
education bill'* and published Monday 7 July 2025 — because that would be **this very defect
committed while fixing it.** Source 6's 7 July is *its* publication date, and its body attributes
the committee's approval to *"the preceding Saturday"*. Two documents dated the same day is what
is established; nothing about the order of the two events is.

**Annotations, both languages, now carry the distinction** rather than leaving the next reader to
rediscover it: the statement's dateline, the capture timestamp, the fourteen days between them,
which one is a publication date, and the fact that this piece's first draft used the wrong one.

## ITEM 2 — the Education World Forum speech is dated **18 May 2026** in the body. The register gives **no date for the speech**; 18 May 2026 is the article's own publication date. **CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.**

The piece read:

> *"**Speaking in London on 18 May 2026**, in a system of 25 million pre-university students,
> Minister Mohamed Abdellatif described the certificate as …"*

The register (source 11, Daily News Egypt) was re-opened today and asked one question: does it
say when he spoke? It does not. Its own lead-in is:

> *"Speaking at the second plenary session of the Education World Forum in London, titled
> 'Education for Future Readiness in a Rapidly Changing World,' Abdellatif stated that …"*

The forum is named. The session is named. The session's title is given. **No date is given for
either**, and the Education World Forum runs over several days. The only date on the page is the
paper's own, 18 May 2026 — and the piece converted it into the day the Minister stood up.

**Same shape as item 1, one surface further out.** The annotation says *"'Egypt overhauls
education system…', 18 May 2026, read in served text 2026-09-19"* — the date of a newspaper
article, stated as the date of a newspaper article. Correct. The body borrowed the digits and
changed what they were about.

**Fixed in both languages by dating the account and never the speech**, which adds no claim and
removes one:

> EN: *"In an account published on 18 May 2026, Minister Mohamed Abdellatif — speaking at the
> Education World Forum in London, in a system of 25 million pre-university students — described
> the certificate as …"*
> AR: «في روايةٍ منشورةٍ في 18 مايو 2026، وصف وزيرُ التربية والتعليم والتعليم الفني محمد عبد
> اللطيف — متحدثًا في منتدى التعليم العالمي بلندن، في نظامٍ فيه 25 مليون طالبٍ قبل جامعي —
> الشهادةَ بأنها …»

**A second finding inside the same paragraph, corrected in the annotation.** The served sentence
carrying the 25 million is the newspaper's framing — *"Egypt is undertaking a comprehensive
transformation of its education system for 25 million pre-university students"* — **not a figure
the Minister is quoted as giving.** The body never attributed it to him and does not now; the
annotation previously left that open and now says so.

## ITEM 3 — the admissions arithmetic is dated **3 August 2026**; that is the portal's publication date and the page **gives no date for the announcing**. **CONFIRMED. Closed in-run, both languages.**

Found by sweeping the held set for the class items 1 and 2 belong to, rather than by reading this
paragraph again — which is the only reason it was found at all, since it had already survived a
five-test pair verdict, an Arabic gate and the first two thirds of this verification pass.

The piece read:

> *"Two weeks earlier, **on 3 August 2026**, the admissions arithmetic was set out: admission for
> baccalaureate graduates runs through the same coordination system applied to Thanaweya Amma
> students …"*

2026** as its own article date, and its opening sentence is *"The Ministry of Education and
Technical Education **has announced** the university admission system for holders of the Egyptian
Baccalaureate certificate."* Perfect tense, no date. The page's last line reads *"Source:
Al-Ahram Gate."*

**So the piece dated a ministry's announcement to a governorate portal's repost of a newspaper's
report of it** — two removes and a borrowed day. The piece already says the two removes out loud,
two sentences later: *"This reaches us through a government portal that names its own source as a
newspaper, so it is reported here as that report and not as the ministry speaking."* **The prose
knew. The date did not.**

**Fixed in both languages by making the date agree with the framing the paragraph already
carries**, one preposition's worth of change and no claim added:

> EN: *"Two weeks earlier, **in an account published on 3 August 2026**, the admissions arithmetic
> was set out …"*
> AR: «وقبل ذلك بأسبوعين، **في روايةٍ منشورةٍ في 3 أغسطس 2026**، عُرِض حسابُ التنسيق…»

*"Two weeks earlier"* still holds: 3 August to 17 August is fourteen days, and both are now
account-dates rather than one account-date and one act-date, so the interval is between two
comparable things. Both annotations now say which of the two the date is.
