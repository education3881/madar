## 2. What the composition found in the English — the attribution verb

The 2026-09-13 weekly review named **the attribution verb** as its forward surface: *said · announced · confirmed · found · estimates each promise a different register, and nothing checks them.* It was tested here for the first time, because Arabic forced the question that English let pass.

The English body read: *"The Principal Investigator **said** it at the launch in as many words."* Arabic cannot render that sentence without choosing a gender for الباحث/الباحثة, so the register had to be re-opened rather than remembered. Re-read as served today, the HSRC release says: **"Reddy cautions that while we applaud the improvement in educational achievement, 'the rate of achievement improvement is decreasing.'"**

Three things were wrong or thin, and all three are fixed in both languages this run:

1. **The verb.** The release's verb is *cautions*, not *said*. A caution is a speech act with a direction; *said* is the flattest verb available and it deletes that direction. #43's register hierarchy applied to our own prose.
2. **The setting of the caution.** The release places it *inside applause* — she applauds the improvement in the same breath. A caution quoted without the applause it is embedded in is a different sentence, and it is the mirror image of the Sudan *"at the ceremony"* item (#47): our own framing, not the register's.
3. **The person.** The Principal Investigator is named and titled on the served page — Dr Vijay Reddy, Principal Investigator of TIMSS 2019 and Distinguished Research Specialist at the HSRC — and the page's pronoun is *she*. The name now rides in the `sources[]` annotation on both sides, which is what anchors the Arabic's الباحثة الرئيسة in a register instead of in an assumption.

The English body now reads that she *put it as a caution rather than a report*, with the applause carried; the Arabic composes the same thing at register (`تنبيهًا لا تقريرًا`, with `تُشيد بالتحسُّن` beside it). **This is the fourth consecutive non-numeric finding in this edition, and the first found by a language crossing rather than by a verification pass.**
