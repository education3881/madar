## Item 1 — FAIL (English only) · an unmarked emendation inside quotation marks

**Where.** EN §"What the ruler found", final sentence of the paragraph beginning *"Those
gains landed against a tide going out."* Also the EOF `sources[]` title in **both** files
(unquoted there, so lower stakes, same wording).

**The piece says (EN):**
> …and the caution that the programme **"does not definitively prove"** outcomes-based
> financing is a better way to fund education.

**The source says, re-fetched as served text 2026-09-09** (EOF, *Challenge Accepted*,
8 December 2025, final third of the body):
> SLEIC does not **definitely** prove that outcomes-based financing is a better way to
> fund education.

**The defect.** *Definitely* → *definitively*, inside quotation marks, unmarked. It is
almost certainly a correction of the register's own slip — the same page also serves
"what is takes to help children learn," so it is a typo-prone register and the intended
word is obvious. **That is exactly why it is an item.** Quotation marks are a promise
about the *register*, not about the meaning: they say *these are the words on the page*.
An emendation that improves the source is still an emendation, and an unmarked one is
indistinguishable, after the fact, from the source having changed underneath us.

**And it is indistinguishable here.** The compose-time read (08-28) recorded the phrase
only in its already-emended form, in the `sources[]` title. No verbatim capture of the
clause exists from that day. So the operation cannot now tell whether the drafter
normalised the word or EOF has since edited the page — which is a second, quieter defect
in the method, and the one that becomes the ruling.

**Not an Arabic item.** The AR renders the caution «لا يُثبِت إثباتًا قاطعًا» — *does not
prove conclusively*. Both English readings collapse to the same Arabic adverb, so the
Arabic is a correct translation of the source under either spelling and needs no change.
**First item in the edition that is asymmetric by the nature of the languages** rather
than by an error on one side: an orthographic emendation has no Arabic twin. Numeral
multiset untouched (#42 satisfied by construction).

**Required fix (EN only):** print the served word. Quotation is verbatim or it is
paraphrase; there is no third thing.
**Status: fix applied in-run 2026-09-09** — EN body and the EOF `sources[]` title in both
files now carry *definitely*, with the read-date stated.

## Item 2 — FAIL (both languages) · a January plan carried as a live future in an August piece

**Where.** EN §closing, penultimate paragraph: *"In March, the same partnership takes the
architecture earlier in the system…"* AR line 30, in parallel: «وفي مارس تأخذ الشراكةُ
نفسُها العمارةَ…».

**The source says** (EOF, *Learning What Works: Closing SLEIC*, 16 January 2026,
re-fetched as served 2026-09-09):
> …the **planned launch** of the outcomes-partnership for early childhood care and
> education **in March**…

**The defect, in two layers.**

1. **The word *planned* was dropped — from the prose only.** #43: *a schedule is a plan.*
   The recon had it right: the 08-28 blocker-clearance memo records the successor signal as
   the partnership's *"planned launch … in March"*, quotation marks and all. And so does
   the piece's own bibliography — the EN `sources[]` says *"the planned March launch"* and
   the AR says «الإطلاقُ المقرَّرُ في مارس». **The hedge was carried correctly in both
   languages' source titles and dropped in both languages' bodies.** The piece cites itself
   more carefully than it writes itself, which is the sharpest possible form of this defect:
   no research failed, no register drifted, and the fence still did not reach the reader.
2. **The plan was five months old at compose.** The piece is dated **28 August 2026**.
   March 2026 was in the past when the sentence was written, and the sentence is in a bare
   present-future — it reads as a thing about to happen. A plan whose date has passed is
   not a plan any more; it is either an event or a slippage, and which one is a question
   with a served answer.

**The served answer, read 2026-09-09** (EOF's own site, first party):

| Register | What it serves |
|---|---|
| `/programme/sierra-leone-early-childhood` | its own programme page — status **Running**, term **2026–2029** |
| the same page's news rail | **"Play & Learn, Measure & Scale."**, News, **24 April 2026**, lead image `alt="Sierra Leone launch"` |
| EOF main navigation (both pages fetched today) | Sierra Leone Early Childhood listed as a standing programme |

So the successor exists and is running — and the owner's own launch register is dated
**April**, not March. Under **#41** (*supersede, don't resurrect* — a dead register's
figure is replaced by the owner's living figure) the January plan is superseded by the
programme page, and under **#43** the podium date was never the document's date anyway.

**Required fix (both languages):** drop the plan-as-future and state the living register.
Deliberately **without adding a figure** — "April 2026" and "2026–2029" would be two new
numerals to carry across the pair for a sentence that is not load-bearing, and the claim
the piece needs is a *status*, not a date.
**Status: fix applied in-run 2026-09-09** in EN and AR, with the programme page added as
`sources[]` entry 8 in both files, read-dated — because a new claim arrives with its
register, never on the strength of an old one.

## Carried note — a hedge dropped from the Chief Education Officer's fact

**The source says (served, 09-09):** frequent breaks and extracurricular time *"**often**

*Often* is a frequency qualifier and it was dropped; the piece states as the general case
what the register states as the common case (#22 — a qualifier keeps its scope). **Not
escalated to an item:** the sentence is not payment-bearing, it is attributed in-line to
the named official who said it, and "most subjects" already carries a distributional
hedge of its own, so the loss is real but small. **Recorded for the wave-gate read**; the
one-word repair (*often get*) costs nothing if the Editor wants it.

## What would have had to happen for an error to survive

Per #27, a clean figure needs two independent routes; per #45, a clean figure needs its
noun read in the source's own sentence. **This piece's two failures needed neither of
those to be broken.** One is a quotation, where the number of routes is irrelevant because
the object being checked is an *orthography*. One is a tense, where both routes agreed —
the January register said "planned … in March" on 08-28 and says it still — and the error
was in reading a five-month-old plan without asking whether its date had passed.

So the axis this piece adds is not about sources at all. It is about **the distance
between the register's clock and the piece's clock**: a plan is only a plan until its date
arrives, and after that the same sentence, unchanged, has become a different claim.
Nothing in the operation's instruments watches that clock.

**Method increments for the persona, filed to the guidebook this run:** a quotation is
verbatim or it is paraphrase (#46); and every dated forward-looking claim is re-read
against the calendar at compose, not only against its source (#46's second corollary).
