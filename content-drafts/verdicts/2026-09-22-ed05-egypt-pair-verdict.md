## Note 1 — the fee clause lost its escalation condition. **Test 1. Closed.**

The piece read:

> *"first sitting free, and a retake bounded at two hundred Egyptian pounds per attempt
> and not exceeding four hundred per subject."*

The statute, Article 37 *mukarrar* 2, read in full on source 1 today:

> «ويكون التقدم للامتحان المرة الأولى مجانًا، ويحدد بقرار من وزير التربية والتعليم
> والتعليم الفنى فئات رسوم التقدم للامتحان للمرات التالية بما لا يجاوز **مائتي جنيه
> للمادة الواحدة فى المرة الواحدة**، **ولوزير التربية والتعليم والتعليم الفنى بعد موافقة
> مجلس الوزراء أن يُصدر قرارًا بزيادة هذا الحد تدريجيًا**، على ألا يتجاوز مجموع الرسوم
> أربعمائة جنيه للمادة الواحدة.»

Two errors in one sentence, and they run in opposite directions:

1. **The unit moved.** The statute's two hundred is *per subject per attempt*. The piece
   printed *per attempt*, and attached *per subject* to the four hundred instead.
2. **A permission became a price.** The four hundred is not a standing bound. It is the
   ceiling on a discretionary escalation the Minister *may* enact, by decree, with
   Cabinet approval. The piece reported it as what a retake costs.

**Where each half came from is the finding.** Three reproductions were read; they
compress the clause differently:

| | the 200 | the 400 | the escalation clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| source 1, legal encyclopaedia | per subject **per attempt** | present | **present, in full** |
| source 3, Youm7 | per attempt only | present | **present, in full** |
| source 2, law practice | quotation **truncated at 200 with an ellipsis** | only in its own plain-language summary and reader FAQ | **absent** |

The unit came from source 3. The ceiling came from source 2's *summary* — a numeral pair
the practice printed **beside** the statutory text it was summarising, never inside its
quotation of it. Source 1, which carries the whole clause, was open on the same screen on
the same day and supplied neither half.

The piece already carried a guardrail about these three reproductions: *they disagree
about which numbered bis-article carries the fee provision, so cite by content and never
by number.* That guardrail is real and it is about the **numbering**. They also disagree
about the **text**, and the existence of the first guardrail is exactly what made the
second disagreement invisible. **Filed as ruling #61 — three reproductions are three
compressions.**

**Fixed in both languages.** The clause is now reported in two halves, from source 1,
with the permission named as one: *"Four hundred is what the law allows a later decree to
reach. It is not what a retake costs."* / «فالأربعمائةُ هي ما يسمح القانونُ لقرارٍ لاحقٍ
أن يبلغه. وليست ما تكلّفه إعادةُ امتحان.» Source 1's annotation now carries the clause
verbatim and says it is the one the provision is taken from; source 2's annotation states
that its quotation truncates and that its 200–400 pair is a summary, **and that this
piece's first draft took the pair from it**; source 3's annotation gains the escalation
clause it serves and a note that its per-attempt unit is short of the statute's.

**Both annotations were wrong in the same way as both bodies**, which is the second
instance in this edition of the shape the `annotation == sentence` hand-off line cannot
structurally catch (slot 3, item 2, 09-17). Only re-opening the register catches it.

## Note 2 — an unmarked emendation inside quotation marks. **Test 1. Closed.**

The piece quoted EIPR's account of the bill's general retake amendment as fees
*"not less than two hundred pounds and not more than two thousand pounds"*. The register,
re-read from the archive capture today, serves *"which **shall not be** less than two
hundred pounds and not more than two thousand pounds"*. One word dropped, inside quotation
marks, almost certainly for grammatical fit with our own lead-in.

**This is ruling #46's origin case repeated** — Sierra Leone, 09-09, *"does not
definitively prove"* for *"does not definitely prove"* — and it is the same reason it
matters: a silent emendation cannot later be told apart from the source having changed.
**Fixed in the body and in the annotation.** The Arabic renders the register in Arabic and
carries no equivalent defect, so this note is EN-only, the edition's second asymmetry that
is a property of the languages rather than an error on one side.

## Note 3 — a `sources[]` entry named a headline the document does not carry. **Test 1. Closed.**

Source 6 was cited as *Al Manassa — 'Egypt's parliament approves optional baccalaureate
system'*. The served page's headline is **'Parliament approves new education bill amid
inequality fears'**, bylined **News Desk**, published Monday 7 July 2025. The date, the
publication and every quoted string in the annotation are correct; the title is not the
document's. **A citation's title is part of the citation** — a reader uses it to find the
thing, and a title we composed reads exactly like a title we read.

**Fixed in both languages.** The Arabic annotation now carries the served English headline
in its own script with a note that no translation stands in for it — the #28a discipline
applied to a headline rather than a publication name.

## Note 4 — the "why now" arrives in section six of eight. **Test 3. Closed.**

Test 3 asks whether the reader knows by paragraph two why they are being shown this now.
They did not. The lede and the frame both open on **25 July 2025**, and the piece's actual
recency — the admissions arithmetic of 3 August 2026, two further ministerial decrees, the
international cooperation agreement of mid-August 2026 — sits in *"The rules kept
arriving"*, six sections in. A piece filed on 19 September 2026 whose newest fact is six
weeks old should not read for six sections like a piece about last year.

**Fixed in both languages with no new fact**, one paragraph added to the frame: *"One more
thing belongs in the frame before the evidence: the publishing has not stopped. The
admissions arithmetic, two further ministerial decrees and an international cooperation
agreement are all dated August 2026, weeks before this piece was written."* / «وأمرٌ أخيرٌ
ينتمي إلى الإطار قبل الأدلة: النشرُ لم يتوقّف…» No figure added; *weeks* is deliberately
unquantified rather than arithmetic the register does not carry.

## Note 5 — the enacted clause was available and the paragraph was poorer without it. **Test 1 / editorial. Closed.**

The EIPR paragraph exists to say *this is an objection to a draft, not to the enacted
chapter*. It made that claim and then left the enacted text unread on the point at issue.
Source 1 carries it: Article 24, as issued, sets the general retake fee at
«ورسوم التقدم لها والتي لا تزيد على ألف جنيه» — **not exceeding one thousand pounds, with
no floor at all**, where the bill EIPR objected to set 200–2,000.

This is the rare note that *adds* to a piece at the verdict stage, and it is admitted for
one reason: it is on the register the piece already cites, it strengthens the piece's own
stated frame rather than softening it, and leaving it out let a reader assume the objected
figure was the law. **Added in both languages, one sentence, from source 1.** The
annotation for source 1 carries the clause.
