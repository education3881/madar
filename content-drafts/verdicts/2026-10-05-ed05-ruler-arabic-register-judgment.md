## Question 1 — ¶48, the indefinite dual under negation. **CONFIRMED DEFECT.**

**EN (unchanged, and correct):**
> …there are four defensible ways to measure it, that they crown four different countries,
> and that **no two of them can be added together**.

**AR as composed:**
> …وأنها تتوّج أربعةَ بلدانٍ مختلفة، **وأنَّ اثنتين منها لا تُجمَعان**.

**The question asked:** does the indefinite dual under negation carry the universal reading,
or does it read as *two particular ones*?

**It reads as two particular ones.** The principle the composition was leaning on is real —
**النكرة في سياق النفي تفيد العموم**, an indefinite in the context of negation yields
generality — but it carries a condition the composed sentence does not meet. The principle
applies when the indefinite falls **inside the scope of the negator**, which in practice
means *after* it: `ما في الدار أحدٌ`, `لا رجلَ في الدار`, `ما جاءني رجلٌ`.

Here the structure is `أنَّ` + **اثنتين** (ism anna, accusative, indefinite dual) + `منها` +
`لا تُجمَعان` (negated passive dual as khabar). The indefinite is the **topic**; the negation
sits in the **predicate** and is asserted *of* it. The indefinite therefore never enters the
negator's scope, and generality is never triggered. Two further facts push the same way:

- **An indefinite ism of `أنَّ` is the unmarked case**, so there is nothing about the form
  that could force a universal reading against the structure.
- **`منها` is partitive (التبعيض)**, which actively recruits the specific reading: *two of
  them* — i.e. *there exist two among them* that cannot be combined.

**Why that is not a small difference.** The partitive reading says some pair is
non-combinable, which **implies the others may combine**. The piece's entire argument is the
opposite, and says so again in its last paragraph — ¶92, `ولا تُتوسَّط` / *"They cannot be
averaged."* The Arabic edition was arguing, at its strongest sentence, for a weaker claim
than the Arabic edition's own conclusion.

**Ruled fix — put the indefinite inside the scope of the negation, and change nothing else:**

> …وأنها تتوّج أربعةَ بلدانٍ مختلفة، **وأنَّه لا تُجمَعُ اثنتان منها**.

`أنَّ` + **ضمير الشأن** (`ـه`) + `لا` + `تُجمَعُ` + **اثنتان** (nominative, post-verbal nā'ib
fā'il, indefinite, **now inside the negation**) + `منها`. The verb is singular because it
precedes its subject. The three-fold `أنَّ` parallelism of the sentence survives intact;
ضمير الشأن is the standard device for carrying a verbal sentence as the khabar of `أنَّ`.
The passive, the partitive and the word order of the surrounding clause are all untouched.

**Two alternatives considered and declined, recorded because the next composer will reach for
them:** `وأنَّ أيَّ اثنتين منها لا تُجمَعان` — `أيّ` does give generality under negation, but
`أيّ` governing a dual is awkward and reads as a calque of the English *any*; and
`وأنَّه ما من اثنتين منها تُجمَعان` — `ما مِن` + indefinite is the most unambiguous universal
negative Arabic owns, and it was declined only because it lifts the register above house
voice (plain, unhurried, no rhetorical heightening) on a sentence that does not need volume
to be clear. **If a later reading finds the ruled fix still ambiguous, `ما مِن` is the
fallback and it is not a close call.**

## Question 2 — ¶60, `دعوتان`. **CONFIRMED DEFECT, and it is lexical rather than syntactic.**

**EN:** *"Those are two different claims, and only the first crowns anybody."*
**AR as composed:** «**وهما دعوتان مختلفتان**، والأولى وحدها تتوّج أحدًا.»

**`دعوتان` is not the dual of `دعوى`. It is the dual of `دعوة`** — an invitation, a call, a
summons. The composed Arabic says *"and they are two different **invitations**."*

The dual of a **مقصور** noun is formed by the position of the alif maqṣūra: if it is the
**third** letter it reverts to its origin (`عصا → عصوان`, `فتى → فتيان`); if it is the
**fourth or later** it is converted to **yāʾ** (`ذكرى → ذكريان`, `كبرى → كبريان`,
`مستشفى → مستشفيان`). In `دعوى` — د · ع · و · **ى** — the alif maqṣūra is the **fourth**
letter, so:

> **دعوى → دعويان**

**Ruled fix:** «وهما **دعويان** مختلفتان، والأولى وحدها تتوّج أحدًا.»

**The noun itself is right and stays.** `دعوى` is the correct and precise word for a claim in
the argumentative sense (`الدعوى والدليل`), and it is the register this paragraph — which
separates PASEC's own income analysis from Le Nestour's plot — actually needs. Only its dual
was wrong. Replacing the noun (`قولان`, `أطروحتان`) would be a larger edit than the defect.
