## Decision 1 — slot 3, verification ITEM 3: **crown the register's own set of four.** Option 1.

**The call.** Ruler two crowns **Gabon, Senegal, Benin and Burkina Faso** — every country
the analyst's own register places above 410 — and the piece states in its own voice that
naming fewer would be our narrowing and not his. Executed today in both languages.

**Why not option 2 (state the basis for the narrowing).** Closed, and not by preference.
No register read for this piece ranks those four by income-adjusted performance.
Composing such a ranking from GDP per capita and the HLO values would be a figure
composed from a pattern — which is the thing #41 forbids and, more to the point, the
thing *this piece's entire argument* forbids. A piece whose thesis is that a rank without
its ruler is an opinion with decimal places cannot invent a rank to defend its own crown.

**Why not option 3 (retire the crown).** Two reasons, one structural and one editorial.
The piece is called *Four rulers, four crowns*: retiring ruler two's crown leaves four
rulers and three crowns and makes the title false, and a title is a claim. And ruler three
already crowns **two** countries (Niger on PASEC, South Africa on TIMSS) — so a crown
awarded to a set rather than to a single country is already this piece's established
shape, not a concession invented to escape this item.

**Why option 1 is the better piece and not merely the permitted one.** The Verifier wrote
that the piece "drops the upper-middle-income country and the near-poorest with no clause
either way." Restoring the band does more than close the item: it *shows the reader the
ruler's own limit*. Four countries at very different levels of national income sit inside
one band on an axis built to adjust for national income, and the register declines to
separate them. That is the piece's subject, arriving as evidence instead of as assertion.

**What was added, and the discipline it was written under.** The new sentences make **no
new factual claim**: every element — the four names, the 410 threshold, the placement
above the global median, the group-level conclusion *not below what we would expect
considering their level of development* — was already in the piece's own paragraph
above, verified verbatim by the Verifier on 09-17. The only addition is a **negative**
about the register (that it never separates the four), and that negative is the
Verifier's own finding, stated in the verdict as *"Nothing in the register singles out
those two on this ruler."* No sentence here was composed from an annotation (2026-09-13).

**Post-edit check, per the edition's standing discipline.** Numeral multiset recomputed
after the edit in both languages: **EN 110 tokens, AR 103** (each gained exactly one
`410`), **zero Arabic-only figures**, and the seven English-only tokens are still the
grade labels Arabic renders as ordinal words. Unchanged in kind from the 09-17 state.

**ITEM 3 IS CLOSED. The wave's blocking editorial item is cleared.**

## Decision 3 — the `countries:` field stays at five. Gabon and Burkina Faso are **not** added.

Raised by decision 1: the body now crowns four countries on ruler two, two of which are
absent from `countries: [Mauritius, Senegal, Niger, Benin, South Africa]`.

**The field reports coverage, not mentions.** This piece reads evidence on five education
systems. It has no reading of Gabon's system and none of Burkina Faso's; both appear only
as members of a band in one line of one analyst's register. Adding them would tell the
reader, the marginalia, the stats panel and `qa_geo_fields` that Madār has covered Gabon,
which Madār has not. That is **#51 exactly — a claim inherits the scope of its register**
— committed by a metadata field instead of a sentence, which is the same defect in a
quieter place. (It is also why the piece names Chad, Madagascar, the DRC and Côte d'Ivoire
in the under-360 line and lists none of them either: the rule is already in force, it had
simply never been tested by a crowning.)

**The tension is real and is recorded rather than smoothed.** A reader may see ruler two
crown Gabon and find no Gabon in the marginalia. The definition the field is carrying —
*covered*, not *named*, not *crowned* — has never been written down anywhere. **Routed to
the weekly review as a definition question**, with this piece as its origin case. Not a
defect; an undefined field that has just been asked its first hard question.

## Decision 4 — Egypt (row 5), draft-blocker 1: **option 1, with the spine corrected.**

**The call: draft without the enrolment figure.** The 99% / ~832,000 does not ride, in any
fencing. It is a single business-newsletter register with an **unread body**, and this
publication's first non-negotiable is that a figure is cited as read. Option 3 (commission
with the figure fenced to its register) fails on that sentence alone: fencing an unread
figure to its register does not read it, it only makes the citation honest about a number
that should not be in the piece at all.

**Why not option 2 (hold for the ministry's count).** It carries no date, and the edition
has no slack — six pieces, the 10-11 gate, ~17 runs owed against a 4–6-run week. A
commissioned row held on an event nobody controls, with no deadline, is exactly the shape
of obligation that gets discovered at the gate (the 09-13 review said so about the
confirmation reads and was right). If the ministry publishes a count before the piece
banks, it rides; nothing waits for it.

**And the spine is not the absence — that is where I part from the Manager's view.**
The Manager's note for the record recommended option 1 on the grounds that a piece whose
spine is a number we could not read would contradict the publication. Agreed, and
insufficient: a piece whose spine is *the absence* of that number is a different piece,
and it is a **negative** one. Edition 05's frame is a **positive posture** set by the
founder, and the standing prohibition carried from the commissions is that the positive
posture never denies context — not that context becomes the subject.

So: **the spine is the doors-published-first finding**, which the 09-16 re-verification
made a year stronger. The Egyptian state published this certificate's tracks, subject
lists, exam format, faculty map, **admissions arithmetic with a worked example**, school
calendar and an international cooperation agreement — much of it before the first cohort
sat. That is a real and reportable act of a state making a reform legible in advance, and
it is the piece.

**The missing count rides as a named limit, in its own paragraph, and nowhere else.** One
paragraph, stating plainly that the enrolment figure is not published in any register this
operation could find on two language routes over two hunts, and that the piece therefore
reports what the certificate *is* and not how many are in it. A limit stated once is
honesty; a limit made into a thesis is a different edition's piece.

**Guardrails carried into the commission unchanged**, both recorded in advance on 09-16:
the *Egyptian* vs *International* Baccalaureate **naming trap** (the served verbs are
*cooperation agreement*, *framework for reviewing*, *approval of the second-year
curricula* — none is accreditation or equivalence, #43's verb hierarchy), and the **two
school-leader meetings a year apart** (~4,000 secondary directors, 31 Aug 2025, to which
the coercion allegation attaches; ~4,500 principals from 21 governorates, Aug 2026, to
which it does not) — any sentence naming such a meeting carries its date and attendee
count in the same clause. Blocker 5 (a voice that is not an official, an MP or an NGO)
remains open and is **not** draft-blocking: if the hunt fails again, the piece says the
registers are official voices and says so once.

**Egypt is unblocked. Drafting may begin on the next run that has the capacity for it.**
