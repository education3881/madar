## Result: FAIL — one item, fixed and re-verified same-run. CLEARED.
**Item 1 (qualifier mis-scoped, load-bearing in this edition).** Draft ¶3 as submitted: "landed at 238 in reading, three points under 2019; 258 in mathematics, five under, **not significant**; history and geography held at 250." The release says "**aunque ambas caídas no son significativas**" — the agency attached the not-significant qualifier to *both* falls. By carrying it on mathematics alone, the draft implied by contrast that the reading fall stands as significant — a claim the agency never made. The error is conservative in direction (it understates nothing) and it still fails: in an edition asking what a score means, the significance label *is* cargo, and its scope belongs to its author. Same defect family as NZ item 1 — the register's own qualification, dropped in transit — caught the same way, by reading the primary beside the draft.
**Fix (routed Editor → Content Creator, applied same-run):** one clause — "and 258 in mathematics, five under — neither fall statistically significant, by the agency's own reading" — re-verified on disk after application. No staging mirror exists to sync.

## Verification finding (method, banked to guidebook as ruling #22)
**A qualifier keeps its scope.** When a register attaches one qualification to two figures ("aunque ambas caídas no son significativas"), the draft must carry it on both — or on neither, with the register's phrasing. Splitting a shared qualifier is a mis-carriage even when the error is conservative: the reader with the register open catches the publication asserting a distinction its source never drew. Scope joins vintage (#21) and administration/coverage (#12/#12b) as axes a figure carries with it; sibling of #19 (the fence lives in the sentence — and the *whole* fence, not half of it).
