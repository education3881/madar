## 2. The three blockers, ruled on individually

### Blocker 1 — the assent date. **CLEARED: 4 June 2026.**

Yesterday this rested on a single secondary source reporting a *future* event ("tomorrow, 4 June"),
with other outlets saying 5 June, and both settling government registers serving empty.

It now rests on three things that fail independently:

1. **Open Zambia, 4 June 2026, read as served text** — reports the signing as *accomplished*, names
   all five Bills, quotes the President. This is the same outlet as yesterday's advance notice, so
   on its own it would only be that outlet's consistency; it is nonetheless the served-text
   register of the event itself rather than a plan for it.
2. **Lusaka Times, two separate items, both 4 June 2026** — "to sign five bills today" and "signs
   five Bills into law." A different outlet, and the pair brackets the event on the same day.
3. **The non-date anchor, which is the one that actually settles it.** The Zambian Observer reports
   the signing as coinciding with the President's **64th birthday**. A birthday is a fixed fact
   about a person, independent of any newsroom's publication schedule and independent of the
   reporting of this event. It is the cheapest kind of corroboration and the hardest to get wrong
   by copying, because it is not a date anyone was reporting — it is a date the reporting happens
   to be pinned to. **The 5 June readings are a day's drift in downstream copy.**

**What the draft may say:** *assented on 4 June 2026.* **What it may not say:** any **Act number**.
`sh.gov.zm` and `znbc.co.zm` still serve empty bodies and no gazette notice has been traced, so
"Act No. __ of 2026" stays out. The piece cites the instrument by **name and assent date**. If a
sub-editor's instinct is to add a number for tidiness, the answer is no.

### Blocker 2 — the committee report behind the provenance wall. **CLEARED, and it is the best source on the piece.**

The wall was never the site; it was our own fetch rule — the URL had only ever appeared inside
another page's body text, so it was out of provenance. Reaching it took one search to bring the URL
into range, which is the standing route-around (#20/#26) and worked on first attempt.

**Read in full as served text:** *Report of the Committee on Education, Science and Technology on
the Performance Audit Report on the Implementation of the Free Education Policy in Enhancing Access
and Quality of Education to Learners in Zambia, 2020–2024*, Fourth Session of the Thirteenth
National Assembly. Auditor General's audit, conducted under Article 250 of the Constitution, the
Public Audit Act and the PFM Act 2018; sixteen witness bodies including the Basic Education
Teachers Union, ZIPAR, the Teaching Council, the Teaching Service Commission, UNESCO and Zambia
Statistics Agency.

This is the independent implementation audit the recon hoped existed. It is now the piece's
verification spine, and it produced **ruling #34** on first reading (see below).

**Two conditions on its use, both binding.**

- **The sampling frame must be named wherever the audit is leaned on.** §2.6, the audit's own
  words: resource constraints meant it *"sampled schools that were near the Central Business
  District."* The mitigation was questionnaires to 61 further districts, of which **27 responded**
  — 44%. This remains the best independent instrument in existence on this policy. It is not a
  national picture and the draft may not let a reader take it for one.
- **It is a committee report *on* an audit, not the audit.** Where it says "the Auditor General
  informed the Committee" or "the Ministry submitted," those are the registers, and the sentence
  in our piece says which one.

### Blocker 3 — the `edu.gov.zm` Education Statistics Bulletin wall. **HOLDS — and I am closing it as resolved anyway.**

Every ESB URL still serves an empty body. There is still no mirror. Nothing changed at the wall.

What changed is that we no longer need what is behind it, and the audit tells us why: the Ministry
stated on the record that the **2022–2023 Bulletin was published only in November 2024** — a
roughly two-year reporting lag — and stakeholders testified that outdated enrolment figures had
limited the Ministry's own capacity to plan and monitor the policy.

That is a better asset than the series would have been. It is the *mechanism* behind the defect the
recon already caught: three government ledgers giving 2.3, 2.5 and 2.6 million children returned,
with no base year and no counting rule. **The national data system was two years behind the claims
being made from it.** We now decline that aggregate citing the government's own account of why it
cannot be relied on, rather than declining it on our own suspicion. Confirmed: the piece runs
**without any "children returned" aggregate**, and says so in the piece rather than around it.

**Standing method note, and it bit twice today.** A **search-result summary is not a served
source.** It is a provenance instrument and nothing else. The identical pair *154,304 → 158,504
teachers* arrived attached to **2022→2023** in one search summary and to **2023→2024** in another —
same two numbers, two different year-pairs, neither served. **The ESB figures remain unusable and
are not to appear in the draft in any form.**

## 3. What the audit gave the piece — and the ruling it produced

**Ruling #34 — a total is a figure, and must be added.** Full reasoning in
`agents/guidebook/2026-08-24-a-total-is-a-figure-and-must-be-added.md`.

The Ministry's teacher-recruitment table, submitted to the audit:

| Level | Planned 2024 | Actual 2024 | Planned 2023 | Actual 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECE | 2,000 | 548 | 435 | 687 |
| Primary | 3,000 | 1,057 | 2,536 | 2,522 |
| Secondary | 3,000 | 2,595 | 3,000 | 4,272 |
| **Printed total** | **8,000** | **7,841** | **5,971** | **7,481** |

Three columns add exactly. The 2024 actual column sums to **4,200** against a printed **7,841** —
short by 3,641. Printed total = 98.0% delivery; line items = 52.5%. **ECE: 548 of 2,000 planned,
27.4%,** in the same year the country legislated day care, nursery and reception into statute.

**How the draft handles it — mandatory, and this is a five-tests matter, not a style preference.**
Give the three line items, then the printed total, then say they do not reconcile and that the
other three columns do. Do not pick a side. Do not round. Do not write "official figures suggest."
The reader gets the components first, because the components are the part we can stand behind.

**Also now available to the draft, all from the same served document:**

- 115 GRZ secondary schools = 69 complete and operational + 36 with contractors re-engaged + 10
  awaiting final accounts. **Adds.**
- ZEEP: 202 secondary schools = 82 operational + 120 under construction. **Adds.**
- **69 + 82 = 151 operational** — which is the composition of the widely-repeated "151 secondary
  schools completed," traced at last, from two independently stated programme lines. **The draft
  may now use 151, with its composition shown.**
- ZEEL early years: 222 centres = 110 hub + 112 satellite, *all at different stages of
  construction* — the qualifier travels with the number or the number does not travel.
- One Education Standards Officer may inspect **up to 69 schools**.
- ZICTA: **480 computers and 24 printers to 24 schools** across ten provinces, 2021–2024.
- Learner-to-textbook ratio in most schools **worse than the recommended 1:1**.
- The 2 million claim, finally sourced — *as the Minister's attribution inside an audit document.*
  Unusable as a figure. Excellent as an example of one.
- **The Grade-7 spine, sourced at both ends:** the 2002 Free Basic Education Policy abolished
  tuition *"for grades 1 to 7"*; the 2026 Act redefines primary as grades 1–6. The policy that
  opened the series and the Act that closes it are defined on the same boundary.

## 4. The commission

**Working title:** *The law that moved the finish line*
**Angle, unchanged from recon and now fully sourced:** Zambia converted a 2022 fee-abolition policy
into an enforceable statutory right and widened it to cover boarding — and the same Act stopped
Grade 7 being the end of primary school, which is the boundary the country's headline access
statistic has been measured on for two decades. *When a country rewrites the law that defines
"primary school," what happens to the statistics that measured whether primary school was working?*

**Posture:** positive about the statute, sceptical about the aggregates, and it says which is which
inside the piece. The accomplishment is institutional and real. The counterweight — a learning
floor among the world's lowest, an audit finding the early-years workforce staffed at a quarter of
plan — is what makes it honest rather than promotional. **A verified win is worth more than an
asserted one**, and this edition's posture sharpens the bar rather than suspending it.

**Load-bearing sources, in order:** the Bill text (N.A.B. 6 of 2026, read in full); the Committee /
Auditor General performance audit (read in full); the 2022 ministerial statement on the 30,000
teacher recruitment (read in full); ZANIS on the 2025 Grade 12 results; UNESCO IICBA for the
learning-floor counterweight.

**Standing prohibitions carried into drafting:** no "children returned" aggregate; no ESB figures;
no Act number; no unadded total; no teacher totals other than the traced ministerial-statement and
audit-table figures; the 70.26% pass rate is scoped in the sentence that uses it (full certificates
among candidates who *sat*, 28.22% receiving partial statements, denominator is entrants and not
the age cohort, and the cohort has just been swollen by the intake the piece is about); the
**August 2026 general election** is named in the piece, not tiptoed around.
