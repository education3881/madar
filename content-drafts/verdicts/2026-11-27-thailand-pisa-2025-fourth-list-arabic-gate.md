## VERDICT: **APPROVED**, both editions — and with one finding the two previous gates could not have made.

## 1. The finding, because it changes what this gate is

Ruling #160 was filed yesterday on a register written **in our own language**: when the primary source is
Arabic, the Arabic edition is the one whose quoted spans can be checked against served bytes and the
English edition carries a translation nothing can match. Today's register is in **neither** language, and
that is a third case, not a repetition of either.

- The Thai notice's one load-bearing sentence appears in **both** editions as this publication's own
  rendering — English in one, Arabic in the other — and **neither** can be verified by matching a span
  against the register's bytes. What can be verified in both is the **Thai original**, whose full 182
  characters are present in the served text, and the **figure trail**, which is identical in both editions.
- The three OECD registers are in English, so this edition keeps their four spans **in English inside the
  Arabic prose**, which is the convention piece 1 of this edition established and ruling #33's corollary
  licenses. All four were re-checked here against the served snapshots and all four are verbatim.

So the asymmetry #86 and #101 describe **disappears** when the register is in a third language: both
editions are equally uncheckable by span-matching and equally checkable by figure. That is worth recording
because it is the first piece in this corpus where the Arabic edition has no evidentiary advantage and no
evidentiary deficit. It is not a new ruling; it is the boundary of #160's, and the content run does not file
rulings.

## 2. Figures, counted against the English edition rather than read

Every numeral in both bodies was extracted and compared as multisets. **No substantive divergence.** The
only differences are the ones Arabic grammar requires: the English edition's "level 2", "level 3",
"90th", "10th" and "15-year-olds" are words in the Arabic — المستوى الثاني، المستوى الثالث، المئين
التسعين، المئين العاشر، ابن خمسةَ عشر عامًا — and four year-tokens differ only by an attached comma.
The nine-row table is identical in both editions, digit for digit, in Western digits as the corpus does.

## 4. Register and composition

This is a piece about an Asian system read out of a Thai register, and the prose is plain modern fusha with
no Gulf colouring borrowed from the edition's earlier pieces and no decorative classicism. Three
constructions were changed at compose time because they read as English wearing Arabic words: a nominal
chain translating "the arithmetic is the finding", a passive rendering "a question nobody asked", and the
verb for "sat PISA". The composition's own idiom carries them: الحسابُ هو الخبر، والسؤالُ الذي لم يسأله
أحدٌ، وجلست تايلاند لبيزا.

The word «تعافٍ» appears once and in the same guarded position as the English *recovery* — inside a
sentence saying the series must be read first. That is the register fidelity this gate exists to protect:
the Arabic is no warmer than the English about a 23-point rise.

## 5. Figures travel with their axes in Arabic too

Each load-bearing number in the Arabic carries scale, both years and whose average it is, in its own
sentence: متوسّط دول المنظمة, قياسًا على بيزا 2022, معدودًا على هذه الصفوف التسعة. The OECD averages the
institute reports are marked as the institute's in Arabic exactly as in English —
«متوسّطات المنظمة التي يذكرها المعهدُ» — so an Arabic reader is not told the OECD said something the
OECD's own registers do not carry. The two equity sentences keep their two intervals: ثلاث سنوات against
عشرًا, and the paragraph that follows them says in Arabic that the registers are not in conflict.

- **The spreadsheet's internal provenance.** The Arabic annotation records the same hash, byte count and
