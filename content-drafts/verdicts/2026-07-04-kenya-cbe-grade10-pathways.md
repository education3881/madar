## What this piece is

In December 2025 Kenya released the first KJSEA — the first exit assessment of the competency-based reform — and ran the largest single placement exercise in its history: a blended score (60% KJSEA + 20% school-scored SBA + 20% KPSEA) read 1,130,459 fourteen-year-olds and assigned each to a senior school and one of three pathways. The spine is **not** "Kenya's placement was chaotic" (the press's beat) and not "Kenya built an algorithm" (a gadget story). It is the **four-STEM-numbers funnel** — target 60 / potential 59.09 / choice ~53 / placement 51 — read as a sentence, plus the thing most systems skip: **the state published its own correction ledger** (355,457 reviews ≈ 1 in 3 contested, 211,636 approved, second window run) and adapted its teacher-qualification rules when provision trailed placement. A *tracking-debate-run-as-an-operation* story.

The centralised-vs-school-led dispute is presented with the strongest honest case on each side ("which failure you fear more"). The state is credited for the ledger, not crowned for the algorithm.
4. **Figure discipline — PASS (the piece's reason to exist).** Ruling #14 applied end-to-end: four numbers kept apart, each with its owner and its question; the >100% potential figures explained; TSC's target-based planning math not conflated with placement outturn; ruling #12 named *inside* the score (the 20% SBA seam, with the explicit Brazil contrast — two published rulers there, one blended number here).
5. **Teaches something durable — PASS.** Two transferable takes: (a) *read the funnel, not any single number* — target, instrument, demand, and seats answer different questions and their gaps are the finding; (b) *the correction ledger is the exportable innovation* — a state that publishes its own error rate and reruns the window is doing accountability at the point most systems go quiet; placement is not provision, and the distance between them is the real deliverable.
