## The handed-up question, which is the only hard thing in this piece

**QUESTION 1 — the coverage restraint. UPHELD, and for a sharper reason than the drafter gave.**

The draft noticed that the chapter states a mechanism — rising PISA coverage brings **"a larger proportion
of low-performing students"** into the sample — and that Uzbekistan's low-performer share fell by 46.1
points, so the mechanism appears to point against the observed direction. It declined to write that down, on
the ground that it would be composing a finding from a pattern. **That instinct was right and the reason is
stronger than stated: the sentence would not merely have been unlicensed, it would have rested on a premise
the chapter never supplies.**

The tempting observation assumes **Uzbekistan's coverage rose**. The chapter does not say that. It names the
systems whose coverage rose most — **"Cambodia, Ecuador, El Salvador, Lebanon, Morocco and Paraguay"** — and
Uzbekistan is not among them, which is an absence from a list and not a measurement of Uzbek coverage. And
if Uzbek coverage **fell**, the same mechanism run backwards predicts a falling low-performer share, which
is exactly what Table I.2.8 reports. **So the register's own mechanism is consistent with the observed fall,
against it, or silent on it, depending on a figure that is not in this chapter.** The draft's formulation —
*this piece does not apply it either, in either direction* — is the only defensible sentence available, and
it is already in the body. **No repair.**

*A qualifier that can be run in two directions is not a qualifier until you know which way the input moved.*
