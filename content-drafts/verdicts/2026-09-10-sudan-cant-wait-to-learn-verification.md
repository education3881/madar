## Item 1 — the 2014 first phase is described with the evaluated programme's subjects (**both languages**, load-bearing, in the opening sentence)

**What the piece said.** Opening: *"…a set of educational games teaching Arabic and mathematics…"* — attached to what Sudan "built, trialled and scaled" **in 2014**. Design paragraph: *"The content taught Arabic and mathematics against the national curriculum."* The Arabic carried the identical claim (*«تُعلِّم العربيةَ والرياضيات»* / *«وعلَّم المحتوى العربيةَ والرياضيات على المنهج الوطني»*).

**What the owner's register serves** (`unicef.org/sudan/stories/sudan-cant-wait-learn`, re-fetched 2026-09-10, second channel):

> "The first phase of the 'Can't Wait to Learn' programme in Sudan started in 2014 focusing on **a numeracy trial** with a project called e-Learning Sudan (eLS), in partnership between UNICEF, War Child Holland, Ahfad University and the Ministry Of Education"

> "The eLS uses **an applied mathematics game** using a tablet, solar power and community facilitators…"

**The defect.** Arabic literacy is real and it is measured — Brown et al. report significantly greater improvement in *Arabic literacy competency* — but that is the **evaluated programme**, not the 2014 first phase. The piece read a later stage's subject list back onto the origin, in the sentence that carries the whole thesis (*Sudan built it before the emergency*). Nothing false was said about the evaluation; something untrue was said about 2014. This is the funnel-stage axis (#14) and the vintage axis (#21) applied to a **description** rather than a figure, which is why no check caught it: there is no numeral in "Arabic and mathematics."

**Fix (no figure added, both languages).** The opening now names the components without the subject list; the design paragraph now states the first phase as the owner states it and lets Arabic literacy arrive with the measurement:

> *"The first phase, in UNICEF's own account of it, was a numeracy trial — an applied mathematics game delivering certified curriculum-level content — aimed at children aged roughly seven to nine who had never been inside a classroom; Arabic literacy was in the programme by the time the evaluation measured it."*

The `sources[]` annotation for register 1 was corrected in both files to carry the owner's own words and today's re-read date.

## Item 2 — the announcement year attached to the laureate class (**both languages**)

**What the piece said.** *"In 2019 UNESCO named the programme … a laureate of its King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize."*

**What the register serves** (`unesco.org`, re-fetched 2026-09-10; article `datePublished` 2019-03-20):

> "Having been selected as one of the **2018 laureates** of the UNESCO King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize…"

**The defect.** Two dates exist — the laureate class (**2018**) and the announcement (**20 March 2019**) — and the piece printed only the second, in a sentence whose object is the first. **The `sources[]` annotation had it right** (*"as a 2018 laureate"*); both bodies dropped it. That is the **exact shape of the 09-09 Sierra Leone item 2**: the piece cites itself more carefully than it writes itself. Second consecutive instance, two different pieces, two different drafters — it is a workflow property, not a slip. The annotation is written with the register open; the body is written from memory of the annotation.

**Fix (both languages, `2018` added to both sides so the multiset stays symmetric).**

> *"In March 2019 UNESCO announced the programme — by then operating in Sudan, Uganda, Lebanon and Jordan under the name Can't Wait to Learn — as one of the 2018 laureates of its King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for the use of technology in education."*

## Item 3 — a venue no register supports (**both languages**)

**What the piece said.** *"The programme's stated ambition **at the ceremony** — 1.5 million children by 2023 — should be read as what it was, an aim announced rather than a number reached."* Arabic: *«الطموحُ الذي أعلنه البرنامجُ في الحفل»*.

**What the register serves.** The 1.5-million figure is a **direct quotation to UNESCO inside a written feature**:

> "Our aim is to reach 1.5 million children by 2023," says Kate Radford.

There is no ceremony in the register. The article is an interview-led feature published on the prize announcement date; it does not describe an event, and its closing line has Radford *hoping* the recognition will help. The piece invented a setting — a small, plausible, entirely unsourced circumstantial detail, and precisely the kind that a hostile competent reader tests first because it is cheap to check.

Note the direction of the error: the piece was being *careful* here. The whole sentence exists to fence the figure as an aim rather than a reach — and the fence was built on a fact the register does not carry. **A guardrail is a claim and a claim is checked** (#33's corollary, applied to our own hedging).

**Fix (both languages, no figure touched).**

> *"The ambition its director stated to UNESCO in that same account — 1.5 million children by 2023 — should be read as what it was, an aim announced rather than a number reached."*

## Two register-restorations (not items; the source's own words returned to prose)

1. **Egypt, the added nationality.** The piece read *"35,000 Sudanese children and 6,700 teachers"*; the Learning Passport register says *"As of April 2024, the Learning Passport is supporting **35,000 learners** and 6,700 teachers in Egypt."* The paragraph is unmistakably about Sudanese children in Egypt, and the platform's own sentence a line earlier is *"the Learning Passport is also providing **Sudanese curricular content that would otherwise be inaccessible**"* — so nothing false was published. But *learners* is the register's word, *children* is narrower, *Sudanese* is our inference, and the whole standing of this figure is that it is the owner's. Restored in both bodies and both annotations, with the "otherwise inaccessible" clause now carried in the owner's grammar.
2. **Darfur and Kordofan.** The piece read *"with the destruction concentrated in Darfur and Kordofan"*; the UNICEF press release says *"**Disruption has been especially acute** in Darfur and Kordofan, where violence, displacement and repeated attacks on civilian infrastructure have…"*. Two shifts in four words: *disruption → destruction*, and *especially acute → concentrated*, the second a distributional claim about where most of the damage is that the register does not make. Restored in both languages.

## Ruling produced: **#47 — a description has a date too** (guidebook row 42)

Filed as `agents/guidebook/2026-09-10-a-description-has-a-date-too.md`.

**The rule.** Every non-numeric attribute of a programme — what it taught, where it ran, what it was called, what prize it held, where a sentence was said — is dated exactly as strictly as a figure, and inherits the vintage of the register it was read in. A description read at one stage of an instrument's life does not travel to another stage without its own register.

**Why it needed filing.** Our entire figure discipline is numeric: the multiset comparison, the one-sided-figure grep, the prohibited-figure grep, the seven axes. **All three of today's items are invisible to every one of them**, because none of the three is a number in the body. Item 2 was invisible *precisely because the correct number was missing.*

Three corollaries, each bitten today:
1. **An award year and its announcement year are two dates.** The register that names the laureate class is the one that rides.
2. **A setting is a claim about a register.** "At the ceremony", "in a speech", "at the launch" must be read off the register, never inferred from the occasion — the mirror of #43 on the reporting side.
3. **A guardrail is a claim and a claim is checked** (#33 applied to our own hedging). Item 3 was inside a fence the piece built to be careful.
