## The question

The Authority's performance report for 2024–2025 prints the **whole four-grade shape** of its
76-school government batch — **26% Outstanding · 24% Good · 30% Satisfactory · 20% Inadequate** — and
the piece prints only the 26%. Should the sentence state the batch's whole distribution? The drafting
desk recommended the clause and recommended **against** any comparison with the cycle percentages.

## Ruling — **the clause goes in, as the batch's own shape, in both editions. The comparison does not.**

Both halves of the recommendation are adopted, and the second half is the load-bearing one.

**Why the clause.** The piece's entire subject is that a four-point scale is only informative when all
four points are used, and its own evidence for that is a *shape* — `350 / 245 / 202 / 85` over the
register, and five per-cycle shapes under it. Printing one number out of four from a first-party
document that publishes all four, in a piece arguing that the shape is the finding, is the one
omission this argument cannot afford. A reader who is told *26% of the batch was Outstanding* and
nothing else is handed the most flattering of the four and left to assume the rest; the full shape
says 20% of the same batch was told it was Inadequate, which is the piece's own point about the bottom
band, made by the Authority about its newest cohort. **Omitting three of four grades is not
restraint. It is a selection, and an unstated one.**

**Why not the comparison.** Hand-off line 2 of the commission binds: *percentages never set beside
cycle percentages without both clocks*, and the standing prohibition carried out of Edition 05 is
blunter — **no composite of rulers.** A batch of 76 schools reviewed in one academic year and a cycle
of 139 reviews published over 21 months are two instruments. Their four-grade shapes are 26/24/30/20
and 16.5/21.6/40.3/21.6, and set side by side those two rows read as a trend no matter what sentence
is wrapped around them — the batch looks *flatter at the bottom*, which a reader will take as
improvement, and improvement is precisely the claim this piece refuses to make because the floor is
unstated and the cycles are not a panel. The piece already carries the correct sentence for this
ground: *three first-party figures about roughly the same period that do not reduce to one another.*
The batch's shape joins that list as a fourth thing the Authority publishes and does not reduce;
it does not become a row in a table beside a cycle.

**So: the clause is a clause, not a table, and it carries the word *batch* inside it.**

## Binding corrections, applied by the drafter in the same commit as this verdict

* **C4 · The sentence states the batch's four grades as the batch's.** *In the performance report for
  the academic year 2024–2025, a batch of 76 government schools was reviewed under the 2024 framework,
  and the Authority prints that batch's whole four-grade shape: 26% at `(1) Outstanding`, 24% at
  `(2) Good`, 30% at `(3) Satisfactory`, 20% at `(4) Inadequate`.* No cycle percentage appears in the
  same sentence or the next. The existing sentence that holds the 26% apart from cycle five's 16.5%
  stays exactly as it is — it is a statement that two instruments are two instruments, which is the
  opposite of a composite.

* **C5 · The English edition's annotation says where the figure was read, and it is not where the
  English edition's own URL points.** This is the condition on C4 and it is not optional. The complete
  distribution was recovered from the **Arabic** edition of the same report
  (`Performance-Report-24-25-AR.pdf`, 5,091,880 bytes, read 2026-11-06), because this operation's own
  extraction of the **English** edition we cite drops the `Good` label and the `Inadequate`
  percentage — 80 cmap glyphs against 207 — and returns the sentence as *"26% of government schools
  received an Outstrating, 24% , 30% Satisfactory, and 2."* A figure is cited **as read** (CLAUDE.md),
  so annotation 4 of the English edition carries the Arabic edition's URL beside the English one, names
  which edition each of the four percentages was read in, and records the two checks the figure passed:
  against the English edition's surviving fragments, and against its own sum to 100. **The reader is
  told that the publication cites the edition it reads worse.** Under #131 a different rendering is a
  different document until somebody reads it for the claim — somebody has now read this one for this
  claim, and that read is what licenses the figure rather than the pair's existence.

* **C6 · The Arabic edition cites the Arabic edition, and quotes nothing from it.** Confirmed as
  already ruled in the recon's §5 and restated here so the Arabic Editor's gate has one place to look:
  sources 3 and 4 are cited in their Arabic editions with the English URL carried beside each; **no
  Arabic verbatim quotation is taken from an extraction whose letter order this operation reversed**
  (#129 — a normalisation is ours and may never be reported as the register's own). The figures are
  digits, survive the extraction upright, and are cited as read.
