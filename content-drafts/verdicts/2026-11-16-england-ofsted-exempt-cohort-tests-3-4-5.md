## TEST 4 — Dignity. **PASS**, and the structural reason is stronger than the absence of a slip.

> *Is everyone in this piece treated as a person rather than as evidence?*

**No school is named anywhere in the piece.** Not one. The cohort is 367 schools and the piece discusses
every one of them without putting a single one on the record as having lost a grade — which is the whole
dignity exposure of this subject, because a school that fell from *outstanding* has staff and families
reading. The only named human in 3,700 words is **Amanda Spielman**, quoted six times, in office, from
her own regulator's releases.

What earns the pass is not the omission but the section that makes it principled. *What the frozen cohort
does and does not prove* states that **the cohort holds the grade on the record constant and does not
hold the school constant** — leadership, intake, neighbourhood, funding all moved over an average of
thirteen years — and it says so **before** the piece draws its conclusion, not in a hedge afterwards. A
piece about an instrument could easily have read the re-grading as a verdict on people. This one
explicitly refuses to, and routes the finding to the instrument where it belongs.

The Spielman quotations are also the right ones: *"The exemption was a policy founded on the hope that
high standards, once achieved, would never drop"* is a judgment on a **policy**. The piece does not reach
for a quotation about schools failing, and the register offers several.
