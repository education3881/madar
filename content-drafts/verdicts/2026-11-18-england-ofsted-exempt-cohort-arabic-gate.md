## 2 · THE ELEVEN GRADE RENDERINGS, JUDGED AS A SET. APPROVED AS WORDS — AND THE SET HAS A PROVENANCE
## PROBLEM THE COMPOSER COULD NOT SEE FROM INSIDE ONE PIECE.

The eleven are approved. *مُتميّز · جيد · يحتاج إلى تحسين · غير ملائم · تفتيش مُصنَّف · تفتيش غير مُصنَّف ·
تحسين عاجل · يحتاج إلى اهتمام · المستوى المتوقع · مستوى قوي · استثنائي.* Each is a defensible label for a
box on a form rather than a translation of a concept, which is the right frame; the composer's rule —
print the register's English beside the Arabic at first use, never let the Arabic carry a figure alone —
is the correct instrument and is applied throughout.

**What the gate adds, by reading the set against the corpus instead of against the piece.** Two of the
eleven are **already in this publication's served Arabic text, and there they are not ours**:

| Arabic | In this piece | In `2026-10-23-bahrain-bqa-five-cycles` (served) |
|---|---|---|
| **غير ملائم** | our rendering of Ofsted's *inadequate* | **the BQA's own published Arabic grade name**, read out of the Bahraini portal's Arabic CSV export: `(4) غير ملائم` 245 |
| **جيد** | our rendering of Ofsted's *good* | **the BQA's own published Arabic grade name**: `(2) جيد` 202 |

**So the identical Arabic string appears in our corpus with two opposite evidential statuses.** On the
Bahrain piece it is a **quotation** — the regulator publishes in Arabic and we read its bytes. On this
piece it is a **composition** — Ofsted publishes nothing in Arabic and we made the word. A reader meeting
`غير ملائم` on both pages has no way to tell which is which, and the two are not equally challengeable:
one can be checked against a register and the other can only be checked against us.

**That is #101 pointed one level further down.** #101 says an acronym is a name with its evidence removed.
**A composed grade name is a quotation with its evidence removed** — and worse than an acronym, because it
*reads* like a quotation. The composer's first-use gloss solves the reader's other problem (returning to
the ruler) and does not touch this one.

**The words are NOT changed, and the reason is evidential rather than aesthetic.** `غير ملائم` is the only
Arabic bottom-grade name this publication has ever served, and it arrived from a regulator rather than
from us. Inventing a second Arabic word for a bottom grade — غير كافٍ, دون المستوى — to avoid a collision
would make this edition *less* consistent with its own corpus in order to look more careful, and would put
a fresh composition where a register's own word already stands. **Where the two scales genuinely differ the
collision cannot arise anyway:** BQA's four are ممتاز / جيد / مرضٍ / غير ملائم and Ofsted's four are
outstanding / good / requires improvement / inadequate, so *مرضٍ* and *يحتاج إلى تحسين* are each unique to
one instrument, and the two labels that do collide sit at the same rank on both scales (2 of 4 and 4 of 4).
That last fact is a convenience and not a justification, and it is written down here so nobody later reads
it as one.
